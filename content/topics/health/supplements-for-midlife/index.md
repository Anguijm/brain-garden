# Supplements for a midlife body: help, not hurt

This is written for a specific person: a 48-year-old, overweight man who is exercising and
wants to use supplements to get healthier, without hurting himself. The honest answer is
that a small handful genuinely help, a bunch are a waste of money, and a few can do real
damage, especially at your age and weight. This section is the map: what is worth taking,
what is situational, and what to avoid.

You already know the "eat right and exercise" part, so this leads with the supplement
angle. But one thing has to be said plainly first, because it shapes everything else.
Claims are labeled FACT (backed by a named research or regulatory source), Assessment (my
read of the evidence), or Speculation. **None of this is medical advice.**

## The one hard truth

Assessment: supplements are the last five percent. The training you do, the protein you
eat, the sleep you get, and being in a calorie deficit are about ninety-five percent of the
result. No pill changes that, and any product selling you otherwise is selling the five
percent as if it were the whole thing.

That said, the top of the pyramid is not nothing, and there really is a short, cheap stack
worth taking. The rest of this section is which parts are real.

## Safety first, because you asked "not hurt"

Assessment: for your profile specifically, four rules matter more than any single
supplement.

- **Get baseline bloodwork before you lean on anything.** FACT: for a man your age and
  weight, standard screening includes blood pressure, a fasting lipid panel, and a fasting
  glucose or HbA1c, and these are recommended for overweight adults 35 and up. (US
  Preventive Services Task Force.) Ask your doctor to add vitamin D, and liver and kidney
  panels, since those decide whether some supplements are safe for you. Get testosterone
  checked only if you actually have symptoms (low energy, low libido), not on a hunch.
- **If you take any medication, have a pharmacist check interactions.** Assessment: this is
  the cheapest safety step there is. Some "natural" supplements (berberine especially)
  interfere with common blood-pressure, cholesterol, and diabetes drugs.
- **Buy only third-party-tested products.** FACT: the FDA does not approve supplements for
  safety or quality before they are sold, and products regularly get spiked with hidden
  drugs; one analysis found hundreds of adulterated supplements, with weight-loss products
  the worst offenders. (FDA; JAMA Network Open.) Look for a **NSF Certified for Sport**,
  **Informed Sport**, or **USP Verified** seal, which means the contents were actually
  tested.
- **Be careful with stimulants.** Assessment: overweight and mid-40s often means blood
  pressure that runs high, and a hard workout on top of a stimulant "fat burner" is exactly
  the risky combination. Know your resting blood pressure before you touch anything with
  caffeine or other stimulants in it.
