# A simple daily plan

This turns the rest of the section into a concrete, do-this routine, built for your profile
and kept deliberately small. The earlier chapters explain the *why* and cite the evidence;
this one is just the *what, when, and roughly how much*. Start with the minimal version and
add from there.

## Before day one

Assessment: do these once, up front, because they decide what is even safe for you.

1. **Get a blood panel and a checkup.** Blood pressure, cholesterol, fasting glucose or
   HbA1c, vitamin D, and liver and kidney panels. This tells you what actually needs fixing
   (and whether you are low on vitamin D or high on triglycerides, which change the plan).
   See the [overview](../supplements-for-midlife) for why.
2. **Show a pharmacist your medications.** If you take anything for blood pressure,
   cholesterol, or blood sugar, have them check it against what you plan to add.
3. **Buy tested products only.** Look for a NSF Certified for Sport, Informed Sport, or USP
   Verified seal. See the [harms chapter](03-weight-loss-and-harms) for why this matters.

## The daily shape

Assessment: here is a whole day, with each item slotted into something you already do so you
do not have to think about it.

![Diagram: a one-day timeline with five stops, morning with breakfast (vitamin D3 if low, omega-3 if needed, creatine), pre-workout (caffeine, only if training and kept early), post-workout (a protein shake or protein meal), dinner (psyllium fiber with a full glass of water before the meal), and bedtime (magnesium glycinate and optional glycine, no caffeine)](img/daily-timeline.png)
*One day, what to take and when. Diagram.*

| When | Take | Dose | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning, with breakfast | Creatine | 3-5 g | strength and muscle; timing does not matter, so this is just an easy slot |
| Morning, with breakfast | Vitamin D3 (if you tested low) | 1,000-2,000 IU | with food, since it needs fat to absorb |
| Morning, with breakfast | Omega-3 (only if triglycerides are high, or you skip fish) | 1-2 g EPA+DHA | with food to avoid fishy burps |
| 30-60 min pre-workout | Caffeine (optional) | 3-6 mg/kg of goal weight | performance; keep it early, never within 8-10 h of bed |
| After training | Protein shake | ~20-40 g | fills the gap to your daily protein target |
| Before dinner | Psyllium fiber | ~5 g, ramping up | with a full glass of water; keep it 2 h from any pills |
| Bedtime | Magnesium glycinate (optional) | 200-400 mg | if your diet is low or your BP runs high |
| Bedtime | Glycine (optional) | ~3 g | a cheap, low-risk sleep nudge |

Assessment: protein is the one that is really about the whole day, not a single slot. Aim
for your daily target (built around your goal weight, see the
[core-stack chapter](01-core-stack)) across your meals, and use one or two shakes only to
fill whatever gap your food leaves.

## Three levels, so you are not doing it all at once

Assessment: do not start eight things on the same day. Build up.

**Level 1, the minimal version (do this first).** Creatine every day, hit your protein
target (with a shake or two to fill gaps), and fix your sleep. That is most of the benefit in
this whole section, for very little money, and none of it is risky.

**Level 2, add once Level 1 is a habit.** Vitamin D3 if your blood test came back low, and
psyllium fiber before your biggest meal.

**Level 3, optional extras.** Magnesium at night if your diet is short on greens and nuts or
your blood pressure is creeping up; omega-3 if your triglycerides are high; caffeine before
workouts; a sleep aid (glycine or magnesium) if you want one. Add these one at a time, a few
days apart, so if something disagrees with you, you know which one it was.

## Roughly what it costs

Assessment: rough monthly ballparks, in US dollars, buying decent third-party-tested
products in normal sizes. Your mileage will vary.

| Item | Rough monthly cost |
|---|---|
| Creatine | ~\$5-10 |
| Whey protein (1-2 scoops/day) | ~\$30-45 |
| Vitamin D3 | ~\$3-5 |
| Psyllium | ~\$5-10 |
| Magnesium glycinate | ~\$5-10 |
| Omega-3 | ~\$10-15 |

Assessment: the minimal Level 1 stack (creatine plus protein) runs roughly \$35-55 a month,
and most of that is the protein, which is really food money, not a supplement splurge. The
fuller stack lands around \$60-90 a month. Compare that to a single tub of a "muscle defense"
or "fat burner" product at \$70+ that does less, and the cheap, boring stack wins easily.

## How to run it

Assessment: a few rules keep this simple and honest.

- **Start low and ramp** the two that can upset your stomach, creatine and psyllium. A few
  grams to begin, building up over a week or two.
- **Add one new thing at a time.** It is the only way to tell what actually agrees with you.
- **Give it weeks, not days.** These are slow, small effects. Creatine takes a few weeks to
  build up, and everything here is a nudge, not a switch.
- **Judge by the right things.** Waist size, how your strength is climbing, energy, and sleep,
  measured about once a month. Ignore the daily scale, especially early, since creatine adds a
  couple of pounds of water that is not fat.
- **Re-check your bloodwork** in a few months to see what actually moved, and to re-dose
  vitamin D to your number rather than guessing.

## The honest reminder

Assessment: none of this replaces the real work. The plan sits on top of lifting, eating
enough protein, sleeping well, and staying in a calorie deficit, which is where almost all
of your results will come from. What this daily routine does is add a few genuine helpers
without wasting money or hurting you, which was the whole point. Keep it small, keep it
consistent, and let the boring basics do the heavy lifting.

This is general information, not medical advice; run it past your doctor or pharmacist,
especially with any health conditions or medications.
