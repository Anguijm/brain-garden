# Navy availability milestones: the planner's calendar from advance planning to close-out

This note is the calendar of a Navy maintenance availability as the planner sees it: the
named milestones in order, what each one fixes, and when it falls relative to the start
date. It also gives the formal definitions of unplanned work, growth work and new work
and says who approves them, because those three terms decide what a yard can be paid for
after the package is locked. The companion note, [Navy maintenance availabilities: the types and who performs
them](topics/shipyards/availability-types), explains the availability types themselves.

## Reading the calendar

Every milestone is measured from the A-date, the first day of the availability, or the
C-date, its completion. FACT: the start of availability is "The first day of the
production period for the executing activity"
([JFMM Vol II, Part II, ch. 2, Appendix D1, row 68](https://web.archive.org/web/20250312071433id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume_II.pdf),
Wayback copy of the NAVSEA PDF, read 2026-09-05). Submarine and carrier calendars
count in months, surface-ship calendars in days.

The Joint Fleet Maintenance Manual (JFMM) publishes four calendars: Appendix A of Part
I, chapter 3 for submarines, Appendix C for carriers, and in Part II, chapter 2,
Appendix D1 for a surface ship under a cost-type contract definitized just before the
start and Appendix D2 for one under a firm-fixed-price (FFP) contract awarded at A-120.
Surface offsets step outward with size, banded at \$5, \$20 and \$100 million. The actors
are the TYCOM (Type Commander, the admiral who owns the ships), SURFMEPP and SUBMEPP
(the Surface and Submarine Maintenance Engineering Planning Programs, keepers of the
class plans), the RMC (Regional Maintenance Center, which acts as Naval Supervising
Authority, NSA) and the LMA (Lead Maintenance Activity, the yard holding the contract).

## Advance planning

The package begins as a class-plan list long before anyone prices it. FACT: the five
stages of Availability Work Package (AWP) development "include Baseline, Preliminary,
Proposed, Approved and Completed"
([JFMM Vol II, Part I, ch. 3, para 3.4.3.3](https://web.archive.org/web/20250312071433id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume_II.pdf),
read 2026-09-05). FACT: for surface ships "BAWPs are developed approximately 22 months
prior to the start of the next CNO availability"
([RAND RR1155, 2015](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_reports/RR1100/RR1155/RAND_RR1155.pdf),
read 2026-09-05). FACT: SURFMEPP's Mid-Cycle Review at A-410 is the "final
verification prior to the BAWP change to the AWP," the BAWP Turnover Letter follows at
A-360 with the Ship Program Manager's Initial Letter of Authorization (LOA, the
modernization list), and "Funding must be received NLT A-365 to ensure Work Item
development and LLTM identification can start at A-360," where LLTM, long lead time
material, "is defined as any material with a delivery date in excess of 30 days" (JFMM
Vol II, Appendix D1, rows 10, 11, 13, 15 and 21, above).

Assessment: A-360 is the day the package becomes the RMC's problem instead of
SURFMEPP's, and the earliest point at which a yard can learn what will be in it.

## Work package definition and the locks

The package is locked in three steps, each a share of the budgeted dollars. FACT: the
intent is that "only 50% of the work, measured by 50% of the budgeted funds... be
'locked' at the 50% lock"
([JFMM Vol II, Part II, ch. 2, para 2.5](https://web.archive.org/web/20250312071433id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume_II.pdf),
read 2026-09-05). FACT: in the cost-contract calendar the 50 percent lock falls at
A-240, the 80 percent lock between A-120 and A-155 by size, and the 100 percent D-level
lock ("100% of D-level maintenance work package 2K's locked based on target control")
between A-75 for availabilities under \$5 million and A-140 above \$100 million;
modernization locks separately at A-120 ("LOA CH 2, 100% Modernization Lock") and the
ship's own work at A-75 (JFMM Vol II, Appendix D1, rows 22, 36, 38, 46 and 47, above).
A 2K is the OPNAV 4790/2K form, the maintenance action request every work item starts
life as.

What the lock means is spelled out. FACT: "The 100% package lock is the official
milestone to mark identification of 100% of the work requirements for an availability
based on the MMBP budget. All work added to or deleted from the package after the 100%
lock will be via errata, addendum or inducted as new work via the Request for Contract
Change Process"
([JFMM Vol II, Part II, ch. 2, para 2.5.4.a](https://web.archive.org/web/20250312071433id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume_II.pdf),
read 2026-09-05). MMBP is the Maintenance and Modernization Business Plan, the budget
the lock is measured against.

The fixed-price calendar locks a full year out. FACT: the TYCOM issues its 100 percent
Lock Letter at A-365, "the complete list of all TYCOM work with the Job Sequence Numbers
that are screened and brokered to the identified CNO availability," and "Additional work
added between package lock (A-365) and A-240 must be locked by A-180" as a Supplemental
Package (JFMM Vol II, Appendix D2, rows 55 and 95, above).

Assessment: the lock a yard should plan to is the 100 percent D-level lock, because the
JFMM says everything after it moves by contract change: two and a half to four and a
half months out under a cost contract, a year out under the A-120 fixed-price model
with a second lock at A-180. Once the contract is awarded or definitized, whatever
arrives is growth or new work, whatever it is called in the meeting.

## Definitization, award and readiness to start

Under a cost-type contract the price is settled just before the ship arrives. FACT:
"Definitize Work Package" is "The culmination of the contractual planning efforts
resulting in a negotiated price for the availability that has been funded via a
supplemental agreement. This step contractually obligates both the government and
contractor to the requirements of the Work Package," at A-35 under \$20 million and A-30
above (JFMM Vol II, Appendix D1, row 65, above). FACT: under the fixed-price model the
LMA Contract Award at A-120 "establishes a legal obligation," with a Supplemental
Package award targeted at A-60 (Appendix D2, rows 121 and 133, above). FACT: the
project then gives a Readiness to Start brief
that includes "A 100-hour beginning of the availability strategy"
([JFMM Vol II, Part I, ch. 3, para 3.2.3](https://web.archive.org/web/20250312071433id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume_II.pdf),
read 2026-09-05). FACT: the A-35 Readiness to Start message for private yards and the
Final Review Estimate (FRE) for public yards "document CNO availability start and end
dates prior to availability start" (para 3.8.a, above).

## Execution: A-date to undocking

FACT: the arrival conference "is scheduled shortly after the start of an availability
and conducted by the industrial activity" (JFMM Vol II, para 3.6.2.1, above). FACT:
for surface ships the NSA and LMA "must conduct 25, 50 and 75 percent completion
conferences," "All open and inspect work must be completed in the first 20 percent of
the maintenance availability," and the LMA reports open-and-inspect results two days
before the 25 percent review "so final adjudication on any growth or new work items may
be completed" (para 3.6.3.4, above). FACT: for a docking availability the industrial
activity holds "both a Docking and Undocking Conference, normally within one week prior
to the expected evolution" (para 3.6.3.3, above).

## Completion and after

Completion is a sequence of trials, not a date. FACT: Fast Cruise "must follow Dock
Trials and precede Sea Trials" (JFMM Vol II, para 3.6.8.1, above), and for surface
ships and carriers "the official end of a CNO Availability will be upon Certification of
work... and completion of the TYCOM approved Sea Trials Agenda"; a submarine
availability is complete when the TYCOM sends the Unrestricted Operations (URO) message
(para 3.7, above). FACT: Days of Maintenance Delay "is defined as the number of days
that an availability extends beyond the originally established end of the availability
or completion date at the start of the availability" (para 3.8.a, above).

FACT: the Departure and Assessment Conference at C+0 is held to "establish the date and
conditions under which the TYCOM and NAVSEA will accept redelivery of the ship," the
Completion Report is due at C+60, the SURFMEPP BAWP Close-Out Meeting at C+70, and the
Completed Work Package within six months (Appendix D1, rows 83 to 85, and para 3.10.1,
above).

## Unplanned, growth and new work

The JFMM defines new work by the contract clock. FACT: "New work is any maintenance
requiring industrial level assistance which is not authorized until after contract award
or definitization," it must be requested from the TYCOM by message or letter, and "The
TYCOM, or the formally designated TYCOM representative, must be the final approving
authority for all new work. For Surface Force Ships only, the NSA Chief Engineer will
review requested new work items and determine whether or not they are technically
required," and for a private-yard availability "the TYCOM will certify that, per Federal
Acquisition Regulations, the accomplishment of the new work is of such unusual and
compelling urgency so as to require waiving of Competition In Contracting Act public
law regulations" (JFMM Vol II, para 3.6.7, above).

The Navy's definitions of growth and new work, as reported by the Government
Accountability Office (GAO), turn on whether the job relates to an existing item. FACT:
"The Navy defines growth work as additional work that is identified or authorized after
contract award that is related to a work item included in the original contract... the
Navy defines new work as any additional work that is identified or authorized after
contract award that is not related to a work item included in the original contract,"
and together they are "unplanned work, which consists of both growth work and new work"
([GAO-20-370, pp. 17-18](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-20-370.pdf), read 2026-09-05).
For the public yards the line is the finalized package, not a contract. FACT:
"Approximately 2 months prior to the start of work on the ships, these planners
finalize the detailed work package and any changes to the detailed work package from
that point forward are considered unplanned work," and "detailed work packages include
a reserve for new work, typically 5 to 10 percent"
([GAO-20-588](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-20-588.pdf), read 2026-09-05).

Approval runs through a business case. FACT: "During the execution of an availability,
growth and new work should be authorized only if the Business Case Analysis indicates
that this is the best course of action," and "Prior to definitization, growth items
that cannot be settled by the Project Team must be forwarded to the Technical Analyst
to be settled in the TAR process"
([JFMM Vol VI, ch. 31, paras 31.3.2.c and 31.4.3.k](https://web.archive.org/web/20241119222606id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume%20VI.pdf),
Wayback copy, read 2026-09-05). TAR is the Technical Analysis Report, the government's
written review of a proposal for scope and fair cost before negotiation (JFMM Vol VI,
para 31.3.2.f, above). FACT: for service craft the TYCOM will "Authorize all new
industrial work items" and "Authorize all growth in an existing industrial work item"
(JFMM Vol VI, ch. 11, para 11.6.1, above).

Assessment: the operational difference is who signs. Growth on an existing item is
settled by the project team, through the TAR if it is still before definitization; new
work needs a TYCOM signature, a business case and, at a private yard, an urgency
certification. A finding tied to an existing work item has the shorter path, and the
open-and-inspect deadline in the first 20 percent is when that tying has to be done.

## How much to trust this

Assessment: the offsets are transcribed from the JFMM's own appendix tables, read from
a Wayback Machine copy of the current NAVSEA PDF (Rev D Change 4, September 2024)
because navsea.navy.mil refuses this network. They are policy, not a record of what
happens. FACT: in the Navy's destroyer data the last schedule, "labeled Y or
'Definitization/Award'," "could be recorded one to six months before an overhaul begins"
([CBO, Maintenance Delays for Conventional Navy Ships, Dec 2025, footnote 5](https://web.archive.org/web/20260122074505id_/https://www.cbo.gov/system/files/2025-12/61507-ship-maintenance.pdf),
Wayback copy, read 2026-09-05), so the A-30 to A-35 definitization date is a target the
record does not always hit. Three things are less certain. The calendar is the
surface-ship one; the submarine and carrier appendices were only skimmed, so their
intermediate milestones are not in the table. The growth definition comes from GAO
reporting the Navy's definition, because the JFMM defines new work but never growth
work as a standalone term; the closest it comes is the service-craft chapter's "growth
in an existing industrial work item." And the two close-out offsets disagree inside the
JFMM (C+45 in the chapter text, C+70 in the appendix), which is reported as found.
