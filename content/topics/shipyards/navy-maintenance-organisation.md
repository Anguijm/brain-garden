# Who runs US Navy ship maintenance: fleets, NAVSEA, RMCs and the yards

This note maps the offices behind a Navy ship availability, so that when a hull shows up in a schedule or a contract announcement the reader can tell who owns the ship, who owns the money, who wrote the work package, who signed the contract and who checks the work on the pier. Every claim is labelled FACT, Assessment, Speculation or Unverified.

**Read this first (added 2026-09-05).** The NAVSEA side of this chart is the one the GAO reports and the Navy's manuals describe, and it was the chart in force until March 2026. In 2026 the Navy moved the public shipyards and the regional maintenance centers under a new office, Portfolio Acquisition Executive Industrial Operations, moved the SEA 21 programme offices and SURFMEPP under Portfolio Acquisition Executive Maritime, and split the Supervisors of Shipbuilding between PAE Maritime and the submarine portfolio. The fleet side (fleet commanders, Type Commanders, the money) has not moved. What changed, who runs the new offices and what is still unconfirmed is in [Portfolio Acquisition Executives](topics/shipyards/portfolio-acquisition-executives). The sections below are kept as written because the manuals a yard works to still name these offices, and because the RMC at the pier is the same office under a different boss.

## The shape of the system

Two chains meet at every availability. The fleets own the ships and the money: U.S. Fleet Forces Command on the Atlantic side and U.S. Pacific Fleet on the Pacific side, each acting through a Type Commander. Naval Sea Systems Command (NAVSEA), the Navy's engineering and acquisition command for ships, owns the technical rules, the contracts and the government-run yards. FACT: the Government Accountability Office (GAO) puts it this way: "U.S. Pacific Fleet and U.S. Fleet Forces Command develop budgets for the operations and maintenance of ships" ([GAO-17-54](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-17-54.pdf), read 2026-09-05), while NAVSEA offices "perform contract administration, program management, and planning for future maintenance periods" ([GAO-22-104510](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-22-104510.pdf), read 2026-09-05). Assessment: the regional maintenance center at each homeport is where the two chains touch, which is why it is the office a repair yard deals with most.

## NAVSEA SEA 21 and the Commander, Navy Regional Maintenance Center

SEA 21 is the NAVSEA directorate that owns surface ships after delivery, and its director also commands the regional maintenance centers. FACT: GAO describes "NAVSEA's Director for Surface Ship Maintenance and Modernization. This office, known as NAVSEA21, provides life-cycle management for surface ships and manages critical modernization, maintenance, training, and inactivation programs" ([GAO-22-104510](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-22-104510.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: in February 2025 Rear Adm. William Greene "holds dual responsibilities as both CNRMC and Navy Seas Systems Command's Director of Surface Ship Maintenance, Modernization, and Sustainability", and Capt. Dan Lannamann was named to relieve him as CNRMC ([DVIDS, SRF-JRMC change of command](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/490220/srf-jrmc-hosts-change-command), read 2026-09-05). FACT: by June 2026 he held the post as "Rear Adm. Dan Lannamann, commander of Navy Regional Maintenance Center (CNRMC)" ([USNI News, 2026-06-04](https://news.usni.org/2026/06/04/leaders-of-u-s-navys-ship-maintenance-facility-in-japan-removed-from-command), read 2026-09-05), so the headquarters was still operating three months after the Portfolio Acquisition Executive for Industrial Operations stood up.

CNRMC, the Commander, Navy Regional Maintenance Center, is the headquarters over the regional maintenance centers, created in 2010 when the Navy decided surface maintenance needed one owner. FACT: GAO records the Navy "establishing SURFMEPP and the Commander, Naval Regional Maintenance Center (CNRMC) in 2010 to oversee the operations of the regional maintenance centers" ([GAO-17-54](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-17-54.pdf), read 2026-09-05). SURFMEPP is the Surface Ship Maintenance Engineering Planning Program, the office that writes the class maintenance plan and the baseline work package for each surface availability; FACT: it "provides centralized life cycle maintenance engineering, as well as class maintenance and modernization planning" ([GAO-25-106990](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-25-106990.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: in 2023 the Navy said CNRMC "oversees seven regional maintenance centers" ([DVIDS, Greene visits FDRMC](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/458399/commander-us-navy-surface-ship-maintenance-visits-forward-deployed-maintenance-locations), read 2026-09-05). Assessment: the work package comes from SURFMEPP and the money from the fleet, so a question about scope and a question about funding go to different offices even though both arrive through the RMC. Since July 2026 SURFMEPP and the SEA 21 programme offices sit in PAE Maritime's Ship Readiness office while the RMCs sit in PAE Industrial Operations, so those two offices no longer share a boss inside NAVSEA; see [Portfolio Acquisition Executives](topics/shipyards/portfolio-acquisition-executives).

## The regional maintenance centers

An RMC is the Navy's maintenance office at a homeport, and not all of them sit in the same chain. FACT: CNRMC "oversees the regional maintenance centers in the United States, including the Mid-Atlantic RMC in Norfolk, VA; Southeast RMC in Mayport, FL; and the Southwest RMC in San Diego, CA", is "responsible for the coordination of maintenance activities" at the Northwest RMC inside Puget Sound Naval Shipyard and the Hawaii RMC "embedded in" Pearl Harbor Naval Shipyard, and "manages overseas locations such as the Forward Deployed Regional Maintenance Center headquartered in Italy" ([GAO-22-104510](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-22-104510.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: in 2022 GAO placed the Japan center outside CNRMC, under NAVSEA's Logistics, Maintenance and Industrial Operations directorate, which "also known as NAVSEA 04, manages and oversees the Navy's shipyards and the Ship Repair Facility and Japan Regional Maintenance Center in Yokosuka, Japan, and its detachment in Sasebo, Japan" (same source). That placement has a date on it and is no longer current. FACT (operator, first-hand, 2026-09-05): SRF-JRMC has moved between CNRMC and NAVSEA 04 several times and currently sits in the CNRMC family. FACT: on 3 June 2026 the yard's commanding officer, executive officer and command master chief were relieved by "Rear Adm. Dan Lannamann, commander of Navy Regional Maintenance Center (CNRMC)" ([USNI News, 2026-06-04](https://news.usni.org/2026/06/04/leaders-of-u-s-navys-ship-maintenance-facility-in-japan-removed-from-command), read 2026-09-05). Assessment: relieving a commanding officer is a parent command's act, so the CNRMC placement is on the public record as well as on the operator's word. The section after the table gives the full history and says why the Japan center is not like the other six.

Assessment: Everett is served through the Bremerton yard's Everett detachment rather than a separate RMC, which is why the table lists it under NWRMC by inference; no staged source says so in those words.

## SRF-JRMC is a shipyard with an RMC's name

The Japan center is not a smaller copy of Norfolk's or San Diego's RMC. It is a government-run industrial yard that does most of the depot work itself, and it runs on the public shipyards' systems rather than the surface-fleet contracting toolset. The domestic RMCs administer contracts and run intermediate-level shops; the depot work at those homeports goes to the private yards on the multiple-award contracts described below. The operator stated all of this from first-hand knowledge on 2026-09-05, and most of it was then checked against NAVSEA's own pages for the command, the Fleet's maintenance manual and Navy releases. What the public record confirms is cited; what still rests on his word is labelled "operator, first-hand".

What the yard is. FACT: NAVSEA's own description of the Yokosuka command calls it "a medium-sized, full-service, non-nuclear naval shipyard providing modernization, maintenance and repair through depot-level maintenance availabilities (including dry-dockings), Continuous Maintenance (CM), Fleet Technical Assistance (FTA), and emergent/voyage repairs (VR)" ([NAVSEA, SRF-JRMC Yokosuka description, undated](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Home/RMC/SRFJRMC/JapanTours/WorkingInJapan/SRFRMCYokoDescription.aspx), read 2026-09-05), and its history page says it has "the capability and capacity of a medium-sized U.S. Naval public shipyard" with "six dry docks with a combined displacement of 530,000 tons, 19 wet berth locations, 10 industrial buildings with combined workshop space of 730,000 square feet" ([NAVSEA, SRF-JRMC Our History, undated](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Home/RMC/SRF-JRMC/About-Us/Our-History/), read 2026-09-05). FACT: GAO says the Navy supports the ships based in Yokosuka with "both private contractors and its Ship Repair Facility and Japan Regional Maintenance Center, which is subsidized by the government of Japan" ([GAO-20-86](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-20-86), read 2026-09-05). FACT: its production department is organised as shipyard shops, among them Shipfitter Shop 11, Sheetmetal Shop 17, Welding Shop 26, Machinist Shop 31, Machine Shop 38, Boiler Shop 41, Electrical Shop 51, Pipefitting Shop 56, Electronics and Weapons Shop 67 and Paint Shop 71 ([NAVSEA, SRF-JRMC Production Department, undated](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Home/RMC/SRF-JRMC/Departments/Production/), read 2026-09-05). Assessment: those are the naval shipyards' shop numbers, so a trade at Yokosuka is named the way a trade at Puget Sound is, not the way an RMC or a private yard names one.

Who does the work. FACT: the command has "120 service members, 325 military civilians and about 2,500 Japanese nationals, according to Naval Sea Systems Command" ([USNI News, 2026-06-04](https://news.usni.org/2026/06/04/leaders-of-u-s-navys-ship-maintenance-facility-in-japan-removed-from-command), read 2026-09-05). FACT: Master Labor Contract employees "are Japanese Government employees assigned to support the U.S. military in Japan and make up over 90% of the command. On average, 70% of the maintenance performed by SRF-JRMC in Yokosuka is done by MLCs with the remainder completed by U.S. and Japanese contractors" ([DVIDS, SRF-JRMC completes its second CVN hangar mezzanine installation, 2025-05-01](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/496772/srf-jrmc-completes-its-second-cvn-hanger-mezzanine-installation), read 2026-09-05). The Master Labor Contract is the agreement under which Japanese nationals work at U.S. bases in Japan; no copy of it was read for this note. FACT: NAVSEA's careers page says the foreign-national workforce "comprises the bulk of the SRF workforce" and that U.S. civil servants provide the "engineering, technical, supervisory, advisory, managerial, financial, security and other services that cannot be delegated to foreign nationals" ([SRF-JRMC Yokosuka description](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Home/RMC/SRFJRMC/JapanTours/WorkingInJapan/SRFRMCYokoDescription.aspx), read 2026-09-05). So the operator's "a good portion of the depot work in house" has a public number attached: about seven tenths of it, done by a Japanese government workforce that is the yard's own.

How it is paid. FACT: the Fleet's maintenance manual gives the yard its own funding code, "BG SRF MISSION FUNDED", listed directly after "BF NAVAL SHIPYARD MISSION FUNDED" and apart from the codes for Type Commander, intermediate-level and contract work ([JFMM Vol II, Part II ch. 1, Appendix A, para 6, Wayback copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20250312071433id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume_II.pdf), read 2026-09-05). Mission funding means the activity is given a budget to exist and do its assigned work, the way the four public yards are, rather than being paid job by job under a contract. FACT: when NAVSEA took over the yard's maintenance functions in 2018, "USPACFLT will retain administrative control and budget authority for SRF-JRMC" ([U.S. Pacific Fleet, NAVSEA assumes maintenance functions for SRF-JRMC, 2018-10-01](https://www.cpf.navy.mil/Newsroom/News/Article/2665533/navsea-assumes-maintenance-functions-for-srf-jrmc/), read 2026-09-05), and the yard's organisation chart still puts the Pacific Fleet maintenance officer above the commanding officer ([NAVSEA, SRF-JRMC Yokosuka organisation chart, undated](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SRF_JRMC/SRF-JRMC%20Org%20Chart.pdf), read 2026-09-05). Assessment: that is the structural reason the yard behaves like a shipyard and not like an RMC. Its work is assigned and funded, not competed and awarded, and its money comes from the fleet that owns the ships.

Who supervises and who executes. The two roles that define an availability are held by the same organisation at Yokosuka. FACT: the Naval Supervisory Authority is "the single Naval activity ... responsible for the integration, oversight and verification of all work accomplished by all activities" in an availability, and the Lead Maintenance Activity is "The single activity responsible for work being accomplished on U.S. Naval ships during any type of availability" ([JFMM Vol II, Part I ch. 2, paras 2.1.1 and 2.1.2, Wayback copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20250312071433id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume_II.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: NAVSEA's page for the yard's engineering department says its chief engineer "Serves as warranted technical authority representing NAVSEA 05 via written warrant" and that "Via naval message designation, SRF-JRMC holds Naval Supervisory Activity authority in all of 7th Fleet with some Naval Support Activity duties delegated to CLWP" ([NAVSEA, SRF-JRMC Engineering and Planning Department, undated](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Home/RMC/SRF-JRMC/Departments/Engineering-and-Planning/), read 2026-09-05). The page's wording wanders between "Supervisory Activity" and "Support Activity", but the role it describes, held by warrant and message, is the manual's Naval Supervisory Authority; CLWP is Commander, Logistics Group Western Pacific, in Singapore. The same page says the yard's chief planner "Reviews and maintains NAVSEA Standard Items (SI), and develops and issues SRF-JRMC local SI", which is a shipyard's job, not a contract office's. FACT: the yard's project superintendents "Plans and executes all emergent and planned shipboard repairs" ([NAVSEA, SRF-JRMC Waterfront Operations Department, undated](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Home/RMC/SRF-JRMC/Departments/Waterfront-Operations/), read 2026-09-05). FACT (operator, first-hand, 2026-09-05): SRF-JRMC is both the Naval Supervisory Authority and the Lead Maintenance Activity for every availability at Yokosuka except the carrier's. Assessment: the public pages support that reading without stating it in those words. The supervisory warrant is documented, the executing activity is the yard's own superintendents and shops, and the manual's rule that a naval shipyard acting as supervisory authority must "Also serve as the LMA" ([JFMM Vol II, Part I ch. 2, para 2.1.1 (5)(a)](https://web.archive.org/web/20250312071433id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume_II.pdf), read 2026-09-05) describes the arrangement even though the manual's table of who may hold each role does not name SRF-JRMC. In plain terms, at a domestic homeport the RMC supervises and a private yard does the work; at Yokosuka the same organisation does both, which is how a public shipyard runs an availability.

The carrier is the exception, and the reason is the reactor. FACT: "Puget Sound Naval Shipyard and Intermediate Maintenance Facility (PSNS & IMF) established a Yokosuka detachment in 2006 to maintain the forward deployed nuclear powered aircraft carrier (CVN) that arrived in Yokosuka in 2008", the detachment "has a U.S. civil service (USCS) workforce of 70 to 75 personnel", and "CVN depot-level repair and maintenance is accomplished by a temporary duty (TDY) workforce provided primarily by PSNS & IMF and augmented by Norfolk Naval Shipyard" ([SRF-JRMC Yokosuka description](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Home/RMC/SRFJRMC/JapanTours/WorkingInJapan/SRFRMCYokoDescription.aspx), read 2026-09-05). FACT: SRF-JRMC's own Carrier Program Office, Code 312, "Plans and executes all depot-level maintenance on USS Ronald Reagan (CVN 76)" and "Coordinates with all outside entities in support of carrier repairs" ([SRF-JRMC Waterfront Operations Department](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Home/RMC/SRF-JRMC/Departments/Waterfront-Operations/), read 2026-09-05; the page was written while Reagan was the Yokosuka carrier). FACT: in April 2025 the yard installed a hangar mezzanine on USS George Washington with "workers from almost every shop in the command", a job of "over 5,000 man-days" ([DVIDS, mezzanine installation](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/496772/srf-jrmc-completes-its-second-cvn-hanger-mezzanine-installation), read 2026-09-05). FACT (operator, first-hand, 2026-09-05): for carrier availabilities Puget Sound Naval Shipyard is the Naval Supervisory Authority and SRF-JRMC is the topside Lead Maintenance Activity and integrator. No public page read says which of the two holds the supervisory warrant for the carrier. What the pages do show is the same split seen from outside: a nuclear yard's detachment and travelling workforce for the carrier's depot repair, and the local yard planning, executing and coordinating the rest. Assessment: "topside" is this desk's reading of the operator's word, meaning the non-nuclear work outside the propulsion plant, the part a yard without a nuclear qualification can take.

The dock. FACT (operator, first-hand, 2026-09-05): the Yokosuka dry dock has not been able to take a carrier since USS Kitty Hawk, the conventionally powered carrier the nuclear one replaced in 2008. Two public descriptions are consistent with that without saying it. FACT: NAVSEA's history page says the docks "can support timely dry-docking of most ships in the U.S. Navy inventory" ([SRF-JRMC Our History](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Home/RMC/SRF-JRMC/About-Us/Our-History/), read 2026-09-05), most and not all, and an undated GlobalSecurity profile written while the Kitty Hawk group was still there says "SRF can handle any ship in the U.S. Navy up to and including conventional aircraft carriers" ([GlobalSecurity, Yokosuka](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/facility/yokosuka.htm), read 2026-09-05; undated, describes the pre-2008 base). What follows for the reader: a Yokosuka carrier availability is a pier-side one, and the docking availability the ship eventually needs lands on a stateside nuclear yard's schedule.

The system. FACT (operator, first-hand, 2026-09-05): SRF-JRMC plans and manages its work in AIM rather than in NDE. AIM is Advanced Industrial Management, the work-planning and execution system the naval shipyards use; NDE is the Navy Data Environment, the database behind the surface fleet's availability planning, which feeds the Navy Maintenance Database the RMCs and private yards report contract work through. FACT: the manual's cost-reporting rules draw exactly that line, with one paragraph for "Private Shipyards Using the Navy Maintenance Database" and the next for "Naval Shipyards and Facilities Using Advanced Industrial Management (AIM) or AIM Express" ([JFMM Vol II, Part II ch. 3, paras 3.4.1.1 and 3.4.1.2](https://web.archive.org/web/20250312071433id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume_II.pdf), read 2026-09-05). No public page read names the system the yard runs on, so this one stays on the operator's word.

Where it reports has moved, and the public record shows three of the stops. FACT: on 1 October 2018 NAVSEA "formally assumed responsibility for ship maintenance availabilities" at SRF-JRMC "from Commander, U.S. Pacific Fleet (USPACFLT)", a change the release tied to "the 2017 Comprehensive Review and Strategic Readiness Review", and the same release counted the yard separately from "the Navy's four public shipyards and seven regional maintenance centers" ([U.S. Pacific Fleet, 2018-10-01](https://www.cpf.navy.mil/Newsroom/News/Article/2665533/navsea-assumes-maintenance-functions-for-srf-jrmc/), read 2026-09-05; the same text ran in [Seapower](https://seapowermagazine.org/navsea-assumes-maintenance-functions-for-srf-jrmc/) the same day). FACT: in 2022 GAO placed it under NAVSEA 04, cited above. FACT: on 3 June 2026 its leadership was relieved by the CNRMC commander, and "Master Chief Petty Officer David Marcus, CNRMC command master chief, has temporarily assumed the duties of SRF-JRMC command master chief" ([USNI News, 2026-06-04](https://news.usni.org/2026/06/04/leaders-of-u-s-navys-ship-maintenance-facility-in-japan-removed-from-command), read 2026-09-05). FACT (operator, first-hand, 2026-09-05): it has moved between CNRMC and NAVSEA 04 several times and currently sits in the CNRMC family. Assessment: the 2026 reliefs put the CNRMC placement on the public record; the number of moves is the operator's, and the public stops are fleet (to 2018), NAVSEA 04 (2022) and CNRMC (2026). One correction to this note's own earlier reasoning: CNRMC's "seven regional maintenance centers" in 2023 cannot be used to show that Japan is one of them, because NAVSEA also counted "seven regional maintenance centers" in 2018 while naming SRF-JRMC separately.

Assessment (the operator's, 2026-09-05, recorded here because it is the point): the gap between SRF-JRMC and the other six RMCs is large enough that it gets in the way of sharing resources and techniques between them. What follows for a reader: a method or a staffing fix that works at Norfolk or San Diego is an RMC-and-contractor method, and it does not transfer to Yokosuka without being re-cut for a yard that supervises and executes its own work on a different system; and a fact about "the RMCs" in a Navy release or a GAO report should be read as a fact about the six unless it names Japan.

## What an RMC does for a private yard's contract

For a repair yard, the RMC is the government's contract administrator and quality checker for surface ship work at that homeport, though the contract vehicle itself is signed in Washington. FACT: MARMC "is responsible for providing surface ship maintenance, management and oversight of private sector maintenance and fleet technical assistance", with core areas of "Fleet Technical Assistance, Depot-level Contractor Administration and Quality Assurance, and Intermediate-level support" ([DVIDS, MARMC change of command](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/427158/mid-atlantic-regional-maintenance-center-holds-change-command), read 2026-09-05). FACT: the Norfolk multiple-award contract announcement says "the Mid-Atlantic Maintenance Center will administer the contract", while "The Naval Sea Systems Command, Washington, D.C., is the contracting activity" ([DoD contracts, Jan. 6, 2022](https://www.defense.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/2891105/), read 2026-09-05). FACT: a second layer sits beside the RMC: since 2011 NAVSEA regional maintenance oversight offices (NRMOs) at Norfolk, Mayport, San Diego and Bremerton give "third party oversight of non-nuclear surface ship maintenance similar to the role of NAVSEA Shipyard Representative's Office and Naval Reactors Representative's Office at the public shipyards" ([DVIDS, NRMOs established, 2012](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/89969), read 2026-09-05).

FACT: to bid on complex work a yard first needs a Master Ship Repair Agreement, which "NAVSEA will grant" after "certifying a ship repair firm's capability and capacity to perform all aspects of shipboard work" ([GAO-17-54](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-17-54.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: a 1995 Secretary of the Navy policy "requires that, whenever possible, ship repair and maintenance work of 6 months or less be performed by shipyards at or near the ship's home port", implementing 10 U.S.C. 7299a (same source). Assessment: work under six months stays at the homeport, so the competitors for your trades are the yards holding a place on that homeport's contract, and the RMC that administers it is the office whose planners you should know before a package locks.

## The four public naval shipyards

The Navy's own shipyards are the nuclear yards, and their customers are carriers and submarines rather than the surface fleet. FACT: "The four naval shipyards are focused almost exclusively on conducting repair and refueling work on the Navy's nuclear-powered aircraft carriers and submarines" ([GAO-17-548](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-17-548), read 2026-09-05). FACT: the four "provide the Navy with the capability to perform depot-level maintenance, emergency repairs, ship modernization, and ship inactivations of the nuclear fleet" ([GAO-20-64](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-20-64.pdf), read 2026-09-05).

| Public shipyard | Location | Nuclear work | Main classes served | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Norfolk Naval Shipyard | Portsmouth, Virginia | Yes; "the only East Coast naval shipyard capable of dry-docking nuclear-powered aircraft carriers" | Carriers, submarines, surface combatants and amphibious ships ("full-service") | [GAO-17-548](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-17-548) |
| Portsmouth Naval Shipyard | Kittery, Maine | Yes | "exclusively repairs nuclear-powered submarines" | [GAO-17-548](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-17-548) |

Assessment: the reader's hulls are mostly not these yards' hulls, with one exception. Norfolk and Pearl Harbor both list surface combatants, so a destroyer availability at those homeports can be worked in the public yard rather than competed, and the Pearl Harbor example below shows the yard and a private firm splitting one availability.

## Supervisors of Shipbuilding at the private builders

SUPSHIP is the Navy's on-site office at each big private builder, and until 2026 it belonged to NAVSEA 04, not SEA 21. As of July 2026 Bath, Gulf Coast and Newport News report to PAE Maritime and Groton's home is unconfirmed; see [Portfolio Acquisition Executives](topics/shipyards/portfolio-acquisition-executives). FACT: "The Supervisors of Shipbuilding, Conversion and Repair (SUPSHIP) serve as the Navy's on-site technical, contractual, and business authority for the construction of Navy vessels at major private shipyards" ([GAO-22-104655](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-22-104655.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: "The four SUPSHIP commands operate under the direction of the Deputy Commander of NAVSEA's Logistics, Maintenance and Industrial Operations Directorate": SUPSHIP Bath (Maine, with detachments in Marinette, Wisconsin and San Diego), SUPSHIP Groton (Connecticut), SUPSHIP Gulf Coast (Pascagoula, Mississippi, with a detachment in Mobile, Alabama) and SUPSHIP Newport News (Virginia) (same source). FACT: their board of directors includes "Naval Sea Systems Command (NAVSEA) 04Z leadership" ([DVIDS, SUPSHIP board meeting 2025](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/548951/supship-board-directors-meeting-strengthens-strategic-alignment-across-enterprise), read 2026-09-05). Assessment: a repair planner meets SUPSHIP when the builder is also the planning yard for a class or does post-delivery work; for homeport availabilities the counterpart is the RMC.

## Fleet commanders and Type Commanders

A Type Commander, written TYCOM, is the admiral who owns every ship of one type in a fleet and answers for its material condition. FACT: "The Navy's TYCOMs are responsible for maintaining, training, and ensuring the readiness of the ships assigned to each fleet. All ships are organized into categories" such as submarine, surface ship and aircraft carrier, giving six TYCOMs (air, surface and submarine, Atlantic and Pacific), and they "have a critical role developing and updating the Joint Fleet Maintenance Manual" ([GAO-22-104510](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-22-104510.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: for surface ships, "Type Commanders are responsible to their fleet commanders for the material condition of their assigned ships, which includes managing the emergent and scheduled maintenance"; Naval Surface Force Pacific answers to U.S. Pacific Fleet and Naval Surface Force Atlantic to U.S. Fleet Forces, and the type and fleet commands schedule the availabilities and work "with the Regional Maintenance Centers to plan and prioritize maintenance needs" ([GAO-25-106990](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-25-106990.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: in their own words, "SURFLANT mans, trains and equips assigned surface forces" ([SURFLANT mission page](https://www.surflant.usff.navy.mil/About-Us/Mission/), undated, read 2026-09-05) and "The mission of CNSP is to man, train, and equip the Surface Force" ([SURFPAC home page](https://www.surfpac.navy.mil/), undated, read 2026-09-05). FACT: the fleet also holds the RMCs' purse: GAO cites "U.S. Fleet Forces Command, which approves RMC budgets" ([GAO-17-54](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-17-54.pdf), read 2026-09-05). Assessment: this is why availability dates move without NAVSEA doing anything. The TYCOM schedules the ship and the fleet funds it, so a slip in a hull's arrival is usually a fleet decision that the RMC is passing on.

## The private yards at each homeport

The private yards doing surface depot work at a homeport are the ones holding a place on that homeport's multiple-award contract, and the award announcements name them. FACT: at Norfolk the January 2022 pool was BAE Systems Norfolk Ship Repair, Colonna's Shipyard, East Coast Repair and Fabrication, Epsilon, Marine Hydraulics International and General Dynamics NASSCO-Norfolk, ordering through January 2027 ([DoD contracts, Jan. 6, 2022](https://www.defense.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/2891105/), read 2026-09-05). FACT: at San Diego the November 2021 complex-ship pool was Epsilon, BAE Systems San Diego Ship Repair, East Coast Repair, Continental Maritime, Pacific Ship Repair and Fabrication and General Dynamics NASSCO, ordering through November 2026 ([DoD contracts, Nov. 30, 2021](https://www.defense.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/2857360/), read 2026-09-05). FACT: at Everett the January 2024 award went to ten firms including Vigor Marine, General Dynamics NASSCO Bremerton, Fincantieri Marine Repair, East Coast Repair and Epsilon, through January 2029 ([DoD contracts, Jan. 26, 2024](https://www.defense.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/3658212/), read 2026-09-05). FACT: at Mayport the April 2024 award went to eight firms including General Dynamics NASSCO-Mayport, BAE Systems Jacksonville Ship Repair, Fincantieri Marine Repair, Colonna's, East Coast Repair and Epsilon, through April 2029 ([DoD contracts, Apr. 24, 2024](https://www.defense.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/3754777/), read 2026-09-05). FACT: at Pearl Harbor a 2023 destroyer availability was executed by the public yard's "Fleet Maintenance Surface" side with Pacific Shipyards International ([DVIDS, Greene visits HRMC](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/455907/rear-adm-greene-visits-hawaii-regional-maintenance-center), read 2026-09-05). Assessment: the same names recur across ports (East Coast Repair, Epsilon, NASSCO, Fincantieri, Colonna's), so the competitor for your trades at one homeport is often the same company sizing a shop at another. Speculation: the Norfolk and San Diego vehicles run out in 2027 and 2026, so recompetes should be under way; none was staged.

## How much to trust this

The organisation chart here comes from GAO reports written for Congress, which name the offices and quote the Navy's own descriptions; those claims are FACT and are as good as secondary sourcing gets, but they are not the Navy's instruction. The Navy's primary documents (NAVSEA organisation pages, the CNRMC and SUPSHIP pages, and the Joint Fleet Maintenance Manual, COMUSFLTFORCOMINST 4790.3) sit on navsea.navy.mil, which refuses this network's ordinary fetcher; Wayback copies of the manual were found later the same day and are cited in [the planning-yards note](topics/shipyards/planning-yards), and the SRF-JRMC command pages were reached with the vault's browser-impersonating fetcher on 2026-09-05. Those command pages carry no date; the history page and the waterfront department page name USS Ronald Reagan as the Yokosuka carrier and the careers page carries a 2015 photograph of its arrival, so they were written between 2015 and 2024, and are cited as undated. The NAVSEA side of this chart is the pre-March-2026 one, as the paragraph at the top says. The DVIDS unit feed pages carry no unit description, so RMC roles are quoted from DVIDS news stories, Navy-written but dated 2012 to 2025. Claims marked "operator, first-hand" in the SRF-JRMC section and table were stated by the operator on 2026-09-05 from direct knowledge; they are labelled FACT on that strength and name their source in the sentence so a reader can weigh them. They were then checked against public sources the same day. The in-house share, the shipyard organisation, the supervisory warrant, the Pacific Fleet funding line and the CNRMC placement are now also cited to NAVSEA pages, the manual and Navy releases. Four things still rest on his word alone: that Puget Sound holds the supervisory authority for the carrier (the pages show the split but not the warrant), that the yard runs on AIM, that the dock has not taken a carrier since Kitty Hawk (two descriptions are consistent with it), and how many times the reporting line has moved. GAO's 2022 placement of the Japan center under NAVSEA 04 is kept as a dated fact, not deleted, because the reorganisations that moved it are themselves evidence. Least sure: the reporting line for the Northwest and Hawaii RMCs is GAO's 2022 statement and may have changed; "seven RMCs" is a 2023 count;  the Everett-to-NWRMC link is inference; contract pools change on recompete. All sources were read on 2026-09-05.
