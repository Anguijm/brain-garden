# Who runs US Navy ship maintenance: fleets, NAVSEA, RMCs and the yards

This note maps the offices behind a Navy ship availability, so that when a hull shows up in a schedule or a contract announcement the reader can tell who owns the ship, who owns the money, who wrote the work package, who signed the contract and who checks the work on the pier. Every claim is labelled FACT, Assessment, Speculation or Unverified.

**Read this first (added 2026-09-05).** The NAVSEA side of this chart is the one the GAO reports and the Navy's manuals describe, and it was the chart in force until March 2026. In 2026 the Navy moved the public shipyards and the regional maintenance centers under a new office, Portfolio Acquisition Executive Industrial Operations, moved the SEA 21 programme offices and SURFMEPP under Portfolio Acquisition Executive Maritime, and split the Supervisors of Shipbuilding between PAE Maritime and the submarine portfolio. The fleet side (fleet commanders, Type Commanders, the money) has not moved. What changed, who runs the new offices and what is still unconfirmed is in [Portfolio Acquisition Executives](topics/shipyards/portfolio-acquisition-executives). The sections below are kept as written because the manuals a yard works to still name these offices, and because the RMC at the pier is the same office under a different boss.

## The shape of the system

Two chains meet at every availability. The fleets own the ships and the money: U.S. Fleet Forces Command on the Atlantic side and U.S. Pacific Fleet on the Pacific side, each acting through a Type Commander. Naval Sea Systems Command (NAVSEA), the Navy's engineering and acquisition command for ships, owns the technical rules, the contracts and the government-run yards. FACT: the Government Accountability Office (GAO) puts it this way: "U.S. Pacific Fleet and U.S. Fleet Forces Command develop budgets for the operations and maintenance of ships" ([GAO-17-54](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-17-54.pdf), read 2026-09-05), while NAVSEA offices "perform contract administration, program management, and planning for future maintenance periods" ([GAO-22-104510](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-22-104510.pdf), read 2026-09-05). Assessment: the regional maintenance center at each homeport is where the two chains touch, which is why it is the office a repair yard deals with most.

## NAVSEA SEA 21 and the Commander, Navy Regional Maintenance Center

SEA 21 is the NAVSEA directorate that owns surface ships after delivery, and its director also commands the regional maintenance centers. FACT: GAO describes "NAVSEA's Director for Surface Ship Maintenance and Modernization. This office, known as NAVSEA21, provides life-cycle management for surface ships and manages critical modernization, maintenance, training, and inactivation programs" ([GAO-22-104510](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-22-104510.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: in February 2025 Rear Adm. William Greene "holds dual responsibilities as both CNRMC and Navy Seas Systems Command's Director of Surface Ship Maintenance, Modernization, and Sustainability", and Capt. Dan Lannamann was named to relieve him as CNRMC ([DVIDS, SRF-JRMC change of command](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/490220/srf-jrmc-hosts-change-command), read 2026-09-05).

CNRMC, the Commander, Navy Regional Maintenance Center, is the headquarters over the regional maintenance centers, created in 2010 when the Navy decided surface maintenance needed one owner. FACT: GAO records the Navy "establishing SURFMEPP and the Commander, Naval Regional Maintenance Center (CNRMC) in 2010 to oversee the operations of the regional maintenance centers" ([GAO-17-54](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-17-54.pdf), read 2026-09-05). SURFMEPP is the Surface Ship Maintenance Engineering Planning Program, the office that writes the class maintenance plan and the baseline work package for each surface availability; FACT: it "provides centralized life cycle maintenance engineering, as well as class maintenance and modernization planning" ([GAO-25-106990](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-25-106990.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: in 2023 the Navy said CNRMC "oversees seven regional maintenance centers" ([DVIDS, Greene visits FDRMC](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/458399/commander-us-navy-surface-ship-maintenance-visits-forward-deployed-maintenance-locations), read 2026-09-05). Assessment: the work package comes from SURFMEPP and the money from the fleet, so a question about scope and a question about funding go to different offices even though both arrive through the RMC. Since July 2026 SURFMEPP and the SEA 21 programme offices sit in PAE Maritime's Ship Readiness office while the RMCs sit in PAE Industrial Operations, so those two offices no longer share a boss inside NAVSEA; see [Portfolio Acquisition Executives](topics/shipyards/portfolio-acquisition-executives).

## The regional maintenance centers

An RMC is the Navy's maintenance office at a homeport, and not all of them sit in the same chain. FACT: CNRMC "oversees the regional maintenance centers in the United States, including the Mid-Atlantic RMC in Norfolk, VA; Southeast RMC in Mayport, FL; and the Southwest RMC in San Diego, CA", is "responsible for the coordination of maintenance activities" at the Northwest RMC inside Puget Sound Naval Shipyard and the Hawaii RMC "embedded in" Pearl Harbor Naval Shipyard, and "manages overseas locations such as the Forward Deployed Regional Maintenance Center headquartered in Italy" ([GAO-22-104510](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-22-104510.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: the Japan center is not under CNRMC; NAVSEA's Logistics, Maintenance and Industrial Operations directorate, "also known as NAVSEA 04, manages and oversees the Navy's shipyards and the Ship Repair Facility and Japan Regional Maintenance Center in Yokosuka, Japan, and its detachment in Sasebo, Japan" (same source).

FACT: Puget Sound Naval Shipyard and IMF has detachments in San Diego, Everett and Yokosuka ([GAO-22-104510](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-22-104510.pdf), read 2026-09-05). Assessment: Everett is served through the Bremerton yard's Everett detachment rather than a separate RMC, which is why the table lists it under NWRMC by inference; no staged source says so in those words.

## What an RMC does for a private yard's contract

For a repair yard, the RMC is the government's contract administrator and quality checker for surface ship work at that homeport, though the contract vehicle itself is signed in Washington. FACT: MARMC "is responsible for providing surface ship maintenance, management and oversight of private sector maintenance and fleet technical assistance", with core areas of "Fleet Technical Assistance, Depot-level Contractor Administration and Quality Assurance, and Intermediate-level support" ([DVIDS, MARMC change of command](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/427158/mid-atlantic-regional-maintenance-center-holds-change-command), read 2026-09-05). FACT: the Norfolk multiple-award contract announcement says "the Mid-Atlantic Maintenance Center will administer the contract", while "The Naval Sea Systems Command, Washington, D.C., is the contracting activity" ([DoD contracts, Jan. 6, 2022](https://www.defense.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/2891105/), read 2026-09-05). FACT: a second layer sits beside the RMC: since 2011 NAVSEA regional maintenance oversight offices (NRMOs) at Norfolk, Mayport, San Diego and Bremerton give "third party oversight of non-nuclear surface ship maintenance similar to the role of NAVSEA Shipyard Representative's Office and Naval Reactors Representative's Office at the public shipyards" ([DVIDS, NRMOs established, 2012](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/89969), read 2026-09-05).

FACT: to bid on complex work a yard first needs a Master Ship Repair Agreement, which "NAVSEA will grant" after "certifying a ship repair firm's capability and capacity to perform all aspects of shipboard work" ([GAO-17-54](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-17-54.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: a 1995 Secretary of the Navy policy "requires that, whenever possible, ship repair and maintenance work of 6 months or less be performed by shipyards at or near the ship's home port", implementing 10 U.S.C. 7299a (same source). Assessment: work under six months stays at the homeport, so the competitors for your trades are the yards holding a place on that homeport's contract, and the RMC that administers it is the office whose planners you should know before a package locks.

## The four public naval shipyards

The Navy's own shipyards are the nuclear yards, and their customers are carriers and submarines rather than the surface fleet. FACT: "The four naval shipyards are focused almost exclusively on conducting repair and refueling work on the Navy's nuclear-powered aircraft carriers and submarines" ([GAO-17-548](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-17-548), read 2026-09-05). FACT: the four "provide the Navy with the capability to perform depot-level maintenance, emergency repairs, ship modernization, and ship inactivations of the nuclear fleet" ([GAO-20-64](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-20-64.pdf), read 2026-09-05).

| Public shipyard | Location | Nuclear work | Main classes served | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Norfolk Naval Shipyard | Portsmouth, Virginia | Yes; "the only East Coast naval shipyard capable of dry-docking nuclear-powered aircraft carriers" | Carriers, submarines, surface combatants and amphibious ships ("full-service") | [GAO-17-548](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-17-548) |
| Portsmouth Naval Shipyard | Kittery, Maine | Yes | "exclusively repairs nuclear-powered submarines" | [GAO-17-548](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-17-548) |
| Puget Sound Naval Shipyard and IMF | Bremerton, Washington | Yes; only West Coast dry dock able to take a carrier; the Navy's only reactor compartment disposal and ship recycling site | "primarily conducts maintenance on nuclear-powered aircraft carriers and submarines" | [GAO-17-548](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-17-548) |
| Pearl Harbor Naval Shipyard and IMF | Pearl Harbor, Hawaii | Yes (part of the nuclear fleet's depot capability) | "focuses primarily on the maintenance and repair of submarines and surface combatants" | [GAO-17-548](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-17-548); [GAO-20-64](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-20-64.pdf) |

Assessment: the reader's hulls are mostly not these yards' hulls, with one exception. Norfolk and Pearl Harbor both list surface combatants, so a destroyer availability at those homeports can be worked in the public yard rather than competed, and the Pearl Harbor example below shows the yard and a private firm splitting one availability.

## Supervisors of Shipbuilding at the private builders

SUPSHIP is the Navy's on-site office at each big private builder, and until 2026 it belonged to NAVSEA 04, not SEA 21. As of July 2026 Bath, Gulf Coast and Newport News report to PAE Maritime and Groton's home is unconfirmed; see [Portfolio Acquisition Executives](topics/shipyards/portfolio-acquisition-executives). FACT: "The Supervisors of Shipbuilding, Conversion and Repair (SUPSHIP) serve as the Navy's on-site technical, contractual, and business authority for the construction of Navy vessels at major private shipyards" ([GAO-22-104655](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-22-104655.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: "The four SUPSHIP commands operate under the direction of the Deputy Commander of NAVSEA's Logistics, Maintenance and Industrial Operations Directorate": SUPSHIP Bath (Maine, with detachments in Marinette, Wisconsin and San Diego), SUPSHIP Groton (Connecticut), SUPSHIP Gulf Coast (Pascagoula, Mississippi, with a detachment in Mobile, Alabama) and SUPSHIP Newport News (Virginia) (same source). FACT: their board of directors includes "Naval Sea Systems Command (NAVSEA) 04Z leadership" ([DVIDS, SUPSHIP board meeting 2025](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/548951/supship-board-directors-meeting-strengthens-strategic-alignment-across-enterprise), read 2026-09-05). Assessment: a repair planner meets SUPSHIP when the builder is also the planning yard for a class or does post-delivery work; for homeport availabilities the counterpart is the RMC.

## Fleet commanders and Type Commanders

A Type Commander, written TYCOM, is the admiral who owns every ship of one type in a fleet and answers for its material condition. FACT: "The Navy's TYCOMs are responsible for maintaining, training, and ensuring the readiness of the ships assigned to each fleet. All ships are organized into categories" such as submarine, surface ship and aircraft carrier, giving six TYCOMs (air, surface and submarine, Atlantic and Pacific), and they "have a critical role developing and updating the Joint Fleet Maintenance Manual" ([GAO-22-104510](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-22-104510.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: for surface ships, "Type Commanders are responsible to their fleet commanders for the material condition of their assigned ships, which includes managing the emergent and scheduled maintenance"; Naval Surface Force Pacific answers to U.S. Pacific Fleet and Naval Surface Force Atlantic to U.S. Fleet Forces, and the type and fleet commands schedule the availabilities and work "with the Regional Maintenance Centers to plan and prioritize maintenance needs" ([GAO-25-106990](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-25-106990.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: in their own words, "SURFLANT mans, trains and equips assigned surface forces" ([SURFLANT mission page](https://www.surflant.usff.navy.mil/About-Us/Mission/), undated, read 2026-09-05) and "The mission of CNSP is to man, train, and equip the Surface Force" ([SURFPAC home page](https://www.surfpac.navy.mil/), undated, read 2026-09-05). FACT: the fleet also holds the RMCs' purse: GAO cites "U.S. Fleet Forces Command, which approves RMC budgets" ([GAO-17-54](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-17-54.pdf), read 2026-09-05). Assessment: this is why availability dates move without NAVSEA doing anything. The TYCOM schedules the ship and the fleet funds it, so a slip in a hull's arrival is usually a fleet decision that the RMC is passing on.

## The private yards at each homeport

The private yards doing surface depot work at a homeport are the ones holding a place on that homeport's multiple-award contract, and the award announcements name them. FACT: at Norfolk the January 2022 pool was BAE Systems Norfolk Ship Repair, Colonna's Shipyard, East Coast Repair and Fabrication, Epsilon, Marine Hydraulics International and General Dynamics NASSCO-Norfolk, ordering through January 2027 ([DoD contracts, Jan. 6, 2022](https://www.defense.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/2891105/), read 2026-09-05). FACT: at San Diego the November 2021 complex-ship pool was Epsilon, BAE Systems San Diego Ship Repair, East Coast Repair, Continental Maritime, Pacific Ship Repair and Fabrication and General Dynamics NASSCO, ordering through November 2026 ([DoD contracts, Nov. 30, 2021](https://www.defense.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/2857360/), read 2026-09-05). FACT: at Everett the January 2024 award went to ten firms including Vigor Marine, General Dynamics NASSCO Bremerton, Fincantieri Marine Repair, East Coast Repair and Epsilon, through January 2029 ([DoD contracts, Jan. 26, 2024](https://www.defense.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/3658212/), read 2026-09-05). FACT: at Mayport the April 2024 award went to eight firms including General Dynamics NASSCO-Mayport, BAE Systems Jacksonville Ship Repair, Fincantieri Marine Repair, Colonna's, East Coast Repair and Epsilon, through April 2029 ([DoD contracts, Apr. 24, 2024](https://www.defense.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/3754777/), read 2026-09-05). FACT: at Pearl Harbor a 2023 destroyer availability was executed by the public yard's "Fleet Maintenance Surface" side with Pacific Shipyards International ([DVIDS, Greene visits HRMC](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/455907/rear-adm-greene-visits-hawaii-regional-maintenance-center), read 2026-09-05). Assessment: the same names recur across ports (East Coast Repair, Epsilon, NASSCO, Fincantieri, Colonna's), so the competitor for your trades at one homeport is often the same company sizing a shop at another. Speculation: the Norfolk and San Diego vehicles run out in 2027 and 2026, so recompetes should be under way; none was staged.

## How much to trust this

The organisation chart here comes from GAO reports written for Congress, which name the offices and quote the Navy's own descriptions; those claims are FACT and are as good as secondary sourcing gets, but they are not the Navy's instruction. The Navy's primary documents (NAVSEA organisation pages, the CNRMC and SUPSHIP pages, and the Joint Fleet Maintenance Manual, COMUSFLTFORCOMINST 4790.3) sit on navsea.navy.mil, which refuses this network's ordinary fetcher; Wayback copies of the manual were found later the same day and are cited in [the planning-yards note](topics/shipyards/planning-yards). The NAVSEA side of this chart is the pre-March-2026 one, as the paragraph at the top says. The DVIDS unit feed pages carry no unit description, so RMC roles are quoted from DVIDS news stories, Navy-written but dated 2012 to 2025. Least sure: the reporting line for the Northwest and Hawaii RMCs is GAO's 2022 statement and may have changed; "seven RMCs" is a 2023 count; whether Capt. Lannamann actually relieved Rear Adm. Greene as CNRMC is not confirmed on any staged page; the Everett-to-NWRMC link is inference; contract pools change on recompete. All sources were read on 2026-09-05.
