# Navy maintenance availabilities: the types and who performs them

This note is a reference for reading a Navy maintenance schedule. It says what an
availability is, what the type codes mean, which are depot work and which intermediate,
which go to the four public naval shipyards and which to private yards, and roughly how
long each runs, so that a planner can tell from the code alone what hull and workload is
coming and who else will be competing for the trades. The companion note, [Navy availability
milestones](topics/shipyards/availability-milestones), gives the calendar inside one.

## What an availability is

An availability is a block of time in which a ship is handed over for maintenance. The
Navy's policy instruction defines the important kind by who scheduled it, not by what is
done: FACT: "A CNO availability is any type of ship availability, which, due to its cost,
schedule, or lifecycle sustainment significance, has been so designated by the CNO"
([OPNAVINST 4700.7N, para 701.1](https://www.secnav.navy.mil/doni/Directives/04000%20Logistical%20Support%20and%20Services/04-700%20General%20Maintenance%20and%20Construction%20Support/4700.7N.pdf),
read 2026-09-05). CNO means the Chief of Naval Operations, the Navy's senior officer;
a CNO availability is one important enough that his staff, not the fleet, fixes its dates.

The Joint Fleet Maintenance Manual (JFMM, the fleet's maintenance rulebook,
COMUSFLTFORCOMINST 4790.3) splits CNO availabilities at six months. FACT: a Major CNO
Maintenance Availability is "An availability of six months or greater duration performed
by industrial activities under NAVSEA management or contract administration," and a
Minor CNO Maintenance Availability is "An availability of less than six months in
duration scheduled by and under Type Commander management"
([JFMM Vol II, glossary](https://web.archive.org/web/20250312071433id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume_II.pdf),
Wayback copy of the NAVSEA original, read 2026-09-05). NAVSEA is Naval Sea Systems
Command, the Navy's ship engineering headquarters. The Type Commander (TYCOM) is the
admiral who owns a category of ships, for example Commander, Naval Surface Forces. The
six-month line decides who manages the job.

## Depot versus intermediate

The three maintenance levels are defined by skill and facility, not by location.
FACT: intermediate-level maintenance is "Maintenance that is the responsibility of and
performed by designated maintenance activities requiring a higher skill, capability or
capacity than organizational-level maintenance and below that requiring the specialized
personnel, facilities or capabilities of a depot-level facility," and depot-level
maintenance "is that maintenance performed on materiel requiring major overhaul or a
complete rebuild of parts, assemblies, subassemblies, or end-items... It consists of
maintenance tasks that require specialized facilities, tooling, support equipment,
personnel with greater technical skill, or processes beyond the scope or capacity of an
IMA"
([OPNAVINST 4700.7N, paras 501.a and 601](https://www.secnav.navy.mil/doni/Directives/04000%20Logistical%20Support%20and%20Services/04-700%20General%20Maintenance%20and%20Construction%20Support/4700.7N.pdf),
read 2026-09-05). An IMA is an intermediate maintenance activity, such as a Regional
Maintenance Center shop or a submarine tender. The JFMM calls the first two levels
"fleet maintenance" and depot work "industrial maintenance"
([JFMM Vol II, Part I, ch. 2, paras 2.2.1 and 2.2.2](https://web.archive.org/web/20250312071433id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume_II.pdf),
read 2026-09-05). Every CNO availability type below is depot work. The one type that is
not a CNO availability, the CMAV, mixes the two: FACT: a Continuous Maintenance
Availability is "A non-CNO-scheduled availability or continuous accomplishment of
intermediate- and depot maintenance and selected modernization"
([OPNAVINST 4700.7N, Appendix B, def. 8](https://www.secnav.navy.mil/doni/Directives/04000%20Logistical%20Support%20and%20Services/04-700%20General%20Maintenance%20and%20Construction%20Support/4700.7N.pdf),
read 2026-09-05).

## Who does the work

Nuclear hulls go to nuclear-capable yards, which for depot work means the four public
shipyards plus two builders. FACT: the four public naval shipyards (Portsmouth, Norfolk,
Puget Sound and Pearl Harbor) "provide depot maintenance on attack submarines and other
nuclear-powered vessels," and the two private shipyards, General Dynamics Electric Boat at
Groton and Huntington Ingalls Industries Newport News Shipbuilding, "build the U.S. Navy's
nuclear-powered ships, including attack submarines, and in some cases provide depot
maintenance for attack submarines"
([GAO-26-109256, pp. 3-4](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-109256.pdf), read
2026-09-05). FACT: Navy policy requires that "Depot-level repair, maintenance, and
modernization for steam plant systems, electric plant systems, and those auxiliary ship
systems supporting reactor plant... must only be assigned to nuclear-capable shipyards"
([OPNAVINST 4700.7N, para 203.2.b](https://www.secnav.navy.mil/doni/Directives/04000%20Logistical%20Support%20and%20Services/04-700%20General%20Maintenance%20and%20Construction%20Support/4700.7N.pdf),
read 2026-09-05). FACT: "TRIDENT Refit Facilities are additionally tasked and resourced
to accomplish industrial restoration level maintenance to SSBN or SSGN 726 Class
submarine components" (JFMM Vol II, Part I, ch. 2, para 2.2.1, above).

Conventional surface ships go to private yards by default. FACT: "Depot level
maintenance will normally be screened to the Private Sector Industrial Activity (PSIA)
contractor," and the maintenance team may use other contract vehicles only when the PSIA
contractor and government cannot agree on cost and scope, the contractor lacks capability
or capacity, an alteration installation team does the work, or the work is outside the
homeport area
([JFMM Vol VI, ch. 31, para 31.3.2.i](https://web.archive.org/web/20241119222606id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume%20VI.pdf),
Wayback copy, read 2026-09-05).

Assessment: for a private surface yard the split is simple. Cruisers, destroyers,
amphibious ships and littoral combat ships are the market; carriers and submarines are
not, unless the yard is Newport News or Electric Boat. The public yards compete for
trades and vendors, not for hulls.

## The surface force types

The surface types are one family built on the SRA. FACT: a Selected Restricted
Availability is "A labor-intensive industrial period assigned to ships in PROG or EOC
maintenance programs for the accomplishment of maintenance and selected modernization";
a Docking SRA is "An SRA expanded to include maintenance and modernization that require
dry-docking"; an Extended SRA is "An SRA expanded to include maintenance and
modernization that cannot be accomplished in a normal SRA"; an Extended Docking SRA is
"A DSRA expanded to include maintenance and modernization that cannot be accomplished in
a normal DSRA"; and a Depot Modernization Period is "A type of overhaul scheduled
primarily for the installation of high priority warfare improvement alterations"
([OPNAVINST 4700.7N, Appendix B, defs. 62, 16, 26, 24 and 12](https://www.secnav.navy.mil/doni/Directives/04000%20Logistical%20Support%20and%20Services/04-700%20General%20Maintenance%20and%20Construction%20Support/4700.7N.pdf),
read 2026-09-05). PROG and EOC are the Progressive Maintenance and Engineered Operating
Cycle strategies, two of four the instruction lists; the other two are the Incremental
Maintenance Plan for carriers and Phased Maintenance, under which "Ships assigned to PM
programs are maintained through PMAs in lieu of overhauls" (same instruction, para
1102.2 and Table 11-1). A Phased Maintenance Availability is "A short labor-intensive overhaul
availability for ships in a PM program," and a Docking PMA "A PMA expanded to include
maintenance and modernization that require dry-docking" (Appendix B, defs. 49 and 14).
The instruction does not say which classes sit in which program.

The Congressional Budget Office (CBO) gives the destroyer numbers. FACT: the 2012 DDG-51
class plan gave each ship "four docking overhauls and eight nondocking overhauls" over
its life, "DMPs were scheduled in the 2012 class plans to occur once in a ship's service
life, when the ship was 16 years old," and the class-plan lengths were 91 days for an
SRA, 126 for a DSRA and 392 for a DMP or EDSRA, against realized averages of 212, 311
and 614
([CBO, Maintenance Delays for Conventional Navy Ships, Dec 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20260122074505id_/https://www.cbo.gov/system/files/2025-12/61507-ship-maintenance.pdf),
Wayback copy because cbo.gov refuses this network, read 2026-09-05). FACT: RAND calls
the EDSRA "the most expensive maintenance availability in the service life of DDG-51
destroyers," "notionally scheduled to begin at 192 months (16 years)"
([RAND RR1155, 2015](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_reports/RR1100/RR1155/RAND_RR1155.pdf),
read 2026-09-05). Assessment: for a private yard the EDSRA and DMP are the same event
in workload terms, a docking that the plan already sizes at more than a year and that
has run closer to two; the table below keeps them on separate lines only because the
definitions differ.

Between CNO availabilities the work is done in CMAVs. FACT: "CMAVs are availabilities
accomplished on Surface Force ships outside of CNO availabilities for non-emergent
maintenance," in two forms, scheduled (XAZ) and unscheduled (XCM)
([JFMM Vol II, Part II, ch. 2, para 2.6.1](https://web.archive.org/web/20250312071433id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume_II.pdf),
read 2026-09-05). FACT: "CMAVs normally last two to six weeks (but can extend to more
than six months"
([RAND RR1155, 2015](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_reports/RR1100/RR1155/RAND_RR1155.pdf),
read 2026-09-05). Forward-deployed ships get the Surface Incremental Availability
instead: FACT: SIAs "are to be schedule like a CNO availability and executed like a
CMAV" (JFMM Vol II, Part I, ch. 3, para 3.2.d, above).

## Carrier and submarine types

Carriers follow the Incremental Maintenance Plan. FACT: a Planned Incremental
Availability is "A labor-intensive overhaul availability with a duration of 6 months or
less for aircraft carriers in an IMP," a Docking PIA is "A labor-intensive dry-docking
availability, with a duration of 16 months or less, for aircraft carriers," a Carrier
Incremental Availability is "A CNO availability for continuous accomplishment of depot
maintenance and selected modernization on aircraft carriers," and a Refueling Complex
Overhaul is "A type of overhaul specific to nuclear aircraft carriers (CVN) in which
reactor refueling is conducted" (OPNAVINST 4700.7N, Appendix B, defs. 51, 15, 1 and 57,
above). FACT: in 2011 the cadence was "carrier incremental availabilities lasting
approximately 1 month each and performed twice in every 32-month operating cycle,"
PIAs of about 6 months once per cycle, DPIAs of about 10.5 months after two PIA cycles,
and an RCOH "lasting about 39 months and performed once near the mid-life of the
carrier, at around 23 years"
([GAO-11-388R, p. 8](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-11-388r.pdf), read 2026-09-05).
Assessment: the 2011 cadence is the shape of the plan, not the current month counts.
FACT: "Carrier Incremental Availabilities are CNO availabilities for scheduling and
budgeting purposes only" (JFMM Vol II, para 3.2.c, above), which is why they run without
the full conference and reporting apparatus.

Submarines have the fewest and longest depot periods. FACT: an Engineered Overhaul is
"A CNO-scheduled availability and type of overhaul used with some ship classes," an
Engineered Refueling Overhaul one "used with nuclear-powered ship classes in which
refueling is accomplished," a Major Maintenance Period "A CNO-scheduled availability for
SSGNs," and an Extended Refit Period "A labor-intensive period typically lasting four to
6 months, during which SSBNs and guided-missile nuclear submarines (SSGN) accomplish
maintenance and modernization which cannot be completed during a normal refit period"
(OPNAVINST 4700.7N, Appendix B, defs. 20, 22, 38 and 25, above). FACT: "attack
submarines may repeat their 18-month operational cycle three to four times before they
enter an extensive depot maintenance period," described as "lasting 24 to 36 months"
([GAO-26-109256, p. 3](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-109256.pdf), read
2026-09-05). FACT: the Navy schedules availabilities "every 2 to 3 years for each
aircraft carrier and every 4 to 6 years for submarines," and their length "can range from
6 months to about 3 years"
([GAO-20-588](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-20-588.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: a
Virginia-class boat's Depot Modernization Period at Norfolk Naval Shipyard is counted as a
CNO availability and is done in dry dock
([DVIDS, USS New Hampshire begins DMP, 5 Sep 2025](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/547296/),
read 2026-09-05).

## How much to trust this

Assessment: the definitions and type list come from OPNAVINST 4700.7N (December 2024)
and the current JFMM (Rev D Change 4, September 2024), both read in full, so the
vocabulary is solid. The JFMM was read from a Wayback Machine capture of the NAVSEA PDF
because navsea.navy.mil returns 403 to this network; every page carries the NAVSEA
header and the copy is treated as authentic.

The durations are the weak part. The Navy's authoritative notional durations live in the
annual OPNAV N83 letter (OPNAVNOTE 4700), which is not published, so apart from the
PIA, DPIA and ERP ceilings and the LCS docking window in the instruction, every
duration here is secondary. The
destroyer numbers are CBO's reading of the 2012 class plan against events from 2011 to
2024, and nothing staged says the class plan is unchanged. The carrier month counts come
from a 2011 GAO letter written for a 32-month cycle. Submarine EOH, ERO, COH and MMP
durations are absent from every staged source. The cell placing ERPs at the TRIDENT Refit
Facilities is an inference from the JFMM's tasking of those facilities with 726-class
industrial work, not a stated fact. The PMA and DPMA rows name no class because neither
instruction does; the common belief that these are the amphibious-ship types is
Unverified here.
