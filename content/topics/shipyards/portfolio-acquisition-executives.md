# Portfolio Acquisition Executives: the 2026 reorganisation and where ship maintenance now sits

In 2026 the Navy moved most of what Naval Sea Systems Command (NAVSEA) used to do into new offices called Portfolio Acquisition Executives, and the offices a repair yard deals with (the regional maintenance centers, the public shipyards, the surface-ship planning office and the Supervisors of Shipbuilding) now report through three of them. This note says what a PAE is, which PAEs a shipyard cares about, who runs them as of September 2026, and what has not moved yet. It exists so the organisation chart does not have to be relearned every week. Every claim is labelled FACT, Assessment, Speculation or Unverified. The pre-2026 chart, which is still the one the Navy's manuals describe, is in [Who runs US Navy ship maintenance](topics/shipyards/navy-maintenance-organisation).

## What a PAE is

A Portfolio Acquisition Executive is a single official made accountable for a whole family of programmes, with the contracting, engineering and sustainment staff that used to sit in a systems command moved under them. The idea came from the Pentagon, not the Navy. FACT: a 7 November 2025 memorandum from the Secretary of War directed the services to "establish PAEs by transforming and reorganizing existing Program Executive Offices with a governance structure that prioritizes speed," to submit plans "transitioning all major acquisition activities to PAE portfolios within two years," and set the chain of authority "from the Defense Acquisition Executive (DAE) to the Service Acquisition Executives (SAEs) to the PAEs," with PAEs "the single accountable official for portfolio outcomes" ([Secretary of War memorandum, 7 Nov 2025](https://media.defense.gov/2025/Nov/10/2003819439/-1/-1/1/TRANSFORMING-THE-DEFENSE-ACQUISITION-SYSTEM-INTO-THE-WARFIGHTING-ACQUISITION-SYSTEM-TO-ACCELERATE-FIELDING-OF-URGENTLY-NEEDED-CAPABILITIES-TO-OUR-WARRIORS.PDF), read 2026-09-05). FACT: the same memorandum tells the services to "align contracting officers to report directly to PAEs" and lets a PAE "waive technical standards and other certification requirements not mandated by statute or safety" (same source).

The Navy's version goes further than renaming its Program Executive Offices. FACT: the Navy's 16 March 2026 announcement says "PAEs will have direct authority not only for program offices but also over associated technical, contracting, and sustainment functions, providing true cradle-to-grave control," quoting Jason Potter, performing the duties of Assistant Secretary of the Navy for Research, Development and Acquisition ([Navy press release, 16 Mar 2026](https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/Press-Releases/display-pressreleases/Article/4435370/navy-reshapes-warfighting-acquisition-system/), read 2026-09-05). FACT: the Navy's May 2026 release puts a number on it: "Approximately 70 percent of these functions and associated personnel will move from systems commands (SYSCOMs) into the PAEs" ([Navy press release, 11 May 2026](https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/Press-Releases/display-pressreleases/Article/4483312/navy-advances-acquisition-reform-strategy-appoints-three-new-portfolio-acquisit/), read 2026-09-05). FACT: Potter told reporters that "within the PAEs toolkit is head of contract authority, most technical authority, lifecycle sustainment planning, and these are all things that had previously been at the SYSCOM that now the PAE both has the authority to do and is accountable for doing," and that "PEOs are not in the middle" ([USNI News, 16 Mar 2026](https://news.usni.org/2026/03/16/secnav-phelan-stands-up-5-new-offices-for-navy-marine-acquisition), read 2026-09-05). The Assistant Secretary for Research, Development and Acquisition, usually written ASN RDA, is the Navy's civilian acquisition chief and the Service Acquisition Executive the Pentagon memo refers to. So the chain is now ASN RDA, then the PAE, then the programme manager.

## PAE Industrial Operations: the public yards and the RMCs

This is the office that now owns the government side of depot maintenance, both the Navy's own shipyards and the regional maintenance centers that award and administer private-yard availabilities. FACT: when Rear Adm. Todd Weeks relieved Vice Adm. Downey on 6 August 2026, the Navy said Weeks "will provide unified leadership over the four Naval Shipyards and seven Regional Maintenance Centers," and described the office as "the Navy's single, accountable office for ship and submarine depot maintenance" whose "non-negotiable mission is to deliver combat-ready ships and submarines back to the fleet on schedule" ([DVIDS, Weeks relieves Downey, 6 Aug 2026](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/571787/weeks-relieves-downey-portfolio-acquisition-executive-industrial-operations), read 2026-09-05). FACT: Downey held the office from its establishment in March 2026 while also commanding NAVSEA, and retired after the ceremony; Weeks came from the post of "Director, In-Service Submarines and Industrial Base for the Director Submarine Programs" and had been PEO Strategic Submarines and PEO Undersea Warfare Systems (same source). FACT: Rear Adm. Pete Small, until then "NAVSEA's Chief Engineer and NAVSEA Deputy Commander for Naval Systems Engineering since June 2024," became interim NAVSEA commander the same day ([USNI News, 10 Aug 2026](https://news.usni.org/2026/08/10/navsea-commander-downey-retires-2-admirals-take-over-roles-in-interim), read 2026-09-05).

What this replaces: before 2026 the public yards sat under NAVSEA 04, the Logistics, Maintenance and Industrial Operations directorate, and the RMCs under the Commander, Navy Regional Maintenance Center, whose commander was also NAVSEA's Director of Surface Ship Maintenance and Modernization (SEA 21); both are documented with GAO citations in [Who runs US Navy ship maintenance](topics/shipyards/navy-maintenance-organisation). FACT: CNRMC was still operating as a headquarters three months after the PAE stood up. On 3 June 2026 "Rear Adm. Dan Lannamann, commander of Navy Regional Maintenance Center (CNRMC)" relieved the commanding officer of the Japan center, SRF-JRMC, and CNRMC's command master chief filled in there ([USNI News, 2026-06-04](https://news.usni.org/2026/06/04/leaders-of-u-s-navys-ship-maintenance-facility-in-japan-removed-from-command), read 2026-09-05). Unverified: whether CNRMC reports to PAE Industrial Operations directly or through some layer that has not been named; the Weeks announcement names the shipyards and the RMCs and nothing between them. SRF-JRMC itself is a government industrial yard rather than a contract-administration office, and the operator, who knows it first-hand, places it in the CNRMC family as of 2026, which the June relief bears out; see [the organisation note](topics/shipyards/navy-maintenance-organisation). Either way it lands in PAE Industrial Operations, since that office holds both the yards and the RMCs.

## PAE Maritime: surface ships, the planning office and three SUPSHIPs

PAE Maritime builds surface ships and carriers, and it also holds the offices that plan surface-ship maintenance, which used to be SEA 21. FACT: a 16 July 2026 memo signed by Will Mahan, performing the duties of ASN RDA, declared PAE Maritime at initial operational capability with five deputy PAEs: Carriers (Rear Adm. Casey Moton), Combatants (Rear Adm. Brian Metcalf), Expeditionary and Mine Warfare (Melissa Kirkendall and Sam Taylor, acting), Auxiliaries (Capt. Cedric McNeal, acting) and Ship Readiness, "led by Rear Adm. Andy Biehn and civilian Lisa Bonacic-Doric," which "includes maintenance for small and combatants, the Arleigh Burke-class DDG 2.0 modification, training systems and international support" ([USNI News, 20 Jul 2026](https://news.usni.org/2026/07/20/new-navy-pae-maritime-shipbuilding-office-reaches-ioc), read 2026-09-05). FACT: the office's own site lists a "Modernization & Sustainment Program Office" containing PMS 421 and PMS 321 (small combatant and expeditionary modernization and sustainment), PMS 21I (inactive ships), PMS 451 (Destroyer Modernization 2.0), the Surface Maintenance Engineering Planning Program, PMS 326 (international fleet support), PMS 339 (surface training systems) and PMS 443 (bridge integration and targeted hull, mechanical and electrical systems) ([PAE Maritime portfolio page](https://www.paemaritime.navy.mil/), undated, read 2026-09-05). The Surface Maintenance Engineering Planning Program, SURFMEPP, is the office that writes the class maintenance plan and the baseline work package for every surface availability, so this is where a repair yard's package now originates. PMS is the Navy's prefix for a ship programme office.

FACT: the same memo has PAE Maritime overseeing "three Supervisor of Shipbuilding offices at destroyer builder General Dynamics Bath Iron Works in Bath Maine, Gulf Coast shipyards centered on amphib and destroyer builder HII Ingalls Shipbuilding in Pascagoula, Miss., and HII Newport News Shipbuilding in Virginia" ([USNI News, 20 Jul 2026](https://news.usni.org/2026/07/20/new-navy-pae-maritime-shipbuilding-office-reaches-ioc), read 2026-09-05). FACT: in early August 2026 Chris Miller "was moved to lead PAE Robotic and Autonomous Systems and Rear Adm. Casey Moton is now leading PAE Maritime" ([USNI News, 10 Aug 2026](https://news.usni.org/2026/08/10/navsea-commander-downey-retires-2-admirals-take-over-roles-in-interim), read 2026-09-05).

## PAE Undersea and the Direct Reporting Portfolio Manager for submarines

Submarines got a different arrangement: one admiral holds both the Navy's undersea portfolio and a Pentagon post that reports to the Deputy Secretary of War directly. FACT: the Navy lists the office as "PAE Undersea / DRPM Submarines: Vice Adm. Robert Gaucher" ([Navy press release, 16 Mar 2026](https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/Press-Releases/display-pressreleases/Article/4435370/navy-reshapes-warfighting-acquisition-system/), read 2026-09-05), and USNI describes Gaucher as "overseeing submarine construction for the Navy as a direct report Deputy Defense Secretary Steve Feinberg" ([USNI News, 10 Aug 2026](https://news.usni.org/2026/08/10/navsea-commander-downey-retires-2-admirals-take-over-roles-in-interim), read 2026-09-05). FACT: Gaucher told Janes that the way SUPSHIP oversees submarine construction "has been overhauled," that SUPSHIP at Electric Boat and Newport News "was previously very much focused on making sure the private shipyards comply with the contract," and that a "756-page operational manual largely driven by checklist compliance" has been replaced "with a 15-page guiding principles" document ([Janes, 14 Aug 2026](https://www.janes.com/defence-intelligence-insights/defence-news/industry/new-us-navy-submarine-programme-reorganisation-leads-to-overhaul-of-submarine-construction-oversight), read 2026-09-05). FACT: in-service submarines have their own director inside the submarine programme: Stephanie Link is "Deputy Director for In-Service Submarines and the Industrial Base for the Department of the Navy," and she credits "the Direct Reporting Portfolio Manager (DRPM) Submarines and the Portfolio Acquisition Executive (PAE) Undersea structure" with "centralized accountability to support on-time delivery of new-construction submarines and sustainment of in-service submarines" ([DefenseScoop, 21 Jul 2026](https://defensescoop.com/2026/07/21/navy-submarine-production-technology-acquisition/), read 2026-09-05).

## Where SUPSHIP lives now

The short answer is that SUPSHIP has been split by the customer it serves. Before 2026 all four Supervisors of Shipbuilding (Bath, Groton, Gulf Coast and Newport News) reported to NAVSEA 04, the same directorate as the public yards, which GAO documented in 2022 and [the pre-2026 note](topics/shipyards/navy-maintenance-organisation) records. FACT: the July 2026 memo puts Bath, Gulf Coast and Newport News under PAE Maritime ([USNI News, 20 Jul 2026](https://news.usni.org/2026/07/20/new-navy-pae-maritime-shipbuilding-office-reaches-ioc), read 2026-09-05). Unverified: SUPSHIP Groton's formal alignment. No staged source names it; Janes has Gaucher describing SUPSHIP at Electric Boat as part of the submarine programme he runs, which points at PAE Undersea, but the memo that would say so has not been read. Assessment: Newport News builds both carriers and submarines, so the Newport News office is probably answering to two PAEs at once, and the "15-page guiding principles" Gaucher describes applies to its submarine work while its carrier work runs under PAE Maritime's rules. For a repair yard the practical point is unchanged: SUPSHIP is the Navy's office at a builder, and the office a repair yard deals with for a homeport availability is still the RMC.

## What stays in NAVSEA, and the technical authority question

NAVSEA has not been abolished; it has been hollowed to an engineering and support core whose final shape is still under study. FACT: Vice Adm. Seiko Okano, the principal military deputy to ASN RDA, said "the SYSCOM is going to continue to play an essential role in engineering, technical expertise and infrastructure," that "the majority of that workforce will be mission aligned" under the PAEs, and that "a small group" would stay under the SYSCOM "just to make sure that we've got independence there" ([USNI News, 16 Mar 2026](https://news.usni.org/2026/03/16/secnav-phelan-stands-up-5-new-offices-for-navy-marine-acquisition), read 2026-09-05). FACT: in July 2026 an RDA spokesperson said "A comprehensive Navy Systems Command study is underway. The study will inform the future roles and compositions of the SYSCOMs" ([USNI News, 20 Jul 2026](https://news.usni.org/2026/07/20/new-navy-pae-maritime-shipbuilding-office-reaches-ioc), read 2026-09-05).

Technical authority is the part that matters to a yard, because it decides who can approve a repair that does not meet the specification. FACT: the governing instruction, SECNAVINST 5400.15D of 19 January 2021, defines technical authority as "the authority, responsibility, and accountability to establish, monitor, and approve technical standards, tools, and processes in conformance with applicable Department of Defense (DoD) and DON policy, requirements, architectures, and standards" (paragraph 4.f(4)), and assigns the systems command commanders to "Exercise TA and certification authority for weapon systems and AIS" and to serve "the PEO's as the TA and operational safety and assurance certification authorities for their assigned areas of responsibility" (enclosure (1), paragraph 4) ([SECNAVINST 5400.15D](https://www.secnav.navy.mil/doni/Directives/05000%20General%20Management%20Security%20and%20Safety%20Services/05-400%20Organization%20and%20Functional%20Support%20Services/5400.15D.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: the Joint Fleet Maintenance Manual in force (Volume II, September 2024) still routes surface-ship change deferrals through "NAVSEA 05D (Ship Design Manager)" and still requires a Naval Supervisory Authority to "have a Chief Engineer holding a NAVSEA Technical Warrant" ([JFMM Vol II, Part I ch. 2 para 2.1.1 and ch. 3 para 3.3.8.9, Wayback copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20250312071433id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume_II.pdf), read 2026-09-05). Assessment: the announcement has run ahead of the paperwork. Potter says "most technical authority" now sits with the PAEs, but the instruction that assigns it still names the SYSCOM commander and the manual the waterfront works to still names NAVSEA 05D, so until SECNAVINST 5400.15 is reissued or NAVSEA publishes a new delegation, a departure from specification still travels the route [the planning-yards note](topics/shipyards/planning-yards) describes. The thing to watch for is a revised 5400.15 or a NAVSEA instruction naming which warrants moved; that document, not a press release, is what changes who signs.

## What this means for a repair yard

Assessment: the two chains that used to meet at the RMC now meet one level higher, and that is likely to show up first as faster package decisions and slower fights over money. Before 2026 the office that wrote the work package (SURFMEPP, under SEA 21) and the office that administered the contract (the RMC, under CNRMC, whose commander was the SEA 21 director) shared one boss inside NAVSEA. Now SURFMEPP reports through PAE Maritime's Ship Readiness office and the RMC through PAE Industrial Operations, and the two PAEs meet only at ASN RDA. The fleet still owns the ships and the maintenance money, which has not moved. So a scope question and a funding question, which already went to different offices, now go to different chains of command. Expect the Ship Readiness office to use its new authority to lock class packages earlier and to push Destroyer Modernization 2.0 work into availabilities on its own schedule, and expect the RMC's contract administrators, whose new boss is measured on "on schedule," to resist growth work harder than before. What to do with that: get the class package questions to SURFMEPP's planners before the package locks, because the office answering them is now further from the pier, and treat any growth-work request as needing a written schedule case, because the person approving it is now graded on the return date.

## What to watch

These are the gaps in this note, in the order they are likely to be filled. The outcome of the systems command study, which will say what NAVSEA keeps. A reissued SECNAVINST 5400.15 or a NAVSEA delegation naming which technical warrants moved to the PAEs. Whether CNRMC, still a headquarters in June 2026, reports straight to PAE Industrial Operations, and whether the NAVSEA regional maintenance oversight offices moved with them. SUPSHIP Groton's formal home. A permanent PAE Industrial Operations and a public page for it, which did not exist when this was written. Each of these is a one-line update to this note when it lands, not a new note.

## How much to trust this

The dates, names and the "four shipyards and seven RMCs" description are FACT from Navy press releases and a Navy-written DVIDS story, which are primary for what the Navy announced but say nothing about what the offices contain. The deputy-PAE structure and the three SUPSHIPs come from a USNI News account of a 16 July 2026 memo the reporter obtained; the memo itself was not read, so those claims are as good as USNI's reading of it. The Pentagon memorandum and SECNAVINST 5400.15D were read in full. The PAE Maritime portfolio page is undated. Least sure: anything about what is inside PAE Industrial Operations beyond the yards and the RMCs, where CNRMC reports, SUPSHIP Groton, and the whole technical-authority section, where the only evidence of movement is a spoken quote and the only evidence of the status quo is documents that predate the change. Chris Miller's move and Moton's appointment rest on one USNI sentence. All sources read 2026-09-05.
