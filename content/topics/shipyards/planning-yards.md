# Who plans the work on each Navy ship class: planning yards, lead yards and technical authority

This note answers a question a repair-yard planner needs settled before a package locks: for the hull arriving at my pier, who owns the drawings, who owns the class design, and who can say yes to a repair that does not meet the specification. The Navy splits those jobs among a planning yard, a lead (or design) yard, and Naval Sea Systems Command's technical authority. The same company can hold two of the three for one class and none for another. The table at the end names the holder for each major class; the sections before it say what each job does for the yard doing the work.

## Three jobs that sound alike

The planning yard keeps the class's technical record and turns modernization into installable work. FACT: the Naval Vessel Register (NVR), the Navy's official ship database, lists planning yards "as assigned by the Fleet Modernization Program" and states that "The planning yard, as the Ship Class Design Agent, has the technical life cycle responsibility for assigned ships" ([NVR, Planning Yard (Hull) Assignments](https://www.nvr.navy.mil/nvr/getByPlanningYard.htm?shipCraft=0&hullNuc=H), read 2026-09-05). FACT: the Joint Fleet Maintenance Manual (JFMM), the Fleet's maintenance rulebook, defines the role as "an industrial activity responsible for maintaining the Ship's Drawing Index current with configuration," and notes it "may also be the Building Yard" ([JFMM Vol I, para 2.7, Wayback copy of the navsea.navy.mil PDF](https://web.archive.org/web/20250523215304id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume_I_D4.pdf), read 2026-09-05).

The lead yard, also called the design agent, owns the class design while ships are still being built. FACT: Bath Iron Works called itself "the lead shipyard and design agent" for DDG 51 when it won a lead yard services contract for "design and technical assistance for design upgrades and major changes to the two shipyards currently building DDG 51-class destroyers" ([General Dynamics, 11 June 2018](https://www.gd.com/Articles/2018/06/11/general-dynamics-bath-iron-works-awarded-50-million-ddg-51-lead-yard-services-contract), read 2026-09-05). FACT: on 2 April 2026 Electric Boat received a \$1,265,790,364 modification "for lead yard support and development studies and design efforts related to Virginia-class submarines" ([war.gov contracts for 2 April 2026](https://www.war.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/4450753/contracts-for-april-2-2026/), read 2026-09-05).

Technical authority is the power to set the requirement, and it belongs to NAVSEA, not to either yard. FACT: a NAVSEA 05D briefing quotes the SECNAVINST 5400.15 definition, "The authority, responsibility, and accountability to establish, monitor, and approve technical standards, tools, and processes," and states that "COMNAVSEA delegates the execution of TA to the NAVSEA Chief Engineer (CHENG) (SEA 05)," who "defines Technical Areas and designates TWHs," meaning Technical Warrant Holders ([NAVSEA 05D, Engineering and Technical Authority Overview, 15 Jan 2019, Wayback copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20221115025356id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/Exhibits/SNA2019/Eng_TechAuth-Lind.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: the same briefing lists planning yards as engineering agents that "Manage and update the engineering technical data that defines a Class of in-service ships," naming Bath Iron Works, HII Pascagoula, Electric Boat, HII Newport News, Puget Sound Naval Shipyard and Norfolk Naval Shipyard (same source).

Assessment: the split is builder-era versus in-service. The lead yard answers to the shipbuilding program while hulls are on the ways; the planning yard answers to the modernization and maintenance programs once they are delivered. For DDG 51 and Virginia the same company holds both, so the distinction is invisible. For the Los Angeles class it is not: the NVR hull page for USS Albany, cited in the table below, shows Newport News planning the hull and Electric Boat planning the reactor plant.

## What a planning yard hands a repair yard, and when

The planning yard's deliverable to an executing yard is the ship installation drawing, the kitted material behind it, and the ship check that proved the drawing fits. FACT: the JFMM's surface ship availability milestones have the planning yard request funding for its assigned work at A-480 (480 days before the availability starts) and "Issue or Deliver SIDs to NSA for contractors and AITs" at A-240, where a SID is a Ship Installation Drawing, the NSA is the Naval Supervising Activity that runs the availability, and an AIT is an Alteration Installation Team ([JFMM Vol II, Part II ch. 2 App. D1, Wayback copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20250312071433id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume_II.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: the executing activity normally plans its own work, but "first of ship class ship alteration advance planning (drawing preparation and material ordering) ... will be performed by the assigned planning yard," and under the firm-fixed-price milestone set "An SC without PY-approved SIDs must be considered not executable by the LMA and at risk of deferral," where SC is a ship change and LMA the lead maintenance activity (same source).

FACT: the DDG 51 planning yard contract buys "expert design, planning and material support services for both maintenance and modernization" ([defense.gov contracts for 4 Feb 2019](https://www.defense.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/1747970/), read 2026-09-05), and Bath Iron Works staffs it in Brunswick, Maine and at the homeports of Norfolk, Mayport, San Diego, Everett, Pearl Harbor, Rota and Yokosuka ([Naval Today, 20 Nov 2020](https://www.navaltoday.com/2020/11/20/bath-iron-works-secures-146m-planning-yard-option-from-us-navy/), read 2026-09-05). FACT: the LCS planning yard contract also carries "the requirement to manage the scheduling of all planned, continuous and emergent maintenance and associated availabilities" ([HII, 1 May 2019](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2019/05/01/1813745/0/en/PHOTO-RELEASE-Huntington-Ingalls-Industries-Wins-LCS-Planning-Yard-Contract-Worth-A-Potential-931-7-Million.html), read 2026-09-05).

Assessment: drawings and kits for alterations arrive from the planning yard on the A-240 schedule, not from the customer, and the planning yard's homeport representative is the person to ask whether a SID has been ship-checked against the specific hull. A ship change that reaches A-240 without a planning-yard-approved SID is the one most likely to be deferred, so it is the item to chase first.

## What an in-service engineering agent is, and how it differs

An in-service engineering agent (ISEA) owns a system, not a ship. FACT: the NAVSEA 05D briefing says an ISEA is "Delegated responsibility by the TWH" and provides "Design and certification of systems or equipment, construction, production or integration for in-service systems" (NAVSEA 05D overview, above). FACT: Naval Surface Warfare Center Port Hueneme, ISEA for surface combat and weapon systems, defines the term: "In-Service Engineering, or ISE, is the term used to describe support for systems already installed on ships - thus, while they are in service- in contrast to future systems that are still being developed" ([NSWC Port Hueneme, In Service Engineering, Wayback copy, undated](https://web.archive.org/web/20260612173919id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Home/Warfare-Centers/NSWC-Port-Hueneme/What-We-Do/In-Service-Engineering/), read 2026-09-05). FACT: an ISEA can be a company: General Dynamics Mission Systems holds "design agent services and lifecycle sustainment services in support of Independence-variant Littoral Combat Ships" under a contract let by NSWC Port Hueneme ([war.gov contracts for 2 April 2026](https://www.war.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/4450753/contracts-for-april-2-2026/), read 2026-09-05).

Assessment: the difference is the boundary of the drawing. The planning yard answers for the ship's configuration: where a system sits, what it is bolted and cabled to, how the change is installed. The ISEA answers for whether the system itself works, is certified and is supported. A yard installing a combat-system change deals with both: the SID from the planning yard, the test and certification requirement from the ISEA.

## Departure from specification: what it is and who signs

A departure from specification (DFS) is the paperwork for work that will not, or did not, meet the requirement. FACT: "A DFS (non-nuclear only) is a lack of compliance with any authoritative document, plan, procedure, instruction, etc.," and "deviations are requested prior to conducting work that will result in a nonconformance and waivers are requested after a nonconformance has been discovered" ([JFMM Vol V, Part I ch. 8, paras 8.2 and 8.1.2, Wayback copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20240927040421id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume_V.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: a Major DFS is one that "affects performance, durability, reliability or maintainability, interchangeability, effective use or operation, weight or appearance (where they are a factor), health or safety, system design parameters ... or compartment arrangements or assigned function"; anything else is Minor (same source, 8.2.5 and 8.2.6).

FACT: who signs depends on whether the condition will be fixed later. "A permanent DFS requires no additional repair effort and is approved by NAVSEA." A temporary Major DFS "is approved by the TYCOM following concurrence by an Authorized Technical Authority," and a temporary Minor DFS by the TYCOM or ISIC, where the TYCOM is the type commander, the admiral who owns ships of that type, and the ISIC the immediate superior in command (same source, 8.2.4). FACT: "FOR SURFACE FORCE SHIPS AND AIRCRAFT CARRIERS, THE TYCOM WILL PERFORM THE IMMEDIATE SUPERIOR IN COMMAND (ISIC) FUNCTIONS OF THIS CHAPTER" (same source, 8.2). FACT: an Authorized Technical Authority is "a representative designated by reference (a) to act for Naval Sea Systems Command (NAVSEA) in dispositioning nonconformance issues," reference (a) being NAVSEAINST 5400.95, the waterfront technical authority policy (same source, 8.1.2.b). FACT: the finding activity reports a DFS "using either a QA form 12, a naval message or electronically using the eDFS program," and a depot-initiated temporary DFS goes to the ISIC representative "for review and concurrence prior to approval" (same source, 8.3.7). FACT: Navy policy requires that "deviating work does not proceed until concurrence from appropriate technical authority is received" ([OPNAVINST 4700.7N, para 902.3, 5 Dec 2024](https://www.secnav.navy.mil/doni/Directives/04000%20Logistical%20Support%20and%20Services/04-700%20General%20Maintenance%20and%20Construction%20Support/4700.7N.pdf), read 2026-09-05).

Assessment: the yard finding the condition writes the DFS and proposes the repair, but its signature commits nothing. A temporary fix the ship will sail with is approved on the waterfront, by the type commander after the local chief engineer concurs. A condition accepted as-is forever goes to NAVSEA. That is the useful thing to know when a Major DFS surfaces late in an availability: a permanent disposition goes to Washington, a temporary one stays local.

## What NAVSEA 05 does that neither yard may do

NAVSEA 05 sets the requirement; the yards work to it. FACT: as "the lead SYSCOM and technical authority for the lifecycle management of ships," NAVSEA must "Develop the technical requirements necessary for performing quality maintenance," including drawings, technical manuals, "calibration and repair standards, test requirements," and "Provide necessary technical support and oversight of NSYs, SUPSHIPs, and RMCs" ([OPNAVINST 4700.7N, para 903.2](https://www.secnav.navy.mil/doni/Directives/04000%20Logistical%20Support%20and%20Services/04-700%20General%20Maintenance%20and%20Construction%20Support/4700.7N.pdf), read 2026-09-05). FACT: a warrant holder "Is independent of programmatic authority" and exercises stewardship "though management of Engineering Managers, Engineering Agents, Planning Yards, etc." (NAVSEA 05D overview, above). FACT: NAVSEA 05D, the Ship Design Manager, adjudicates change deferral requests within ten business days, with warrant holders and ISEAs providing "approval or disapproval recommendations to NAVSEA 05D" ([JFMM Vol II, Part I ch. 3, paras 3.3.8.8 and 3.3.8.9](https://web.archive.org/web/20250312071433id_/https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/Volume_II.pdf), read 2026-09-05).

Assessment: planning yard and ISEA are both engineering agents working under a warrant holder; they draw, kit, test and recommend. Only the warrant holder can change what the specification requires, accept a permanent nonconformance, or defer a class change. The rule exists so the organization paid to install the work is never the one deciding whether it was good enough.

And the Constellation class has no planning yard yet; when one is named it will be the first public signal of how the Navy intends to sustain that class.

## How much to trust this

Every planning yard assignment is FACT from the NVR, the Navy's own register, read on 2026-09-05 and dated 11 August 2026 on its footer; the hull-by-hull tables were retrieved by submitting the page's own form and are appended to the staged capture. The definitions of planning yard, DFS, technical authority and ISEA are quoted from the JFMM, OPNAVINST 4700.7N and a NAVSEA 05D briefing, all primary. Three caveats. The JFMM and the briefing were read from Wayback Machine copies because navsea.navy.mil refuses this network; the copies are Rev D Change 4 (2024) for Volumes I and II and Change 3 (2023) for Volume V, and a later change may have moved paragraph numbers. The 05D briefing carries "Distribution D" in some page footers and "Statement A: Approved for Release. Distribution is unlimited" on the slides quoted; it was posted on NAVSEA's public site as a 2019 Surface Navy Association exhibit, but the markings are mixed. NAVSEAINST 5400.95 and 5400.97, the instructions that define the Authorized Technical Authority and the warrant structure, were not read; the note relies on how the JFMM and the briefing describe them. The lead-yard cells marked Unverified were not researched, not found absent, and the "Washington versus the waterfront" reading of DFS approvals is Assessment built on the quoted paragraphs.
