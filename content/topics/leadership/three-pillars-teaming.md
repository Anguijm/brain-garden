# The three pillars of a teaming culture

This short piece is the culture-side companion to [teamwork on the fly](teamwork-on-the-fly).
If "teaming" is coordinating on the fly with a shifting mix of people, Amy Edmondson argues
that in the most innovative companies teaming *is* the culture, and leaders build it by growing
three dispositions in everyone.

FACT: this is a 2013 HBR.org article by Amy Edmondson. (Edmondson, "The Three Pillars of a
Teaming Culture.")

## Curiosity, passion, empathy

![Diagram: three pillars holding up a "teaming culture." Curiosity (find out what others know). Passion (fuel effort and care). Empathy (see others' perspective). Under each, the leader behavior that builds it: ask and listen; show enthusiasm; attune to everyone.](img/teaming-pillars.png)
*Three dispositions a leader grows to make teaming the culture. Diagram.*

- **Curiosity** — drives people to learn what others know and bring; leaders build it by asking
  genuine questions and actually listening.
- **Passion** — fuels the effort to stretch for a shared goal; leaders build it by showing
  visible enthusiasm (it's infectious).
- **Empathy** — seeing another's perspective, which Edmondson calls critical to collaborating
  under pressure; leaders build it by attending to everyone, regardless of rank.

Assessment: the point underneath is that people's *natural* instinct on an unfamiliar team is
self-protection (you can't look good or be right all the time), which shuts down all three. So
the culture takes conscious work. FACT: the worked example is patient-safety pioneer Julie
Morath, who shifted a resistant hospital not by arguing harder but by *asking* ("was everything
as safe as you'd like this week?").

## How much to trust this

Assessment: the three pillars are a memorable, sensible frame, and the "answer resistance with
curiosity" lesson from the Morath story is genuinely good. It's essentially psychological safety
in different clothes.

The honest caveat: this is a short opinion piece resting on a single example, so the three
pillars are asserted, not tested, and it overlaps heavily with Edmondson's fuller work.
Assessment: treat it as a nice distillation of ideas better grounded in the
[psychological safety](psychological-safety) and [teamwork on the fly](teamwork-on-the-fly)
notes.
