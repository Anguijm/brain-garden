# How to read the evidence in this library

Every note in this library ends with an "evidence check" that grades its framework against
the peer-reviewed research. Those checks quote statistics like "r = .30" and "d = 0.28", and
this page is the decoder ring: what the numbers mean, what the verdicts mean, and, honestly,
how far to trust the checks themselves.

## The stats, in plain English

**Correlation (r)** measures how strongly two things move together, from 0 (no relationship)
to 1 (in lockstep). **Effect size (d, or Hedges g)** measures how far apart two groups are,
in units of normal spread; as a rough rule, d is about double the r scale. What counts as
"big" is smaller than most people expect:

A footer box reads: always ask, correlation or experiment? Most management findings are survey correlations, which show what goes together, not what causes what.](img/effect-sizes.png)
*The working scale for the numbers in the evidence checks. Diagram.*

A few more terms the checks lean on:

- **Meta-analysis:** a study of studies, pooling many samples into one estimate. Stronger
  than any single study, but only as good as the studies going in.
- **Cross-sectional / correlational:** measured once, from surveys. Shows what goes
  together, not what causes what. Most management research is this.
- **Same-source (common-method) bias:** when the same person rates both things (say, their
  boss's emotional intelligence *and* their boss's leadership), the correlation inflates,
  sometimes dramatically. Several checks cite correlations "collapsing" once raters differ.
- **Replication:** an independent redo of a study. A finding that fails replication (like
  the oxytocin-trust effect) should lose most of its weight, whatever its fame.
- **Corrected correlation:** statistically adjusted upward for measurement error. Honest
  practice, but it means the raw relationship is weaker than the quoted number.

## What the verdicts mean

Each check carries a verdict. **Well-supported** means independent meta-analyses or
replications back the core claim. **Mixed** (most of the library) means the direction holds
but the strength, precision, or generality was oversold. **Weak** means the note's central
mechanism or evidence failed independent testing. No note in the library was flat-out
contradicted; the recurring correction is the same each time: keep the direction, lower the
confidence, strip the inflated numbers.

## How far to trust the checks themselves

Being honest about our own method, because that is the point of this library. Assessment:
the 2026 evidence review was **AI-run**: fleets of research agents graded each framework
against the literature, and a second pass verified that every cited study is a real paper
(fetching each DOI and checking title, authors, and findings). That catches fabrication and
misattribution well. What it does not replace is a human reading the primary sources, so
every re-anchored claim in the checks is labeled Assessment, not FACT.

The six load-bearing papers, the ones the most checks lean on, are the ones worth a human
read first (all links verified to resolve):

1. [Colquitt, Scott & LePine 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.92.4.909) — trust
   meta-analysis (132 samples); behind the trust notes and rule 1.
2. [Lacerenza et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0000241) — leadership-training
   meta-analysis (335 samples); behind the development notes and rule 7.
3. [Frazier et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/peps.12183) — psychological-safety
   meta-analysis (136 samples); behind the safety notes and rule 9.
4. [De Dreu & Weingart 2003](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.88.4.741) — conflict and
   team-performance meta-analysis; behind the conflict notes.
5. [Judge et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.87.4.765) — personality and
   leadership meta-analysis; behind the authenticity notes.
6. [Bernerth et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/job.2708) — team-size meta-analysis
   (208 samples, 21,435 teams); behind the team notes and rule 10.

Assessment: read those six and you have personally verified the spine of the whole library.
Until then, the checks are a sourced starting point with moderate-to-high confidence, not
gospel, exactly the standard this vault holds every other confident source to.
