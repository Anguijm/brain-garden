# NFX: what an early-stage venture firm teaches founders

NFX is a venture firm that invests at the very earliest stage of a startup. It gives away a lot of its thinking:
two free video courses, a long back catalog of talks on YouTube, and a weekly newsletter. This series is a reader's
guide to that material, built for someone who wants to judge a business idea, find a way to stand out from
competitors, or understand how small AI-first companies are being organized. Nothing here is advice to start a company.
It is a set of tests and questions that the firm uses, written out in plain English.

FACT: [NFX's own site](https://www.nfx.com/masterclass/the-speed-masterclass) says its masterclass is free and "always will be", with a free login that
unlocks the episodes, transcripts and an audio version. There is nothing to buy; the firm uses the courses to attract
founders to its own pipeline, together with free tools such as [BriefLink](https://brieflink.com/) and
[Signal](https://signal.nfx.com/).

## The notes in this series

1. [Copy a lot, but not all](copy-a-lot-but-not-all): the firm's answer to "everyone is already doing this". Several
   different answers, and where they disagree.
2. [Wedges, timing and idea screens](wedges-timing-and-idea-screens): twelve ways to get a foothold, the three-part
   test for timing, and the technology-window cycle.
3. [The Speed Masterclass, episode by episode](speed-masterclass): ten episodes on moving faster than a bigger rival.
4. [The Network Effects Masterclass, episode by episode](network-effects-masterclass): ten episodes, including a map of
   16 kinds of network effect.
5. [AI-era companies](ai-era-companies): small teams, the death of the thin AI wrapper, and how to defend a company
   once the first speed advantage fades.
6. [A question bank for challenging an idea](question-bank): eighteen questions, grouped by what they test.
7. [What is on the channel](channel-guide): the series on NFX's YouTube channel and what was and was not read.

## How the claims in this series were checked

Assessment: this matters, so it comes first. An earlier draft was written from machine summaries of the videos, and
when those were checked against the real transcripts most had errors: wrong lengths, invented examples and company
names, and lists regrouped or padded. Everything below was rewritten from the official transcripts on nfx.com (for
the two masterclass courses) or the YouTube transcripts (for the other videos), read on 2026-10-22, and from NFX's
own web posts. A claim labeled FACT is something a named speaker said in a source that was read. The YouTube
captions are automatic and garble some names, so where a speaker could not be confirmed the note says "an NFX video"
instead of naming a person. Figures that a speaker gave from memory (revenues, valuations, user counts) are the
speaker's own claims, not independently checked.
