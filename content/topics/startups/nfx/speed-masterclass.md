# The Speed Masterclass, episode by episode

The Speed Masterclass is season two of NFX's free founder course, ten episodes taught mostly by James Currier. Its
thesis, in plain English: a small company cannot out-spend a big one, so it has to out-move it, and most slowness
comes from the founder's own habits and fears, which can be changed. The episodes are on
[nfx.com](https://www.nfx.com/masterclass/the-speed-masterclass) behind a free login, with transcripts, and also on
YouTube. Everything below was read from the transcripts.

## 1. Welcome and course breakdown (2 minutes)
FACT: [Currier opens](https://www.nfx.com/masterclass/the-speed-masterclass/welcome) by defining speed as learning
faster and removing things, not longer hours, and says it compounds every other part of a business. He adds that speed
only pays off if the idea is right.

## 2. The Speed Talk (1 hour)
FACT: [Currier says](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2tEETESsz4s) a startup's only reliable edge against larger
rivals is speed, measured as moves and experiments per unit of time. Slowness comes from a speed bar set too low by
school and corporate life, fear, and complexity. He gives twelve ways of thinking to get out of your own way (among
them: the product is not you, build the skateboard before the car, borrow what already works, and try things behind
the scenes under another name). He also gives a "No List" of early-stage things to skip, including usability studies,
marketing spend, business-development deals and a budgeting process, and sorts employees into commandos, soldiers and
police, saying to hire commandos first and move police out.

## 3. 22 mindsets that kill your speed (21 minutes)
FACT: [Currier calls](https://www.nfx.com/masterclass/the-speed-masterclass/22-mindsets-that-kill-speed) founder
psychology the greatest point of leverage and gives 22 mindsets that can be learned (he skips number 11 in the
slides, so there are 21 items on screen). He then covers personality, citing a finding that tolerance for ambiguity
and having founder parents are the two strongest predictors, and notes that different investors screen for different
traits.

## 4. The hidden patterns of great startup ideas (40 minutes)
Covered in [copy a lot, but not all](copy-a-lot-but-not-all) and [wedges, timing and idea
screens](wedges-timing-and-idea-screens).

## 5. Magic words (30 minutes)
FACT: [Currier argues](https://www.nfx.com/masterclass/the-speed-masterclass/magic-words) that the language,
positioning and name of a company set how fast customers, employees and investors move, so finding the "magic words"
is the founder's first job, ahead of product and engineering, and only founders can own it.

## 6. Speed across your market map (14 minutes of transcript)
FACT: [Currier says](https://www.nfx.com/masterclass/the-speed-masterclass/speed-across-your-market) founders go faster
when they know the terrain better than anyone: current and past competitors, substitutes, customer segments and market
size. Building the map by hand, in detail, puts it in the founder's head. (The course page lists the episode at 33
minutes but the transcript covers about 14.)

## 7. Speed to revenue: pricing and conversion (32 minutes)
FACT: [Currier ranks](https://www.nfx.com/masterclass/the-speed-masterclass/speed-of-revenue) why customers pay: "make
me money" first, then compliance or security, ego, and urgency, with saving money, doing the job better and saving
time last. He says to price the same as the benchmark (a competitor or the thing you replace) and go lower only if your
costs can be lower, to test higher prices, and not to cut prices out of fear.

## 8. Fundraising speed: the ladder of proof (22 minutes)
FACT: [Currier's tool](https://www.nfx.com/masterclass/the-speed-masterclass/fundraising-speed) is a ladder of proof:
anyone judging you, whether investor, journalist or recruit, is asking which rung you are on. The rungs run from team and
idea through market, expert advisors, a proven product build, customers, retention and revenue to rapid growth. He says
the metrics only move after six "big rocks" are in place: team, story, name, mindset, customer and channel.

## 9. The 3-person unicorn (6 minutes)
FACT: [Currier says](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=llsqVsQrQog) large teams move slowly and small teams fast, and AI
strengthens that, so a few people running AI agents can build very large companies. He names three roles: a visionary
founder, a numbers person and a words person. He is careful to say he is not telling everyone to build a company of
three.

## 10. The 40 tactics of speed (25 minutes)
FACT: [Currier gives](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yo_sk2dHaQQ) 40 tactics collected over about 15 years in three
groups (planning and thinking, team and culture, operations), plus two bonuses: decide fast, separating reversible
from irreversible decisions, and say "forget it" often and move on. A few from the list: find the fast-moving water in
your market; hire for speed and make every new hire ship something on day one; keep teams under about six; keep the
board to three; set externally imposed deadlines; put one person in charge; and copy non-core features instead of
reinventing them.
