# Master of Darkness (Master System)

**Developer:** SIMS | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1992

---

## Introduction

Master of Darkness is the Master System's Castlevania, and it is unusually good at being one. You play Dr Ferdinand Social, a Victorian psychiatrist investigating Whitechapel murders, and the trail leads to Dracula. It is 1992 gothic horror on 8-bit hardware, arriving right at the end of the system's life in the West.

The Castlevania comparison is fair and openly worn: whip-adjacent melee, sub-weapons, candles that drop items, a stairway-and-platform level grammar. What it adds is a **weapon system with real variety** and a setting — Victorian London — that Castlevania never used.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move, duck.
- **Button 1:** Jump.
- **Button 2:** Attack with the current melee weapon.
- **Up + Button 2:** Use the sub-weapon.

---

## Weapons

Unlike Castlevania's single whip, your primary weapon changes and each has different reach and speed:

| Weapon | Character |
|---|---|
| **Knife** | Starting weapon. Short, fast, weak. |
| **Cane sword** | Longer reach, good speed. The workhorse. |
| **Axe** | Slow, heavy, high damage. |
| **Rapier** | Longest reach, low damage per hit. |

**Sub-weapons** are thrown: pistol, boomerang, shuriken, and holy water equivalents, each with limited ammunition found in candles and crates.

**Reach beats damage in most fights.** Being able to hit an enemy before it reaches you is worth more than hitting harder, particularly against the flying enemies that make up much of the mid-game.

---

## Stages

Six stages moving through Victorian London and beyond: the fog-bound streets of Whitechapel, a waxworks, a graveyard, a ship, and eventually Dracula's castle.

**The waxworks stage** is the one people remember — mannequins that animate when you pass, which is a genuinely effective bit of 8-bit horror staging.

**Bosses** are pattern fights with clear tells, in the Castlevania mould. Each has a safe position and a punish window.

---

## Strategy

**Take the cane sword and keep it.** The knife is a starting weapon, not a choice; the axe's slowness gets you hit.

**Break every candle and crate.** Sub-weapon ammunition, health and points are all in the scenery, and the game expects you to be topped up.

**Learn the knockback.** Taking a hit knocks you backwards, and on a platform that means falling. Fighting with your back to a gap is how most deaths happen.

**Sub-weapons for flying enemies.** Melee against airborne targets is a losing trade.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Master System core with **rewind enabled**, which suits the knockback-into-pit deaths this genre specialises in.

The ROM is `Master of Darkness (Europe, Brazil)`. **Note the region tags: this game had no North American release.** It shipped in Europe and Brazil only, which is why it is absent from most American retrospectives despite being one of the better late-life Master System titles. Under this library's US-only rule it is a documented exception, kept because there is no US version to prefer.

It was also released in Japan as *Vampire: Master of Darkness*.
