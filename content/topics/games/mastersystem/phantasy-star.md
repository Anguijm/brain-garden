# Phantasy Star (Master System)

**Developer:** Sega | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1987

---

## Introduction

Phantasy Star was extraordinary for 1987 and it is still worth playing. While Final Fantasy was doing top-down villages and static battle screens, Sega shipped a console RPG with **fully animated first-person dungeons that scrolled smoothly in three dimensions**, a science-fantasy setting spanning three planets, and a female protagonist — Alis — at a time when that was close to unheard of.

The cartridge had battery backup and a custom chip for the dungeon rendering. It looks and moves better than most 8-bit RPGs made years later.

It is also hard in the specific way early RPGs were hard: it expects you to map, to grind, and to talk to everyone twice.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move. In dungeons, up walks forward, left/right turn.
- **Button 1:** Confirm / talk / examine.
- **Button 2:** Menu — status, items, magic, equip.
- **Start/Pause:** Menu in some contexts.

---

## The party

You gather four characters, and unlike most RPGs of the era they are fixed individuals rather than a class you build.

| Character | Role |
|---|---|
| **Alis** | The protagonist. Balanced fighter, some magic. |
| **Myau** | A cat-like creature. Fast, and the one who can use certain transport items. |
| **Odin** | Heavy fighter, high HP, no magic. |
| **Noah** | The mage. Fragile, and the reason you survive the late game. |

You cannot swap them out and you cannot lose them permanently, which keeps the focus on the story rather than on party management.

---

## The three planets

**Palma** — the starting world. Towns, the first dungeons, and where you learn the game's rhythm.

**Motavia** — desert planet. Introduces the land rover vehicle and larger dungeons.

**Dezoris** — ice planet. Late game, and the most punishing dungeons in the game.

Travel between them requires spaceships and passports, which are gated by story progress. **The game does not tell you where to go.** Directions come from NPCs, and missing one conversation can leave you wandering.

---

## What makes it hard

**The dungeons are 3D mazes with no automap.** They loop, they contain one-way doors, and they look identical in every direction. Most players map them on paper. If you are not willing to do that, use save states liberally — this is the single biggest quality-of-life difference between playing it in 1987 and playing it now.

**Encounter rate is high** and random encounters can kill an under-levelled party quickly.

**Money is tight early.** Equipment upgrades matter more than levels, and you will need to grind for them.

**Some items are non-obvious.** Progress frequently depends on having bought or found a specific object — the Laconian items especially — with only a passing NPC mention to tell you.

---

## Strategy

**Talk to everyone, in every town, after every story event.** The game's entire hint system is conversational and it updates as you progress.

**Map the dungeons or use save states.** There is no shame in either. The mazes are the main source of lost time.

**Buy armour before weapons.** Surviving a random encounter matters more than shortening it.

**Keep Noah alive.** The mage carries the late game; a dead Noah in a Dezoris dungeon usually means a long walk back.

**Save often.** The battery save exists — use it before every dungeon.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Master System core, and **rewind is enabled** on this system. In Phantasy Star rewind is genuinely transformative: it removes the sting of a random encounter wiping a party deep in an unmapped maze, which is the failure mode that ends most modern attempts at this game.

Save states are worth using before entering any new dungeon.

The ROM is `Phantasy Star (World) (Sega Ages)`. One cartridge image served all regions, so this is the correct North American file despite carrying no `(USA)` tag; the "Sega Ages" designation refers to the later re-release packaging of the same dump.
