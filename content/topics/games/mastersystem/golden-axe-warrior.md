# Golden Axe Warrior (Master System)

**Developer:** Sega | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1991

---

## Introduction

Golden Axe Warrior is the Master System's answer to *The Legend of Zelda*, and it is a far better game than "clone" suggests. It borrows the structure openly — overworld screens, nine labyrinths, a map and compass in each, heart containers, a sword that fires when you are at full health — and then executes it with more variety than the original.

It has nothing to do with the *Golden Axe* arcade beat-em-up beyond the name and setting. Sega attached a known brand to an original adventure game.

It arrived in 1991, at the very end of the Master System's Western life, which is why almost nobody played it.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move in four directions.
- **Button 1:** Sword. Fires a projectile at full health.
- **Button 2:** Use the selected magic or item.
- **Pause:** Inventory, map, magic selection.

---

## Structure

Nine labyrinths, each holding a **crystal**, plus the final confrontation with Death Adder. The overworld connects them and hides a great deal.

**Each labyrinth contains a map, a compass and a key item**, exactly as in Zelda. The key item is usually what opens the next area, so the order is loosely fixed.

**Magic replaces Zelda's item variety.** You collect elemental magic — earth, water, fire, thunder — and each has both a combat and a puzzle use. Some walls only yield to a specific element.

---

## What it does better than its inspiration

**The overworld is signposted.** Villagers give real directions rather than cryptic hints, and there is far less bombing of random walls.

**The magic system has more depth** than Zelda's item slots, because elements interact with terrain.

**Enemy variety is higher**, and enemies scale in a way that makes mid-game exploration feel earned rather than punishing.

---

## Strategy

**Stay at full health for the ranged sword.** As in Zelda, dropping below full turns your sword into a melee weapon and roughly halves your safety. Walking back to a heart drop is often the right move.

**Talk to every villager.** The hint system is genuinely useful here, unlike its inspiration.

**Buy the armour upgrades.** Flat damage reduction, and they compound.

**Try each magic on obstacles.** When a route seems blocked, the answer is usually an element you have not tried on it.

**Map the labyrinths or grab the in-dungeon map early.** The maps are in the dungeons for a reason.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Master System core with **rewind enabled**.

The game uses battery saves rather than passwords, so it is friendlier to modern play than most of the system's library.

The ROM is `Golden Axe Warrior (USA, Europe, Brazil)`. Worth repeating that this is unrelated to the *Golden Axe* beat-em-ups also on this system — if you want that, it is a separate game entirely.
