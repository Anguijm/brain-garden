# Ys: The Vanished Omens (Master System)

**Developer:** Nihon Falcom / Sega | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1988

---

## Introduction

Ys is an action-RPG with the strangest combat system of its era: **you attack by walking into enemies.** There is no attack button. Adol Christin damages things by colliding with them, and whether he wins the exchange depends entirely on *how* he collides.

That sounds broken and isn't. It is a positioning game — offset your approach so you strike the enemy's side or back rather than meeting head-on, and you take little or no damage while dealing plenty. Meet an enemy square-on and you both take damage.

Once it clicks, combat is fast and fluid in a way turn-based contemporaries were not.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move. **This is also your attack.**
- **Button 1:** Menu — items, equipment, magic.
- **Button 2:** Use selected item / talk.

---

## The bump-combat rule

**Never hit an enemy dead centre.** Approach offset — aim for a shoulder rather than the middle — and Adol strikes without taking a counter-hit.

Approaching from behind is best of all. Circling round is often quicker than trading blows from the front.

**Levels matter enormously.** Ys expects you to be roughly the right level for an area, and being under-levelled turns trivial enemies into threats. Being over-levelled makes them harmless. The game is short, so grinding a level or two is quick and usually the right answer to a wall.

---

## Progression

Equipment is bought in towns, and each tier is a step change rather than an increment. **Buy the best sword, shield and armour you can afford** on reaching a new town — the difficulty of the next area assumes you did.

**Rings** provide passive effects: healing over time, increased power, protection. The Healing Ring in particular changes how sustainable exploration is.

**The books** are the story spine. Collecting the six Books of Ys drives the plot, and each is behind a dungeon.

---

## Darm Tower

The final dungeon is the game's centrepiece and its reputation — a tall, multi-floor tower with puzzles, a boss on several floors, and the memorable climb. It is long, and it is where an under-levelled or under-equipped party discovers the fact.

---

## Strategy

**Offset every approach.** It is the whole combat system.

**Grind briefly when stuck.** Ys is short; two levels solves most walls and takes minutes.

**Buy equipment immediately in each new town.**

**Save before Darm Tower.** It is a long commitment.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Master System core, **rewind enabled** — which pairs unusually well with bump combat, since a mistimed approach can cost a lot of health quickly and the correction is a two-second rewind rather than a walk back to an inn.

The ROM is `Ys - The Vanished Omens (USA, Europe, Brazil) (En)`. The series continued for decades; this is the first game, and the Master System version is a distinct adaptation of Falcom's PC original rather than a straight port.
