# Master System Game Guides

Standalone walkthroughs for the Master System titles on the UM790Pro. Sega's 8-bit machine
lost badly to the NES in North America and Japan, and won convincingly in Europe and Brazil —
where Tectoy kept it in production for decades. That geography shapes the library: several of
its best games never had a US release at all.

## Play this Sonic, not the Game Gear one

Ancient built **one** 8-bit Sonic and shipped it on both machines — same levels, same physics,
same content. The difference is the viewport: 256x192 here against 160x144 on Game Gear.
Everything that makes the handheld version feel unfair is a consequence of not being able to
see hazards coming. Both are in this library; this is the one to play.

## Where rewind earns its place, and where it does not

Rewind is enabled for this system. It matters most on **R-Type**, where death strips your Force
and every weapon and respawns you mid-stage — a late death without them usually ends the run,
so unwinding two seconds preserves twenty minutes of accumulated loadout. It matters nearly as
much on **Phantasy Star**, whose 3D dungeons have no automap and where a random encounter can
wipe a party deep inside an unmapped maze.

**Alex Kidd** is a one-hit-death game with rock-paper-scissors bosses, so rewind removes a lot
of loss that teaches nothing.

## Two naming traps in this library

**Shinobi** here is the arcade conversion — hostages, two-plane layout. The **Shinobi** in the
Game Gear folder is *The GG Shinobi*, an original design where you swap between five ninja.
Same name, unrelated games.

**Golden Axe Warrior** has nothing to do with the *Golden Axe* beat-em-ups. It is an original
Zelda-style adventure with the brand attached.

## One documented exception

**Master of Darkness** is `(Europe, Brazil)` — it had **no North American release**. Under this
library's US-only rule it is kept as a documented exception, because there is no US version to
prefer over it.
