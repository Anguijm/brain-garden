# Wonder Boy III: The Dragon's Trap (Master System)

**Developer:** Westone | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1989

---

## Introduction

The Dragon's Trap is the best game on the Master System and one of the best of its generation. It is a Metroidvania four years before *Super Metroid*, built around a mechanic nobody else used: **you are cursed into animal forms, and each form is a different moveset.**

You begin as Hu-Man, defeat a dragon, and are transformed into Lizard-Man. From then on every dragon you kill transforms you again, and each body opens parts of the world the others cannot reach. It is a genuinely elegant progression system — your "items" are your body.

The 2017 remake by Lizardcube is beautiful and faithful. This is the original, and it holds up.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move, duck.
- **Button 1:** Jump.
- **Button 2:** Attack — sword, or the form's equivalent.
- **Up + Button 2:** Use the selected item (fireball, arrow, boomerang, thunder).
- **Pause:** Inventory and equipment.

---

## The five forms

| Form | Ability |
|---|---|
| **Hu-Man** | The starting body. Best equipment options; no special traversal. Regained at the end. |
| **Lizard-Man** | Breathes fire. Cannot use swords well. The first curse. |
| **Mouse-Man** | Small, and **sticks to checkered walls and ceilings**. Reaches tiny passages. |
| **Piranha-Man** | **Swims properly.** Everything underwater requires him. |
| **Lion-Man** | Strong attack that **strikes upward and downward**, breaking blocks above and below. |
| **Hawk-Man** | **Flies freely.** The most mobile form and the last acquired. |

**You can change form at will** once you find the transformation rooms, and the game is built around switching constantly: Mouse to climb a shaft, Piranha to cross the water at the bottom, Hawk to reach the ledge on the far side.

---

## Structure

One connected world, no level select. The hub is Monster Land's town, where you buy equipment and heal. From there the map branches, and each region is gated by which form you have.

**Equipment matters and is form-specific.** Some armour and weapons cannot be used by some forms, so a shopping trip is worth doing as Hu-Man where possible.

**Charm points** are a hidden stat that determines what shopkeepers will sell you. Higher charm unlocks better shops. It is raised by equipment, and a few shops are otherwise inaccessible — this is the game's most obscure system and the reason some players never see certain items.

---

## Strategy

**Switch forms constantly.** The commonest mistake is settling into one body. Every region has a form that makes it easy.

**Explore backwards.** Each new form opens routes in areas you have already cleared. When stuck, return to earlier regions with your newest body.

**Mouse-Man for the checkered walls.** They are visually distinct and they are everywhere once you start looking.

**Buy armour early.** Damage reduction compounds across a long game.

**Passwords, not saves.** The original uses a password system. Write them down, or use save states.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Master System core with **rewind enabled**, which suits the platforming sections over instant-death hazards.

Use save states rather than the password system unless you enjoy transcribing long strings — passwords here encode your form, equipment and progress, and a single mistyped character loses everything.

The ROM is `Wonder Boy III - The Dragon's Trap (USA, Europe)`. Note the confusing series naming: this is *not* the same game as *Wonder Boy III: Monster Lair*, an unrelated arcade shooter released the same year, and it is a direct sequel to *Wonder Boy in Monster Land*.
