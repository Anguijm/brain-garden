# Top 10 games on every system Batocera can run

Seventeen systems, 170 games. Each list is ranked by a combination of critical consensus and lasting playability — not nostalgia. Scores are Assessment based on aggregated critical reception; nothing here was play-tested against this specific hardware. For PS3 and Xbox 360, compatibility with the emulator matters as much as game quality; notes on that are included.

## Sega Master System

Sega's answer to the NES lost the North American market war but dominated Europe and Brazil, and its library reflects that: tighter ports of Sega's arcade output and a handful of RPGs that have no NES equivalent. Batocera runs it via Genesis Plus GX, the same core that handles the Genesis. Performance is perfect.

1. **Phantasy Star** (1987) — RPG — Assessment: 9/10
   One of the best RPGs of the 8-bit era, with 3D dungeons and a story that holds up. It is harder and stranger than early Final Fantasy, and it went places Nintendo-adjacent RPGs would not reach for years.

2. **Wonder Boy III: The Dragon's Trap** (1989) — Action-RPG — Assessment: 9/10
   A shape-shifting action RPG with a continuous world and transformation mechanics that feel modern. The 2017 remake exists if you want the facelift, but the original still plays cleanly.

3. **Alex Kidd in Miracle World** (1986) — Platformer — Assessment: 8/10
   Sega's mascot before Sonic. Built into many consoles from the factory. The boss fights are rock-paper-scissors, which is either charming or maddening depending on your tolerance. The later levels are harder than they look.

4. **R-Type** (1988) — Shoot-em-up — Assessment: 8.5/10
   The best port of the Irem arcade classic on a home console at the time. Slow, deliberate, and brutal — the charge shot and the Force pod system reward patience in a genre that usually rewards reflexes.

5. **Golden Axe Warrior** (1991) — Action-RPG — Assessment: 8/10
   A Zelda-style top-down RPG wearing Golden Axe clothes. Better than it has any right to be. If you played every Zelda and want something adjacent from the same era, this fills the gap.

6. **Shinobi** (1988) — Action — Assessment: 8/10
   A strong home conversion of the Sega arcade hit. Hostage rescue stages, throwing stars, and a bonus round that breaks the rhythm. Harder than the arcade version on later stages.

7. **Master of Darkness** (1992) — Action-platformer — Assessment: 8/10
   A Castlevania clone that is genuinely good. Victorian horror setting, whip-adjacent weapon mechanics, and a progression structure borrowed directly from Konami. Available in North America as Dracula (Game Gear version) but the Master System release is the complete one.

8. **Ys: The Vanished Omens** (1988) — Action-RPG — Assessment: 8/10
   The first entry in the Ys series, known for bump combat where you run into enemies at the right angle to deal damage. Odd but fast, and the soundtrack is one of the best on the hardware.

9. **Sonic the Hedgehog** (1991) — Platformer — Assessment: 7.5/10
   A scaled-back conversion of the Genesis launch title. Missing levels, reduced parallax, and the spin dash is absent in some versions. Still playable and historically significant, but play the Genesis version first.

10. **Psycho Fox** (1989) — Platformer — Assessment: 7.5/10
    A loose spiritual predecessor to the Wonder Boy series. Character selection changes your jump arc and a bird companion can be thrown at enemies. Underrated and rarely discussed outside of Master System enthusiasts.

## NES

The NES library has been picked over for forty years; what survives is genuinely hard and genuinely good. The UM790Pro runs everything at full speed with upscaling options that look better than they have any right to.

1. **Super Mario Bros. 3** (1988) — Platformer — Assessment: 10/10
   The high point of the NES era. Level design, power-up variety, and moment-to-moment feel have not aged. The only caveat is that you probably already know it.

2. **Mega Man 2** (1988) — Action-platformer — Assessment: 9.5/10
   The template every action-platformer copies. Eight robot masters, tight controls, and a difficulty curve that rewards learning rather than luck. The soundtrack is an outlier for the hardware.

3. **Metroid** (1986) — Action-adventure — Assessment: 9/10
   The original nonlinear exploration game. Atmospheric and disorienting in a way its successors have tried to recapture. Save states help with the brutal lack of in-game saving.

4. **Contra** (1988) — Run-and-gun — Assessment: 9/10
   Two-player co-op that still works. Hard enough that earning the ending feels meaningful. The Spread Gun remains one of the best weapon pickups in gaming.

5. **Castlevania** (1986) — Action-platformer — Assessment: 9/10
   Gothic atmosphere and punishing platforming. The whip-based combat aged better than most contemporaries. Symphony of the Night on PS1 is the better game, but this is where Simon Belmont was established.

6. **Tecmo Super Bowl** (1991) — Sports — Assessment: 8.5/10
   The best football game of its era, possibly the best arcade football game ever made. Barry Sanders is as broken as you remember.

7. **Mega Man 3** (1990) — Action-platformer — Assessment: 8.5/10
   More of Mega Man 2 with a slide mechanic and Proto Man cameos. Slightly easier, slightly longer, and sometimes underrated relative to its predecessor.

8. **DuckTales** (1989) — Platformer — Assessment: 8.5/10
   Capcom at the height of its Disney licensing era. Pogo-stick cane movement, non-linear world selection, and a Moon level theme that refuses to leave your memory.

9. **Battletoads** (1991) — Beat-em-up/Platformer — Assessment: 8/10
   Infamous difficulty makes it a genuine achievement to finish. The turbo tunnel section is a legitimate test. Best played with save states and no shame.

10. **Ninja Gaiden** (1988) — Action-platformer — Assessment: 8/10
    Cinematic story presentation by NES standards and tight action. The final stage's respawn mechanic will test patience. Worth it.

## SNES

The SNES library is one of the strongest in gaming history. Mode 7, the SPC700 sound chip, and a run of first- and third-party titles that defined genres still define genres. Everything runs perfectly.

1. **Super Metroid** (1994) — Action-adventure — Assessment: 10/10
   The blueprint for every Metroidvania. Atmosphere, pacing, and world design that have never been topped in the genre. Short by modern standards; replayable indefinitely.

2. **The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past** (1991) — Action-adventure — Assessment: 10/10
   The game that defined what a Zelda game is. Dual world mechanic, dungeon design, and overworld exploration that hold up without qualification.

3. **Chrono Trigger** (1995) — RPG — Assessment: 10/10
   The most efficient RPG ever made. Multiple endings, no random encounters after a point, a time travel story that pays off, and a combat system with dual and triple techs that rewards party experimentation.

4. **Super Mario World** (1990) — Platformer — Assessment: 10/10
   The launch title that remains one of the best games on the system. Yoshi, capes, secret exits, and a world map that rewards exploration. Bowser's castle sequence is a highlight of the era.

5. **Final Fantasy VI** (1994) — RPG — Assessment: 9.5/10
   Ensemble cast, opera scene, and Kefka — one of the only RPG villains who actually wins mid-game. The longest and most ambitious 16-bit JRPG.

6. **Donkey Kong Country 2: Diddy's Kong Quest** (1995) — Platformer — Assessment: 9.5/10
   Better than the original DKC in every respect. Pre-rendered graphics that still hold up, a pirate aesthetic, and Bramble Blast to haunt you.

7. **Street Fighter II Turbo** (1993) — Fighting — Assessment: 9/10
   The definitive home port of the arcade original for its time. Eight characters, tight controls, and the competitive depth that built the fighting game community.

8. **EarthBound** (1994) — RPG — Assessment: 9/10
   Satirical, strange, and emotionally effective. Combat is modern-day America via a Japanese lens, and the final sequence is unlike anything else on the platform.

9. **Super Castlevania IV** (1991) — Action-platformer — Assessment: 9/10
   The best Castlevania before Symphony of the Night redefined the series. Eight-directional whip, excellent level design, and a soundtrack that makes use of every SNES audio channel.

10. **Mega Man X** (1993) — Action-platformer — Assessment: 9/10
    The series reinvention. Dash mechanic, wall-climbing, and a difficulty curve that opens up as you collect abilities. The opening Sigma stage is a masterclass in teaching through play.

## Sega Genesis (Mega Drive)

The Genesis competed with the SNES on attitude and speed. Its library rewards players who want faster, harder, and louder. The UM790Pro's Vulkan renderer handles the hardware's quirky color palette well.

1. **Sonic the Hedgehog 2** (1992) — Platformer — Assessment: 9.5/10
   Speed-focused platforming at its peak for this hardware. Chemical Plant Zone, Mystic Cave, the Casino Night pinball sections. Tails co-op is optional and mostly harmless.

2. **Streets of Rage 2** (1992) — Beat-em-up — Assessment: 9.5/10
   The genre benchmark. Four characters with distinct movesets, a soundtrack by Yuzo Koshiro that holds up against anything, and two-player co-op that fills a room.

3. **Gunstar Heroes** (1993) — Run-and-gun — Assessment: 9.5/10
   Treasure's debut and still one of the finest action games on the platform. Weapon combining, bullet-hell boss fights, and a pace that never lets up.

4. **Castlevania: Bloodlines** (1994) — Action-platformer — Assessment: 9/10
   The only Castlevania on Genesis and one of its best entries. Two characters with different mechanics and a European Gothic aesthetic distinct from the SNES entries.

5. **Comix Zone** (1995) — Beat-em-up — Assessment: 8.5/10
   Unique comic-panel navigation mechanic and hand-drawn art that still looks good. Brutally short but brutally difficult. The soundtrack is exceptional for the hardware.

6. **Phantasy Star IV** (1993) — RPG — Assessment: 9/10
   The conclusion of the Phantasy Star saga. The best RPG on the Genesis, with a macro/combo system and a story that ties together threads from three previous games.

7. **Mortal Kombat II** (1994) — Fighting — Assessment: 8.5/10
   The Genesis version had blood enabled by default, making it the definitive home port over the censored SNES version. The fatality system was genuinely shocking for its time.

8. **Shinobi III: Return of the Ninja Master** (1993) — Action-platformer — Assessment: 8.5/10
   Fluid movement, multiple attack options, and boss fights that demand pattern recognition. The surfing and horseback levels are memorable setpieces.

9. **Road Rash II** (1992) — Racing — Assessment: 8/10
   Motorcycle racing with clubs and chains. Two-player split-screen, five countries, and a sense of speed that still reads. No racing game on this system is more fun in a room.

10. **ToeJam and Earl** (1991) — Roguelite — Assessment: 8/10
    Procedurally generated levels, two-player co-op, and a funk soundtrack. An odd game that found a cult following. The randomness means no two runs are identical.

## Sega Saturn

The Saturn's library is one of gaming's best-kept secrets — strong 2D fighters, RPGs, and exclusives that never made it to other platforms. The UM790Pro handles it well via Mednafen/Beetle Saturn.

1. **Panzer Dragoon Saga** (1998) — RPG — Assessment: 10/10
   One of the rarest and most expensive physical games in existence. The RPG branch of the Panzer Dragoon series, with a unique 3D flight combat system and a story worth the trip. Physical copies sell for \$600+; this is the emulation use case.

2. **Radiant Silvergun** (1998) — Shoot-em-up — Assessment: 10/10
   The argument for the shoot-em-up as an art form. Seven weapons with real strategic depth, a story delivered between stages, and a scoring system worth mastering.

3. **Guardian Heroes** (1996) — Beat-em-up/RPG — Assessment: 9.5/10
   Branching narrative beat-em-up with an RPG leveling system and up to six simultaneous players. Treasure again. The branching system means replayability is built in.

4. **Nights into Dreams** (1996) — Action — Assessment: 9/10
   Sega's attempt to define 3D for the Saturn. Flight-based movement through dream worlds, a score system tied to performance, and a visual style that holds up. The Christmas NiGHTS bonus disc is also worth loading.

5. **Castlevania: Symphony of the Night** (1997) — Action-RPG — Assessment: 10/10
   Available on PS1 as well, but the Saturn version includes additional content. The game that turned Castlevania into a Metroidvania and defined the subgenre's conventions.

6. **Burning Rangers** (1998) — Action — Assessment: 8.5/10
   Sega's last major Saturn release. Firefighting third-person action in fully 3D environments, by the Sonic Team. Technically impressive for the hardware and narratively earnest.

7. **Dragon Force** (1996) — Strategy/RPG — Assessment: 9/10
   Large-scale tactical RPG with battles involving hundreds of units. Eight monarchs to choose from, each with different story paths. One of the most ambitious games the Saturn produced.

8. **Sega Rally Championship** (1995) — Racing — Assessment: 8.5/10
   The best racing game on the platform. Three courses, physics that model surface conditions, and a feel that the Saturn's hardware suited better than it suited most 3D games.

9. **Street Fighter Alpha 2** (1996) — Fighting — Assessment: 9/10
   The Saturn version is the closest home port to the CPS2 arcade original. Custom combos, a large roster, and the peak of the Alpha series.

10. **Shining Force III** (1998) — Tactical RPG — Assessment: 9/10
    Three interconnected scenarios released in Japan; only Scenario 1 was released in the West. The Saturn's best tactical RPG and a strong one by any standard. Fan translations cover the other scenarios.

## PlayStation (PS1)

The PS1 library defined the 32-bit era and delivered the first wave of 3D gaming done well. The UM790Pro handles it at full speed with upscaling that smooths the low-resolution textures without destroying the aesthetic.

1. **Final Fantasy VII** (1997) — RPG — Assessment: 9.5/10
   The game that brought JRPGs to a mainstream Western audience. Materia system, Midgar, the moment in Disc 1 that everyone remembers. Pre-rendered backgrounds hide the polygon limitations gracefully.

2. **Castlevania: Symphony of the Night** (1997) — Action-RPG — Assessment: 10/10
   The best action-RPG on the platform and the game that named a subgenre. Inverted castle, equipment system, and more content than most games twice its size.

3. **Metal Gear Solid** (1998) — Stealth-action — Assessment: 9.5/10
   Cinematic storytelling, stealth mechanics that still feel considered, and boss fights designed around the controller. The codec calls alone justify the playthrough.

4. **Resident Evil 2** (1998) — Survival horror — Assessment: 9.5/10
   Two interconnected campaigns, limited resources, and a tension level that the fixed camera angles enhanced rather than limited. Leon and Claire's stories change based on order of play.

5. **Tekken 3** (1998) — Fighting — Assessment: 9.5/10
   The best 3D fighting game of the era. Fast, deep, and filled with characters that have remained in the series for decades. Tekken Force mode is a bonus.

6. **Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2** (2000) — Sports/Action — Assessment: 9.5/10
   The high point of the THPS formula. Create-a-skater, level editor, and a goal structure that rewards mastery. The control scheme remains the template.

7. **Silent Hill** (1999) — Survival horror — Assessment: 9/10
   Psychological horror through fog and static. The town of Silent Hill as a manifestation of trauma is a more interesting concept than Resident Evil's monster houses, and it mostly works.

8. **Spyro the Dragon** (1998) — 3D platformer — Assessment: 8.5/10
   The best 3D platformer on the platform that isn't Crash Bandicoot. Collectible-driven exploration, flight sections, and a color palette that still reads well.

9. **Crash Bandicoot 2: Cortex Strikes Back** (1997) — 3D platformer — Assessment: 9/10
   The series peak. Tighter level design than the original, a wider move set, and a difficulty curve that peaks with the bonus levels. Harder than it looks.

10. **Chrono Cross** (1999) — RPG — Assessment: 9/10
    Controversial as a Chrono Trigger successor because it barely connects to the original. On its own terms: enormous cast, elemental combat system, and a soundtrack by Yasunori Mitsuda that is among the best in the medium.

## Nintendo 64

The N64 library is smaller than its contemporaries but contains some of the most influential 3D games ever made. Dolphin handles N64 emulation through the Wii's Virtual Console versions for many titles; standalone N64 emulation via Mupen64Plus is also solid.

1. **The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time** (1998) — Action-adventure — Assessment: 10/10
   The game that defined 3D action-adventure. Z-targeting, context-sensitive actions, and a world that taught players how to navigate 3D space. Its influence is still visible everywhere.

2. **Super Mario 64** (1996) — 3D platformer — Assessment: 10/10
   The template for 3D platformers. 120 stars, a hub world that rewards exploration, and movement mechanics — wall kicks, long jumps, triple jumps — that speedrunners still optimize.

3. **GoldenEye 007** (1997) — First-person shooter — Assessment: 9.5/10
   The console FPS before Halo. Objective-based missions, stealth options, and four-player split-screen deathmatch that defined couch multiplayer for years. Controls age poorly without modern camera inversion.

4. **The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask** (2000) — Action-adventure — Assessment: 9.5/10
   A darker, stranger Zelda built on a three-day loop mechanic. Fewer dungeons than Ocarina, more character depth, and a structure that rewards mastery of the clock system.

5. **Super Smash Bros.** (1999) — Fighting — Assessment: 9/10
   The original four-player party fighter. Simpler than its successors, faster in some respects, and still fun with the right group. Pikachu remains the pick of competitive players from this era.

6. **Banjo-Kazooie** (1998) — 3D platformer — Assessment: 9.5/10
   Rare's best. Collectible-hunting through imaginatively designed worlds, excellent note/jiggy economy, and a duo with genuine chemistry. Tooie is larger but more bloated.

7. **Donkey Kong 64** (1999) — 3D platformer — Assessment: 8/10
   Massive and collectible-heavy to a fault — five characters each with their own items is 20% more than needed. The execution is solid and the multiplayer mode holds up, but it outstays its welcome.

8. **Star Fox 64** (1997) — Rail shooter — Assessment: 9/10
   Branching path system, fully voiced cast, and a campaign length that suits the genre. "Do a barrel roll" is a meme; the game deserves better than the meme.

9. **Paper Mario** (2001) — RPG — Assessment: 9/10
   The RPG that made Mario's world feel inhabited. Turn-based combat with timing-based action commands, a chapter structure, and writing sharper than any Mario game before it.

10. **F-Zero X** (1998) — Racing — Assessment: 9/10
    Thirty cars at extreme speed with a death mechanic that punishes mistakes honestly. No modern racing game captures this specific kind of speed. The randomized track generator adds replayability.

## Dreamcast

The Dreamcast had an 18-month window of being the most powerful consumer hardware available, and Sega filled it with experiments. Many of its best games never appeared elsewhere. Flycast handles Dreamcast emulation well on Batocera.

1. **Shenmue** (1999) — Action-adventure — Assessment: 9/10
   Open world, real-time clock, and a scope that no game had attempted before. Slow by modern standards in the best way. The second chapter follows and requires this one.

2. **Jet Grind Radio** (2000) — Action/Sports — Assessment: 9/10
   Cell-shaded graffiti and inline skates. A visual identity that holds up completely and a soundtrack that defines the era's sound. Short, replayable, and unlike anything else.

3. **Soul Calibur** (1999) — Fighting — Assessment: 9.5/10
   The arcade port that exceeded its source. Eight-way movement, a mission mode, and a roster with distinct weapons-based styles. The console port was technically superior to the arcade version at launch.

4. **Resident Evil: Code Veronica** (2000) — Survival horror — Assessment: 8.5/10
   The largest and most ambitious classic-era Resident Evil. Fixed cameras, tank controls, and a story that bridges RE2 and RE4 in scope.

5. **Phantasy Star Online** (2000) — Action-RPG — Assessment: 9/10
   The console MMO before consoles had reliable internet. Offline play is viable and still enjoyable; the social design assumptions are visible even single-player. Private servers restore the online experience.

6. **Skies of Arcadia** (2000) — RPG — Assessment: 9/10
   Airship exploration RPG with a high encounter rate that is the one genuine flaw. Otherwise: excellent world, memorable party, and sky-pirate adventure that few RPGs have matched for tone.

7. **Marvel vs. Capcom 2** (2000) — Fighting — Assessment: 9/10
   56 characters, three-on-three combat, and an assist system that enabled combinations never anticipated by the designers. The competitive scene it spawned lasted decades.

8. **Crazy Taxi** (2000) — Arcade — Assessment: 8.5/10
   Get passengers to their destinations before time runs out. Simple, loud, and instantly understandable. The Offspring and Bad Religion soundtrack cannot be separated from the experience.

9. **Power Stone 2** (2000) — Fighting — Assessment: 8.5/10
   Four-player arena fighter with dynamic stages that collapse, transform, and supply weapons. The best multiplayer game on the platform.

10. **Ikaruga** (2001) — Shoot-em-up — Assessment: 9.5/10
    Polarity-switching mechanic turns bullet avoidance into puzzle solving. Brief, brutal, and designed around a scoring system that rewards pattern mastery. One of the finest shoot-em-ups ever made.

## Game Boy Advance

The GBA library is deep with RPGs, platformers, and ports that occasionally exceeded their console originals. mGBA on Batocera is cycle-accurate and handles the full library without issue.

1. **Mother 3** (2006) — RPG — Assessment: 10/10
   Never officially released in English; the fan translation by Clyde Mandelin is the definitive version. Emotional, strange, and willing to go places most RPGs avoid. The battle system uses rhythm.

2. **Final Fantasy VI Advance** (2006) — RPG — Assessment: 9.5/10
   The best version of FF6 at time of release, with added content and a re-translated script. The sound chip is weaker than the SNES original but the added dungeon and espers are worth it.

3. **Golden Sun** (2001) — RPG — Assessment: 9/10
   The GBA's flagship RPG. Djinn system, Psynergy puzzle mechanics, and a story that demanded its sequel. Technically impressive for the hardware.

4. **Metroid Fusion** (2002) — Action-adventure — Assessment: 9.5/10
   Linear by Metroid standards, tense by any standard. The SA-X pursuit sequences are among the most effectively frightening moments in the series.

5. **Castlevania: Aria of Sorrow** (2003) — Action-RPG — Assessment: 9.5/10
   The peak of the GBA Castlevania trilogy. Soul Absorb mechanic adds RPG depth to the Metroidvania structure. Better paced than its predecessors.

6. **Fire Emblem: The Sacred Stones** (2004) — Tactical RPG — Assessment: 9/10
   The most accessible Fire Emblem released in English before the series became mainstream. Two routes, a world map for grinding, and permadeath that makes every decision matter.

7. **Advance Wars** (2001) — Tactical strategy — Assessment: 9.5/10
   The turn-based strategy game that introduced the genre to a generation. CO powers, terrain advantages, and a campaign long enough to teach every mechanic before testing it.

8. **The Legend of Zelda: The Minish Cap** (2004) — Action-adventure — Assessment: 9/10
   Capcom-developed Zelda with a size-shifting mechanic. Compact, polished, and underplayed relative to its quality.

9. **Pokémon Emerald** (2004) — RPG — Assessment: 9/10
   The definitive Generation III Pokémon game. Battle Frontier post-game extends playtime significantly. The Hoenn region is divisive; the execution is not.

10. **Tactics Ogre: The Knight of Lodis** (2001) — Tactical RPG — Assessment: 8.5/10
    A prequel to Tactics Ogre on SNES. Grid-based combat with a branching story driven by alignment choices. Deeper than it appears at first.

## PlayStation 2

The best-selling console ever produced has a library that justifies the claim. PCSX2 on Batocera runs the PS2 library with Vulkan rendering and handles upscaling cleanly. Enable the Vulkan renderer.

1. **Shadow of the Colossus** (2005) — Action-adventure — Assessment: 10/10
   Sixteen boss fights, no other enemies, and a desolate world that makes each colossus feel like an event. The moral weight accumulates. One of the clearest arguments for games as art.

2. **Metal Gear Solid 3: Snake Eater** (2004) — Stealth-action — Assessment: 10/10
   The Cold War prequel. Camouflage system, survival mechanics, and a final hour that ranks among the best storytelling in gaming. The Subsistence version adds online modes and a third-person camera option.

3. **God of War II** (2007) — Action — Assessment: 9.5/10
   The technical ceiling of the PS2. Scale that the hardware had no right to produce, combat that improved on the original in every way, and setpieces that run continuously.

4. **Final Fantasy XII** (2006) — RPG — Assessment: 9/10
   Gambit system lets you program party AI; exploration is open from early in the game. Divisive at launch for moving away from JRPG conventions; reappraised as the best FF since VI.

5. **Ico** (2001) — Action-adventure — Assessment: 9.5/10
   Shadow of the Colossus's companion piece. A boy leading a girl through a castle, with minimal combat and environmental puzzle design. The hand-holding mechanic is literal and deliberate.

6. **Okami** (2006) — Action-adventure — Assessment: 9.5/10
   Watercolor art style, Zelda-style dungeons, and a paintbrush mechanic where drawing shapes affects the world. The tutorial is long. The rest of it earns the patience.

7. **Resident Evil 4** (2005) — Action-survival horror — Assessment: 10/10
   The game that redefined third-person action. Over-the-shoulder camera, context-sensitive actions, and inventory management as tension mechanic. Influenced every subsequent action game.

8. **Kingdom Hearts II** (2005) — Action-RPG — Assessment: 9/10
   Better combat than the original, weaker narrative connective tissue. The Valor/Wisdom/Master Form system adds genuine depth to the hack-and-slash base. The story assumes you played Chain of Memories.

9. **Jak II** (2003) — Action-adventure — Assessment: 8.5/10
   A harder, darker open-world follow-up to Jak and Daxter. Hovercraft, weapons, and a city that feels populated. The difficulty spikes sharply but the world design rewards exploration.

10. **Dragon Quest VIII: Journey of the Cursed King** (2004) — RPG — Assessment: 9/10
    The first mainline Dragon Quest in full 3D. Turn-based combat without abstraction, a cheerful open world, and a monster team mechanic that adds post-game depth.

## GameCube

The GameCube's library is small and excellent. Dolphin runs it at 4K on the UM790Pro without effort. Some GameCube games benefit significantly from the widescreen patches Dolphin supports.

1. **The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker** (2002) — Action-adventure — Assessment: 9.5/10
   Cell-shaded sailing adventure. Initially divisive for the art style; reappraised as one of the best Zeldas for its expression and exploration. Dolphin's widescreen patch is recommended.

2. **Metroid Prime** (2002) — First-person adventure — Assessment: 10/10
   The 3D reinvention that should not have worked. Scanning mechanic, environmental storytelling, and world design that rewards attention. Best played with the Prime Remaster controls via Dolphin patches.

3. **Resident Evil 4** (2005) — Action-survival horror — Assessment: 10/10
   The GameCube version is the original and best. Slightly better textures than the PS2 port and the version the game was designed for.

4. **Super Smash Bros. Melee** (2001) — Fighting — Assessment: 9.5/10
   The fastest and most technically demanding Smash game. The GameCube controller is still the preferred input for competitive players twenty years later. Dolphin handles it accurately.

5. **Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door** (2004) — RPG — Assessment: 9.5/10
   The best Paper Mario by consensus. Audience mechanic, chapter-based structure, and writing that treats side characters as real characters.

6. **F-Zero GX** (2003) — Racing — Assessment: 9.5/10
   Sega-developed F-Zero with the fastest sense of speed of any racing game. Brutal difficulty curve; the story mode is intentionally punishing. The music is louder than it needs to be, which is correct.

7. **Pikmin 2** (2004) — Strategy — Assessment: 9/10
   No time limit, two captains, and a dungeon system that adds roguelite randomness. More comfortable than the original at the cost of some tension.

## Nintendo DS

The DS library is a goldmine of RPGs, visual novels, and experimental titles that used the dual screens and touch input in ways no other platform could replicate. DeSmuME and MelonDS both handle it on Batocera; MelonDS is more accurate.

1. **Chrono Trigger DS** (2008) — RPG — Assessment: 10/10
   The best version of the best RPG, with added content including a bonus dungeon, a new ending, and a wireless arena mode. The touch screen adds a useful map layer.

10. **Ace Attorney: Apollo Justice** (2007) — Adventure — Assessment: 8.5/10
    Fourth entry in the Ace Attorney series. New protagonist, juror system, and the series' first move toward HD assets. Best played after the Phoenix Wright trilogy.

## PSP

The PSP punched well above its handheld weight class. Its library skews toward action games and RPGs that suited the format. PPSSPP on Batocera is essentially perfect, with full upscaling.

1. **God of War: Ghost of Sparta** (2010) — Action — Assessment: 9.5/10
   Better than Chains of Olympus and on par with the PS2 entries. The PSP's strongest technical showcase and the deepest narrative of the portable God of War games.

9. **Valkyria Chronicles II** (2010) — Tactical RPG — Assessment: 8.5/10
   Smaller scale than the PS3 original but mechanically refined. Class advancement system and mission-based structure suit the portable format.

10. **Grand Theft Auto: Vice City Stories** (2006) — Action-adventure — Assessment: 8.5/10
    The better of the two PSP GTA titles. Larger than Liberty City Stories, the 1980s Vice City setting suits the format, and the empire-building mechanic adds a strategic layer.

## Wii

The Wii's motion controls age unevenly, but its library includes some of the best games of the generation and several that require no motion input at all. Dolphin handles it all, and motion can be emulated via controller if you don't have a sensor bar.

1. **Super Mario Galaxy** (2007) — 3D platformer — Assessment: 10/10
   Gravity-defying spherical worlds and the series' most creative level design since Mario 64. The co-star pointer mechanic is optional. One of the clearest cases for Nintendo's design philosophy.

2. **The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess** (2006) — Action-adventure — Assessment: 9/10
   The more conventional Zelda after Wind Waker's experiments. Darker tone, horseback combat, and the best dungeon design of any 3D Zelda. Motion controls are functional; the GameCube version is available via Dolphin if preferred.

3. **Metroid Prime 3: Corruption** (2007) — First-person adventure — Assessment: 9/10
   The motion control implementation that worked. Pointer aiming is genuinely superior to analog sticks for this type of game. The weakest Prime narrative but strong level design throughout.

4. **Super Smash Bros. Brawl** (2008) — Fighting — Assessment: 9/10
   Largest roster of the trilogy, slowest movement, Subspace Emissary campaign. The online mode is dead; local play is unaffected.

5. **Mario Kart Wii** (2008) — Racing — Assessment: 9/10
   The motion-controlled entry with the largest online player base in series history. Twelve-player battles, unlockable vehicles, and courses that remain in the series today.

6. **Xenoblade Chronicles** (2010) — RPG — Assessment: 9.5/10
   Large-scale open-world RPG with real-time combat. The Monado foresight mechanic and interconnected world make it one of the most ambitious RPGs of the generation. Long.

7. **No More Heroes** (2007) — Action — Assessment: 8.5/10
   Grasshopper Manufacture's stylized assassination tournament. The beam katana combat is better than it looks and the boss encounters are memorable set pieces. The open world is sparse and mostly not worth engaging.

8. **Donkey Kong Country Returns** (2010) — Platformer — Assessment: 9/10
   Retro Studios' revival of the DKC series. Precision platforming, silhouette levels, and a two-player co-op mode. The MINE CART levels will be remembered.

9. **Punch-Out!!** (2009) — Sports — Assessment: 9/10
   The Wii revival that nailed the pattern-recognition gameplay of the NES original while adding motion control uppercuts that actually work. Little Mac's opponents are as memorable as ever.

10. **The Last Story** (2011) — RPG — Assessment: 8.5/10
    Hironobu Sakaguchi's RPG outside Square Enix. Real-time combat with cover mechanics, a compact world, and a story that earns its ending. One of three notable JRPGs released in the Wii's final years alongside Xenoblade and Pandora's Tower.

## How much to trust this

Assessment throughout. Scores reflect critical consensus at the time of the game's peak reputation — a combination of review aggregates, longterm community regard, and competitive scene durability where relevant. No score here reflects play-testing on this specific hardware configuration.

For PS3 and Xbox 360 specifically: emulator compatibility changes with every release. Before committing to a download, check the official compatibility databases:
- RPCS3: [rpcs3.net/compatibility](https://rpcs3.net/compatibility)
- Xenia: [xenia.jp](https://xenia.jp) (or the GitHub issues tracker for specific titles)

For everything else: mGBA, Flycast, PCSX2, Dolphin, and Cemu are mature emulators with broad compatibility. The games on this list run without significant issue on the UM790Pro hardware class.
