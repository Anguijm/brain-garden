# The Games Batocera Ships With

Six titles on this machine were not sourced by us. They came with Batocera, which bundles a small
set of freely-redistributable homebrew games so that a freshly installed system has something to
run before anyone has added anything.

They are grouped here rather than given individual guides, because each is a short arcade-style
game rather than something needing a walkthrough — but they are games on the box, so they are
accounted for.

| Game | System | What it is |
|------|--------|------------|
| **2048** | NES | The sliding-tile number puzzle, ported to the NES |
| **Old Towers** | Mega Drive | A tile-based puzzle game about reaching an exit |
| **Reflectron** | PC Engine | A homebrew action game |
| **Santatlantean** | PC Engine | A homebrew shooter |
| **SpaceTwins** | Game Boy Advance | A homebrew arcade game |
| **Donkey Kong Classic** | SNES | A homebrew take on the arcade original |

---

## Why they are there

Batocera is a Linux distribution for retro gaming that ships as a complete, bootable system. It
cannot legally include commercial ROMs, so it includes homebrew — games written by hobbyists for
these consoles long after the consoles were discontinued, and released freely by their authors.

**Homebrew development for old hardware is an active scene.** People are still writing new NES,
Mega Drive and PC Engine games, and some are sold as real cartridges. The ones bundled here are
the freely-licensed end of that.

---

## 2048 (NES)

The 2018-era browser puzzle where you slide numbered tiles together, doubling as they merge, aiming
for a 2048 tile. Slide in any direction and every tile moves; a new tile spawns each turn; you lose
when the board fills.

**It works well on a D-pad** — arguably better than on a phone, since the four directions are
discrete rather than swipes.

---

## Old Towers (Mega Drive)

A puzzle game about moving through a tower to reach an exit, where your movement continues until
it hits something. The genre is sometimes called "ice sliding": you commit to a direction and stop
only at an obstacle, so each room is about choosing the order of moves.

---

## Donkey Kong Classic (SNES)

A homebrew rendition of the 1981 arcade game on SNES hardware. Worth noting that this box also has
the **real** arcade Donkey Kong lineage in two other places: the [Game Boy Donkey Kong](topics/games/gb/donkey-kong)
from 1994, which opens with the four arcade stages and then continues for 97 more, and MAME.

---

## The rest

**Reflectron** and **Santatlantean** are PC Engine homebrew, and **SpaceTwins** is a Game Boy
Advance homebrew arcade game. All three are short, score-driven, and best treated as curiosities
demonstrating what the hardware can still be made to do.

---

## Should you delete them?

No reason to. They occupy a trivial amount of space and they do no harm sitting in their systems'
lists. If they clutter the view, the gamelist `<hidden>` flag suppresses a title without deleting
it — the same mechanism used on this box to collapse multi-disc games like Shenmue and Panzer
Dragoon Saga into single entries.
