# Castlevania: Bloodlines (Mega Drive / Genesis)

**Developer:** Konami | **Publisher:** Konami | **Year:** 1994

---

## Introduction

The only Castlevania on Sega hardware, and the odd one out in the series in several ways. It is set in **1917**, across Europe rather than in a single castle, and it connects the series to *Dracula* the novel — Quincy Morris of Bram Stoker's book is established as a Belmont descendant, and his son John is the protagonist.

Released in Europe as *Castlevania: The New Generation*, censored slightly.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move; down to crouch.
- **A:** Sub-weapon. **B:** Attack. **C:** Jump.
- **Hold attack:** Depending on character, charge or hold the weapon out.

---

## Two characters, two games

| Character | Weapon | Ability |
|---|---|---|
| **John Morris** | Whip | Can **swing from ceilings** with the whip, reaching alternate routes |
| **Eric Lecarde** | Spear | **Higher jump**, longer reach, and can pole-vault upward |

**They take different paths through the same stages.** Certain platforms are reachable only by John's swing, others only by Eric's vault, so the levels have two layouts and a full playthrough of both is genuinely different.

**Eric is easier.** Longer reach and a better jump forgive more.

---

## Sub-weapons and hearts

Axe, holy water, boomerang cross — collected from candles, fuelled by hearts. As in every Castlevania, **hearts are ammunition, not health**.

Sub-weapons can be **powered up** by collecting a second of the same type, which changes them substantially rather than just adding damage.

---

## What the hardware does

Konami used the Mega Drive's capabilities for real effects rather than decoration: the rotating room, the sequence where the floor tilts, and a water level that distorts. It is one of the better technical showings on the console.

---

## Difficulty

**This is a hard game**, harder than [Super Castlevania IV](topics/games/snes/super-castlevania-iv) on the SNES by a wide margin. The whip only goes forward, knockback into pits is the main cause of death, and the later stages assume you have learned each enemy's exact position.

---

## Strategy

**Play Eric first** if you want to finish it; play John for the harder, more traditional Castlevania.

**Crouch-whip almost everything.** It avoids most projectiles and hits the majority of enemies.

**Power up one sub-weapon and keep it.** The upgraded versions are much stronger.

**Learn the knockback.** Getting hit near a pit is fatal, and the stages are built knowing that.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Mega Drive core with **rewind enabled**, which meaningfully changes how approachable this game is — the difficulty comes almost entirely from memorisation and knockback deaths.

Michiru Yamane, who later scored *Symphony of the Night*, wrote the music here.
