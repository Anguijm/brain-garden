# Phantasy Star IV: The End of the Millennium (Mega Drive / Genesis)

**Developer:** Sega | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1995

---

## Introduction

The conclusion of a story Sega had been telling since 1987, and the best role-playing game on the Mega Drive by a comfortable margin. It released in 1995, very late in the console's life, which is why comparatively few people played it and why original cartridges are expensive.

The Algo system is dying. Chaz Ashley, a hunter, takes a job that turns out to be the end of a thousand-year arc.

**It is science fiction, not fantasy.** Spaceships, androids and a computer-run solar system, with swords in it — a combination the series had been doing since before Final Fantasy existed.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move. **A:** Confirm/talk. **B:** Cancel. **C:** Menu.
- **Start:** Pause.

---

## Macros, which are the reason combat works

Standard turn-based fighting, with one addition almost nothing else had: you can **record a sequence of commands as a macro** and replay it with a single button.

Set up "everyone attacks, healer heals" as a macro and routine fights collapse to one keypress. **This makes grinding painless in a way most games of the era never managed**, and it is worth setting up macros within the first hour.

---

## Combination attacks

Certain skills and spells, used by different characters **on the same turn**, combine into something much stronger — a fire spell plus a wind spell becomes a firestorm, and so on.

**The game never tells you these exist.** There are dozens. They are discovered by experiment or by looking them up, and several are strong enough to change how boss fights go.

---

## The manga panels

Story scenes are presented as **comic-book panels** rather than sprite conversations. In 1995 on a cartridge this was ambitious, and it still reads well — it lets the game show scale it could not animate.

---

## Structure

Linear story across the three planets of the Algo system, with a party that changes as characters join and leave permanently. Hunter's guild jobs provide optional side content and are where a lot of the world-building sits.

---

## Strategy

**Set up macros immediately.** They are the difference between the combat being pleasant and tedious.

**Experiment with combination attacks** whenever the party composition changes.

**Take the guild jobs.** They are short, they pay, and they carry the setting.

**Do not sell old equipment blindly.** A few pieces matter later.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Mega Drive core with **rewind enabled**.

The cartridge used a battery-backed save; emulated saves have no battery to fail, which matters because surviving original cartridges frequently cannot save at all any more.

The three earlier games are Master System and Mega Drive titles; this one stands alone well enough that the missing context is a bonus rather than a requirement.
