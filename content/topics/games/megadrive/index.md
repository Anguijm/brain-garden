# Mega Drive / Genesis Game Guides

Sega's 16-bit console, sold as the Mega Drive everywhere except North America, where it was the
Genesis. It got there two years before the Super Nintendo and spent the whole generation being the
faster, harder-edged machine with the worse colour palette and the better bass.

## The three-button problem, and how it is fixed here

The Mega Drive shipped with a **three-button controller**. *Mortal Kombat II* needs five attack
buttons plus block; *Street Fighter* needs six. Sega released a six-button pad in 1993 for exactly
this reason, and games detect which one is plugged in.

**This box is configured for the six-button pad** on the Mega Drive system, which is what gives
Mortal Kombat II a separate low punch and high punch. If the punches ever collapse into one button,
the emulator has fallen back to three-button mode and the Mega Drive controller device type is the
setting to check.

## Two games that are structurally strange

**ToeJam & Earl** is a roguelike released on a console in 1991 — randomly generated levels,
unidentified items, permadeath. Nothing else on the system works like it, and it was directly
inspired by *Rogue*.

**Comix Zone** charges you health for breaking scenery, because the scenery is the comic page the
hero is standing on. Destruction is simultaneously how you find items and how you die, and it is
why the game has a reputation for brutality.

## The soundtracks

Yuzo Koshiro wrote the music for **Streets of Rage 2** and part of **Shinobi III** using a custom
sound driver he built for the hardware. The Mega Drive's FM synthesis chip was harsher than the
SNES's samples and generally considered the weaker of the two; in Koshiro's hands it produced
club music nothing on Nintendo's machine sounds like. Worth decent speakers.
