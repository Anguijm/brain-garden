# ToeJam & Earl (Mega Drive / Genesis)

**Developer:** Johnson Voorsanger Productions | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1991

---

## Introduction

Two aliens crash on Earth and have to find the ten pieces of their ship, scattered across randomly generated floating islands full of hostile humans.

**It is a roguelike, in 1991, on a console.** Randomly generated levels, permanent item identification puzzles, permadeath in the original mode. Nothing else on the Mega Drive is structurally like it, and it was inspired directly by *Rogue*.

The tone is the other reason it endures: funk soundtrack, deadpan writing, and enemies that are ordinary suburban Americans, which is the joke.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move.
- **A:** Use selected present. **B:** Open present menu / select.
- **C:** Run (drains energy) — **hold to sprint**.
- **Start:** Status.

---

## Presents, which are the mechanic

Everything you find is a **wrapped present with an unknown effect**. Opening one identifies that type permanently for the rest of the run.

Effects range from very good to very bad:

| Good | Bad |
|---|---|
| Icarus Wings (fly) | Randomiser (re-shuffles all identifications) |
| Rocket Skates (fast, uncontrollable) | Total Bummer (instant death) |
| Tomatoes (throwable weapon) | Rain Cloud (follows and shocks you) |
| Root Beer (speed) | Earthling (spawns an enemy) |
| Super Hi-Tops (permanent speed) | Schoolbook (puts you to sleep) |

**Identification is the game.** An unidentified present near an elevator is a gamble; an identified Icarus Wings is a free level.

---

## The islands

Each level is a floating island with holes. **Falling through a hole drops you to the previous level**, which is a setback but not a death — and it is sometimes the fastest way to get somewhere.

**Elevators go up** once found. Ship pieces are scattered, one or two per level, and you need all ten.

---

## Two modes

**Fixed** mode uses hand-designed levels in a set order — the easier, more predictable game.

**Random** mode generates everything, including item identities, every run. This is the real version and the one that makes it a roguelike.

---

## Co-op

Two-player split-screen, and it is the intended way to play. The screen splits when players separate and rejoins when they meet. Players can **give each other presents** — including bad ones, deliberately, which is most of the entertainment.

---

## Strategy

**Open unknown presents somewhere safe**, never next to an enemy or a hole.

**Sneak past humans rather than fighting.** There is no real combat; you have tomatoes and that is it.

**Hold C to run only when escaping.** It drains energy, which is health.

**Ask the Wiseman** — the carrot-eating hermit — to identify presents for a fee.

**Falling is not always bad.** Dropping down deliberately to reach an unexplored part of a lower island is a legitimate tactic.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Mega Drive core with **rewind enabled**, which somewhat undermines a roguelike but is available if a Total Bummer ends a good run.

The sequel, *Panic on Funkotron*, is a completely different game — a side-scrolling platformer — and disappointed people who wanted more of this.
