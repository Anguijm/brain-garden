# Shinobi III: Return of the Ninja Master (Mega Drive / Genesis)

**Developer:** Sega | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1993

---

## Introduction

The best Shinobi game, and one of the best-controlling action platformers of the 16-bit era. Joe Musashi moves faster and more fluidly than almost any contemporary, and the game is built around that movement rather than around obstacles.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move; **hold forward and tap forward to dash**; down to crouch.
- **A:** Ninjutsu (magic).
- **B:** Attack — shuriken at range, sword up close.
- **C:** Jump. **Double-tap C in air:** double jump.
- **Jump toward a wall:** cling, then climb or jump off.
- **Hold B while dashing:** running sword slash.

**The dash-slash is the signature move.** Dashing into an enemy with attack held cuts straight through them without stopping, and chaining it across a room is both the fastest and the most satisfying way to play.

---

## Ninjutsu

Four spells, one use each per life, chosen from the pause menu:

| Spell | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Ikazuchi** | A shield that absorbs one hit |
| **Kariu** | Screen-clearing fire damage |
| **Fushin** | Higher jumps |
| **Mijin** | Kills you and does massive damage — a last resort |

**Ikazuchi is the practical pick** for most stages: one free hit is worth more than a screen clear in a game where you die in a few hits.

---

## Shuriken

Ranged attacks are limited by a shuriken count, refilled by pickups. **When you run out you can still attack with the sword**, which does more damage but requires being close. Running dry is not fatal, and hoarding shuriken often means fighting badly at range when the sword would have been faster.

---

## Structure

Seven stages, each with a distinct setting and a boss, plus surfing and horseback set pieces that the Mega Drive handles better than it should. The difficulty is fair throughout, which is unusual for the genre and era.

---

## Strategy

**Dash constantly.** Walking is for reading the screen; dashing is for playing.

**Wall-cling to reset your position.** Clinging halts you and lets you re-time a jump.

**Use the double jump for horizontal distance**, not just height — it adds forward momentum.

**Save Ikazuchi for boss approaches**, not for corridors.

**Sword over shuriken when you can close.** It kills in fewer hits.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Mega Drive core with **rewind enabled**.

The soundtrack was partly composed by Yuzo Koshiro, who also did [Streets of Rage 2](topics/games/megadrive/streets-of-rage-2) — the two games share a sound driver and a certain hardness in the bass that is characteristic of his Mega Drive work.
