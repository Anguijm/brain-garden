# Metroid Fusion (Game Boy Advance)

**Developer:** Nintendo R&D1 | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 2002

---

## Introduction

Metroid Fusion is chronologically the **last game in the Metroid timeline**, and for over twenty years it was the end of the story — nothing followed it until *Metroid Dread* in 2021, which picks up directly from its final scene.

It released on the same day in Japan as *Metroid Prime*, ending an eight-year gap in which no Metroid game existed at all.

Samus is infected by the X parasite, saved by a vaccine made from Metroid DNA, and sent to a research station where the X have got loose. She is weaker than she has ever been, and something wearing her old suit is hunting her.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move; down to morph ball, up to aim.
- **A:** Jump. **B:** Shoot. **L:** Diagonal aim lock. **R:** Missiles.
- **Start:** Map. **Select:** Item toggle.

---

## What makes it different from other Metroid games

**It is directed.** A computer called Adam issues objectives, doors lock behind you, and the game tells you where to go. Traditional Metroid drops you into a map and says nothing.

This split opinion permanently. It is the tightest, most tense game in the series and also the least free. If open-ended exploration is the part of Metroid you value, [Super Metroid](topics/games/snes/super-metroid) is the one to play; if atmosphere and pacing are, this is the better game.

**The freedom returns late.** The final stretch opens the station up considerably, and the sequence-breaking community has found a great deal the designers did not intend.

---

## The SA-X

Something is walking the station in Samus's old Power Suit, with all the weapons she lost. It cannot be fought. When it appears, the music stops and you hide.

**These sequences are scripted, but the fear is not.** The SA-X patrols on fixed routes and will kill you in seconds if it sees you. It is the best thing in the game.

---

## Abilities

The usual Metroid progression, reframed: rather than finding equipment, Samus **absorbs X parasites** and regains abilities she already had. Morph Ball, Bombs, Hi-Jump, Speed Booster, Space Jump, Screw Attack, Gravity Suit, plus Charge Beam and the missile tiers.

**Every ability is both a weapon and a key.** When a route is blocked, name the ability you most recently acquired and try it there.

---

## Strategy

**Shoot everything, including scenery.** Blocks that break are visually identical to blocks that do not. Bomb floors, shoot ceilings.

**The map marks what you could not open.** Use it; the map screen is the game's actual notebook.

**When the music stops, stop.** That is the SA-X cue.

**Do not skip the navigation rooms.** They are saves as well as objectives.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy Advance core with **rewind enabled**.

**Set the brightness correctly.** GBA games were drawn for an unlit screen and are mastered dark; on a bright modern panel they look washed out and flat. Batocera's GBA colour-correction shader compensates for this, and Fusion in particular is a game about darkness. It is worth turning on.
