# Advance Wars (Game Boy Advance)

**Developer:** Intelligent Systems | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 2001

---

## Introduction

Nintendo had been making the *Wars* series in Japan since 1988 and had never released one in the West. *Advance Wars* was the first, and it landed in North America in September 2001 — days before the attacks of the eleventh, which delayed its European release by well over a year.

It is turn-based military strategy with cartoon commanding officers, and it is close to perfectly balanced.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move cursor. **A:** Select/confirm. **B:** Cancel; hold to speed the cursor.
- **L:** Cycle to next unit. **R:** Toggle terrain and damage information.
- **Start:** Menu. **Select:** Options.

---

## How it works

Two armies on a grid map. Each turn you move every unit, attack, capture and build.

**Money comes from property.** Cities, bases, airports and ports are captured by moving infantry onto them and holding for one or two turns. Each captured property pays income every turn, and bases are where new units are built.

**This is the whole economy, and it is why infantry are the most important unit in the game** despite being the weakest. New players build tanks and lose to players who built infantry and took the map.

---

## Combat rules

- **Terrain matters.** Mountains, woods and cities give defence; a tank in a city is much harder to kill than one in the open.
- **Damage is deterministic** apart from a small random band, and the game shows you the forecast before you commit. Read it every time.
- **Units do not heal by resting.** They heal on friendly property, two HP per turn, for a cost.
- **Fuel and ammunition are finite.** Air units in particular fall out of the sky when fuel runs out, which catches everyone once.

---

## Commanding Officers

Each CO has a passive bonus and a **CO Power** that charges as you fight. Andy repairs his whole army; Max has stronger direct units and weaker indirect ones; Grit is the reverse; Sami captures faster with stronger infantry.

**The CO changes the correct strategy more than the map does.** Playing Grit like Max loses.

---

## Structure

A campaign that doubles as a long tutorial, then War Room maps scored on how fast and how cheaply you win, plus a map editor and versus play.

---

## Strategy

**Capture before you fight.** Income compounds; a kill does not.

**Never attack without reading the damage forecast.**

**Keep infantry alive.** They are the only unit that captures.

**Indirect units — artillery, rockets — cannot move and fire the same turn.** Position them a turn ahead.

**Watch the enemy CO's power meter.** Attacking into a full meter hands them the counter-punch.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy Advance core.

**Rewind is deliberately disabled for this game** on this box, along with the other turn-based strategy titles. Rewinding a bad attack removes the only thing the genre is about, and per your instruction the toggle is off rather than left as a temptation.

Versus play used a single cartridge passed between players, which emulation does not reproduce; the campaign and War Room are the single-player game and they are substantial.
