# Golden Sun (Game Boy Advance)

**Developer:** Camelot Software Planning | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 2001

---

## Introduction

A launch-window Game Boy Advance RPG that looked, at the time, impossible — full-screen summon animations, rotating effects, a world map with weather, all on a handheld released months earlier.

Camelot had made the *Shining Force* games at Sega; when they moved to Nintendo they built this instead. It is a traditional turn-based RPG with one strong idea layered on top.

**It is also half a game.** The story ends on a cliffhanger and concludes in *Golden Sun: The Lost Age*, which continues from a save file transferred over.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move. **A:** Confirm/talk. **B:** Cancel/run.
- **L / R:** Cycle Psynergy shortcuts.
- **Start:** Menu. **Select:** Djinn overview.

---

## Psynergy, in and out of battle

Psynergy is magic, and unlike most RPGs it is used **constantly in the overworld**. Move pillars with Move, grow vines with Growth, reveal hidden things with Reveal, freeze puddles with Frost.

**Every dungeon is a Psynergy puzzle**, and much of the optional content is hidden behind field abilities you may not have equipped. The game rarely signals which one a puzzle wants.

---

## Djinn, which is the system worth understanding

You collect **Djinn**, elemental creatures. Each Djinn is attached to a character, and this is where the depth is:

- A Djinn **Set** to a character changes that character's **class**, stats and available Psynergy.
- Djinn can be **Unleashed** in battle for an effect, which puts them on Standby.
- Djinn on Standby can be spent to **Summon**, which is the game's heavy artillery.
- Standby Djinn then take turns to recover.

**Class changes are dramatic.** Moving two Mercury Djinn onto a Venus character rebuilds them into a different job entirely. Most players never touch this and beat the game anyway; the ones who do find it becomes a genuine strategy layer.

**The trap:** Unleashing Djinn for damage puts them on Standby, which silently downgrades your class mid-fight and can strip the Psynergy you were relying on.

---

## Structure

Linear-ish story with a large explorable world, a substantial number of hidden Djinn, and optional summons that are easy to miss permanently.

---

## Strategy

**Cast Psynergy at everything.** Reveal and Mind Read in particular find content that is otherwise invisible.

**Collect every Djinn you pass.** Several are missable and they are the power curve.

**Understand Standby before a boss.** Summoning is strong but it leaves your party miscast for several turns.

**Keep the save.** *The Lost Age* imports it, and carries over your Djinn and levels.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy Advance core with **rewind enabled**.

The save-transfer to *The Lost Age* worked through either a password or a link cable on hardware. On emulation the password route works normally — a very long password, but it works, and it carries more data than the cable-free option suggests.

**Use the GBA colour-correction shader**; Camelot's palette was chosen for the unlit screen.
