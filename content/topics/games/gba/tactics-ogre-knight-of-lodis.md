# Tactics Ogre: The Knight of Lodis (Game Boy Advance)

**Developer:** Quest | **Publisher:** Atlus | **Year:** 2001 (US)

---

## Introduction

A prequel to *Tactics Ogre: Let Us Cling Together* — which is also on this box, in its [PSP remake](topics/games/psp/tactics-ogre-let-us-cling-together) — and the last game Quest made before Square absorbed the studio.

Yasumi Matsuno, who created the Ogre Battle series and went on to *Final Fantasy Tactics*, was gone by this point; Hiroshi Minagawa directed. It is a smaller, tighter game than its predecessors, and it is one of the best strategy RPGs on the hardware.

You play Alphonse Loeher, a knight of the Holy Lodis Empire, sent to the island of Ovis.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move cursor. **A:** Select. **B:** Cancel.
- **L / R:** Rotate the isometric camera — **essential**, because units hide behind terrain.
- **Start:** Menu.

---

## Height, which is the mechanic

The battlefield is **isometric and genuinely three-dimensional**. Elevation is not decoration:

- Attacking **downhill** adds damage; attacking uphill loses it.
- **Archers gain enormous range from height**, and an archer on a roof is the strongest unit on most maps.
- Units cannot climb more than a set height difference, so terrain is a wall.

**The first move of every battle should be toward high ground.** Battles lost in this game are usually battles fought in a valley.

---

## The emblem system

Unique to this entry. Units earn **emblems** for doing specific things — killing an enemy of a certain type, surviving at low health, never retreating. Emblems change stats, unlock classes, and gate certain promotions.

**Some are permanent and some are negative.** A unit that flees repeatedly earns an emblem marking it a coward, with consequences.

This is a quiet, poorly explained system that shapes your army considerably.

---

## Classes and alignment

Units have a class, and classes require both stats and emblems to change. Each unit also has an **alignment** — Law, Neutral, Chaos — that governs which classes are available and how they perform.

---

## Structure

Chapter-based with branching paths and multiple endings determined by choices in dialogue and by your army's composition. A New Game Plus carries progress forward, and seeing the whole story genuinely requires more than one run.

---

## Strategy

**Take the high ground first, always.**

**Rotate the camera constantly.** Units and objectives hide behind terrain, and the isometric view lies about what is reachable.

**Do not let units flee.** The emblem penalty is real.

**Watch alignment when recruiting.** A Chaos unit in a Law army is limited in ways the game does not spell out.
