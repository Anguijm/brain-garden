# Fire Emblem: The Sacred Stones (Game Boy Advance)

**Developer:** Intelligent Systems | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 2005

---

## Introduction

The second Fire Emblem released in the West and the most approachable of the Game Boy Advance three. Twin royals Eirika and Ephraim flee the fall of Renais, and the campaign splits so you follow one or the other for a stretch.

**It is the friendliest entry** because it brings back the world map and free battles from the Japan-only *Gaiden*, meaning you can grind. The other GBA Fire Emblems give you a fixed number of experience points across the whole game and no way to make more.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move cursor. **A:** Select/confirm. **B:** Cancel; hold to speed movement.
- **R:** Unit information and combat forecast. **L:** Cycle units.
- **Start:** Menu, end turn.

---

## Permadeath, which is the point

A unit that falls in battle is **gone permanently**. No revival, no returning next chapter. Their story is over and their supports with everyone else stop.

There are three ways people play:

1. **Accept losses** and finish with whoever survives. The game is beatable this way and was designed for it.
2. **Reset on any death**, the near-universal approach, which turns each map into a puzzle to solve without casualties.
3. **Let them go** but grind replacements, which Sacred Stones uniquely permits.

**Decide which you are doing before chapter five**, because it changes how you play every map.

---

## The weapon triangle

Swords beat axes, axes beat lances, lances beat swords. Advantage grants a hit and damage bonus; disadvantage costs the same. Magic has its own triangle — anima beats light, light beats dark, dark beats anima.

**Read the forecast every attack.** Press R over a target and the game shows exact damage, hit chance and whether they double-attack. Losing units almost always traces back to committing without reading.

---

## Weapons break

Every weapon has a limited number of uses and then it is destroyed. Iron weapons are cheap and plentiful; steel and silver are strong and finite. **Legendary weapons should be saved**, and most players save them so hard they finish the game unused.

---

## Promotion and branching

At level 10 a unit can promote with a class item, and **Sacred Stones lets most units choose between two advanced classes**. A Cavalier can become a Paladin or a Great Knight; a Mercenary becomes a Hero or a Ranger. The choice is permanent.

There is also a **Trainee** tier unique to this game — three weak recruits who promote twice and end up stronger than anyone else if you invest the experience.

---

## Strategy

**Read the forecast. Every time.**

**Use the world map.** Free skirmishes let you train units the plot would otherwise leave behind, and this game is the only GBA entry that allows it.

**Build supports.** Units who fight beside each other repeatedly gain conversations and combat bonuses, and they are also the story.

**Do not over-grind.** The game scales nothing; grinding past a point removes the tension the design depends on.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy Advance core.

**Rewind is deliberately off for this game** on this box, as with the other turn-based strategy titles, per your instruction. It is worth saying why it matters here more than anywhere else: rewind removes permadeath entirely, and permadeath is the mechanic the whole design rests on. The intended equivalent is the reset, which costs you the chapter rather than the turn.
