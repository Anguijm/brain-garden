# Pokémon Emerald (Game Boy Advance)

**Developer:** Game Freak | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 2005 (US)

---

## Introduction

The definitive third-generation game, standing to Ruby and Sapphire as [Crystal](topics/games/gbc/pokemon-crystal) stood to Gold and Silver. Set in Hoenn, with the largest post-game facility the series had built to that point.

Generation 3 was a hard reset: it could not trade with the Game Boy Color games at all, so the entire Pokédex had to be rebuilt from scratch. That break is why Hoenn feels so unfamiliar compared to Johto and Kanto.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move. **A:** Confirm. **B:** Cancel / run (with Running Shoes).
- **L / R:** Assigned in options (L=A is worth setting). **Select:** Registered item.
- **Start:** Menu.

---

## What Emerald adds over Ruby and Sapphire

- **Both legendary teams.** Ruby had Magma, Sapphire had Aqua; Emerald has both, plus Rayquaza resolving the conflict.
- **The Battle Frontier.** Seven facilities, each with its own rule set, and the strongest post-game content in any Pokémon game. This is the reason to pick Emerald.
- **Animated battle sprites** for every Pokémon.
- **Gym leader rematches** with much stronger teams.
- **Rayquaza is catchable** as part of the main story rather than a bonus.

---

## Generation 3 systems

**Abilities.** Every Pokémon now has a passive ability — Intimidate, Levitate, Torrent. This is the single biggest mechanical addition in the series' history and it still governs competitive play.

**Natures.** Each Pokémon has a personality that raises one stat by 10% and lowers another. Combined with the hidden effort-value system, this is where serious team building begins.

**Double battles** arrive here.

**Contests**, a beauty-pageant minigame with its own stat system, which you can ignore entirely.

---

## Route

Eight gyms — Roxanne (Rock), Brawly (Fighting), Wattson (Electric), Flannery (Fire), Norman (Normal, and your father), Winona (Flying), Tate & Liza (Psychic, a double battle), Juan (Water) — then Victory Road and the Elite Four.

**Hoenn is water-heavy.** The stretch around Route 124–126 is a long surf and it is where most people lose patience. Repels are the answer to the encounter rate.

---

## Strategy

**Pick a starter knowing the gym order.** Mudkip has the smoothest run by a wide margin; Torchic is the hardest early and strong late.

**Get the Running Shoes and set L=A** in options. Both transform the pace.

**Do not neglect the Battle Frontier.** It is roughly half the game's content and it starts after the credits.

**Watch out for Wally.** The rival fights are irregular and the final Wally battle catches people unprepared.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy Advance core with **rewind enabled**.

**The real-time clock works.** Generation 3 cartridges used a battery for berry growth and tides, and on real hardware that battery is dead in almost every surviving copy — the notorious "dry battery" message. Emulated, the clock runs off host time and berries grow normally, which is something you cannot easily get on original hardware any more.

**Trade evolutions remain unavailable** on a single copy, as does the Kanto-region content that required linking to FireRed and LeafGreen.
