# The Legend of Zelda: The Minish Cap (Game Boy Advance)

**Developer:** Flagship (Capcom) | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 2005 (US)

---

## Introduction

The last Zelda that Capcom made, after the two [Oracle](topics/games/gbc/oracle-of-seasons) games, and the best-looking 2D Zelda ever produced. It is also the origin story for **Vaati**, the villain of the *Four Swords* games, and for the Picori Blade that becomes the Four Sword.

The hook: a talking cap named Ezlo lets Link **shrink to the size of an insect**.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move.
- **A:** Sword / context action. **B:** Assigned item.
- **L / R:** Second and third item slots — a real improvement on the Oracle games' two-slot squeeze.
- **Start:** Map. **Select:** Menu.

---

## Shrinking

Step on a **Minish portal** — a stump, a jar, a specific pot — and Link becomes tiny.

At that scale the world is completely different. A puddle is a lake, a book is a building, a floorboard gap is a corridor. The Minish, a race of tiny people, live in it and are invisible at normal size.

**Every area has both scales.** Being stuck at normal size nearly always means there is a portal nearby you have not stood on. Being stuck while small usually means you need to grow to reach a switch a tiny Link cannot press.

---

## Kinstones

Coloured half-medallions, found everywhere. Find someone holding the matching half, **fuse** with them, and something changes somewhere in the world — a chest appears, a path opens, a new character shows up.

**This is the game's main optional system and it is enormous.** Over a hundred fusions exist. Several heart pieces and one significant item are locked behind them, and the game gives almost no guidance about who holds what.

**Fuse with everyone, always.** There is no cost and no wrong fusion.

---

## Items worth knowing

- **Gust Jar** — vacuums; pulls objects, enemies and the fabric of certain puzzles
- **Mole Mitts** — dig through soft walls and floors
- **Cane of Pacci** — flips objects, and launches Link out of holes
- **Roc's Cape** — a real jump, unusual for 2D Zelda
- **Four Sword** — creates copies of Link for the multi-Link switch puzzles

---

## Structure

Six dungeons, an overworld that keeps reopening as your abilities grow, and an unusually dense town. The dungeons are shorter than a console Zelda's and denser with mechanics.

---

## Strategy

**Talk to everyone, then fuse with everyone.** Both are the game's actual content.

**Look for portals before looking for solutions.**

**The Gust Jar solves more than it appears to.** It pulls enemies, drags objects, and reveals things under piles.

**Collect figurines.** The shell-trading side game unlocks a gallery and a few real rewards.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy Advance core with **rewind enabled**.

It is the visual high point of 2D Zelda — hand-drawn, densely animated, and mastered for the GBA's dim screen. **Turn the GBA colour-correction shader on**; without it the art looks bleached on a modern panel, which is the opposite of the problem it was drawn to solve.
