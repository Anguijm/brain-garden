# Final Fantasy VI Advance (Game Boy Advance)

**Developer:** Tose / Square Enix | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 2007 (US)

---

## Introduction

The handheld version of a game frequently named the best in the series. **On this box you also have the SNES original**, listed under its North American name [Final Fantasy III](topics/games/snes/final-fantasy-iii) — Square renumbered the Western releases, and the game called *Final Fantasy III* on the SNES is the game called *Final Fantasy VI* everywhere since.

This Advance version is the same story with a new translation, restored content, and four bonus dungeons.

---

## Which version to play

**Advance**, in most cases:

- **A far better translation.** Ted Woolsey's SNES script was written under severe cartridge space limits and censorship rules. The Advance script is complete and much closer to the Japanese.
- **Bug fixes.** The infamous Vanish/Doom exploit and the broken Evade stat are repaired, which makes the game work as designed.
- **Extra content:** the Dragons' Den and Soul Shrine, plus four additional espers.

**The SNES original wins on two counts:** the sound is better (the GBA's audio hardware is weaker and the remastered tracks are muddier), and some of the Woolsey lines are beloved regardless of accuracy.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move. **A:** Confirm. **B:** Cancel / dash.
- **L / R:** Cycle party in menus.
- **Start:** Menu. **Select:** Map (world).

---

## Structure

Fourteen playable characters, no single protagonist, and an opera scene people still talk about thirty years later. Roughly halfway through, **the villain wins**, the world is destroyed, and the second half is a scattered, open search for survivors across a ruined map.

**Several characters are permanently missable in the second half.** Umaro, Gogo, Mog and Shadow all require specific detours, and the game will let you finish without them.

---

## Espers and the customisation trap

Espers are summons that also **teach magic** and grant stat bonuses on level up.

Because any character can learn any spell from any esper, **everyone can become the same character**. Late-game parties often converge on identical mages with different sprites.

**Resist that if you want the game to stay interesting.** Assigning espers by role — keeping Sabin physical, keeping Edgar on tools — preserves the cast's identity. The game does not require it, and it does not stop you.

---

## Strategy

**Learn what each character's unique command does before deciding they are useless.** Sabin's Blitz inputs, Gau's Rage list and Setzer's Slot are all far stronger than they appear.

**Do not miss Shadow.** Waiting for him on the Floating Continent is the difference between having him and not, and the wait feels wrong.

**Stat-boosting espers matter.** Levelling with the right esper equipped compounds over the whole game.

**Save before the opera.** It is a timed sequence and failing it is easy.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy Advance core with **rewind enabled**.

**Turn on the GBA colour-correction shader.** This is the version where it matters most: the port's palette was brightened for the unlit GBA screen, and on a modern display without correction it looks bleached compared to the SNES original sitting next to it on this same box.
