# Guardian Heroes (Sega Saturn)

**Developer:** Treasure | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1996

---

## Introduction

Treasure's beat 'em up, from the same studio as [Gunstar Heroes](topics/games/megadrive/gunstar-heroes), and one of the two or three best games on the Saturn.

It is a brawler with a **fighting game's move list**, an **RPG's levelling system**, and a **branching story with multiple endings**. The mix should not work and does.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move; **up and down switch between three parallel planes** rather than moving diagonally.
- **A:** Weak attack. **B:** Strong attack. **C:** Jump.
- **X / Y / Z:** Magic, guard, and plane switching depending on setup.
- **Quarter-circle and charge motions + attack:** Special moves, as in a fighting game.

### The three-plane system

The battlefield has **three distinct depth lanes**, and you jump between them with up and down rather than walking freely in depth.

**This is the game's defining mechanic.** You retreat to an empty lane to heal, push enemies into a crowded one, and attack across lanes with ranged magic. Ordinary brawlers let you drift in depth; here it is a discrete tactical choice.

---

## The Undead Hero

You find a **skeleton warrior** early who fights alongside you and takes commands — attack, defend, follow, hold position. He is powerful, he can be levelled, and **how you use him affects the story branch**.

---

## Levelling

Defeating enemies grants experience, and on level-up you **distribute points** across strength, vitality, magic, intelligence and agility. Builds diverge substantially: a magic-heavy character plays nothing like a strength build.

**Points carry over to a new game**, so replaying — which the branching demands — starts you stronger.

---

## Branching

At the end of most stages you **choose where to go next**, and the choices lead to genuinely different routes, different bosses and **six endings**. Some branches are morally weighted and the game does not signal which.

Seeing the whole game takes several playthroughs, which the carried-over levels are designed to support.

---

## Versus mode

A **six-player** versus mode with almost every character and enemy in the game unlockable. This was extraordinary in 1996 and is still a lot.

---

## Strategy

**Use all three planes.** Standing in one lane and swinging is how the game gets hard.

**Command the Undead Hero deliberately.** Leaving him on default wastes the strongest ally you have.

**Pick a build and commit.** Spreading points evenly produces a character bad at everything.

**Replay for the branches.** One run sees maybe a third of the content.
