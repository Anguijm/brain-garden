# Panzer Dragoon Saga (Sega Saturn)

**Developer:** Team Andromeda | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1998

---

## Introduction

The most valuable mainstream Saturn game and one of the most sought-after RPGs ever made. Sega released it in North America in **1998, after it had already given up on the Saturn**, and printed roughly 20,000 English copies. A complete original sells for well over a thousand dollars.

It is also, separately from its rarity, very good — a role-playing game built out of a rail shooter series, with a combat system nothing else has copied.

Edge, a young excavator, is shot and left for dead, and wakes to find a dragon.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad / stick:** Move the dragon around the enemy.
- **A:** Confirm / fire. **B:** Cancel. **C:** Lock-on laser.
- **L / R:** **Rotate around the target** — front, left, right, rear.

---

## Combat, which is the reason to play it

Turn-based, but you are **flying around the enemy in real time while your gauge fills**.

The screen shows the enemy with a ring around it divided into zones — usually green, yellow and red — indicating how dangerous each position is and how effective your attacks will be from there. **A boss's rear may be safe and weak to lasers while its front is armoured and lethal.**

So each turn is two decisions: where to be, and what to do from there. Move to the rear, wait for the gauge, fire. The enemy is also moving, the zones change as it turns, and the whole fight is spatial in a way turn-based combat almost never is.

**The dragon's form is customisable** across four attributes, and changing it mid-fight changes your speed, attack and defence balance.

---

## Structure

Four discs, largely linear, roughly twenty to twenty-five hours. It is unusually **short and unpadded** for a 1990s RPG — very little grinding, no random encounter slog, and a plot that keeps moving.

The world is the same one as the two rail-shooter *Panzer Dragoon* games, and the setting is one of the best in the genre: a dead civilisation's biotechnology, monsters that are machines, and almost no exposition.

---

## Strategy

**Always be moving.** Sitting still in a red zone is how fights are lost.

**Read the ring every turn.** The safe position changes as the enemy repositions.

**Use the lock-on laser on swarms**, direct fire on single targets — the split is meaningful.

**Reconfigure the dragon between fights**, not just at the start. A boss that outruns you wants a different build.

## Emulation Notes

Runs on Batocera's Saturn setup using the **beetle-saturn** core, which is the accurate one. The Saturn is a famously difficult console to emulate — two CPUs and several coprocessors — so this needs the hardware to work for it, which the UM790Pro has.

**This is a four-disc game.** Disc swapping on this box is handled by an `.m3u` playlist so all four discs appear as a single entry; the game prompts for a disc change and the emulator's disc-control menu switches without leaving the game.

Never re-released, never remade, and not on any modern service — emulation is realistically the only way to play it.
