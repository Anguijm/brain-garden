# Shining Force III (Sega Saturn)

**Developer:** Camelot Software Planning | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1998

---

## Introduction

The last Shining Force made by Camelot, and the one with the most complicated release history in Sega's catalogue.

**In Japan it is three separate games**, called Scenarios 1, 2 and 3, telling the same war from three different armies' perspectives, with save data carrying between them so that events in one appear in the others.

**Only Scenario 1 was released in English.** Sega of America gave up on the Saturn before the other two arrived. A fan translation project eventually completed Scenarios 2 and 3, decades later.

The disc on this box is **Scenario 1**, the English release, which is a complete game on its own and ends at a natural point.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move cursor. **A:** Select. **B:** Cancel. **C:** Camera / information.
- **L / R:** **Rotate the battlefield** — necessary, because units hide behind terrain.

---

## How the battles work

Turn-based tactical combat on a 3D grid. Each unit moves and acts, and when two units fight, the view cuts to an **animated battle scene**.

Key rules:

- **Terrain gives defence and costs movement.** Forests and hills are worth standing in.
- **Facing does not matter** here, unlike some tactics games, but position does.
- **Ranged units and mages have limited range** and are fragile; keep them behind the line.
- **A fallen ally is not permanently dead** — unlike [Fire Emblem](topics/games/gba/fire-emblem-sacred-stones), Shining Force revives characters at a church for a fee. This is the friendlier of the two great strategy RPG traditions.

---

## Promotion

At level 10 or above, units **promote** to an advanced class, resetting their level to 1 with much better growth. Promoting late gives slightly better final stats; promoting early gets you through the next few battles.

**Promote around level 15 to 20** as a working compromise.

---

## Building the force

You recruit far more characters than you can field. The **force** is your selected roster, and everyone left behind stops levelling.

**Commit to a core team.** Spreading experience across twenty units produces twenty units too weak for the late battles.

---

## Strategy

**Rotate the camera every turn.** The 3D battlefield hides units and the flat view lies about reachability.

**Fight on terrain.** A knight in a forest survives what a knight in a field does not.

**Keep mages behind the front rank.** They die in one hit and they are the damage.

**Do not fear losses.** Revival is cheap, and the game is designed around it, which frees you to play aggressively in a way Fire Emblem never does.

## Emulation Notes

Runs on Batocera's Saturn setup using the **beetle-saturn** core.

**Rewind is not enabled for Saturn** on this box, in line with the other turn-based strategy games. Save states remain available.

Original English copies are expensive and Scenarios 2 and 3 were never officially localised, so this is the practical way to play it.
