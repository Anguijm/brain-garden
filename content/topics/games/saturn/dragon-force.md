# Dragon Force (Sega Saturn)

**Developer:** J-Force | **Publisher:** Sega / Working Designs | **Year:** 1996

---

## Introduction

A strategy role-playing game about conquering a continent, in which battles are fought by **two armies of up to a hundred soldiers each rendered simultaneously on screen**. On a 1996 console that was startling, and it is still the thing people describe first.

Eight rulers are fighting for the continent of Legendra. You pick one, and the campaign differs substantially depending on who.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Navigate the map and menus. **A:** Confirm. **B:** Cancel. **C:** Information.
- **In battle:** Issue orders to your general from a small command list; the soldiers fight on their own.

---

## Two layers

**The strategic layer** is a map of castles. You move generals between them, each leading a unit of troops, and time passes as they march. Castles produce income, generals must be recruited and kept loyal, and the enemy is moving at the same time.

**The battle layer** happens when two armies meet. You do not control individual soldiers — you choose a **formation and a tactic** for your general's troop, then watch it play out, intervening with commands as the fight develops.

If both generals survive their troops, it goes to a **one-on-one duel**.

---

## Troop types and the rock-paper-scissors

Each general commands one type: soldiers, archers, cavalry, mages, monks, dragons, beasts, harpies, samurai, ninja and zombies among others.

**The matchups are decisive.** Cavalry destroy archers, archers shred beasts, mages counter cavalry. Sending the wrong unit into a fight loses it before the tactics matter.

**Read the enemy general's type before committing.** The strategic map shows it, and this single habit is most of the skill.

---

## Recruiting

Defeated enemy generals can be **captured and persuaded to join you**. Their loyalty depends on how they were beaten and how you treat them, and some will defect back.

Building an army of a hundred generals across a campaign is the long game, and it is why people replay it.

---

## Eight campaigns

Each of the eight rulers has their own story, starting position and difficulty. **They are genuinely different games**, not palette swaps, and the plot only fully resolves across several.

---

## Strategy

**Match troop types before you attack.** Everything else is secondary.

**Do not overextend.** A castle taken and left undefended is a castle lost next turn.

**Capture rather than kill** where you can. Generals are the resource that compounds.

**Keep a reserve.** The AI counter-attacks into gaps.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs on Batocera's Saturn setup using the **beetle-saturn** core.

**Rewind is not enabled for Saturn** on this box, which suits this game: it is turn-based strategy, and rewinding a battle removes the decision the game is made of. Save states are available if you want an insurance policy.

The Working Designs localisation is well regarded, and the game never received a Western sequel — *Dragon Force II* stayed in Japan.
