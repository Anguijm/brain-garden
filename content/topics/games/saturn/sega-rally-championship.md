# Sega Rally Championship (Sega Saturn)

**Developer:** Sega AM3 | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1995

---

## Introduction

The arcade racer that taught console players what **surface grip** means. Sega Rally's whole contribution to racing games is that tarmac, gravel, dirt and mud each behave differently under the car, and you can feel the transition as you cross it.

The Saturn conversion was one of the console's genuine successes — close to the arcade, running at a solid frame rate when most Saturn 3D did not.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad / analogue:** Steer. **A / B:** Accelerate and brake.
- **C:** Handbrake. **X / Y / Z:** Gear changes on manual, camera.

**Analogue steering makes a large difference** and your controller has it. On a D-pad the car is either turning fully or not at all, which fights the entire surface-grip design.

---

## Driving it

The technique is not the technique for a circuit racer:

**Brake before the corner, then power through it.** Braking mid-corner on gravel loses the back end and there is no recovering at speed.

**Steer into the slide.** The car is meant to be sideways through loose corners. Fighting the slide straightens you into the scenery.

**Use the handbrake for hairpins only.** It is a tool for tight turns, not general cornering.

**Lift, do not brake, for small adjustments.** Easing the accelerator settles the car; braking unsettles it.

---

## The surfaces

| Surface | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Tarmac** (Desert) | High grip, brake late, minimal slide |
| **Gravel** (Forest) | Moderate slide, the classic rally feel |
| **Mud / snow** (Advanced) | Very low grip, everything happens slowly and then all at once |

**The Lancia Delta and Toyota Celica** handle differently enough to matter — the Celica is more forgiving, the Delta faster in expert hands.

---

## The co-driver

A voice calls the corners ahead — "easy right", "hard left", "caution". **Listening to it is how you drive a course you do not know**, and it is the same system real rally uses. Most players ignore it and then memorise the tracks instead, which works but takes far longer.

---

## Structure

Three championship stages plus a hidden fourth, a time attack, and a two-player split screen. It is short by design — an arcade game — and the depth is in shaving seconds.

---

## Strategy

**Listen to the co-driver.**

**Brake in a straight line, then turn.**

**Take the racing line wide-in-wide** on gravel; on tarmac, tighter.

**Learn one car properly** rather than switching.
