# Neutopia (TurboGrafx-16)

**Developer:** Hudson Soft | **Publisher:** Hudson Soft / NEC | **Year:** 1989

---

## Introduction

Neutopia is unashamedly *The Legend of Zelda* on a different console, and it is good enough that the resemblance stops being a criticism about an hour in. Top-down overworld, four spheres to recover, eight-way sword, bombs to open walls, a heart-based life bar, dungeons called Crypts. If you have played the 1986 original, you already know the grammar.

What it adds is a gentler on-ramp. The overworld is more clearly signposted than Zelda's, the dungeons are less maze-like, and there is far less "bomb every wall until something happens." It is the friendliest game of its type from that era.

Four spheres, four regions, one final confrontation with Dirth.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move in eight directions.
- **Button II:** Sword. At full health it fires a projectile, exactly as in Zelda.
- **Button I:** Use the equipped item.
- **Run:** Pause / inventory. **Select:** cycle item.

---

## Progression

Neutopia gates by item rather than by map, so when you are stuck the answer is almost always "you need a thing you have not found."

| Item | Opens |
|---|---|
| **Bombs** | Cracked walls, hidden caves |
| **Moonbeam Moss** | Lights dark rooms |
| **Wings** | Fast travel between visited points |
| **Fire Wand / Rod** | Ranged attack; some puzzles |
| **Shield upgrades** | Blocks stronger projectiles |
| **Armour upgrades** | Reduces damage taken |
| **Medallions** | Access to later regions |

**Health is hearts, and full health means ranged sword shots.** As in Zelda, dropping below full turns your sword into a melee-only weapon, which changes how you fight entirely. Keeping topped up is a strategy, not a luxury.

---

## The four regions

Each region is an overworld area containing several Crypts, ending with a boss holding a sphere.

**Region 1** — grassland and the gentlest Crypts. Learn the rhythm here: explore the overworld, find the Crypt entrance, clear it, take the sphere.

**Region 2** — desert and water. Introduces environmental damage and the first genuinely maze-like Crypt.

**Region 3** — mountains. Vertical layouts, more aggressive enemies, and the point at which armour upgrades stop being optional.

**Region 4** — the final region, leading to Dirth. Enemies here punish careless movement, and the Crypts assume you have every tool.

---

## Strategy

**Talk to everyone and read every sign.** Neutopia signposts more clearly than its inspiration, and most "where do I go" problems are answered by an NPC you walked past.

**Buy the armour and shield upgrades when you can afford them.** They are flat damage reduction and they compound; the game gets noticeably easier with each.

**Bomb the suspicious walls, but not every wall.** Cracks and off-colour tiles mark real secrets. Unlike Zelda, Neutopia does not hide much behind unmarked walls, so exhaustive bombing is wasted time.

**Use the Wings.** Backtracking on foot across four regions is the only genuinely tedious thing in the game, and the Wings exist to remove it.

**Fight at full health.** The ranged sword shot roughly doubles your effective safety. If you are below full and not in danger, walk back to a heart drop rather than pressing on.

## Emulation Notes

Light on the hardware; runs perfectly on Batocera's PC Engine core, and the bright overworld art benefits visibly from integer scaling at 4K.

Neutopia uses a **password system** rather than battery saves. Passwords are long and easy to mistype, so save states are the practical choice — and **rewind is enabled** on this system, which covers the other common loss, walking into a boss under-equipped.

A sequel, *Neutopia II*, followed in 1991 and is more of the same at a slightly larger scale.
