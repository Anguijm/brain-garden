# Bonk's Adventure (TurboGrafx-16)

**Developer:** Red Company / Atlus | **Publisher:** Hudson Soft / NEC | **Year:** 1990

---

## Introduction

Bonk was NEC's answer to Mario and Sonic: a bald caveman with an enormous head who attacks by headbutting things. He was the TurboGrafx-16's mascot in North America, and the game is genuinely good rather than merely a mascot vehicle — bright, inventive, and built around one idea executed thoroughly.

That idea is the head. Bonk has no weapon and no projectile. He headbutts forward on the ground, spins into a dive attack in the air, and can headbutt upward to stick into ceilings and climb. Everything the game asks of you is a variation on where to point your skull.

Five worlds, dinosaur theme throughout, and a length that suits a platformer of its era — a couple of hours, or considerably more if you want everything.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move. Down to crouch.
- **Button II:** Jump.
- **Button I:** Headbutt (on the ground).
- **Button I in the air:** Spin dive — Bonk flips head-down and drills straight down. This is your main attack and your main mobility tool.
- **Button I at the peak of a jump:** A short hover-ish flip, useful for adjusting.
- **Button II while against a ceiling:** Bite into it, then climb by repeating.

### The move that unlocks the game

**Jump, then press I at the top.** Bonk flips and descends head-first, killing anything he lands on and gaining more height on the bounce. Chaining dive attacks off enemies is how you cross large gaps and reach the higher routes. Players who only walk and jump find the game cramped; players who dive find it opens up.

---

## Meat and the rage mechanic

Bonk's power-ups are pieces of meat, and they change his state rather than giving him a weapon.

- **Small meat** — Bonk gets angry. Attacks do more damage, and his headbutt has more reach.
- **Large meat** — Bonk goes into a full rage. He becomes briefly invincible, and stomping the ground stuns every enemy on screen.
- **Losing health** drops you back down the ladder.

**Smileys** are the extra-life currency, and **hearts** restore health. Bonk's health is shown as a row of small faces; he can take several hits before dying, which makes the game noticeably friendlier than its contemporaries.

---

## World notes

**World 1 — Uluru-ru** teaches the dive attack and introduces the ceiling-climb. The boss, Huey, is a small dinosaur beaten by simple dive attacks.

**World 2 — Mahaloha** brings water sections. Bonk swims slowly; the dive attack still works and is still the answer to most things.

**World 3 — Bonk's Mother** is the vertical world, built around climbing. Ceiling-biting is mandatory here rather than optional.

**World 4 — Toots' Dream** is the strange one: shifting backgrounds and enemies that behave unexpectedly, appropriate to a dream.

**World 5 — King Drool's Castle** is the finale, ending with King Drool himself. He is large, telegraphs heavily, and goes down to repeated dive attacks on his head — the same skill the whole game has been teaching.

---

## Strategy

**Dive on everything.** The spin dive is stronger than the ground headbutt, kills most enemies outright, and bounces you higher. When in doubt, get airborne.

**Eat meat before a boss**, not during. Arriving already enraged shortens every fight.

**Explore upward.** Bonk's Adventure hides bonus rooms and smiley caches above the obvious path, reachable by ceiling-climbing. The main route is easy; the collectibles are where the game is.

## Emulation Notes

Undemanding and runs perfectly on Batocera's PC Engine core. With integer scaling on the 4K panel the sprite work looks exactly as intended — Bonk is one of the better-drawn mascots of the era and the art holds up.

**Rewind** is on for this system. Bonk is forgiving by 1990 standards (a health bar rather than one-hit deaths), so rewind matters less here than on the shooters, but the bottomless pits in World 3 are where you will reach for it.
