# Military Madness (TurboGrafx-16)

**Developer:** Hudson Soft | **Publisher:** Hudson Soft / NEC | **Year:** 1989

---

## Introduction

Military Madness — *Nectaris* in Japan — is a turn-based strategy game on hexagons, set on the Moon in 2089, and it is the best strategy game on the system by a wide margin. It predates Advance Wars by a decade and shares its shape: small maps, simple unit roster, no fog of war, no resource economy. Everything is visible, so every loss is your own fault.

There are 32 maps. You lose units permanently. There is no base-building and no production beyond capturing factories, so an army spent badly on map 12 is an army you do not have on map 13.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move the cursor.
- **Button I:** Select a unit, confirm a move, confirm an attack.
- **Button II:** Cancel / back out.
- **Run:** Menu — end turn, view maps, check unit data.

---

## The two rules that decide every battle

**Terrain sets the odds, not unit type.** A unit on a mountain or in a crater defends far better than the same unit on open ground. Attacking uphill is how good armies die. Before committing, look at what the defender is standing on.

**Numbers multiply, they do not add.** A unit attacked by several of your units in the same turn suffers escalating damage — the *zone* bonus. Three tanks hitting one target in one turn do far more than three tanks hitting it across three turns. This is the single most important mechanic in the game and the reason a careful player beats a reckless one so consistently.

---

## Units

The roster is small and each unit has a clear role.

| Unit | Role |
|---|---|
| **Infantry / Rifle** | Cheap, weak, takes and holds factories. Good in rough terrain. |
| **Bazooka** | Anti-armour infantry. Beats tanks, loses to everything fast. |
| **Tank (several grades)** | The core of any army. Strong on open ground, poor in mountains. |
| **Anti-air** | Shreds aircraft, helpless against armour. |
| **Artillery** | Attacks at range without being counter-attacked. Fragile; keep it behind the line. |
| **Fighter / Bomber** | Fast, ignores terrain, dies quickly to anti-air. |
| **Supply / Support** | Restores unit strength. Underrated and usually the difference in a long map. |

**Factories** are the only reinforcement source. Capture them with infantry and they produce units each turn. Losing a factory mid-map is usually the point a run turns.

---

## Strategy

**Never attack with one unit.** Move your line forward together and strike a single target with everything in range. The zone bonus makes concentrated attacks vastly more efficient, and a wounded enemy that survives will heal or retreat.

**Let them come to you.** Sitting on high ground and waiting is often correct. The AI attacks into bad terrain and grinds itself down.

**Take factories early.** They are the whole economy. A map where you hold two factories and the enemy holds one is usually already decided.

**Artillery wins sieges.** It attacks without being counter-attacked, so a couple of guns behind a defensive line can dismantle an entrenched enemy for free.

**Protect your supply units.** They are unarmed and the AI targets them. A supply unit behind a mountain keeps an army alive for many turns longer.

---

## The 32 maps

The campaign escalates from small skirmishes to large maps with several factories and mixed unit types. There are no branching paths — you take them in order, and a password is given between maps so progress can be resumed.

Maps in the twenties introduce enemy armies that materially outnumber you, and those are won by terrain and the zone bonus rather than by force. If a map feels impossible head-on, the answer is nearly always to fall back to defensible ground and let the attack break on it.

## Emulation Notes

Trivially light on the hardware and runs perfectly on Batocera's PC Engine core. Being a turn-based game with static screens, it is the one title on this system where **integer scaling** matters most for legibility — the hex grid and unit icons stay sharp rather than smearing.

**Rewind is enabled** on this system and it is arguably too powerful here: it lets you undo a bad attack after seeing the result, which removes the permanence that gives the game its weight. Worth avoiding on principle unless you are learning the maps.

The password system means you do not need save states, though they are more convenient. The Japanese original is *Nectaris*, and later remakes exist on several platforms; this is the 1989 original.
