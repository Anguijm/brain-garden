# Dungeon Explorer (TurboGrafx-16)

**Developer:** Atlus | **Publisher:** Hudson Soft / NEC | **Year:** 1989

---

## Introduction

Dungeon Explorer is a top-down action RPG in the Gauntlet mould, and its claim to fame is **five-player simultaneous co-op** — made possible by the TurboGrafx's TurboTap multitap, at a time when two players was the ceiling almost everywhere else. Played with a full group it is chaos of the good kind.

Solo it is still a solid action game: eight character classes, a large connected overworld, dungeons full of things to kill, and a satisfying magic system. It is less punishing than Gauntlet because your character actually grows.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move in eight directions.
- **Button II:** Attack — fires in the direction you face.
- **Button I:** Cast the currently selected magic.
- **Run:** Pause / status.
- **Select:** Cycle magic.

---

## The eight classes

Class choice matters more here than in most games of the type, because they differ in attack shape, not just numbers.

| Class | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Fighter** | Strong melee-range shot, high HP. The safe pick. |
| **Thief** | Fast movement, weak attack. Rewards good positioning. |
| **Princess** | Balanced, good magic. A strong all-rounder and the usual recommendation for a first solo run. |
| **Bishop** | Best magic, poor attack. Excellent in a group, awkward alone. |
| **Elf** | Long-range attack, fragile. Very effective if you never get hit. |
| **Wizard** | Devastating magic, very low HP. A group class. |
| **Hermit** | Odd, high magic. |
| **Warlock** | Slow, hits hard. |

In a group, spread the classes: a Fighter to hold the front, a Bishop or Wizard for magic, an Elf for range.

---

## Magic

Magic is collected as items and cast from a shared pool. Each spell has a distinct use rather than being a damage ladder:

- **Bomb** — heavy area damage; the boss-killer.
- **Fire** — a spread attack, good against groups.
- **Cure** — healing, and the reason a party lives.
- **Speed** — temporary movement boost, useful for escaping a swarm.
- **Reflect** — bounces projectiles.

Magic is finite and does not regenerate quickly, so it is worth hoarding for bosses rather than spending on ordinary rooms.

---

## Structure

The world is a connected map of towns, caves and dungeons rather than a level select. Towns give you healing, hints and occasionally new magic. Dungeons hold the bosses and the story items.

**Death sends you back to the last town**, keeping your level. It is a setback rather than a run-ender, which is what makes the game approachable.

**Bosses are pattern fights.** Each has a movement and attack cycle, and most have a specific vulnerable moment. Bombs shorten them dramatically.

---

## Strategy

**Keep moving.** Standing still in a room full of spawners is how you die. The game is designed around circling a room while firing.

**Face where you are going.** Your attack fires in your facing direction, which is set by movement — so retreating means firing away from the thing chasing you. Learning to strafe by tapping the direction you want to face is the core skill.

**Save bombs for bosses.** The temptation is to clear rooms with them; the correct use is to delete a boss's health bar.

**In co-op, do not all take melee classes.** Five Fighters is a wall with no range and no healing. One Bishop changes the whole run.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's PC Engine core.

**The multiplayer needs configuring before you launch.** Five-player play used the TurboTap on real hardware; in emulation you need five controllers mapped in Batocera first. Map them all before starting the game — hot-plugging a pad mid-session is generally not picked up.

**Rewind is enabled** on this system, and it is well-suited here: death costs progress across a large map, and unwinding a bad swarm encounter saves a walk back.
