# Blazing Lazers (TurboGrafx-16)

**Developer:** Compile | **Publisher:** Hudson Soft / NEC | **Year:** 1989

---

## Introduction

Blazing Lazers is the reason people bought a TurboGrafx-16. It is a vertical shooter by Compile, the studio that also made Zanac and Aleste, and it is essentially *Gunhed* — the Japanese release — renamed for the West after the film tie-in went away.

What separates it from the crowd is the power-up system. Most shooters of the era give you a weapon and a few upgrades. This one gives you four distinct main weapons, each with eight levels, plus four independent sub-weapon types that stack alongside them, plus shields and options. Fully loaded, the screen is a wall of your own firepower.

Nine areas, a boss at the end of each, and a difficulty curve that stays fair as long as you keep your weapon level up.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move.
- **Button II:** Fire. Hold for continuous fire.
- **Button I:** Deploy or detonate your sub-weapon, depending on type.
- **Run:** Pause.

---

## The weapon system

This is the whole game, so it is worth understanding before you start.

**Main weapons** — collect a numbered pod to switch, collect the same again to level it up (to 8).

| Main | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **1 — Standard** | Straight forward shot, widening as it levels. Reliable, boring, always adequate. |
| **2 — Photon Cannon** | A thick forward beam. Highest single-target damage; poor coverage. Best on bosses. |
| **3 — Ring Blaster** | Rings that expand outward. Wide coverage, weak per hit. Best for crowded fields. |
| **4 — Multi-body** | Fires in several directions at once. The all-rounder most players settle on. |

**Sub-weapons** stack on top and are collected separately:

- **Multibody** — small drones that shadow your ship and add fire.
- **Shield** — absorbs hits. The single most valuable pickup in the game.
- **Full Fire** — adds rear and side coverage.
- **Homing** — seeking missiles that clear stragglers while you concentrate forward.

**The rule that matters: dying costs you everything.** You revert to a bare ship in the middle of whatever was killing you, which is how a good run collapses. Play cautiously at high power rather than aggressively.

---

## Area notes

**Area 1** — gentle introduction, generous power-ups. Build to level 4-5 before the boss.

**Area 2** — the asteroid field. Rings or Multi-body handle the spread far better than the Photon Cannon.

**Area 3** — narrow corridors; contact with terrain kills. Slow down.

**Area 4** — heavy enemy density, the first real test of whether you kept your shield.

**Area 5** — the boss rush area. Conserve your weapon level.

**Area 6-7** — organic-looking stages with enemies that emerge from the background rather than the top of the screen. Watch the whole field, not just ahead.

**Area 8-9** — the finale. Full power plus a shield is close to mandatory; without both, expect to learn the patterns the hard way.

---

## Strategy

**Weapon level beats weapon choice.** A level-8 Standard shot outperforms a level-2 anything. When a pod appears for a weapon you do not want, consider whether taking it is worth dropping your current level — usually it is not.

**Grab every shield.** It is the difference between a mistake costing a moment and costing the run.

**Learn to park.** Many bosses have a single safe position where their attack pattern cannot reach. Finding it turns a hard fight into a damage race.

**Do not chase power-ups into danger.** The most common death in this game is flying into a bullet while reaching for a pod.

## Emulation Notes

Runs cleanly on Batocera's PC Engine core. The TurboGrafx-16's native output is 256x239, so integer scaling on a 4K panel gives crisp uniform pixels — this is one of the systems that benefits most from that setting.

**Rewind is enabled on this box** and it suits Blazing Lazers particularly well: because death strips your entire loadout, an un-rewound mistake in Area 7 effectively ends the run. Unwinding two seconds preserves an hour of accumulated power.

The ROM is `Blazing Lazers (USA)`; the Japanese release is *Gunhed*, and the two are the same game with different branding.
