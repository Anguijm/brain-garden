# The King of Fighters '98 (Neo Geo)

**Developer:** SNK | **Publisher:** SNK | **Year:** 1998

---

## Introduction

Subtitled *Dream Match Never Ends*, and the subtitle is the point: KOF '98 has **no story**. SNK took a year off from the plot and built a pure balance patch containing nearly every character from the series so far, tuned properly.

The result is the most played competitive KOF in history. Tournaments still run it.

---

## Controls

- **A:** Light punch. **B:** Light kick. **C:** Heavy punch. **D:** Heavy kick.
- **Joystick:** Move; back to block.
- **A+B:** Roll. **C+D:** Blowback attack.

---

## Three-on-three

You pick **three characters** and fight in sequence. When one loses, the next comes in with the health they have. There is no tagging mid-round — this is elimination, not tag team.

**Team order matters enormously.** Your first character sets the pace, your last inherits whatever meter and momentum remain. Conventional wisdom puts your most reliable character last.

---

## Advanced and Extra modes

Before the match you choose a **system**, and they play completely differently:

**Advanced** — meter builds by attacking, stores up to three stocks, and you can spend one to power up. Short hops, quick rolls, and the mode almost everyone plays.

**Extra** — meter is charged manually by holding buttons, you can dodge, and once charged you enter a temporary powered state. Slower, more deliberate, and closer to the older *Fatal Fury* feel.

**Pick Advanced unless you know why you want Extra.**

---

## The fundamentals

**Short hop.** Tapping up briefly gives a low, fast jump that beats anti-airs. Learning it is the single biggest jump in skill.

**Roll.** A+B rolls through attacks and past opponents, with vulnerability at the end. It is the escape tool and it is punishable.

**Blowback.** C+D is a heavy knockback attack that can be cancelled and is a combo starter for most of the cast.

---

## Strategy

**Learn one team of three**, not thirty characters.

**Short hop constantly.** Full jumps get you hit.

**Do not roll predictably.** The recovery is punishable and good players wait for it.

**Save meter for the last character.** Being able to finish a round with a super is worth more than spending stock early.

## Emulation Notes

Runs on Batocera's FinalBurn Neo core, launch-tested here.

**Note the romset:** this is `kof98`, the arcade version. The same collection contains Mega Drive, SNES and Neo Geo Pocket games with identical filenames — different games entirely, sharing a name. The arcade set is the one that matters.

A later revision, *KOF '98 Ultimate Match*, rebalanced and expanded the roster; purists still play the original.
