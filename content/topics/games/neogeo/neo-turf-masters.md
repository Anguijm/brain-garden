# Neo Turf Masters (Neo Geo)

**Developer:** Nazca Corporation | **Publisher:** SNK | **Year:** 1996

---

## Introduction

Known in Japan as *Big Tournament Golf*, and made by **Nazca** — the same studio that made [Metal Slug](topics/games/neogeo/metal-slug) the same year. It is routinely called the best arcade golf game ever made, and it has a small, devoted following that has kept saying so for thirty years.

The reason is that it is fast. A round takes minutes, the swing system is readable, and it never stops to explain itself.

---

## Controls

- **Joystick:** Aim, select club, adjust stance.
- **A:** The **three-press swing** — start, power, accuracy.
- **B:** Change club. **C:** Change shot type / view.

---

## The three-press swing

The system almost every golf game uses, and this one has the cleanest version:

1. **Press once** to start the power meter moving.
2. **Press again** at the top for maximum power — or earlier for less.
3. **Press a third time** in the marked zone for a straight shot.

**The third press is the one that matters.** Miss it left and the ball hooks; miss right and it slices. Power is easy; accuracy is the skill.

---

## What makes it arcade rather than simulation

**Wind is displayed plainly** and matters enormously.

**Six courses** across different countries, each with its own character — the Japanese course is tight and technical, the Australian one is wide and windy.

**Four golfers** with different power and control balances. **Torres** hits furthest, **Suzuki** is the most controllable.

**A time limit** per shot, which is what makes it an arcade game. You cannot deliberate.

---

## Strategy

**Aim into the wind**, not with it. The wind indicator shows where the ball will drift.

**Take less club and swing full** rather than more club and swing soft — accuracy is more reliable than power modulation.

**Learn the third-press timing per golfer.** It differs slightly by character.

**Use topspin and backspin.** The shot-type button is under-used and it is how you hold a green.

## Emulation Notes

Runs on Batocera's FinalBurn Neo core, launch-tested here.

**The romset is `turfmast`.** Note that `tturf` in the same collection is *Tough Turf*, an unrelated Sega beat 'em up — the names are similar enough to grab the wrong one.

Two-player alternating play is how it was meant to be enjoyed.
