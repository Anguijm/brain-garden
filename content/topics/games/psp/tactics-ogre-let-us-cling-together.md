# Tactics Ogre: Let Us Cling Together (PSP)

**Developer:** Square Enix | **Publisher:** Square Enix | **Year:** 2011 (US)

---

## Introduction

A remake of the 1995 Super Famicom game, rebuilt by **the original creative team** — Yasumi Matsuno directing, Hiroshi Minagawa on design, Hitoshi Sakimoto on music. That team had gone on to make *Final Fantasy Tactics* and *Final Fantasy XII* in the interim, and they came back to fix the game that started it.

It is the best strategy RPG on the PSP and one of the best in the genre. The [Game Boy Advance prequel](topics/games/gba/tactics-ogre-knight-of-lodis) is also on this box.

The setting is the Valerian Isles after a civil war, and the plot is about ethnic cleansing, political compromise and which atrocities you are willing to be party to. It is considerably darker than the genre norm.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad / stick:** Move cursor. **X:** Select. **O:** Cancel.
- **L / R:** **Rotate the battlefield** — essential.
- **Triangle:** Unit information. **Square:** Camera height.

---

## Height and facing

Battles are isometric and fully three-dimensional. Two rules govern almost everything:

**Height.** Attacking downhill adds damage and range; uphill subtracts. **Archers on high ground are the strongest units in the game**, and the first move of most battles is a race for elevation.

**Facing.** Attacking from the side or behind does substantially more damage and is far more likely to hit. Enemies get the same benefit against you.

---

## The two systems the remake added

**The Wheel of Fortune** lets you rewind up to fifty turns of a battle. This is not a save state — it is a designed mechanic, and it means a battle lost on one bad move can be recovered without replaying an hour.

**The Chariot Tarot / World system** lets you jump back to earlier story branch points after finishing, keeping your army, so the three routes can be explored without a fresh campaign each time.

**Together these solve the biggest problem the 1995 game had**, which was that a mistake forty minutes into a battle cost you the battle.

---

## Branching

Early on you are asked to do something monstrous. Your answer sets you on the **Law**, **Chaos** or **Neutral** route, and these are genuinely different campaigns with different battles, recruits and endings.

**There is no correct choice.** The game is built to make all three uncomfortable.

---

## Classes and skills

Units have classes with equipment restrictions and skill slots. Skills are bought with points and slotted, and the combination matters more than raw level.

**The remake caps level by chapter**, which stops grinding from trivialising the game and forces you to build properly instead.

---

## Strategy

**Take the high ground first, every battle.**

**Attack from behind.** The damage difference is large enough to change fights.

**Use the Wheel of Fortune freely.** It is a designed mechanic, not a cheat.

**Rotate the camera constantly.** Units hide behind terrain and the isometric view misleads.

**Build a small core team.** Spreading experience produces an army of underpowered units.

## Emulation Notes

Runs well on Batocera's PPSSPP core, which is one of the most mature emulators in existence — PSP games often run better here than on the original hardware.

**Internal resolution can be increased** past the PSP's native 480x272. This game's sprites are 2D and scale cleanly, so 2x or 3x looks sharp rather than smeared. That is not true of every PSP game.

Rewind is not enabled for PSP on this box, and it does not need to be: the Wheel of Fortune is a better version of the same idea, built in.
