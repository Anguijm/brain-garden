# PlayStation Portable Game Guides

Sony's first handheld was roughly a PlayStation 2 that fit in a bag, with a good screen and one
analogue stick. That missing second stick shaped a decade of design compromises, and emulation on
a real controller quietly undoes most of them.

## The second stick, and why this matters here

The PSP had **one analogue stick**. Every 3D game on it had to put the camera somewhere else,
usually the D-pad, which your thumb cannot reach while the other thumb is on the stick.

**Monster Hunter Freedom Unite** is the extreme case. Japanese players developed a grip called
"the claw" — hooking the index finger over the console to work the D-pad — and it caused genuine
hand injuries. On this box, with a modern two-stick controller, the right stick is the camera and
that entire problem disappears. It is the single biggest quality-of-life difference emulation makes
to this library.

## Internal resolution: when to raise it

PPSSPP can render above the PSP's native 480x272. Whether that helps depends on the game:

- **Fully 3D games** — God of War, Crisis Core, Vice City Stories, Valkyria Chronicles II — look
  dramatically better at 3x or 4x. Crisis Core in particular stops looking like a handheld game.
- **2D sprite games** — Persona 3 Portable, Lumines — gain little, and mixing high-resolution 3D
  with native-resolution sprites can look worse than leaving it alone.

## Three strategy RPGs, three difficulty levels

**Final Fantasy Tactics** is the deepest and the harshest — the Riovanes Castle fight has ended
playthroughs. **Tactics Ogre** is nearly as deep but added a fifty-turn rewind mechanic that removes
the worst of the frustration. **Jeanne d'Arc** is the gentle one, and the right starting point for
anyone who has bounced off the genre.
