# Final Fantasy Tactics: The War of the Lions (PSP)

**Developer:** Square Enix | **Publisher:** Square Enix | **Year:** 2007

---

## Introduction

The remake of the 1997 PlayStation game, which is itself the most respected strategy RPG Square ever made. Yasumi Matsuno wrote and directed the original after leaving Quest, where he had made [Tactics Ogre](topics/games/psp/tactics-ogre-let-us-cling-together) — also on this box, and the two games are unmistakably siblings.

The War of the Lions is a war of succession in Ivalice, told through Ramza Beoulve, a nobleman's son who ends up erased from the official history. It is a story about class, church and who gets to write the record, and it is unusually serious for its time.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad / stick:** Move cursor. **X:** Select. **O:** Cancel.
- **L / R:** **Rotate the battlefield.**
- **Triangle:** Unit status. **Select:** Toggle grid display.

---

## What the PSP version changed

**A new translation.** The 1997 script was famously garbled — the "Ivalician" retranslation here is elaborate, Shakespearean, and much better regarded, though a minority prefer the original's bluntness.

**Cutscenes** in hand-drawn animation.

**Two new classes**, Onion Knight and Dark Knight, plus guest characters including Balthier from *Final Fantasy XII*.

**Multiplayer** modes, which are dead on emulation.

**The one real flaw:** the PSP version has **slowdown during spell animations** that the PlayStation original did not. It is noticeable and it was never patched. Emulation can mitigate it by raising the frame limit, but it is a genuine defect of this version.

## The Job system

The heart of the game. Every unit gains **job points** in their current job and can change job freely.

Crucially, **abilities learned in one job can be equipped in another**. A Knight with White Magic learned as a White Mage is a different unit entirely. The combinations are where the depth lives:

- **Calculator/Arithmetician** — casts spells for free on every unit whose level, height or experience is divisible by a number. Broken, and famously so.
- **Ninja** — two weapons, enormous speed.
- **Monk** — no equipment cost, high damage, self-healing.
- **Chemist with Auto-Potion** — the reliability backbone of many parties.

**Move and Support abilities carry across jobs too**, and Move+2 or Ignore Height changes positioning more than most active skills.

---

## Height, facing, and speed

Same three rules as Tactics Ogre: **height** adds damage and range, **attacking from behind** greatly raises accuracy and damage, and **speed** determines turn frequency, which makes it the most valuable stat in the game.

---

## The difficulty spikes

**Chapter 2's Riovanes Castle** is the notorious one. A fight against Wiegraf as Ramza **alone**, immediately followed by a second phase, with no chance to change equipment or party in between. Many people have had to restart the game there.

**Save in multiple slots before Riovanes.** This is the single most useful piece of advice about the game.

---

## Strategy

**Learn Job abilities broadly early**, then specialise.

**Speed above everything** when choosing growth.

**Never leave a unit's back exposed.**

**Save in rotating slots.** The game will let you walk into an unwinnable state.

## Emulation Notes

Runs well on Batocera's PPSSPP core. **Raising the internal resolution helps the 3D backgrounds** but the sprites are 2D and can look mismatched at high multipliers; 2x is the comfortable setting.

The spell-animation slowdown is in the game's code, not the emulation.
