# Monster Hunter Freedom Unite (PSP)

**Developer:** Capcom | **Publisher:** Capcom | **Year:** 2009

---

## Introduction

The game that made Monster Hunter enormous in Japan, and the one that Western players mostly did not understand until *Monster Hunter: World* a decade later.

It is a game about hunting large animals with elaborate weapons, carving them up, making armour out of them, and hunting larger animals. There is no experience or levelling: **your character never gets stronger, only your equipment does, and your own skill**.

It has an estimated 500 hours of content and a reputation for being obtuse and unforgiving. Both are accurate.

---

## Controls, and the claw

- **Stick:** Move. **D-Pad:** **Camera** — this is the problem.
- **Triangle / Circle:** Attacks. **X:** Dodge / sheathe.
- **L:** Lock camera behind you. **R:** Item use.

**The PSP has no right analogue stick.** Camera control lives on the D-pad, which your thumb cannot reach while the left stick is in use.

Japanese players solved this with **"the claw"** — hooking the left index finger over the top of the console to work the D-pad while the thumb stays on the stick. It is a real technique with a real name and it caused real hand pain.

**On this box you have a modern controller with two sticks.** Batocera's PPSSPP setup maps the right stick to the camera, which removes the single worst thing about this game. That is not a small improvement; it is the difference between the game being playable and being a physical endurance test.

## How a hunt works

You take a quest, load into a zone divided into areas, and find the monster. Then:

- **Learn its tells.** Every monster telegraphs its attacks. This is the entire skill of the game.
- **Attack during recovery windows**, not whenever you like. Weapons are slow and committed; a swing you start cannot be cancelled.
- **Sharpen your weapon** with a whetstone as it dulls, in a safe moment.
- **Eat before the hunt** for stat bonuses that last the quest.

**Fifty minutes per quest, three faints and you fail.**

---

## Weapons

Fourteen types, each a genuinely different game. Great Sword is slow charged hits, Dual Blades is fast and mobile, Hunting Horn buffs the party, Lance blocks almost everything, Bowgun is ranged.

**Pick one and learn it properly.** Sampling all fourteen is how people bounce off.

---

## Progression

Hunt a monster, **carve materials from the corpse**, craft armour and a weapon from those materials, use them to hunt something harder. That loop is the whole game.

**Armour skills** come from set combinations and are where the depth is late.

---

## Strategy

**Use the right stick for the camera.** It is why this is playable here.

**Learn one monster completely before moving on.**

**Bring traps and flash bombs.** They are not optional against fast monsters.

**Eat every hunt.** Free stats.

**Do not chase the monster.** Wait for it to come to you and punish the recovery.
