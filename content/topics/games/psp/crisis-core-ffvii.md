# Crisis Core: Final Fantasy VII (PSP)

**Developer:** Square Enix | **Publisher:** Square Enix | **Year:** 2008 (US)

---

## Introduction

A prequel to *Final Fantasy VII*, following **Zack Fair** in the years before Cloud's story starts. Anyone who has played VII knows how it ends, and the game is built knowing that you know.

It was the PSP's flagship role-playing game and one of the best-looking things on the handheld.

---

## Controls

- **Stick:** Move. **X:** Attack. **Square:** Guard. **O:** Dodge roll.
- **L / R:** Cycle materia and items.
- **Triangle:** Confirm menus.

Real-time action combat, not turn-based — the first Final Fantasy VII game to work this way.

---

## The DMW, which is either brilliant or infuriating

The **Digital Mind Wave** is a slot machine that runs constantly in the top-left corner during battle. You do not control it.

When reels match, things happen:

- **Two matching numbers** — a limit break fires automatically
- **Three sevens** — full heal and temporary invincibility
- **Certain combinations** — Zack levels up, or a materia levels up

**Levelling is controlled by the slot machine.** You do not gain experience toward a level in the ordinary way; the DMW decides when you level, influenced by accumulated experience behind the scenes.

**People either find this a fascinating expression of Zack's mental state or an intolerable loss of control.** Both readings are defensible. What is certain is that it makes the game unpredictable in a way no other Final Fantasy is.

---

## Materia

Familiar from VII: slotted abilities, magic and support. **Materia fusion** is the depth — combining two materia produces a stronger one, and adding items to the fusion grants stat bonuses.

**Fusion is how you break the game.** A properly fused materia set makes Zack enormously overpowered, and the game does not explain the system well.

---

## Missions

**Three hundred optional side missions**, accessible from any save point, each a short combat map. They hold most of the best equipment and materia in the game.

They are also repetitive — they reuse a small number of maps — so treat them as a resource to dip into rather than a completionist target.

---

## Strategy

**Learn to dodge-roll.** Guarding is worse than dodging in almost every situation.

**Fuse materia deliberately** once you have a few duplicates. It is the difference between a fair game and a trivial one.

**Do missions in batches** when you want specific gear, not all at once.

**Do not chase the DMW.** You cannot influence it directly and trying will only frustrate you.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs well on Batocera's PPSSPP core, and this is one of the games that benefits most from **increasing the internal resolution** — it is fully 3D, and at 3x or 4x it looks like a modern game rather than a handheld one.

A full remake, *Crisis Core: Reunion*, released in 2022 with modernised combat. The original is still worth playing; the DMW is intact in both.
