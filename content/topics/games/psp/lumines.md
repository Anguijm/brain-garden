# Lumines: Puzzle Fusion (PSP)

**Developer:** Q Entertainment | **Publisher:** Ubisoft | **Year:** 2004

---

## Introduction

A PSP launch game, and one of the very few launch titles anyone still plays. Tetsuya Mizuguchi — who made *Rez* and later *Tetris Effect* — built a falling-block puzzle game where **the music and the clearing mechanic are the same system**.

It is deceptively simple and extremely hard to stop playing.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move the block left and right; **down to drop**.
- **Square / Triangle:** Rotate.

---

## The rules, which are not Tetris

Blocks fall as **2x2 squares made of two colours** in some arrangement.

You do not clear lines. You form **2x2 squares of a single colour** anywhere on the field. Those squares are marked, and then:

**A vertical "timeline" sweeps across the field left to right, in time with the music, and erases every marked square it passes.**

That is the whole game, and it changes everything:

- **Squares are not cleared when you make them.** They are cleared when the timeline arrives.
- **So you can build several squares before the sweep** and clear them all at once for a much bigger score.
- **The timeline's speed changes with the music.** A slow track gives you time to build large combos; a fast track clears constantly and leaves less room.

**New players clear each square as they make it and wonder why their score is low.** The game is about deliberately holding a mess together until the sweep.

---

## Skins

Each "skin" is a complete audiovisual set — music, colours, block art, timeline speed — and they change as you progress. The music is not a backing track; **the tempo directly controls the clearing rate**, so a skin change is a difficulty change.

---

## Modes

**Challenge** is the endless main mode, progressing through skins as you survive. **Puzzle** mode asks you to build specific shapes. **Time Attack** and **Versus** round it out.

---

## Strategy

**Build, then let the sweep collect.** Clearing one square at a time wastes the mechanic.

**Watch the timeline position**, not just the falling block. Knowing how long until the sweep is the core skill.

**Keep one colour dominant on one side.** Mixed-colour fields produce nothing.

**Rotate to fit, do not drop in a panic.** Dropping is fast but the field fills quickly.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's PPSSPP core — it is a 2D game and undemanding.

**Audio timing matters more than usual here**, because the timeline is synchronised to the music. If the sound stutters, the game becomes unplayable in a way a visual stutter would not, so this is a good test of whether PSP audio is configured correctly on the box.

Q Entertainment made several sequels; the original is still regarded as the cleanest.
