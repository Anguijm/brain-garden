# Mike Tyson's Punch-Out!! (NES)

**Developer:** Nintendo R&D3 | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 1987

---

## Introduction

Punch-Out!! is not a boxing game. It is a pattern-recognition game wearing boxing clothes, and once you accept that, it becomes one of the most satisfying things on the console. Every opponent has a fixed set of attacks, each telegraphed by a specific animation frame, and each with exactly one correct response. You are not out-fighting them; you are learning to read them.

Little Mac is small, weak, and slow to recover. He cannot trade punches with anyone. The whole game is dodge, counter, dodge, counter.

The Mike Tyson version is the original 1987 release. When Nintendo's licence with Tyson expired in 1990 the game was reissued as *Punch-Out!! Featuring Mr. Dream*, identical except the final opponent is a generic white-clad boxer with the same patterns. This ROM is the original.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad Left/Right:** Dodge to that side.
- **D-Pad Down:** Duck. Also blocks body blows.
- **A / B:** Body punch (right / left) — used when the opponent's guard is up.
- **Up + A / Up + B:** Face punch (right / left) — used when the opponent's guard is open.
- **Start:** Throw a star punch, if you have stars. This is a knockdown-grade uppercut.
- **Start (while down):** Mash A and B to get up.

### Stars

A star is earned by landing a specific counter at a specific moment — usually punching an opponent in the face during a particular wind-up frame. Up to three can be held, and each **Start** press spends one on an uppercut that does enormous damage. Stars are lost if you are hit.

Star opportunities are the core skill of the game. They are not random; each opponent has an exact frame that grants one.

## The opponents

**Glass Joe** — Dodge his telegraphed jab, counter with face punches. Star: counter his very slow wind-up.

**Von Kaiser** — Same, faster. He telegraphs an uppercut by twitching his eyebrows; duck it, then counter.

**Piston Honda (1st)** — Attacks in bursts. When he steps back and rushes, dodge and counter for stars.

**Don Flamenco (1st)** — Waits. Punch him once to make him swing, dodge, counter. He goes down easily.

**King Hippo** — The first wall. He is immune to face punches, and his body is guarded until he opens his mouth to attack. Punch his face when the mouth opens; he clutches his stomach and his trunks drop, exposing a bandage. Body-punch the bandage. Repeat. Once down, he cannot get up.

**Great Tiger** — Telegraphs his teleport punch with a flashing jewel on his turban. Punch him the instant it flashes for a star. His rapid jabs must be dodged individually.

**Bald Bull** — The second wall, and the famous one. His **Bull Charge** — three hops across the ring — is countered by a body punch at the exact moment he starts the third hop. Too early or late and you take heavy damage. That single counter puts him straight down.

**Piston Honda (2nd)** — As before, faster, with an added rush sequence.

**Soda Popinski** — Drinks between rounds to recover. Dodge his hooks and counter; he leaves long openings after missing.

**Don Flamenco (2nd)** — Now genuinely defensive. He blocks everything until he attacks, so patience is the whole fight.

**Mr. Sandman** — Fast, heavy, and his triple uppercut will end you. Dodge each of the three, then counter.

**Super Macho Man** — His spin punch is telegraphed by winding his arm; dodge and counter each rotation.

**Mike Tyson** — Round 1 is the test: for the first ninety seconds, every one of his uppercuts is a one-hit knockdown. You cannot block them, only dodge. Watch his gloves — he flashes them before the uppercut. Survive that opening stretch and he switches to slower, readable combinations you can counter normally. Most losses happen in the first minute.

---

## General strategy

**Never throw the first punch** against anyone past Don Flamenco. The game rewards reacting, not initiating. Punching into an opponent's wind-up is how you get countered.

**Dodging beats blocking.** Blocking absorbs some damage; dodging avoids it entirely and opens a counter window. Only duck when you specifically need to.

**Between rounds**, mash A and B to recover a little health. It is not much, but it matters against Tyson.

**Learn one opponent at a time.** The game is short in playtime and long in learning. There is no shame in losing to Bald Bull thirty times; the thirty-first is when you see the third hop coming.
