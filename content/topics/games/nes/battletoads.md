# Battletoads — NES Walkthrough

**Developer:** Rare | **Publisher:** Tradewest | **Year:** 1991

---

## Introduction

Battletoads is one of the hardest games ever released for the NES. That is not hyperbole for the sake of legend — it is a factual description of a product whose difficulty curve stops the overwhelming majority of players at the third stage, a speed-bike level called the Turbo Tunnel, which has no parallel in the annals of pure difficulty before or since. The game is not impossible, but finishing it without save states requires a level of commitment most people do not have for entertainment.

The story: three anthropomorphic toads — Rash, Zitz, and Pimple — are escorting Princess Angelica across space when the Dark Queen attacks their ship. Pimple and the Princess are captured. Rash and Zitz must fight their way across twelve stages of Ragnarok's World to rescue them and defeat the Dark Queen.

This guide covers all twelve stages in full, with complete boss strategies, the Turbo Tunnel obstacle sequence in as much useful detail as can be documented, save-state recommendations for emulation, and two-player notes. You are playing on Batocera with save states enabled. Use them without shame. The game was designed in an era when memorization across dozens of deaths was expected entertainment; you can short-circuit that with F5 and F9 and still have a genuine experience.

---

## The Characters

You play as either **Rash** (Player 1, in green) or **Zitz** (Player 2, in blue). They are functionally identical in the NES version — same speed, same moves, same attack power. The only difference is the color and which player controls which. Pick whoever you like; the game does not reward a particular choice.

## Controls & Combat

### Basic Controls

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| D-Pad | Move / duck (hold Down) |
| A | Attack |
| B | Jump |
| A + B simultaneously | Special (varies by stage) |
| Select | Pause |
| Start | Start game / unpause |

### Combat Moves

The combat system in Battletoads builds on basic inputs. The attack button produces different moves depending on your speed and context.

**Standing Punch:** Press A while standing still. Quick jab, short range. Use it to start combos.

**Kick:** Press A while walking toward an enemy. Faster than the punch and covers more horizontal distance.

**Running Headbutt:** Hold the d-pad in one direction long enough to break into a run, then press A. Your toad lowers their head and charges into the enemy. One of your best tools — it covers ground fast and sends enemies flying.

**Smash Attacks (Charged Hits):** Hold A without pressing anything else. After about a second, you wind up a special Smash Hit. Release A to execute. There are multiple Smash Hits and they animate spectacularly:
- **The Big Bad Boot** (hold A from a standing position): Your toad rears back and delivers an enormous kick. Covers the full height of an enemy, high damage.
- **Nuclear Knuckles** (hold A after a short run): A massive punch that sends enemies tumbling across the screen. Your bread-and-butter Smash Hit for groups.
- **Battletoad Butt** (context-sensitive): Body slam. Used at close range when an enemy is directly beside you.

**Grab:** Walk directly into an enemy. Your toad grabs them automatically. Once grabbed:
- Press A to punch them repeatedly.
- Press Up + A to throw them forward into other enemies.
- Press Down + A to slam them on the ground (useful against harder enemies).
- Just hold them and walk them into a wall or pit.

**Jump Kick:** Press B to jump, then press A at any point during the arc. Hits airborne enemies and clears groups from above.

**Drop Kick:** Jump, then press B again mid-air and A simultaneously to deliver a diving two-footed kick downward. Useful for clearing enemies on a lower platform.

## Lives & Continues

You begin with **3 lives**. In the US NES version, you get **3 continues**, which restart you at the beginning of the stage you died on. Losing all continues sends you back to the title screen.

You earn an extra life at 40,000 points and again at 80,000 points, and an occasional 1-Up icon appears in certain stages. Collect every 1-Up you see — this game will take them all.

**With save states:** Save before every difficult section. The Turbo Tunnel, Volkmire's Inferno, and the Clinger-Winger each warrant saves every few screens. Do not wait for checkpoints that do not exist.

---

## Two-Player Notes

Battletoads supports two simultaneous players, and it is genuinely harder in two-player mode despite the extra body. The reason: enemies split their aggression between both players, which sounds helpful until you realize the hit detection does not distinguish friendly from enemy — **your partner can damage and kill you with their attacks**. In the vehicle stages, one player crashing into the other counts as a death for the victim.

Two-player does have advantages: boss fights go faster with twice the damage output, and the grab-and-throw technique is devastating when coordinated (one player holds an enemy, the other smashes them). Just be aware that Stage 3 (Turbo Tunnel) is notoriously brutal in co-op because the bikes share the same lane and crashes propagate.

For emulation with save states, two-player is viable and fun. Without save states, single-player is genuinely recommended.

## Stage-by-Stage Walkthrough

### Stage 1: Ragnarok's Canyon

**Type:** Side-scrolling beat-em-up  
**Difficulty:** Easy  
**Boss:** General Slaughter

This is the tutorial. The canyon is a horizontal side-scroller: beat the enemies on screen, advance, repeat. The game introduces you to the punch, kick, and headbutt mechanics against the Pig Soldiers — large anthropomorphic pigs in armor who patrol the canyon.

**Pig Soldiers:** The standard enemy. They walk toward you and throw punches. The headbutt is your best move against them — break into a run, close the distance, and headbutt them off the screen. Against grouped pigs, grab one and use it as a club against the others.

**Vultures (riding birds):** Flying enemies that swoop from above. Jump-kick them as they approach or back off and let them fly past, then attack from behind.

**Walker Machines:** Near the end of the stage, bipedal walker machines appear. These take more hits than the pigs and fire lasers from their single eye. Dodge the laser horizontally and headbutt the body — it takes 5–6 hits to destroy each walker. You can also grab the laser projectile if your toad's hands connect with it mid-air, though this is difficult to do reliably.

**Boss: General Slaughter**

General Slaughter arrives in a larger bipedal walker and opens the fight by firing laser balls in arcs. He fires three balls in quick succession. Move to the far side of the screen and avoid the arc, then close in and hit the walker's body repeatedly while he is in his recovery animation. The pattern is predictable: fire three times, pause, fire three times. Get in as many headbutts as you can during each pause. Around 20–25 hits destroy him.

**Save State:** None strictly needed. If you die here, you have done something remarkable.

### Stage 2: Wookie Hole

**Type:** Vertical descent on ropes  
**Difficulty:** Moderate  
**Boss:** Big Blag (first fight)

The Wookie Hole is a vertical cavern. You descend by climbing down ropes that hang from the ceiling. The hazards are turret platforms, enemy toads on ropes, and electric barriers at certain depths.

**Rope mechanics:** Press Up and Down to climb. Press B to jump between ropes. You can attack while hanging by pressing A — your toad punches horizontally from the rope. If you fall off a rope onto a platform, you can re-grab by pressing Up toward a rope above you.

**Turrets:** Fixed platforms with cannons that fire horizontally. They alternate left and right. The trick is timing your descent between bursts — watch the barrel and move when it fires, not before. Turrets can be destroyed with enough punches.

**Enemy Toads:** Other toads appear on ropes above and below. If one is directly below you on the same rope, they will kick upward. If one is above, they punch downward. Move to a parallel rope and attack sideways, or time a jump to kick them from the air.

**Electric Barriers:** Appear at certain depths as horizontal beams. Swing from a rope to pass them — the arc of the swing lets you pass below the beam. Do not try to climb through them.

**Boss: Big Blag**

Big Blag is a very large pig who hops from side to side. He is the most imposing thing you have fought so far, and he is not especially dangerous if you understand the timing. He hops toward you in a slow arc — jump to avoid the landing (which creates a shockwave) and move toward him while he is airborne. Hit him 3 times per exchange. His shockwave travels the length of the screen; you must jump over it or be on a platform above the floor.

After 10–12 hits, Big Blag goes down. He reappears as a sub-boss in later stages.

**Save State:** Save at the bottom of the descent before the boss arena.

### Stage 3: Turbo Tunnel

**Type:** Speed-bike racing / obstacle dodge  
**Difficulty:** Extreme  
**Boss:** None

The Turbo Tunnel is where Battletoads ends for most players. You ride a speed bike through a corridor at high speed. Walls, ramps, and obstacles appear in your path. You cannot slow down — the bike moves at a fixed speed that increases as the stage progresses. You must dodge or jump every obstacle perfectly. A single hit kills you.

**Controls on the bike:**
- D-pad Left/Right: steer left and right
- B: jump (short hop)
- A: boost (briefly accelerates; mostly unnecessary)
- A + B: you can attempt to crash through certain soft obstacles, but usually useless

**Obstacle types:**
- **Wall (Left/Right):** A full-height wall blocking the left or right half of the track. Move to the open side.
- **Low Wall / Floater:** A wall that does not reach the floor or does not reach the ceiling. Jump over it (B) or pass under it depending on which gap is open.
- **Ramp (Up/Down):** A ramp that launches your bike upward. Jump from the ramp crest to clear what follows. If you don't jump at the peak, you land hard and usually die on whatever comes next.
- **Rocket Rats:** Enemy vehicles that enter from behind or in front. Move to avoid them — they travel in lanes.

**The Stage:**

The Turbo Tunnel is divided into sections separated by brief flat stretches. The early sections are slower and more forgiving. The final sections are fast enough that reaction time alone cannot save you — you must know what is coming.

**Section 1 (Slow, Introduction):**
The first walls are spaced wide apart and alternate simply: right wall, left wall, right wall. The pace is slow enough to react. Get a feel for the steering here — the bike does not turn on a dime; you need to be moving to the correct side before the wall arrives.

**Section 2 (Medium Speed, First real test):**
Walls begin appearing in pairs. A right wall followed immediately by a left wall — you must cross the full width of the track between them. Ramps appear: ride up the ramp, and at the crest, press B to jump the gap or low wall that follows.

**Section 3 (Medium-Fast, Low Walls introduced):**
The low walls (floater walls) appear here. These are walls that do not touch the ceiling — jump them with B. Mixed sequences: full right wall → jump the low wall → full left wall. The timing window for the jump is tight. Hit B as the wall comes into view, not after.

**Section 4 (Fast, Sequences tighten):**
Walls come in rapid succession: Right → Left → Low → Right → Left. The cross-screen movement must be committed — start moving to the new position as soon as you clear the previous wall, not after you've landed. Rocket Rats appear here too. They travel in one lane; stay in the other lane whenever one is visible.

**Section 5 — The Wall Run (Final section, Maximum speed):**
This is the section that ends most runs. The walls come so fast that your only hope is to have memorized the sequence. The general pattern for the final wall run, from left to right:

1. Open Left (move to left side)
2. Open Right (move to right side)
3. Low Wall — Jump
4. Open Left
5. Open Left (stay left)
6. Ramp — Jump at crest
7. Open Right
8. Open Left
9. Open Right
10. Open Right (stay right)
11. Low Wall — Jump
12. Open Left — and you're through.

**This sequence is approximate.** The exact frame timings vary. The crucial lesson is to over-commit: when you need to be on the left side, be on the far left edge of the track, not the middle-left. When you need to jump, jump immediately as the ramp starts rising, not at the peak. The instinct to wait and react is wrong — commit early.

**Save State Strategy for Turbo Tunnel:**
Save at the start of Section 4. If you die in Section 5, reload and do Section 4 again to build the muscle memory into the Section 5 entry. When you reliably get to Section 5, save again at its entrance. Then die, and reload at the Section 5 save until you map the pattern. This is how you learn it: not through brute consecutive attempts, but through isolating the target section.

**There is no boss.** Clear the final wall and the stage ends. The transition to Stage 4 is your reward.

### Stage 4: Arctic Caverns

**Type:** Exploration, combat  
**Difficulty:** Moderate  
**Boss:** Robo-Pig

The Arctic Caverns is a side-scrolling stage through an ice cave. The floor is slippery (your toad slides a short distance after stopping) and the ceiling is lined with stalactites that fall if you walk under them. The sliding is not dramatic — more a slight momentum overshoot — but it affects your positioning against enemies.

**Stalactites:** Dozens of stalactites hang from the ceiling throughout. If you walk under one, it begins to tremble and then drops within 2 seconds. The kill is instant. Either punch the stalactite first (one hit knocks it down safely while you're not under it) or go around. You can use knocked-down stalactites as weapons by walking them into enemies — they slide on the ice floor.

**Ice Soldiers:** Enemy soldiers in ice armor. Tougher than the pig soldiers from Stage 1. The headbutt is your primary tool — run across the slick floor and headbutt them before they can punch back. They sometimes throw grenades in a short arc; jump over the grenade, not away from it.

**Snowballs:** Some enemies throw snowballs in a curving arc. Dodge by moving perpendicular to the throw direction.

**Boss: Robo-Pig**

A mechanical pig in a suit that fires missiles from its shoulders. The missiles fly horizontally at mid-height — duck to avoid them. Wait for the missile volley to end (it fires 3 in a row), then rush in and headbutt the body. The Robo-Pig moves left and right during the fight; stay on the side away from the wall so you have room to dodge the missiles. Around 15 hits end the fight.

**Save State:** Save at the boss arena entrance.

### Stage 5: Surf City

**Type:** Surfboard riding  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Boss:** Big Blag (rematch)

You ride surfboards down an underground river. The controls are different here: Up on the d-pad accelerates, Down slows you. Left and Right steer. The river has strong current sections that pull you regardless of steering, and obstacles — rocks, piranhas, and coral — that knock you off.

**River mechanics:**
- Ride the middle of the stream in clear sections.
- In current sections (marked by faster-moving water), steer slightly against the current to maintain your line.
- Piranhas leap out of the water in arcs — timing a jump over them works, but most are easier to simply dodge by moving horizontally.
- Rocks sit stationary. Give them wide berth.

**Snakes:** Giant snakes appear on the riverbanks and reach into the water to bite. Watch the edge of the screen — when a snake head appears, move to the far side of the stream.

**Speed Gates:** Some sections have speed boost pads that you must pass through on the correct side. Missing them is not fatal, but hitting the barrier beside them is.

**Walker Leg weapon:** Before the boss encounter, you will pass through a section with walker machines on the bank. One of them can be punched until it drops its leg, which falls in the river. Ride over it to pick it up. It functions as a melee weapon that does high damage to Big Blag — you can hold it while surfing.

**Boss: Big Blag (rematch)**

Big Blag is back and angrier. He hops across the water faster than before. The pattern: he hops toward you, lands, creates a shockwave, then hops again. Jump over the landing shockwave — it travels faster than in Stage 2. If you have the Walker Leg, hit him each time he lands. Without it, the headbutt still works but you need more hits. Around 15–18 hits. Watch for his charge: when he lowers his head and rushes in a straight line, jump over him. After the charge, he pauses — hit him then.

**Save State:** Save at the boss transition.

### Stage 6: Karnath's Lair

**Type:** Snake-riding rail stage (pseudo-3D)  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Boss:** Karnath**

The Karnath's Lair is a 3D tunnel viewed from behind. Giant snakes named Karnath fill the tunnel and you must ride them — jump from snake to snake — while enemies attack from the sides.

**Snake-riding mechanics:**
Your toad stands on top of a snake's back. The snake moves forward automatically. When you reach the end of a snake's body, jump (B) to the next snake ahead. Misjudge the jump and you fall into the abyss.

**Timing jumps:** Jump when you are near the tail of the current snake and the head of the next one is at screen center. The sweet spot is a medium jump — not a long hold (you overshoot) and not a tap (you fall short). Practice on the first few snakes where the penalty for missing is a life rather than a continue.

**Side attacks:** Bat-like enemies fly in from the left and right of the screen. Duck (hold Down) to avoid them or punch them when they're within reach. Most of them target you in pairs — one from each side. If both arrive simultaneously, duck; if they're staggered, punch the first and duck the second.

**Stage structure:** The stage is divided into three sections. The first is slow and teaches the mechanics. The second introduces enemy attacks while you jump. The third increases snake movement speed significantly.

**Boss: Karnath**

The Karnath boss is the largest snake in the lair. He fills the width of the screen and moves in an S-curve. Your toad stands on a platform to one side. Karnath sweeps across the screen in a pattern: sweeping low (jump over his body), sweeping high (duck under), then lunging at you (jump diagonally away). Hit his head each time it passes within range. Around 12 hits ends the fight.

**Save State:** Save at the start of Section 3 and before the boss.

### Stage 7: Volkmire's Inferno

**Type:** Jet-bike flying stage  
**Difficulty:** Extreme  
**Boss:** Scuzz

This is the Turbo Tunnel's bigger, meaner older sibling. Instead of a speed bike on a flat surface, you fly a jet-bike through a fire-lined tunnel. The tunnel has walls, floors, ceilings, fireballs, and rotating fire columns. You control vertical height with Up and Down and horizontal position with Left and Right. You cannot stop.

**What makes this harder than Turbo Tunnel:**
The plane of movement is fully 2D — you must dodge obstacles that come from all four directions simultaneously. The fire columns rotate and fill the tunnel width for a moment before withdrawing. The fireball clusters travel in patterns you need to memorize.

**Fire columns:** These appear in groups and rotate clockwise or counter-clockwise. The gap between them is the passage. Position yourself at the gap's expected arrival point before the columns start rotating, not after.

**Floor and ceiling spikes:** Some sections reduce the flyable height to a thin strip, then expand again. Stay in the center when you see the height narrowing.

**Fireballs:** Travel in four-directional bursts from wall cannons. They fire in spreads. The safe position is usually the center of the screen — the bursts target the edges — but individual patterns vary.

**Boss: Scuzz**

Scuzz is a large rat enemy piloting a craft. He fires missiles in pairs: one high, one low. Jump the low missile or duck the high one (in the jet bike, Up to go high, Down to go low). Between missile pairs, he moves to a vulnerable position — ram him with your jet bike (fly directly into his craft) for damage. Around 10 rams destroy Scuzz.

**Save State:** Save at the beginning of each fire column sequence. This stage warrants saving aggressively.

### Stage 8: Intruder Excluder

**Type:** Vertical elevator climb, beat-em-up  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Boss:** Robo-Manus

You are inside a Dark Queen spaceship elevator shaft. The screen scrolls upward and you must stay on the platforms while fighting enemies. Fall below the screen and you die.

**Platform types:**
- **Solid platforms:** Stand, jump between them.
- **Moving platforms:** Float left and right or up and down. Time your jumps to land on them.
- **Conveyor platforms:** The surface moves, pushing you in one direction. Jump against the conveyor to stay in position.

**Enemy types:**
- **Pig Sentries:** Return of the basic soldiers, now with energy weapons. Same headbutt strategy applies. Priority is clearing the platform before the screen scrolls them off the bottom.
- **Gun Turrets (fixed):** Mounted on walls, fire in fixed patterns. Approach from below or above where their barrel cannot angle.
- **Jet Pack enemies:** Enemy toads with jet packs who fly between platforms. Jump-kick them mid-air or let them land and headbutt.

**Keep moving up:** The screen scrolls regardless of what you're doing. Never spend too long fighting a single enemy when falling behind the scroll means death. Prioritize enemies who are between you and where you need to go.

**Boss: Robo-Manus**

Robo-Manus is a large mechanical boss at the top of the shaft. He fills about one-third of the screen width and fires projectiles from both shoulder cannons simultaneously.

**His pattern:**
1. Fire a diagonal spread of 3 bullets from each shoulder.
2. Pause. Jump-kick his body.
3. He lunges forward. Jump back.
4. Pause and fire again.

Duck the diagonal spread if it targets low, or jump if it targets high. The jump-kick is your best attack — it connects with his upper body where the armor is weakest. The headbutt also works during the pause window but puts you at risk of the lunge.

**When his health drops below half:** He speeds up the pattern (shorter pause, faster lunge). Stay at maximum comfortable range and use jump-kicks only — the headbutt's recovery time becomes too long.

Around 20–25 hits total. Save before the boss.

### Stage 9: Terra Tubes

**Type:** Underwater swimming, combat  
**Difficulty:** Very Hard  
**Boss:** Eyerock

The Terra Tubes take place underwater, inside submerged pipes and tunnels. You swim through with modified controls and fight aquatic enemies. There are strong currents, enemy blowfish, and grate sections that can trap you.

**Swimming controls:**
- D-pad in any direction: swim that direction. You do not fall — gravity is reduced underwater.
- A: punch (short range but still functional)
- B: kick (longer range, works better underwater)
- Hold A: smash build-up works underwater but the animation is slower.

**Currents:** Multiple sections have strong water flow that pushes you in one direction. Against a strong current, you can fight it (slower progress, but possible) or find a wall alcove to shelter in temporarily.

**Blowfish:** Inflate and fire spines in 8 directions. Do not approach an inflating blowfish from any direction — wait for the spine burst to clear and attack during the deflation. The kick works best here since it has longer reach and lets you stay outside spine range.

**Grates:** Metal grates can be punched through (the smash attack destroys them) but they slow your progress. Some are timed — they open and close. Pass through on the open cycle.

**Exit pipes:** The stage is maze-like. When you reach a fork, the correct path usually has more enemies (the wrong paths are empty). If you've been traveling in a direction for more than 30 seconds without encountering an enemy, you have probably taken a wrong turn.

**Boss: Eyerock**

Eyerock is a giant eyeball mounted in a rock wall. He fires laser beams from his pupil — they track your position and fire a half-second after his gaze locks onto you. Move constantly to prevent lock. Attack the eye itself (his pupil) with jump-kicks or punches during the moments he is not firing. He also spawns smaller enemy blowfish periodically — clear them fast or they crowd the arena.

Around 15 hits. The lasers do heavy damage; lose health here means you enter Stage 10 weakened, so use healing items (floating hearts appear occasionally in the arena) before finishing him.

### Stage 10: Rat Race

**Type:** Race against clock  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Boss:** None (time limit is the boss)

The Rat Race is a vertically-scrolling sprint. You run upward through a building while rats chew through the wiring in the basement — if the wire bundle is fully chewed before you reach the top, the stage fails. There is no life loss for running out of time; the stage just resets.

**Running the race:**
You move upward through multiple floors connected by ladders and platforms. Enemies block the path on most floors. You can fight them or use the smash attacks to clear space and sprint past them.

The optimal approach is to **fight only what you must.** The headbutt clears a path forward without stopping, and you want to keep moving. On floors with two enemies blocking a narrow corridor, the headbutt + immediate sprint combination gets you through before they recover.

**Ladders:** Press Up near a ladder to climb. You cannot fight on ladders — any hit knocks you off. Watch for enemies positioned at ladder tops before ascending.

**Rat timer:** The rats are shown in the lower-left corner. There are multiple rats and they gnaw in sequence — when all of them finish, you fail. The timer is lenient enough on the first three floors but becomes tight on floors 4–6. Never fight a prolonged exchange when a sprint-past is possible.

**There is no boss.** Reach the top of the building and the stage ends.

**Save State:** Save on each floor after clearing it. Losing the race resets the whole floor sequence.

### Stage 11: Clinger-Wingers

**Type:** Track-chasing puzzle  
**Difficulty:** Extreme  
**Boss:** None

The Clinger-Winger is one of the most unusual stages in any NES game. You ride a disk called the Clinger-Winger along a winding monorail track, and a spinning spiked orb chases you from behind. If the orb catches up, you die. If you fall off the track, you die. The track loops and turns, and you must navigate it at high speed while the orb gains.

**Controls:**
- The disk moves automatically. You can only influence direction at track junctions.
- At a junction: press Left or Right to take the corresponding turn.
- The disk must be moving in the correct direction at a junction to make the turn — if you are drifting to the right and press Left at the last moment, the disk often continues right. Pre-select your turn.

**The orb:** It maintains roughly constant speed slightly faster than your disk. You gain separation on tight corners (the orb has a wider turning radius) and lose it on straights. The strategy is to take corners tightly and efficiently.

**The track pattern:** The track loops in a figure-eight with several branch points. The correct path makes a single complete loop that places you ahead of the orb at the finish. Wrong paths loop back and cost time — the orb will catch you if you take two wrong turns consecutively.

**Without memorization:** This stage, like the Turbo Tunnel, eventually requires knowing the route. The save state strategy: save at each junction, take the turn, and if the path looks wrong (heads back to a section you've already passed), reload and take the other turn.

**There is no boss.** Complete the lap and exit.

### Stage 12: The Revolution

**Type:** Final stage, combat + boss  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Boss:** The Dark Queen

The Revolution is the final stage. It opens with a combat section very similar to Stage 1 — fighting through groups of enemies while advancing horizontally. The Dark Queen's throne room serves as the arena for the final fight. All of your combat skills are tested against the toughest enemy groups in the game.

**Enemy groups:** The stage mixes everything you have fought: Pig Soldiers with upgraded stats, Jet Pack enemies, and armored versions of enemies from Stage 8. Priority order: armored enemies first (they soak damage without flinching), then jet pack enemies (mobile), then pig soldiers (easy to deal with but threatening in numbers). The headbutt clears paths; the grab-throw combination handles clustered enemies.

**Boss: The Dark Queen**

The Dark Queen is the final boss. She is a tall humanoid villain who uses magic and melee attacks. She has significantly more health than any previous boss.

**Phase 1 — Melee:**
She walks toward you slowly and delivers a powerful claw strike at close range. The strike has wide arc — back away the instant she raises her hand. She is most vulnerable during her walking approach, before the strike: hit her with jump-kicks during the walk. After the strike, she pauses — that is your window for 2–3 headbutts before she resumes walking.

She also spawns small electric orbs that float toward you. Jump them or punch them to destroy.

**Phase 2 — Tornado Form (when her health is about half):**
She transforms into a tornado and sweeps across the floor. The tornado does not hurt on contact — only the center core does. Stay at the edge of the screen and jump over her as she passes. After the sweep, she briefly reforms in human shape — hit her with a smash attack immediately. She reforms for only about 2 seconds before retransforming.

**Phase 3 — Combined (final quarter of health):**
She alternates between melee and tornado faster. The pattern: 2 melee sequences → tornado sweep → 2 melee → tornado sweep. Maintain your pattern: back off during melee approach, jump-kick during walking, wait for the sweep to pass in tornado form, smash immediately on reform.

Around 30–35 total hits across all phases. She is not the hardest boss if you are patient and stick to the pattern.

**Ending:** After the Dark Queen is defeated, the game shows the victory sequence: Pimple and the Princess rescued, the toads returning to their ship. You get an ending screen with a score tally. On the NES, there is no difficulty-based alternate ending — you see the same ending regardless of difficulty or whether you used continues.

## Tips & FAQ

**Q: How many continues do I get?**  
A: Three continues in the US NES version. Each continue restarts you at the beginning of the current stage with 3 lives. Running out of continues sends you to the title screen.

**Q: Is there a level select or warp?**  
A: There are four warps hidden in stages 1, 2, 3, and 5. The Wookie Hole (Stage 2) warp is the most useful — it skips to Stage 5, bypassing the Turbo Tunnel. In the Wookie Hole, near the beginning, there is a marked section of rope (slightly brighter or textured differently). Climb down past the 3rd turret platform and press A + B simultaneously while hanging. If you hit the warp point, you jump to Stage 5. The precise position is approximately 2/3 down the first descent.
