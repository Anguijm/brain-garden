# DuckTales — NES Walkthrough

**Developer:** Capcom | **Publisher:** Capcom | **Year:** 1989

---

## Introduction

DuckTales is a non-linear platformer developed by Capcom in 1989, based on the Disney animated series. You play as Scrooge McDuck, searching for five legendary treasures hidden across the globe. Unlike most NES platformers, you can tackle the five main stages in any order you choose, and your total score — represented as money collected — determines which of three endings you receive.

The game is short by modern standards, around 30–45 minutes for a first clear, but it rewards replaying once you know where the hidden treasures are. The core mechanic — the pogo bounce — is one of the most satisfying movement tools in NES history, and mastering it is the difference between struggling through stages and breezing through them.

Your three nephews (Huey, Dewey, and Louie) and Webby are scattered through the stages. Finding them unlocks hints about treasure locations and boss weaknesses. Gyro Gearloose provides you with gadgets in some stages. Launchpad McQuack is your exit — press Select at any time to call him and return to the stage select screen (your money is kept, but the stage resets).

## Controls

| Button | Action |
|--------|--------|
| D-Pad | Move left/right, duck (hold Down) |
| A | Jump |
| B (on ground) | Cane swing — hits enemies and breaks rocks directly ahead |
| Down + B (in air) | Pogo bounce — the central mechanic |
| Start | Pause |
| Select | Call Launchpad McQuack (return to stage select) |

---

## The Pogo Mechanic

The pogo bounce is the heart of the game. While airborne, hold Down on the D-Pad and press B. Scrooge plants his cane beneath him and launches back upward. Holding Down keeps the bounce going automatically each time he lands — you ride the cane like a pogo stick.

Why this matters:

- **Offense:** The pogo tip damages almost any enemy on contact from above. Walking into enemies or touching them from the sides kills Scrooge. The pogo is how you attack everything safely.
- **Traversal:** The pogo can break certain floor tiles (indicated by their cracked appearance), revealing treasure chests or passages below. It also lets you reach higher platforms by chaining bounces.
- **Defense:** Staying airborne on the pogo keeps you out of range of ground-level hazards and gives you control over where you land.

The single most common mistake new players make is walking into enemies. Almost nothing in this game should be approached on foot. Jump over, then pogo on the way down.

---

## The Cane Swing

Press B while standing on the ground. Scrooge swings his cane horizontally in front of him. This breaks rocks (which sometimes hide items or passages), knocks back enemies at the same height, and is the only way to hit some objects. It does not work in the air.

The cane swing is less useful than the pogo but essential for clearing blocked paths and grabbing certain items from rocks. When you see a rock, hit it — it usually contains something.

## Health and Items

Scrooge starts with three hit points, displayed as hearts in the top-left corner. Two additional hit points are hidden in the game (see Secrets section). You can find food items in treasure chests that restore health.

**Common treasure chest contents:**

| Item | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Ice cream cone | Restores 1 HP |
| Cake | Restores 1 HP |
| Scrooge doll | 1-Up (extra life) |
| Coin | Adds to money score |
| Small diamond | Adds \$500–\$1,000 to score |
| Large diamond | Adds \$1,000–\$5,000 to score |
| Purple diamond | Highest value diamond |
| Red ruby | High value gem |

Scrooge dolls (1-Ups) are rare and worth going out of your way for. The game has no continues, though emulation's save states remove this concern entirely.

## Stage Select and Recommended Order

From the map screen, you can enter any available stage. The **African Mines** are locked at the start — you need to retrieve a key from Transylvania first (a nephew in Transylvania hands it to you after you find him). Everything else is accessible immediately.

Recommended order for a first playthrough:

1. **Amazon** — easiest layout, good introduction to the pogo on standard terrain
2. **Transylvania** — get the African Mines key; contains a hidden HP upgrade
3. **African Mines** — now unlocked; underground mine setting
4. **Himalayas** — ice physics make it the trickiest regular stage
5. **Moon** — save for last; the most visually distinct and contains some of the best music on the NES
6. **Final Castle** — unlocks automatically after all five treasures are collected

If you're going for maximum score (the best ending), collect every chest you can find in each stage before finishing — money does not carry over from a stage you abandon via Launchpad, so finish each stage once you start hunting seriously.

## Full Walkthrough

### Stage 1: Amazon

**Setting:** Jungle temple  
**Treasure:** Scepter of the Incan King  
**Nephews/helpers:** Louie (gives you a hint about the boss)

The Amazon is the gentlest introduction to the game. The jungle has two main paths — upper and lower — and connects through vine sections. Most hazards are ground-based enemies: spiked bouncing balls, frog-like creatures that leap toward you, and piranha plants. All are handled the same way: approach from above, pogo.

**Layout overview:**
- From the start, head right. You'll pass two vine sections where you can swing between levels.
- The lower path has more treasure chests but more enemies.
- The upper path is faster but skips money.
- About two-thirds through, you'll find Louie, who tells you that the boss throws barrels.

**Hidden treasure:** Early in the stage, look for cracked floor tiles near the first horizontal platform section. Pogo through them to find a chest below.

**Boss: Pete**  
Pete (a large cat in overalls) stands on a platform and hurls wooden barrels at you in a downward arc. He doesn't move. Wait for him to raise his arm, then move to the side the barrel isn't aimed at. When the barrel has passed, jump forward and pogo Pete's head. He takes three pogo hits to defeat. The pattern is throw — step — throw — step — pogo. Do not attempt a cane swing; you need the elevated hit from above.

---

### Stage 2: Transylvania

**Setting:** Castle interior with multiple floors  
**Treasure:** Coin of the Lost Realm  
**Nephews/helpers:** Huey, Dewey, and Webby (spread through the stage)  
**Special:** Contains the African Mines key and a hidden HP upgrade

Transylvania is the most complex stage. The castle has three floors connected by staircases and ladders, and the boss door is locked behind three keys you must collect. The stage is populated by ghosts (which float toward you unpredictably), skeletons, and Beagle Boys.

**The three keys:**
- **Key 1:** Near the start of the stage, inside a chest in the main hall.
- **Key 2:** On the upper floor, past the ghost-heavy corridor. A nephew will point you toward it.
- **Key 3:** In the lower dungeon area, past the section with bone-throwing skeletons. The chest is partially hidden — use the cane swing on nearby rocks to clear the path.

Once you have all three keys, the boss door in the center of the castle opens.

**African Mines key:** One of the nephews (Huey) hands this to you automatically when you reach him. You cannot miss it as long as you find him.

**Hidden HP upgrade — the illusionary wall:**  
On the second floor of the castle, head all the way to the right. You will see a large coffin carved with "R.I.P." Jump onto the top of the coffin with a pogo bounce, then walk right. The wall here is an illusion — Scrooge passes straight through it. Inside the hidden room is a chest containing an extra heart, raising your maximum HP from 3 to 4.

**Boss: Magica De Spell**  
Magica is a witch who flies across the room and occasionally swoops low to cast spells. When she swoops down to your level, jump and pogo her as she passes. She flies at an unpredictable angle, so position yourself near the center of the room where you have the most reaction time. Three pogo hits. The cane swing cannot reach her.

---

### Stage 3: African Mines

**Setting:** Underground gold mine  
**Treasure:** Giant Diamond of Inner Earth  
**Nephews/helpers:** Dewey (gives map hint)

The mines are a vertical stage with lots of up-and-down movement via ladders and platforms. Enemies include stubby troll-like creatures that bounce toward you, ceiling-mounted spitters, and falling stalactites triggered by movement. Many of the ore rocks contain gems — hit every one with the cane swing.

**Layout overview:**
- Start on the upper level; descend through mine shafts via ladders.
- The treasure-dense lower section has several rocks in a row that all yield money items.
- Dewey is on a mid-level platform and warns you about the boss.
- The boss is at the bottom of the mine.

**Hidden treasure:** On the middle level, there is a section where a gap in the platforms seems impassable. From the left ledge, pogo-bounce across by using consecutive pogo hops — each tap of B gives you a fresh bounce even without landing on terrain if you maintain the input correctly. The chest on the far side contains a purple diamond.

**Boss: Flintheart Glomgold**  
Glomgold hops toward you with a fixed rhythm — three short hops, then he slides to a stop. When he slides, pogo him from above. He speeds up after the first hit. The pattern stays the same; just give yourself more room to react at the faster speed. Three hits.

---

### Stage 4: Himalayas

**Setting:** Snowy mountain  
**Treasure:** Lost Crown of Genghis Khan  
**Nephews/helpers:** Louie (upper path)

The Himalayas introduce ice physics: many floors are icy and Scrooge slides when he lands on them. You cannot stop quickly on ice, so plan your movement in longer arcs. The pogo is less affected — you still bounce vertically even on ice, but horizontal drift while airborne is exaggerated.

Enemies include snowball-rolling Yeti scouts, ice golems, and falling icicles. The icicles drop when Scrooge walks beneath them — listen for the cracking sound, which gives you a half-second to move.

**Layout overview:**
- The stage has two parallel paths from the start.
- Upper path (right fork): shorter, less money, leads directly to the boss.
- Lower path (left fork): longer, has multiple treasure chests and the second hidden HP upgrade.

**Hidden HP upgrade:** On the lower path, in the upper-right section of a large ice cavern, there is a chest tucked behind a cluster of ice blocks. Use the cane swing to break the blocks, then open the chest. This raises your maximum HP to 5 if you found the Transylvania one, or to 4 if you haven't.

**Boss: The Yeti**  
The Yeti stands in the center of the room and hurls large ice boulders in a high arc. Each boulder shatters into two smaller shards when it lands. The boulders always land at the same two distances from the Yeti — one close, one mid-range. Stand between these landing zones (the gap between the arcs) and wait. When the Yeti lowers his arms to pick up the next boulder, jump forward and pogo his head. He recoils and begins another throw. Three hits. The shard timing overlaps with your approach window, so move quickly once you jump in.

---

### Stage 5: Moon

**Setting:** Lunar surface and underground caverns  
**Treasure:** Green Cheese of Longevity (and Gold Plate in some versions)  
**Nephews/helpers:** Webby (gives Moon map)

The Moon is the most iconic stage in the game, partly because of its gravity-modified sections and partly because the music — composed by Yoshihiro Sakaguchi — is among the most celebrated NES compositions ever written. You will be hearing it in your head for days.

The Moon has two zones: the surface (normal gravity, alien enemies, low-gravity hops off certain launch pads) and the underground caverns (tighter spaces, more treasure).

**Surface section:**
- Move right across the lunar landscape. Alien enemies float toward you at varying heights; pogo anything at or below your jump apex.
- Look for green launch pads: stepping on them sends Scrooge flying much higher than a normal jump. These reach otherwise inaccessible platforms and treasure chests.
- Webby is near the mid-point of the surface section. She gives you a rough map of the underground.

**Underground caverns:**
- Enter via a passage at the right side of the surface.
- The caverns are maze-like with narrow vertical passages. The pogo is essential for navigating upward; the cane swing clears the rock walls.
- Several rocks contain the highest-value gems in the game (purple and red diamonds in close succession). Hit every rock.
- The boss is at the far end of the underground.

**Hidden treasure:** On the surface, there is a platform accessible only via a launch pad in combination with a pogo bounce at the peak of the launch. The chest on it contains a Scrooge doll (1-Up) and a large diamond.

**Boss: Alien Commander**  
The commander is a floating alien in a round ship. It moves back and forth across the top of the room in a sweeping arc, occasionally dipping low. When it dips to head height, jump and pogo it. Three hits. The danger is staying patient — it doesn't dip on a fixed interval, so don't anticipate. Watch for the downswing and react. If you move to the center of the room and stay there, you'll always be in range regardless of which side it dips from.

---

### Final Stage: McDuck Manor (Endgame Castle)

After collecting all five treasures, Launchpad takes you to the endgame castle. This is a linear gauntlet: no branching paths, no hidden chests. Every enemy type from the game appears here in combination.

The stage is a straight push from left to right through multiple floors, with increasing enemy density. You will face ghosts, skeletons, Beagle Boys, and ice creatures in the same corridors. Maintain pogo discipline — do not rush on foot.

**Boss: Dracula Duck**  
Dracula Duck is the final boss and has substantially more health than any stage boss — he takes five or six pogo hits to defeat rather than three. He moves across the room and periodically transforms into a bat form that swoops unpredictably. In standing form, pogo him from above. In bat form, stay back and wait for him to land before jumping in. Do not attempt the cane swing.

After the last hit, the ending sequence plays based on your total money score.

## Hidden Treasures and Money Guide

To reach the best ending (\$10,000,000+), you need to find as many hidden chests as possible and avoid wasting time. Key money sources:

| Location | Item | Approximate Value |
|----------|------|-------------------|
| Amazon — cracked floor early stage | Chest | \$1,000–\$2,000 |
| Transylvania — illusionary wall room | Extra HP + chest | +1 max HP |
| African Mines — pogo-gap far chest | Purple diamond | \$5,000 |
| Himalayas — lower path ice cavern | Extra HP chest | +1 max HP |
| Moon — surface launch pad platform | 1-Up + large diamond | 1-Up + \$3,000 |
| Any stage — rocks hit with cane swing | Gems, coins | Varies |

The Moon stage consistently has the highest gem density per minute of playtime. If you want to maximize score, clear the Moon underground section methodically and hit every rock.

## The Three Endings

Your ending is determined entirely by your money total when Dracula Duck is defeated.

- **Bad ending:** Very low score (near zero). Scrooge receives a single small coin purse. The game acknowledges that he has not enriched himself.
- **Normal ending:** Moderate score (typical first-playthrough result). A chest of coins and gems.
- **Best ending:** \$10,000,000 or more. Scrooge sits atop an enormous mountain of gold coins. This is the ending that matches the show's characterization of him as the world's richest duck.

On a first playthrough with moderate treasure hunting, most players finish with \$3,000,000–\$6,000,000. The best ending requires deliberately finding hidden chests and replaying the Moon stage's underground section.

## Secrets and Tips

**The bonus stage:**  
When your current money score has a 7 in the ten-thousands digit — for example, exactly \$70,000, \$170,000, or \$270,000 — call Launchpad by pressing Select. Instead of returning to the stage select screen, he takes you to a secret bonus stage loaded with treasure chests. This is difficult to trigger reliably but worth knowing exists.

**Time bonus glitch:**  
Each stage has a countdown timer; a time bonus is added to your score when the boss is defeated. If you land the killing pogo hit on a boss at the exact moment the timer reaches zero, the counter wraps around into corrupted values and awards approximately \$325,000 in bonus money. This is extremely difficult to do intentionally on original hardware but can be set up with emulation frame-advance tools.

**Prioritize the Moon:**  
The Moon stage has the best money-per-minute ratio in the game. Its underground section has multiple high-value gem rocks in close proximity. If you need to farm score for the best ending, the Moon is where to do it.

**Launchpad abort is safe:**  
Pressing Select and calling Launchpad keeps all money you've collected in that session — it does not reset your total. However, the stage resets completely, including enemy positions and treasure chests. Use this to replay stages for more money, but note that the ending stage (McDuck Manor) cannot be aborted once entered.

**Learn the pogo chain:**  
You can bounce continuously on the pogo even without hitting enemies or solid floors, as long as you maintain the Down + B input and keep pressing B at the apex of each bounce. This lets you cross small gaps and maintain elevation in open spaces. Practice this in the Amazon before attempting the Moon's underground narrow corridors.

**Enemy recycling:**  
Enemies respawn when you scroll the screen back toward them. This is rarely useful for score farming (they drop only small amounts) but is worth knowing if a room feels newly populated on a return pass.

## Version Notes

The North American NES release of DuckTales (1989) is the version reviewed here and the one in your ROM set (`Duck Tales (U) [!].nes`). A Japan-only Famicom release has minor differences in difficulty. The Remastered version (2013, WayForward) is a different game built around the same stages — it has its own secrets and is not covered here.
