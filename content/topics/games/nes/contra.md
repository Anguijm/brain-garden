# Contra (NES)

**Developer:** Konami | **Publisher:** Konami | **Year:** 1988

---

## Introduction

Contra arrived on the NES in February 1988 as a port of Konami's 1987 arcade game, and the home version is widely considered superior to the original. The arcade cabinet gave players three lives and pulled quarters. The NES version gave players the Konami Code, a two-player co-op mode that still holds up, and eight stages of run-and-gun combat tight enough that every death teaches you something.

The premise is threadbare: two commandos, Bill and Lance, assaulted an island base run by Red Falcon, an alien-influenced terrorist organization. The attraction is the weapons and the movement. Contra gives you directional shooting in eight directions, lets you hold down fire to auto-shoot, throws enemies at you from every angle, and rewards aggression over caution. Standing still gets you killed. Moving forward while shooting constantly gets you through.

The game is remembered for two things: the Konami Code and the two-player mode. Both are worth addressing directly. The Konami Code (Up Up Down Down Left Right Left Right B A Start) gives 30 lives on the title screen, which makes the game survivable for newcomers. The two-player simultaneous co-op is among the best on the NES — both players share lives, which creates interesting tension, but the gameplay is genuinely better with a partner because enemies that would overwhelm one player can be divided between two.

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move the character left/right. Hold Up while moving to climb sloped terrain without stopping. Hold Down to lie flat on the ground — useful for avoiding bullets that track at waist height.
- **A:** Jump. Hold A while jumping for a slightly higher arc. Can jump and shoot in all eight directions simultaneously.
- **B:** Fire. Hold B to autofire — essential for all combat after the first thirty seconds. Never tap B when you can hold it.
- **A + Down:** Drop through a bridge/platform. Used in side-scrolling sections to fall to a lower tier.
- **Up + B:** Shoot directly upward. Used constantly.

### Shooting Directions

You can fire in eight directions relative to your facing: left, right, up-left, up-right, up, down-left, down-right, and down. You cannot fire down while running on flat ground. To fire diagonally downward, you must be in mid-air. This matters for certain enemies who attack from below.

## The Konami Code

On the title screen, before pressing Start:

**Up Up Down Down Left Right Left Right B A Start** (one player)  
**Up Up Down Down Left Right Left Right B A Select Start** (two player)

This gives you 30 lives. It is not a cheat in any meaningful sense of the word — Contra is genuinely difficult, and the original arcade game was designed to consume quarters. Use it without guilt until you can clear the game without it.

## Weapons

Weapons drop from white Pill Boxes (the flying capsule-shaped enemies) when shot. Every weapon upgrades your current gun; picking up a different weapon replaces what you have. You lose your weapon on death and respawn with the default Rifle.

**Rifle (default):** Standard straight shot. Functional. Get a better weapon immediately.

**Machine Gun (M):** Higher fire rate than the Rifle with faster bullet travel. Significant upgrade over the default. Worth keeping if nothing better is available.

**Spread Gun (S):** Five simultaneous bullets in a spread pattern — straight ahead, 45 degrees up, 45 degrees down, and diagonally left and right relative to the facing direction. The best weapon in the game by a significant margin. It clears crowds, damages bosses faster than any other weapon, and its coverage means aiming precisely matters less. Every experienced Contra player prioritizes keeping the Spread Gun above all else. Never drop it for anything.

**Fireball (F):** Fires a bouncing wave of fire that travels along the ground and curves upward at walls. Useful against bosses but less reliable than the Spread Gun for general combat because the projectile path is harder to predict. Worth using until Spread Gun is available.

**Laser (L):** A thin beam that penetrates through enemies rather than stopping at the first contact. High damage against bosses in direct line-of-sight situations. The catch: it fires slowly and only one beam exists on screen at a time (you cannot overlap shots). Against crowds it underperforms compared to Spread Gun. Against single boss components in a direct sightline, it is competitive.

**Rapid Fire (R):** Increases your bullet travel speed and fire rate dramatically. Not a weapon per se — it applies to whatever weapon you currently hold. Picking up R with a Spread Gun creates the game's best possible configuration. Less useful for general play without a good underlying weapon.

**Barrier (B):** A temporary shield that makes you invincible for several seconds. Does not replace your weapon. A short-term lifesaver, worth grabbing when available, but do not reorganize your route around finding one.

## Stage Overview

### Stage 1: Jungle

A side-scrolling stage. The first stage introduces the game's mechanics with manageable enemy density. Enemies approach from the right side of the screen and from the ground. The Pill Box carrying the Spread Gun appears early — prioritize it.

The boss is a cannon turret mounted on a wall, flanked by two rotating guns. Shoot the central cannon mouth directly. The flanking guns rotate; shoot them when they face you. The Spread Gun trivializes this fight.

### Stage 2: Base 1 (3D Base Entry)

The first of the "3D corridor" stages — a top-down forced-scroll perspective. Bill or Lance runs toward the screen while enemies run at you from the back wall. The goal in all Base stages is to shoot the glowing sensors (orange circles) to blow open the door panels, and then destroy the core inside.

The sensors explode with splash damage when hit, so position yourself to avoid the blast. There are always eight sensors arranged in two doors plus a final core room. The pattern for every Base stage: shoot sensors, advance, repeat, destroy core.

Base stages are the most forgiving in the game. The fixed perspective means enemies have limited attack angles and the Spread Gun clears rooms efficiently.

### Stage 3: Waterfall

A side-scrolling vertical stage — Bill and Lance climb up through a waterfall setting while enemies attack from above and from platforms on both sides. The screen scrolls upward and you control the scroll rate by advancing or holding position.

Key enemy type: soldiers rappelling down ropes from the top of the screen. Shoot them while they descend; they fall freely and move faster if you let them reach the ground. The Spread Gun catches multiple rope soldiers simultaneously.

The boss is a series of rotating gun emplacements mounted on a wall. Shoot from below into the weak point (usually an exposed orange sensor). Stay mobile.

### Stage 4: Base 2

The second 3D corridor base. Same structure as Base 1 but with more sensors, faster enemies, and a final room with turrets that rotate toward you. Same strategy: spread gun, shoot sensors from the sides to avoid blast radius, advance.

### Stage 5: Snowfield

A side-scrolling stage in a winter setting. Enemy soldiers now wear white camo and blend with the background more than the jungle enemies. The stage introduces soldiers who leap from below the screen edge and from the tops of hills. The movement is faster here.

The boss is a large gun battery with multiple gun ports. Destroy the gun ports first to reduce incoming fire, then target the central emitter. The Spread Gun handles this efficiently by covering multiple ports simultaneously.

### Stage 6: Base 3

The third 3D corridor base. This one introduces enemy soldiers who charge directly at you (rather than shooting from distance) and boss guards positioned around sensors. The corridor rooms are longer. Same sensor-and-core approach, but be ready to jump over the charging soldiers.

### Stage 7: Ice Field

Side-scrolling across a glacial terrain. The Ice Field is considered the hardest non-final stage in the game. Enemy density increases significantly, projectiles are faster, and the terrain features platforms over bottomless chasms that will kill you if you fall regardless of remaining lives.

Key section: a stretch with firing pylons on both sides of the screen. The Spread Gun covers both sides simultaneously. Machine Gun or Laser requires more precise targeting of the pylons in sequence. If you lose your weapon here, the difficulty spikes sharply.

The boss is a pair of tanks plus a final commander. Destroy the tanks first (shoot at the treads — the top is armored), then the commander.

### Stage 8: Alien Lair

The final stage. Two sections: a side-scrolling run through the lair's exterior, followed by the final boss room.

**Alien Lair Exterior:** Fast enemies, tight corridors, limited cover. This is the stage where inexperienced players exhaust their life supply. Hold B continuously. Keep moving right. Do not stop to assess — the enemies spawn faster than you can safely pause.

**Final Boss:** A screen-sized alien construct with multiple components. Attack in this order:
1. Destroy the two side pods (outer components, vulnerable from below)
2. Destroy the eye on each side wall (they fire tracking shots while active)
3. Destroy the two brain cores — the large pulsing components visible after the eyes are gone
4. The alien head appears as the final form — dodge the projectile pattern and shoot directly into the mouth

The Spread Gun is mandatory for the final boss. Without it, the boss's surface coverage makes precise targeting of each component extremely slow and attrition damage will likely run your lives dry. If you lost the Spread Gun before reaching the final boss, you will typically need the extra lives from the Konami Code to have enough attempts to relearn the pattern.

## General Strategy

### Aggression Is Defense

Standing still in Contra is almost always wrong. Enemies continuously spawn and their projectiles track your position — if you stay put, projectile density increases. Moving forward reduces screen pressure because the screen can only hold a finite number of enemies, and advancement pushes them off the left edge.

The one exception is during boss fights where the arena doesn't scroll — there, controlled positioning near a safe spot beats constant movement.

### Spread Gun Preservation

When you have the Spread Gun, do not die. This sounds circular, but it changes your behavior. With the Spread Gun you take calculated risks to advance; without it you play conservative and try to find another Power Pill carrier. If a power-up appears while you hold the Spread Gun, the only upgrade worth taking is Rapid Fire — everything else is a downgrade.

### Two-Player Coordination

In two-player, coordinate who takes each enemy type. One player handles ground-level threats; one handles aerial. Avoid walking into the same spot because both players die to the same projectile. The shared lives pool means one reckless player can end runs for both — communicate before doing anything risky.

### Memorize the Pill Box Locations

White Pill Boxes appear at fixed screen positions each time through the game. After two or three playthroughs you will know exactly where to look for the Spread Gun on Stage 1 and how to get to it quickly without dying in the approach. That knowledge is worth more than any in-session tip.

## Deaths and Respawning

When you die, you respawn at a fixed point on the current screen (side-scrolling) or at the beginning of the current room (Base stages). You respawn with the Rifle regardless of what weapon you had. If co-op partners both die simultaneously — or the last remaining player dies with no lives left — it is Game Over.

In two-player, you share lives with your partner. If one player dies but lives remain, they respawn immediately. This means a highly-skilled partner can carry a weaker one through most of the game by maintaining the life reserve.
