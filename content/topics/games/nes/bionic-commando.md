# Bionic Commando (NES) — Complete Walkthrough

**Developer:** Capcom | **Publisher:** Capcom | **Release:** 1988 (US)

---

## Introduction

Bionic Commando is one of the most mechanically distinctive games on the NES, and the mechanic is a constraint: you cannot jump. Not at all. Not even a little. Ladd Spencer's bionic arm is the only way to gain elevation, cross gaps, or reach any platform above the floor you're standing on. Every screen in the game has to be read through that lens before you move.

The payoff is that once you internalize the arm — its reach, its arc, its timing — the game opens into something surprisingly fluid. You swing across pits, climb building interiors, yank enemies toward you, and chain grapples through compound obstacles that would be trivial in any other game. Players who try to treat it like a normal platformer die constantly. Players who commit to the arm find a game with a movement grammar unlike anything else on the system.

The story: Ladd Spencer is a soldier in the Federal Army sent into Albatross-occupied territory to rescue Super Joe — the hero of Capcom's earlier game Commando — and neutralize Operation Albatross before the enemy can resurrect a devastating weapon. You communicate with allied command via radio, spy on enemy transmissions, and fight your way through twelve areas and a final stronghold. Read your transmissions. They matter.

## Controls

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| D-Pad Left/Right | Move |
| D-Pad Down | Crouch |
| D-Pad Up | Aim arm directly upward |
| A Button | Fire/retract bionic arm |
| B Button | Fire current weapon |
| Start | Pause |
| Select | Cycle weapons |

**Arm firing direction:** The arm fires at an angle based on which direction you hold on the d-pad when you press A. Holding Up fires straight overhead. Holding Up+Right or Up+Left fires diagonally in that direction. Neutral fires straight ahead horizontally (useful for grabbing enemies). The arm has a fixed range — it will not attach if the target is too far.

**Arm retraction:** After attaching, press A again (or hold Up) to retract and climb toward the attachment point. Release and you drop. You can also swing by releasing and re-firing mid-arc.

## The Bionic Arm — Core Mechanics

Everything in Bionic Commando comes back to the arm. Here is what you need to know before Area 1:

**Attachment points:** The arm attaches to any solid surface — ceilings, walls, the undersides of platforms. It will not attach to empty air or pits. Before every gap, scan for what's above you and to the sides.

**Swing momentum:** After attaching and releasing, you swing forward on the arc. Fire the arm again at the top of your forward swing to chain to the next attachment point. This is the standard traversal rhythm for wide gaps: attach, swing, re-fire at the apex, attach again.

**Reaching higher:** You cannot jump to reach the arm's attachment point — you can only climb by retracting. But if you fire diagonally and pull up, you gain height. Practice firing up-diagonal to ledge edges to climb platforms that look unreachable.

**Using the arm in combat:** A horizontal arm shot grabs enemies in your path and pulls them toward you. This is how you deal with soldiers at close range when shooting would be slower. Against grouped enemies, grab one and let the collision damage the others. You can also use a grabbed soldier as a brief shield.

**Getting caught on ledges:** If your arm attaches and you swing but hit a wall mid-arc, you'll stop and cling to the wall surface. From a wall-cling you can fire the arm again — useful for climbing narrow shafts.

**The arm cannot reach past pits from too far back:** If you're standing far from a gap, the arm may not reach the ceiling on the other side. Move as close to the edge as the ground allows before firing.

## Weapons

Press Select to cycle between weapons you're carrying. You can carry multiple weapons, but your ammo for each is separate. The Normal Gun has unlimited ammo. Everything else does not.

**Normal Gun:** Your starting weapon. Single shots, maximum two bullets on screen simultaneously. Unlimited ammo. Effective at long range, underpowered up close compared to what you'll find later.

**Wide Cannon (Area 4 reward):** Fires three bullets simultaneously — one straight ahead and two at 45-degree angles upward and downward. Doesn't travel as far as the Normal Gun, but the spread makes it excellent against enemies at multiple elevations. You will use this through the midgame.

**Rapid Fire Device (Area 3 reward):** Upgrades the Normal Gun into a machine gun — rapid continuous fire as long as B is held. Does not consume separate ammo; it modifies the Normal Gun behavior. Extremely useful in tight enemy rooms.

**Rocket Launcher (Area 5 reward):** The single most important weapon in the game. High damage, required against later bosses and certain barriers. Ammo is limited, so conserve it for fights that demand it. Resupply at neutral areas.

**Signal Gun:** Found in certain areas. Fires a flare that illuminates darkened sections of stages. Situational — you'll know when you need it.

## Items

**Ladd Spencer Doll:** Extra life. Maximum one per area. Delivered by allied airdrop (parachuted item box — shoot or arm-grab to collect).

**Energy Canteen:** Restores health. Found in item boxes and neutral areas.

**Bulletproof Vest / Crucifix Charm (Area 2 reward):** Absorbs a single bullet hit. Passive protection until triggered.

**Rapid Fire Device (Area 3 reward):** See Weapons above.

**Permit (Area 6 reward):** Required to enter Neutral Area 14, which contains something necessary to enter the second set of areas (7–12). You cannot reach the endgame without this.

**Communicators:** Colored radio devices used to access communication rooms in different area sets. Red (α) for Areas 1, 4, 5. Green (β) for Areas 2, 3, 6. Blue (γ) for Areas 7, 8, 9. Orange (δ) for Areas 10, 11, 12. You find communicators in neutral supply areas.

## The Overworld Map

The world is displayed as a top-down tactical map. Your starting areas are in the lower portion; the Albatross stronghold is in the upper section. Areas are numbered and colored. You navigate between them by helicopter along road routes.

**Enemy interception:** On many routes between areas, Albatross helicopters intercept you. You fight a side-scrolling aerial battle — use your current weapon to shoot down enemy helicopters and soldiers while avoiding their fire. Survive the interception to reach your destination. These fights get harder as you push deeper into enemy territory.

**Neutral Areas (13–19):** Safe supply zones — no enemies, no combat. You can resupply ammo, pick up communicators, talk to allied contacts, and recover health. Visit them between missions. Key neutral areas:

- **Neutral Area 13:** Basic resupply. Accessible early.
- **Neutral Area 14:** Requires the Permit from Area 6. Contains something essential for accessing the final areas — do not skip this.
- **Neutral Area 15:** Has the Blue (γ) and Orange (δ) communicators needed for the second half of the game.
- **Neutral Area 16:** Has the Green (β) communicator.
- **Neutral Areas 17–19:** Resupply stations.

## Communication Rooms

Inside most areas, you will find a communication console. Walk up to the center of the console. You are given two options:

**Communicate:** Official channels. Allied transmissions, mission briefings, updates from your handlers. Always informative, sometimes essential. These tell you what you are looking for in the current area and what items or contacts to find.

**Wiretap:** You splice into enemy communications and listen in on Albatross transmissions. The intelligence can be critical — item locations, enemy movement, and one transmission near the end of the game reveals how to kill the final boss. The risk: there is a chance the enemy detects the wiretap. If caught, two knife soldiers charge from both sides and enemy troops parachute in until you exit the room. The detection risk increases in later areas.

**Key transmissions you must not miss:**

- **Area 5 wiretap:** An Albatross soldier reports that the Rocket Launcher is somewhere in the area. Cross-reference with the ally communication to know where to look.
- **Area 9 communicate:** Intel on Super Joe's location and the layout of Area 12.
- **Area 12 / Final Area transmissions:** Multiple contacts confirm where Super Joe is being held. More importantly, enemy transmissions and allied contacts both hint — and one says it plainly — that the enemy commander's head is his weak point. When his helmet comes off, shoot him in the head. This is not flavor text. This is the kill condition.

## Recommended Area Order

The game does not force a fixed sequence after the starting areas, but the item dependencies create a natural progression. Following this order ensures you always have the gear you need:

**1 → 4 → 5 → Neutral 16 → 2 → 3 → 6 → Neutral 14 → Neutral 15 → 8 → 9 → 7 → 10 → 11 → 12 → Final Area**

The reason for this order:
- Area 4 gives you the Wide Cannon before areas that need it.
- Area 5 gives you the Rocket Launcher, the best weapon, before the harder areas.
- Area 3 gives the Rapid Fire Device, useful from the midgame on.
- Area 6's Permit unlocks Neutral Area 14, which unlocks 7–12.
- Areas 8 and 9 are more manageable than 7, which has a harder barrier puzzle.

## Area-by-Area Walkthrough

### Area 1 — Introduction

**Communicator needed:** Red (α) — you start with it.

You drop into the area by parachute at the lower left. The stage teaches the arm: the first obstacle is a barrel you cannot walk over and cannot jump. Fire the arm at the ceiling above and to the right of the barrel, pull up, and swing over. That is the game in miniature.

The interior section has elevators — grapple to the ceiling to cross the elevator shaft gaps while the platform moves. The communication room is near the end; use it. An ally transmission describes the general situation and confirms your mission target.

**Boss:** A soldier commander in an armored suit. The Rocket Launcher is the cleanest kill; if you don't have it yet (you won't on your first visit here), use the Wide Cannon or Normal Gun. Aim for the torso and keep moving.

**Reward:** Mission objective item. Proceed to Area 4.

---

### Area 4 — Wide Cannon

**Communicator needed:** Red (α).

More complex interior layout than Area 1. The communication room here has a useful allied contact. The key item — the Wide Cannon — is in this stage. Look for the item drop and arm-grab it.

The Wide Cannon's three-spread makes the next several areas significantly easier, especially rooms where enemies are above or below your current elevation.

**Boss:** Mid-tier commander. Wide Cannon handles it well — the spread hits even when the boss is moving vertically.

---

### Area 5 — Rocket Launcher

**Communicator needed:** Red (α).

The most important area in the first half of the game. The Rocket Launcher is here. The wiretap in this area hints at its location. The ally communication confirms: get the Rocket Launcher before you try anything else in this stage.

This area is also the first time you may pick up a transmission involving Super Joe — a faint signal that sounds like someone trying to establish their position. It is Super Joe. Note the frequency.

**Boss:** First tough commander. Use the Rocket Launcher. This is partly a tutorial for the weapon — you will need it for every boss from here forward. Fire, take cover, re-engage. The boss does not move quickly enough to dodge sustained rocket fire.

---

### Area 2 — Crucifix Charm

**Communicator needed:** Green (β) — get it from Neutral Area 16 first.

After picking up the green communicator from Neutral Area 16, Area 2 opens. The layout introduces longer grapple chains — rooms where you need three or four arm fires in sequence to cross a stretch without touching the floor. The quicksand mechanic is in Area 3, not here, but this area has terrain that punishes slow movement.

**Reward:** Crucifix charm. Passive protection against one hit. Equip it immediately.

---

### Area 3 — Rapid Fire Device

**Communicator needed:** Green (β).

This area introduces quicksand — floor sections that look passable but will slowly swallow Ladd if he stands in them too long. You can run through quicksand, but stopping is fatal. Grapple to the ceiling above quicksand sections and swing across rather than risking it.

**Reward:** Rapid Fire Device. This converts your Normal Gun to rapid-fire. If you've been conserving other ammo, this makes the Normal Gun viable again in non-boss situations.

---

### Area 6 — The Permit

**Communicator needed:** Green (β).

Area 6 is the gate between the game's two halves. The Permit you get here is not an in-stage weapon or upgrade — it is literally a permission slip that unlocks Neutral Area 14. Without Area 14, you cannot enter Areas 7 through 12. Do not skip the communication room here; the transmission confirms what the permit opens.

After Area 6, visit Neutral Area 14 (now accessible) and then Neutral Area 15 for the blue and orange communicators.

---

### Area 8 — Second Half Opens

**Communicator needed:** Blue (γ).

The second half of the game significantly increases enemy density and arm-challenge complexity. Area 8 is a reasonable entry point — slightly easier than 9 and much easier than 7. Conserve Rocket Launcher ammo for bosses; use the Rapid Fire Device for crowds.

Communication room wiretap in this area reveals Albatross movement toward the inner stronghold. Ally contact confirms Super Joe is alive and in the inner complex.

---

### Area 9 — Setup for Area 7

**Communicator needed:** Blue (γ).

Complete Area 9 before Area 7. Area 9 provides a reward that you need to break the barrier in Area 7. The specific order is non-obvious but important — without the Area 9 reward, Area 7's barrier section is impassable.

The ally communication here gives detailed intel on the Area 12 layout, including the two electric barriers and the power systems that control them.

---

### Area 7 — The Barrier

**Communicator needed:** Blue (γ).

Area 7 has an electric barrier that blocks progress. The Rocket Launcher or the reward from Area 9 is required to destroy it. If you completed Area 9 first, you have what you need. Fire directly into the barrier mechanism until it goes down.

The interior sections here are the most arm-intensive of the second half — narrow vertical shafts that require precise upward grappling with no room for error. Take it one section at a time.

---

### Area 10 — Approach to the Stronghold

**Communicator needed:** Orange (δ) — from Neutral Area 15.

The final three areas form the Albatross inner stronghold. Enemy interception on the overworld becomes more frequent and more dangerous. Stock up on Rocket Launcher ammo at every neutral area before proceeding.

Area 10 increases arm precision requirements again. The wiretap here is worth the detection risk — enemy transmissions in this area discuss the inner command structure and hint at what lies ahead.

---

### Area 11 — Holding Position

**Communicator needed:** Orange (δ).

Similar difficulty to Area 10. The ally communication here mentions Super Joe's exact holding location in Area 12. You're close.

---

### Area 12 — Super Joe

**Communicator needed:** Orange (δ).

The final main area. Area 12 is divided by two electric barriers, each powered by a separate power system in the stage. Destroy both power systems — they are guarded but reachable — before trying to push to Super Joe. The barriers will not come down otherwise.

Super Joe is held in a room in this area. Communicate with him; he gives you intel on the final structure, the layout of the Albatross command chamber, and — critically — he corroborates what the transmissions have been building toward about MasterD's weakness.

After rescuing Super Joe (communication, not physical escort), push toward the exit. The boss here is the hardest commander fight in the game. Use the Rocket Launcher, stay mobile, and save enough energy for what comes next.

---

### Final Area — The Albatross Command

The final area is a direct push to MasterD. No communicator is required — the door is open. The layout is the most arm-demanding in the game. Energy conservation matters; if you took significant damage in Area 12, visit a neutral area if you can.

The path is largely linear but requires precision grappling over large open sections with enemy fire coming from multiple angles. Clear enemies before crossing major gaps when possible — a hit mid-swing often means falling into a pit.

**MasterD (Final Boss):**

MasterD is the Albatross supreme commander, referred to in the Japanese release by his actual name, censored for Western audiences. He stands at the far end of the command chamber.

Phase 1: He is armored. Fire Rocket Launcher shots into his body. He will take damage and move. Continue firing. The armor degrades.

Phase 2: His helmet comes off, exposing his head. This is the moment every transmission has been building toward. Stop aiming for the body. Shoot him in the HEAD. The Normal Gun works if you're out of rockets — aim high, fire quickly. His head is the kill point and only the head.

When the shot connects with enough accumulated damage, the cutscene plays. MasterD's head explodes in one of the most memorably shocking endings on the NES — deliberate, animated, and nothing like what any contemporary game was doing with a villain's defeat. The Western release toned down the context (removed Third Reich iconography, changed the name) but left the ending intact.

You get the briefing screen, the credits, and a congratulations message. The game returns to the title screen.

## Boss Strategies — General Principles

Every boss in the game follows a similar pattern:
- They move back and forth or up and down in a limited space
- They fire projectiles on a pattern you can learn in about thirty seconds
- The Rocket Launcher kills them in significantly fewer hits than any other weapon
- Getting hit while attacking them costs resources you need — prioritize not getting hit over trying to maximize DPS

Specific tip that applies everywhere: position yourself at medium range. Too close and their melee or close-range fire hits you before you can react. Too far and your shots may not connect cleanly. The Rocket Launcher has enough range that medium distance is always safe.

## Secrets, Codes, and Advanced Tips

**Airdrop item boxes:** During stages, watch for item boxes parachuting down from allied aircraft. They are easy to miss if you're focused on enemies. Shoot the box or arm-grab it. Contains extra lives, energy, or item upgrades. Maximum one extra life per area per visit.

**Resupply routing:** The neutral areas refill specific resources. Before any of the final three areas, make a resupply run. Running out of Rocket Launcher ammo on a boss with only the Normal Gun is survivable but slow and painful.

**Wiretap risk management:** Early-game wiretaps are low risk — detection chance is low. Late-game wiretaps (Areas 10–12) carry meaningfully higher detection risk. The intel is often worth it, but have your weapon ready for the knife soldiers if you're caught.

**Enemy grab for crowd control:** In tight rooms with multiple enemies, the horizontal arm grab is often safer than shooting. Grab the nearest enemy, let the impact stagger anyone behind them, then shoot the survivors. This rhythm keeps you from taking hits from both sides simultaneously.

**Don't rush outdoor sections:** The outdoor portions of stages — where you're moving across larger open terrain — are less dangerous than they look if you keep moving forward. Standing still to aim is where players take unnecessary hits. Fire while moving, grapple while firing, keep the momentum.

**The interception fights:** On the overworld, enemy helicopters become a serious threat in the second half. The Wide Cannon's spread makes these fights faster; the Rapid Fire Device's sustained fire also helps. If you're running low on everything, these fights drain more resources than you gain. Learn the patterns — the helicopter positions are not random.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**I can't reach the ceiling on this one section. What am I missing?**
You need to be closer to the gap before firing. Move right up to the ledge edge, then fire up-diagonal. If you still can't reach, look for a different route — some sections have an alternate path above or below that requires a different grapple approach.

**I keep getting caught on wiretaps in the late areas. Is it worth it?**
The final few areas have transmissions worth hearing, but the detection risk is real. Accept that you may get caught and be ready to fight out of the room immediately. If you've already completed the critical Area 12 transmissions, later wiretaps are optional flavor.

**I got to MasterD and I can't kill him. I'm shooting him but he won't die.**
Stop shooting his body. When his helmet is off, only head shots count. Adjust your aim upward and fire at head level. Normal Gun works if you're out of rockets — it just takes more shots.

**What's in Neutral Area 14 that's so important?**
Neutral Area 14 contains an item (related to a communicator or access credential) required to open the routes into the second set of areas (7–12). Without visiting it after getting the Permit from Area 6, you will find those area roads closed to you on the overworld.

**Can I go back and replay areas?**
Yes. You can re-enter any cleared area from the overworld. Items that were one-time pickups will not regenerate, but enemy enemies and resupply from neutral areas remains available.
