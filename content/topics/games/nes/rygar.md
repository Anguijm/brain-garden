# Rygar — NES Walkthrough

*Tecmo, 1987. Action-platformer with RPG stat growth and open-world exploration.*

---

## Introduction

Rygar on the NES is not the game its arcade namesake suggests. Where the arcade original is a linear side-scroller, the NES version is something stranger and more interesting: a semi-open action game that lets you roam across a connected overworld, build your character through combat, and unlock new regions as you find new tools. It predates Metroid by about a year in concept — you are exploring a world where your weapon and your stats gate your access to new areas — and it does this without a map, without save states, and with enemy spawns that reset every time you leave a room.

The realm of Argool has been conquered by the demon lord Ligar, who sits in a floating castle beyond the reach of any ordinary warrior. You are Rygar, a legendary warrior resurrected from death to fight back. Your weapon is the Diskarmor — a bladed shield on a chain — and your power grows with every enemy you destroy. The game ends when you reach Ligar and kill him. Getting there requires finding five Indora gods, each of whom guards a tool or blessing that opens the next section of the world.

There is no password system and no save battery. You play through in a single session, or you continue from the area you died in (continuing resets your stats to a minimum threshold, which is a serious setback late in the game — avoid dying). A full run for a knowledgeable player takes about 90 minutes to two hours. First-timers should expect longer.

## Controls

| Button | Action |
|--------|--------|
| D-Pad | Move left/right; duck (down); climb (up on ropes/ledges) |
| B | Attack with Diskarmor (long range by default) |
| Down + B | Short-range Diskarmor strike |
| A | Jump |
| A + B | Jump and attack simultaneously |
| Start | Pause; open stat/item screen |
| Select | Cycle through equipped sub-items |

The grappling hook, once found, fires automatically when you walk into a ledge-grab point and press up. You do not need to switch to it manually.

## The Diskarmor

The Diskarmor is your only weapon. It is a bladed disk on a long chain that Rygar hurls in an arc. Understanding how it works is the foundation of surviving.

**Long-range attack (B alone):** The disk flies forward and arcs slightly downward before returning. This hits enemies at screen-crossing range and is your default attack. It is safer than short range because it keeps enemies at a distance.

**Short-range attack (Down + B):** Rygar slams the disk directly in front of or behind him with a tight, fast motion. This does more damage per hit than the long-range throw. Use it on bosses when you are close enough to land it reliably — the damage advantage is significant.

**Hitting multiple enemies:** The Diskarmor can pass through enemies on its outward arc, hitting several in a line. Position yourself to take advantage of tight enemy clusters.

**Crouching attacks:** When enemies come at you low to the ground, crouch (hold Down) and press B. The disk strikes at a lower angle and catches enemies that your standing attack would sail over.

**Damage intake:** Getting hit knocks Rygar back slightly but does not interrupt his attack animation. You can absorb a hit and keep attacking, which matters against fast bosses.

## The Tone and Last Systems

Rygar has no experience level in the traditional sense. Instead, killing enemies raises two values permanently: **Tone** and **Last**.

### Tone (Attack Power)

Tone starts at 30. Every enemy killed raises Tone by some amount (stronger enemies raise it more). Higher Tone means each Diskarmor strike deals more damage. The cap is 4095 Tone, which is what you need to have a realistic chance against Ligar. You will not reach the cap through casual play — deliberate grinding is required before the final area.

There is no visible indicator of how much damage you are dealing. You can estimate your power by how quickly bosses lose health. If Ligar seems to take no damage, you need more Tone.

### Last (Maximum Health)

Last starts at 10. As it accumulates, your health bar grows. Every 48 Last points adds one health unit, up to a maximum of 12 health units. The starting health is 3 units. Getting all 12 requires significant Last accumulation — you will not reach the cap from normal play either.

More health matters most against Ligar, who hits hard. Aim for at least 8–10 health units before the floating castle.

**Key rule:** Death is expensive. When you continue after dying, your Tone and Last drop to a set minimum. If you have spent hours grinding to high stats and die in the floating castle without reaching Ligar, you may need to re-grind before you can realistically win. Use save states in emulation to avoid this entirely.

## Mind Points and Magic

Killing certain enemies drops star-shaped items that give you **Mind Points**. These power three spells you can use on demand:

| Spell | Cost | Effect |
|-------|------|--------|
| Power-Up | 3 points | Dramatically increases Diskarmor speed, range, and damage for the rest of the current life |
| Attack & Assail | 5 points | Your next 10 attacks flash white and hit every enemy on screen simultaneously |
| Recover | 7 points | Restores your health to full |

**Use Recover before you die, not after.** Mind Points drop when you continue. Save 7 points whenever you are low on health in a dangerous area.

**Power-Up is the most transformative spell.** Activated before a boss fight, it turns the Diskarmor into a near-projectile weapon with enormous range and faster return speed. If you have 3 Mind Points going into Sagila or Ligar, use it.

## World Overview

Rygar's world has two modes: **side-scrolling zones** where you fight through rooms, and **overhead traversal areas** where you walk across a map in a top-down perspective. The overhead areas connect the zones and contain safe rooms with Indora gods.

The major regions, in rough encounter order:

**Garloz** — The overhead starting area. Grass platforms, relatively few hazards. The path splits in multiple directions from here, but most are blocked until you have the right item.

**Gran Mountain / Ligar's Side** — The initial side-scrolling area you enter from the starting screen. Moderately difficult early game. Gets you moving toward the first Indora encounter.

**Valley of Rolsa** — Opens after getting the Grappling Hook. Features traversal sections requiring the hook to cross gaps.

**Eruga's Forest** — A side-scrolling zone with dense enemies. Eruga is the first true boss. The Wind Pulley (also called the Pulley) is the reward.

**Sagila's Cave / Mt. Primevil** — A challenging underground zone leading to Sagila. The Crossbow is the reward for surviving it.

**Dorago's Palace** — An overhead zone with pit hazards, reachable only by using the Crossbow to create a rope bridge to the floating island. Dorago guards the Indora's Armor, which enables you to recover health in safe rooms.

**Lapis** — A connecting zone between Dorago's Palace and the final approach.

**Floating Castle** — Ligar's domain. The hardest zone in the game. No checkpoints (without save states). Ligar himself is at the end.

## All Key Items and Where to Find Them

Five Indora gods give you the tools to progress. Each is found inside a specific zone, in a room you must reach and enter.

### 1. Grappling Hook (Locket)
**Zone:** Garloz / first Indora encounter  
**What it does:** Fires automatically when you approach a wall or ledge anchor point. You swing or climb. Unlocks the Valley of Rolsa and most subsequent areas.  
**How to reach it:** From the starting area, follow the overhead path in Garloz to the first glowing room (safe rooms glow). Enter and speak with the god inside.

### 2. Wind Pulley
**Zone:** Eruga's Forest, after defeating Eruga  
**What it does:** Allows you to traverse rope lines and certain overhead cable sections.  
**How to reach it:** Enter Eruga's Forest through the Garloz overhead path (requires Grappling Hook for some sections). Fight through the forest zone and defeat Eruga.

### 3. Crossbow
**Zone:** Sagila's Cave, after defeating Sagila  
**What it does:** Fires a bolt that creates an anchor point, enabling you to create a rope bridge to Dorago's Palace. Also functions as a ranged attack option in some rooms.  
**How to reach it:** Travel through the Mt. Primevil approach and through Sagila's Cave. Defeat Sagila.

### 4. Indora's Armor (Coat of Arms)
**Zone:** Dorago's Palace, after defeating Dorago  
**What it does:** Allows you to restore health in the glowing safe rooms. Without this item, safe rooms offer advice but no healing. This is important — without it, you cannot recover health at all during the run except through the Recover spell.  
**How to reach it:** Use the Crossbow to bridge to Dorago's floating island. Navigate Dorago's overhead palace and defeat Dorago.

### 5. Indora's Bow
**Zone:** A later Indora shrine, approaching the Floating Castle  
**What it does:** Significantly increases the Diskarmor's attack power beyond what Tone alone provides. Required to deal meaningful damage to Ligar.  
**How to reach it:** After Dorago's Palace, follow the path through Lapis toward the Floating Castle. The shrine is on the approach.

### Sage Tablets
Scattered throughout safe rooms. Each tablet reveals a piece of information: where the next item is, what a boss's weakness is, or how to navigate a tricky section. Read every one. Some are cryptic; none are misleading.

## Recommended Route

This order minimizes backtracking and keeps you at a useful stat level for each zone.

**Step 1 — Leave the starting area and enter the side-scrolling zone.** Kill everything. You want to raise Tone from the start. Do not rush past enemies in the early game; the grinding is less painful here than it will be later.

**Step 2 — Navigate Garloz overhead to the first Indora shrine.** Get the Grappling Hook. Return to the overhead map and you will see new paths you could not take before.

**Step 3 — Find and enter Eruga's Forest.** This is the hardest section early-game because Eruga hits harder than anything before him. Before entering, grind in Garloz's side-scrolling zone until your health bar has at least 5 units. Defeat Eruga. Get the Wind Pulley.

**Step 4 — Travel to Sagila's Cave.** The cave is long and enemy-dense. Keep health above half using Recover if needed. Defeat Sagila. Get the Crossbow.

**Step 5 — Use the Crossbow to bridge to Dorago's Palace.** The bridge point is visible in the overhead map — look for a small island beneath a floating structure. Fire the Crossbow upward at the anchor and the bridge forms. Navigate Dorago's Palace overhead (pit hazards; fall = death, go slowly). Defeat Dorago. Get the Indora's Armor.

**Step 6 — Return to every safe room you have found and heal.** Now that you have Indora's Armor, healing works. Fill to maximum before proceeding.

**Step 7 — Approach the Floating Castle via Lapis. Find the final Indora shrine and collect Indora's Bow.** If your Tone is below 2000, grind in Lapis before entering the Castle. If it is near 4095, proceed. The Bow boosts your effective power enough to matter against Ligar.

**Step 8 — The Floating Castle.** Linear but punishing. Clear every room. Ligar is at the end.

## Boss Strategies

### Eruga
**Location:** End of Eruga's Forest  
**Pattern:** Eruga moves back and forth across the screen and launches projectiles at you in an arcing pattern. He is relatively slow.  
**Strategy:** Stay mobile. Position yourself so the projectiles arc over or past you. Attack during his movement pauses with short-range strikes if you can close distance safely; otherwise use long range. If you have Power-Up, use it before the fight. He has moderate health — this fight is long without it.

### Sagila
**Location:** End of Sagila's Cave  
**Pattern:** Sagila is a serpent/cave boss that fills a large portion of the room. Moves in waves, occasionally drops toward the floor. Projectiles fire when the mouth opens.  
**Strategy:** Stay near the ground. Attack the head when it is at floor level using the short-range Diskarmor. Do not get cornered — Sagila can block movement. Recover if you drop below 3 health units; this fight can run long. Power-Up dramatically shortens it.

### Dorago
**Location:** End of Dorago's Palace  
**Pattern:** Dorago is a dragon-type boss in an overhead room. It circles and charges, breathing fire in patterns.  
**Strategy:** Maintain distance and attack in bursts. The fire breath has a short range — circle away when it fires, close in and strike during the recovery pause. This boss rewards patience over aggression. Do not spam attacks or you will take hits during the animation.

### Ligar
**Location:** End of the Floating Castle  
**Pattern:** Ligar is a lion/lizard hybrid with fireball attacks. He fires continuously in spreading bursts and has enormous health.  
**Requirements:** You should have 4095 (or close to it) Tone, Indora's Bow equipped, at least 10 health units, and Indora's Armor for any healing.  
**Strategy:** The short-range Diskarmor strike deals the highest damage. You need to get close, which means eating some fireballs. The fireballs come in predictable clusters — there is always a gap in the spread. Stand in the gap and fire short range during the pause between his bursts. Use Power-Up if you have 3 Mind Points saved. Use Recover any time you are below half health — do not be conservative here, this is the final fight. If you have Indora's Armor and can reach a safe room mid-run (emulation only via save states), do so. On the original hardware without save states, the Floating Castle is a one-shot affair.

## Grinding Tips

**Best early grind zone:** The side-scrolling entrance area off Garloz. Enemies here respawn every time you re-enter and are manageable. Run it until you have 5+ health units before attempting Eruga's Forest.

**Mid-game grind:** The Valley of Rolsa enemies give solid Tone gains. Farm this area between Eruga and Sagila if your Tone feels low.

**Pre-Ligar grind:** The approach to the Floating Castle in Lapis has enemies that give good Tone. If you arrive here with Tone below 2000, budget time here. Ligar at low Tone is functionally unbeatable — he has too much health for your damage output.

**Last grinding:** Last accumulates more slowly than Tone. If you want maximum health for the Ligar fight, you will need to grind specifically for Last in areas with high enemy density. The mid-game zones (post-Grappling Hook, pre-Sagila) are best for this.

**Recover vs. grinding:** If you have 7 Mind Points, Recover is always the right call when you are low on health during a grind session. Do not spend points on Attack & Assail during grinding — save them for boss fights.

## Secrets and Advanced Techniques

**Air walking:** Duck (hold Down) and rapidly press B repeatedly while moving. Rygar will appear to walk in air momentarily by resetting gravity through the attack animation. This is a glitch but useful for crossing certain gaps without the Grappling Hook.

**Slow descent:** Rapidly mash B while falling and Rygar descends more slowly. The attack animation interrupts the fall physics. Useful for avoiding damage from long drops.

**Invisible platforms in the Floating Castle:** The game's collision detection is inconsistent in the Castle. There are solid floors where the visual suggests a pit. If you are falling in an area that seems to have a floor somewhere, try moving horizontally before you hit the bottom — you may land on geometry that is not visible.

**Power-Up stacks with Indora's Bow:** Both increase your damage output through different mechanisms. Activating Power-Up after obtaining Indora's Bow gives you the maximum possible damage. If you have 3 Mind Points saved for Ligar, use Power-Up at the start of the fight.

**Safe room locations matter:** Map your safe room locations mentally as you explore. Indora's Armor is worthless if you do not know where the closest safe room is when you need health. After getting the Armor, re-run familiar paths and note every glowing room entrance.

**The continue penalty:** Continuing after death sets your Tone and Last to a minimum floor. The exact values depend on how far you have progressed, but you will feel the stat loss. In emulation with save states, this penalty is irrelevant — save before every boss fight.
