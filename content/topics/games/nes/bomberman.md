# Bomberman (NES)

**Developer:** Hudson Soft | **Publisher:** Hudson Soft | **Year:** 1987

---

## Introduction

Bomberman is a maze game about controlled demolition. You are in a grid of indestructible pillars and destructible bricks, you place bombs, the bombs explode in a cross, and you must not be standing in the cross when they do. Clear the enemies, find the exit hidden under a brick, leave.

The NES original is single-player only — the four-player mode Bomberman became famous for arrived later on the TurboGrafx-16 and SNES. What is here is fifty stages of increasingly nasty enemy mixes, and a difficulty curve that assumes you will die a great deal.

The premise is quietly odd: this Bomberman is a robot escaping a factory, and the ending reveals he becomes the protagonist of Hudson's *Lode Runner*. That is a real connection, not a fan theory.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move on the grid.
- **A:** Detonate, once you have the Remote Control power-up.
- **B:** Place a bomb.
- **Start:** Pause.

---

## Power-ups

Hidden under destructible bricks, one per stage at most.

| Power-up | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Bomb** | Carry one more bomb at a time |
| **Fire** | Longer blast radius in all four directions |
| **Detonator (Remote Control)** | Bombs explode on A rather than a timer — the single best item |
| **Wall Pass** | Walk through destructible bricks |
| **Bomb Pass** | Walk through your own bombs |
| **Flame Pass** | Immune to explosions |
| **Mystery** | Temporary invincibility |
| **Speed** | Move faster — a mixed blessing, see below |

**The Detonator changes the game.** With a timer you are always predicting; with remote detonation you place a bomb, walk to safety, and choose the moment. It makes enemy hunting deliberate instead of hopeful.

**Speed is a trap.** Bomberman moves on a grid, and extra speed makes it much easier to overshoot a junction and run into your own blast. Many experienced players avoid it.

---

## How to not kill yourself

This is most of the game.

**Blasts travel in a cross, not a circle.** Standing diagonally adjacent to a bomb is always safe. Standing in line with it, however far, is not — until a pillar or brick blocks the line.

**Bombs chain.** A blast touching another bomb detonates it immediately. This is how most self-inflicted deaths happen: you place a second bomb thinking you have time, the first one reaches it, and the chain catches you.

**Always plan the exit before placing.** Look at where you will stand, not where the bomb is. The habit that keeps you alive is placing a bomb and immediately stepping around a corner.

**Bricks block blasts, once.** A blast destroys the first brick it hits and stops there. It does not continue through to the next tile.

---

## Enemies

They escalate from slow and wall-bound to fast, wall-passing, and actively pursuing. The important distinctions:

- Early enemies move randomly and slowly; trap them in dead ends.
- Mid-game enemies pursue you and move faster than you do without Speed.
- Late enemies pass through destructible bricks, so no position is safe by geometry alone.

**Killing every enemy is required** before the exit works. The exit is under one of the bricks, and blowing it up while enemies remain spawns more of them — a punishing mistake that has ended many runs. Clear enemies first, then hunt for the exit.

---

## Strategy

**Open the middle early.** The starting corner is safe but useless. Clear bricks outward to create escape routes before enemies get fast.

**Dead ends are weapons.** An enemy in a one-tile corridor with a bomb at the mouth cannot escape.

**Do not blast the exit brick carelessly.** If you find the exit and enemies are still alive, do not detonate near it again — additional blasts on a revealed exit summon enemies.

**Count your bombs.** With several bombs available the chain risk grows, not shrinks. More bombs means more ways to trap yourself.

## Emulation Notes

Trivial to emulate; FCEUmm runs it perfectly. **Rewind** (on for this box) is well suited to Bomberman specifically, because deaths here are instantaneous and almost always self-inflicted — you walk one tile too far and the blast catches you. Unwinding two seconds and taking the other route is exactly the kind of mistake worth undoing.

Note this is the single-player NES original. If you want the multiplayer Bomberman, that is *Saturn Bomberman* on Saturn or the TurboGrafx-16 entries, not this cartridge.
