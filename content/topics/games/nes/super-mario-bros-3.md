# Super Mario Bros. 3 — NES Walkthrough

**Platform:** NES | **Year:** 1990 (US) | **Genre:** Platformer | **Players:** 1-2 alternating

---

## Introduction

Super Mario Bros. 3 is the high point of the NES library and one of the finest platformers ever made. It expands every concept from its predecessors: eight distinct worlds with their own visual identities, a map screen with meaningful choices, a power-up suit system that changes how you move and fight, and a depth of secrets that took years of community effort to fully document. The game is long by NES standards, but the pacing never drags. Each world introduces something new before the next one arrives.

This walkthrough covers all eight worlds, every boss, both fortress layouts, every Warp Whistle location, and the hidden White Mushroom Houses. The goal is to get you through the game with a solid understanding of what every item does and where the most useful secrets are. Save states are your friend if you are playing on Batocera.

## Controls

| Button | Action |
|--------|--------|
| D-Pad | Move, duck (Down), look up (Up) |
| A | Jump; hold for higher jump |
| B | Run (hold); throw fireballs or hammers |
| Start | Pause |
| Select | Open inventory on the map screen |
| A + B (running) | Wall-kick off certain surfaces |

**Raccoon/Tanooki flight:** Hold B to build speed until the P-Meter (bottom of screen) fills completely, then press A to lift off. Once airborne, tap A to stay in the air.

**Sliding:** Hold B (run) and press Down on a slope to slide. Knocks out enemies in your path.

**Ducking on a platform:** Press Down while standing on a white platform to pass through it and drop to the level below.

## Power-Ups and Items

### In-Level Power-Ups

**Super Mushroom:** Transforms Small Mario into Super Mario. Doubles your size and lets you take one extra hit.

**Fire Flower:** Gives Super Mario the ability to throw bouncing fireballs with B. Most enemies die in one hit. Fireballs bounce across terrain.

**Super Leaf:** Transforms Mario into Raccoon Mario. You grow a tail that defeats enemies and blocks some projectiles (press B to wag it). You can also fly once your P-Meter is full. The most important power-up in the game.

**Tanooki Suit:** Raccoon Mario's bigger sibling. Same flight ability, same tail attack, plus the ability to press B while holding Down to transform into an invincible stone statue temporarily. Statues can dodge almost anything. Rare.

**Frog Suit:** Mario swims in a straight line underwater, dramatically improving control in water stages. On land, you can only hop awkwardly. Get this before any water world.

**Hammer Suit:** Mario throws arcing hammers that penetrate most defenses. Crouching in this suit blocks fireballs and many projectiles. Extremely powerful and rare.

**Goomba's Shoe (Kuribo's Shoe):** Appears only in World 5-3. Stomp the Goomba wearing it to take the shoe, then hop in. Lets you safely jump on spiny enemies and spiked surfaces. You lose it at the end of that level.

**Starman:** Temporary invincibility. Defeats everything you touch.

**1-Up Mushroom:** Extra life. Hidden in specific blocks throughout the game.

### Inventory Items

These are carried between levels on the map screen. Open your inventory with Select.

**P-Wing:** Fills your P-Meter instantly and lets you fly for the entire level, not just briefly. Invaluable for long or dangerous stages. Single use.

**Jugem's Cloud:** Skips the current level on the map screen without completing it. The level stays skipped.

**Music Box:** Puts all Hammer Bros. on the map to sleep for a short time, stopping them from pursuing you.

**Anchor:** Prevents the airship from moving when you fail a stage. Without it, the airship retreats and you have to chase it back down.

**Hammer:** Used on the world map to break rock formations blocking hidden areas or paths.

**Warp Whistle:** Opens a Warp Zone. Use one to teleport ahead; use a second whistle inside the Warp Zone itself to jump straight to World 8.

## World-by-World Walkthrough

### World 1: Grass Land

World 1 introduces the basics and is the only world most players could complete blind. It also contains two of the three Warp Whistles, so it is more important than it looks.

**1-1:** Classic opener. The staircase at the end has an invisible block directly above the second step from the top. The 1-Up Mushroom rolls out to the right if you hit it. Collect 44 coins here for the White Mushroom House (see secrets section).

**1-2:** Straightforward level. There's a hidden pipe in the ceiling of the underground section leading to a room full of coins.

**1-3:** This level contains **Warp Whistle #1** (see Warp Whistle section below).

**1-Fortress:** Two-room fortress with moving platforms. The first room's platforms move over a spike pit. Time your movement and do not rush. The second room has more platforms over water. Boss is Boom Boom: jump on him three times. He hops faster each hit. Beating the fortress opens access to the rest of the world map.

**1-4:** Collect 44 coins here to trigger the White Mushroom House on the map.

**1-5 and 1-6:** Short levels. 1-5 introduces Big Goombas. 1-6 has moving platforms over a long pit.

**Airship:** Larry Koopa's ship. The airship has cannon Blasters firing in arcs and Bullet Bills. Use a Leaf/Raccoon suit here if you have one. **Larry Koopa** hops back and forth and fires a magic shot that ricochets off the floor and ceiling. He hits the same predictable beat. Stand in the center of the room, jump his hop, and land on his head. Three stomps wins. Recovering the Wand restores World 1's king.

### World 2: Desert Land

Desert world with sandy overworld stages, a pyramid dungeon, and airborne quicksand.

**2-1:** Introduction to Piranha Plants in pipes. Careful timing on the pipes.

**2-2:** Collect 30 coins to trigger the White Mushroom House. The coins are reachable with careful play.

**2-3:** Quicksand stage. The bottom of the screen kills you. You can run across quicksand at full speed, but standing still sinks you.

**2-Pyramid:** Underground pyramid with Spike enemies (the slow-rolling spiked balls). The key is keeping your speed up. Do not stop. Boom Boom again at the end.

**2-5:** Vertical autoscroll stage with Boomerang Bros. Duck their first boomerang throw, then advance.

**Airship:** **Morton Koopa Jr.** is slow but his ground pound sends a shockwave across the floor. Jump over the shockwave and hit him while he's recovering from the stomp. Three hits. His chamber has less floor space than Larry's, so jump the shockwave, do not walk under him.

**Note:** The World 2 map holds **Warp Whistle #3**, reachable with the Hammer (see below).

### World 3: Water Land

The water world. Get a Frog Suit from a Toad House before starting water stages.

**3-1, 3-2:** The early land stages feature pools and amphibious terrain. Frog Suit is clumsy here; save it.

**3-3:** Pure water level. With the Frog Suit, this is easy. Without it, the eel enemies make the tight passages dangerous.

**3-Fortress 1:** Two fortress rooms. The second has Boo ghosts that chase you when you face away. Face toward them to stop them, then move while not looking. Boom Boom at the end.

**3-4 to 3-8:** Mix of water and land stages. 3-8 has a high coin count, triggering the White Mushroom House at 44 coins.

**3-Fortress 2:** Second fortress is more complex with rotating block puzzles.

**Airship:** **Wendy O. Koopa** bounces rings that travel across the floor and walls. There is a safe spot: stand near the center and jump the rings rather than trying to chase her. Wait for her to come to you, stomp her when she passes. Three stomps.

### World 4: Giant Land

Everything is bigger: big pipes, big enemies, big blocks. Visually impressive, mechanically similar to earlier worlds.

**4-1 to 4-4:** Large terrain with Big Goombas, Giant Piranha Plants, and oversized everything. The hit detection on Big enemies works the same; you still just jump on them.

**4-Fortress 1 and 2:** Two fortresses with firebars and more complex room layouts. Both use Boom Boom.

**Airship:** **Iggy Koopa** stands on a platform over a long drop. He fires a chain of fireballs and hops erratically. Stomp him once and he slides on his shell. You need to chase him down and stomp again before he recovers. The platform is sloped, which makes recovery harder. Keep pressure on him and hit him three times before he orients himself.

### World 5: Sky Land

Split into two parts: the lower land area and the upper sky area. You need a Koopa shell thrown at a specific panel to reach the sky portion.

**5-1:** Normal ground stage. There is a panel near the top of 5-1 that unlocks the sky section when you fly into it as Raccoon Mario.

**Sky stages (5-4 to 5-7):** Cloud platforms with Para-Goombas and Lakitus. Para-Goombas come in flying clusters. The Raccoon tail knocks them away cleanly.

**5-3: Kuribo's Shoe level.** This is the only level where the Goomba's Shoe appears. Three Goombas with shoes patrol the stage. Jump on one to steal its shoe and wear it. You can safely walk over spiny enemies and spike patches. You automatically lose it when you grab the flag or touch the goal gate.

**5-Fortress 1 and 2:** Both use firebars. The second fortress introduces rooms with moving blocks.

**Airship:** **Roy Koopa** pounds the floor with a massive stomp that cracks it into chunks. The floor becomes unstable after enough stomps, so you need to defeat him before the floor collapses. Jump his shockwave, land on his head. Three hits. He retreats into his shell between hits and slides around.

### World 6: Ice Land

Ice physics. Mario slides on most surfaces. Plan all your landings and leave extra time to stop.

**6-1 to 6-10:** Long world with extensive ice floors. The sliding means you take wider arcs when jumping and cannot stop cleanly. Practice on the early levels.

**6-5:** Autoscroll ice cave. The ice blocks filling and shifting the passage are the main hazard.

**6-Fortress 1 and 2:** Ice fortresses with firebars and Boos.

**Airship:** **Lemmy Koopa** bounces on a large ball and summons clone balls. The real Lemmy is the one that moves toward you first. Wait for him to come to you on the ball, time a stomp, and repeat. Three hits. The clone balls are decoys but harmless. Collect 78 coins in World 6-7 for the White Mushroom House.

### World 7: Pipe Land

A maze of underground passages connected by pipes. The pipes are the navigation mechanic; wrong pipes send you to the wrong sections. Mapping the connections in your head matters here.

**7-1 to 7-5:** The above-ground sections use pipes to enter underground passages. Underground passages connect to other sections of the map. If you take a wrong pipe, you may loop back.

**7-Fortress 1 and 2:** Both fortresses have rotating blocks and more complex Boom Boom setups.

**Airship:** **Ludwig von Koopa** is the most dangerous Koopaling. He shoots a tracking fireball and does an earthquake stomp. The fireball homes in on you for a brief time before dissipating. Jump over it, then stomp Ludwig when he lands. He retreats into his shell and slides after each hit, making subsequent stomps require precision. Three hits. Collect 46 coins in 7-2 for the White Mushroom House.

### World 8: Dark Land

The final world. No Toad Houses, no breaks. Everything from the previous seven worlds shows up here in harder configurations.

**8-1 and 8-2:** Tank stages. You fight on moving tank convoys. Cannons fire in arcs; Rocky Wrenches pop from hatches. Target the hatches to stop Rocky Wrenches. The end of each tank stage is a boss tank with a cannon you destroy.

**8-3:** Naval stage with ships and cannons.

**8-Fortress:** The final fortress before Bowser's castle. Extremely long. Stock up on items before entering.

**8-4 to 8-7:** A gauntlet of infantry, tanks, and aerial stages leading to Bowser's castle.

**Bowser's Castle (8-Hand):** Bowser's castle is a series of hand-jump obstacles and rotating fire before the final chamber.

**Bowser:** The final boss. His floor is made of breakable blocks arranged in rows. He hops across the room and breathes fire. Jump his fire, land on his head when he returns to the floor. Each stomp breaks the floor blocks under him one row at a time. After enough stomps, the floor beneath him collapses and he falls into the abyss. You do not need to deal a set number of stomps; you need to survive long enough for the floor to break through to the pit. The fire breath timing is consistent: he fires twice in quick succession, then pauses to hop. Jump both bursts and land a stomp on the pause.

## Warp Whistles

Three Warp Whistles are hidden in the game. Using one opens a warp zone with portals to Worlds 2 through 8. Using a second whistle inside the warp zone itself sends you straight to World 8.

### Warp Whistle 1: World 1-3

Near the end of 1-3 there is a white platform in the background, positioned behind black scaffolding. Stand on the white platform and hold Down on the D-Pad for about five seconds without pressing anything else. Mario slides off the back of the level into a hidden passage behind the visible play field. Move right. A Toad House appears on the map. Open it to receive the Warp Whistle.

### Warp Whistle 2: World 1-Fortress

You need the Super Leaf (Raccoon Mario) before entering the fortress. In the first room of the fortress, there are moving platforms over a spike pit. Above the door leading to the next room, the ceiling has a hidden gap. Build your P-Meter on the moving platforms and fly upward through the ceiling before reaching the door. You enter a hidden room with a chest. Open the chest for the Warp Whistle.

### Warp Whistle 3: World 2 (Desert Land Map)

You need the Hammer item from a Hammer Bros. encounter. On the World 2 map, there is a rock formation in the upper-right area blocking a path. Use the Hammer (open inventory, select Hammer) to smash it. A Boomerang Bros. encounter appears behind it. Defeat them. A Toad House reveals itself; open it for the third Warp Whistle.

## White Mushroom Houses

Collecting a specific coin total in certain levels causes a White Mushroom House to appear on the map. These houses give rare items not found in normal Toad Houses: P-Wings and Anchors.

| Level | Coins Required | Reward |
|-------|---------------|--------|
| 1-4 | 44 | P-Wing |
| 2-2 | 30 | Anchor |
| 3-8 | 44 | P-Wing |
| 4-2 | 24 | Anchor |
| 5-5 | 28 | P-Wing |
| 6-7 | 78 | Anchor |
| 7-2 | 46 | P-Wing |

The coin count resets when you die on the way to the level. Collect the coins in a single attempt. The White House appears on the map after you clear the stage; if you already used a previous White House reward, a new one does not appear from the same stage.

## Coin Ship

A special Coin Ship with a hold full of coins and 1-Ups spawns on the overworld map under a specific set of conditions in Worlds 1, 3, 5, and 6. The ship appears when your total score ends in the same digit as the number of lives you have, and the last digit of your coins matches the world number you are entering. The conditions are difficult to hit naturally; if you want to farm lives, farm the shell trick in 1-1 instead.

## Secrets and Tricks

### 1-Up Shell Trick (World 1-1)

Near the end of 1-1, there is a staircase with a Koopa Troopa on it. Kick its shell into the base of the stairs and immediately hold Right while standing on the second or third step. The shell bounces back and forth, hitting the step repeatedly. Each bounce registers as an enemy stomp. After eight consecutive bounces, every subsequent bounce generates a 1-Up. Hold position until you have as many extra lives as you want, then release.

### Infinite Lives (N-Spade Panel)

N-Spade matching panels appear on the map when your total score ends in a multiple of 80,000. These are memory-matching card games. Match all pairs and you win the displayed number of 1-Ups. The panel disappears after one use.

### Coin Ship Farming

See above. More practically useful is just stockpiling P-Wings from White Mushroom Houses and using them on stages that cost you multiple lives, bypassing the stage entirely.

### Skipping Levels

Each world's map has levels that are not required to reach the airship. The Jugem's Cloud item skips the current level without clearing it. This is useful for especially hard optional stages in Worlds 6 and 7.

### P-Wing Conservation

A P-Wing gives unlimited flight for one level. Use it on levels where the challenge is platforming rather than enemies, since flight bypasses most of the platform geometry entirely. Especially useful in 5-Sky and 7-Pipe Maze where the navigation is the main threat.

### Hammer Bros. Avoidance

Hammer Bros. wander the overworld map. Moving quickly after entering a world lets you sometimes avoid their path entirely. The Music Box item puts all of them to sleep; use it if a Hammer Bros. is blocking a critical path.

## Boss Reference

| World | Boss | Pattern | Weakness |
|-------|------|---------|----------|
| 1 | Larry Koopa | Hops, shoots one magic shot | Three stomps |
| 2 | Morton Koopa Jr. | Ground pounds, shockwave along floor | Three stomps, jump shockwave |
| 3 | Wendy O. Koopa | Fires rings that bounce off walls | Three stomps between ring passes |
| 4 | Iggy Koopa | Erratic hopping, fires fireballs | Three stomps, then chase sliding shell |
| 5 | Roy Koopa | Ground pounds, floor cracks | Three stomps, jump shockwave |
| 6 | Lemmy Koopa | Bounces on ball, sends decoy balls | Three stomps on the real Lemmy |
| 7 | Ludwig von Koopa | Tracking fireball, earthquake stomp | Three stomps, dodge fireball first |
| 8 | Bowser | Fire breath, hops, breakable floor | Stomp until the floor collapses |

All Koopalings require three successful stomps. Stomping them causes them to retreat into their shell and slide. Some (Iggy, Ludwig) require you to catch up to the sliding shell and stomp again before they recover.

## Tips and FAQ

**Q: I keep running out of lives in the later worlds. What do I do?**

Farm the 1-Up shell trick in World 1-1 before heading into Worlds 5-8. Thirty lives before World 5 gives you room to experiment. Alternatively, stock P-Wings and use them on the hardest stages.

**Q: Can I go back to earlier worlds?**

No. Once you complete a world and move to the next, earlier worlds are closed. Stock up on items from White Mushroom Houses before moving forward.

**Q: The airship moved after I failed a stage. How do I get it back?**

It retreats several map squares toward the beginning of the world map. Re-enter the previous stage it was at, complete it, and the airship returns to its final position. The Anchor item prevents this from happening entirely.

**Q: What is the best power-up for general use?**

Raccoon Mario (Super Leaf). The tail attack kills most enemies safely, the flight lets you bypass hard sections, and it is common enough to find in normal play. In water stages, switch to Frog Suit.

**Q: Do I need to collect every coin in the White Mushroom House levels?**

You need to hit the exact required count in a single run of that level. You do not need every single coin in the level, just the required number. Plan your route through the stage to hit all the visible coins and check any hidden blocks.

**Q: The Tanooki statue transformation seems useless. When do I actually use it?**

Against Boom Boom and certain Koopalings, you can transform mid-air during a boss fight to dodge their counterattack after a stomp. It also passes through firebars while in statue form. The main use is emergency invincibility when you have landed somewhere bad.

**Q: Is there any way to continue without a Game Over screen?**

No built-in continue code exists. Each Game Over returns you to the start of the current world's map with whatever lives you had in your last reserve. In emulation, save states make this a non-issue.
