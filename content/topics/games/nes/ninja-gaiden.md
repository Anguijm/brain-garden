# Ninja Gaiden — NES Walkthrough

**Platform:** NES | **Publisher:** Tecmo | **US Release:** March 1989 | **Genre:** Action-Platformer

---

## Introduction

Ninja Gaiden arrived in North America in 1989 and immediately set itself apart from every other NES action game by doing something nobody had done before: it told its story through animated cinematic cutscenes between stages. Full scrolling panels with voiced text and dramatic music played between every act, delivering a genuine narrative about Ryu Hayabusa, a young ninja whose father has been murdered, and his journey into a shadowy organization's web of ancient demons and betrayal. These cutscenes were revelatory in 1989, and they still hold up as an example of how to build atmosphere on limited hardware.

The gameplay underneath them is uncompromising. Ninja Gaiden is a precision action-platformer with tight controls, relentless enemy pressure, and a learning curve that rewards mastery over brute force. The game has six acts spanning twenty stages. You will die, often and repeatedly, particularly in the final act. What separates players who finish it from those who don't is understanding two things: how wall-climbing works, and how enemy respawning works. This guide covers both in detail, then takes you through every act and boss.

**Story overview:** Ryu Hayabusa travels to America after his father's death, following clues toward the Malice Four — a group of servants to an ancient demon named Jashin. Behind them is the archaeologist Guardia de Mieux and a woman named Irene Lew, who may be an enemy or an ally. The cutscenes tell the rest. Pay attention to them — the game earned those scenes.

## Core Mechanics

### Wall-Climbing

This is the single most important mechanic in the game and the one new players spend the most time fighting. To cling to a wall, jump directly into it — Ryu grabs it automatically. To stay on the wall, hold the d-pad toward it. To jump off, press the d-pad away from the wall and press A simultaneously. You can jump between two parallel walls by repeatedly jumping toward the opposite one.

Wall-climbing is not optional. Many stages require it to reach platforms that are otherwise inaccessible. More importantly, it is your primary escape tool against enemies on platforms you cannot reach from the ground. Eagles, in particular, are nearly impossible to handle without wall-clinging.

One critical limitation: if you jump into a wall and immediately let go of the d-pad, you fall. You must actively hold toward the wall. Practice this in Act 1 before the game demands it.

### Slash-Canceling

During a jump, you can swing your sword. If you swing and immediately press Down on the d-pad and B again while still in the air, Ryu performs a second slash before landing. This "slash-cancel" doubles your damage output in the air and is the correct approach against tall bosses who need hits at head height. It takes practice to execute consistently but becomes second nature by Act 4.

### Ninpo (Sub-Weapons) and Spiritual Energy

Sub-weapons are activated with Up + B. They consume Spiritual Energy, shown as the number in the upper right. Blue orbs dropped by enemies restore 5 points; red orbs restore 10. The game caps your maximum at 99.

**Never use sub-weapons indiscriminately.** In later acts, Spiritual Energy becomes a critical resource for surviving boss fights. Prioritize conserving it through regular stages and spending it on enemies that genuinely threaten you, not on easy kills you could take with the sword.

### Respawning Enemies

Enemies in Ninja Gaiden respawn whenever you scroll the screen backward even slightly. This is not a bug — it is the game's core difficulty mechanism. The practical rule is: **never voluntarily scroll backward.** Once you clear a section and move forward, keep moving forward. If you die and restart at a mid-stage checkpoint, enemies will respawn as expected, but during an active run, moving backward recreates everything you just killed.

The one exception is deliberate energy farming: if you need Spiritual Energy before a boss, you can sometimes find an enemy near a checkpoint that you can kill, collect the orb, die, respawn, and repeat. This is slow but effective before brutal boss fights.

### Health and Lives

Health is displayed as the green meter on the left. You have three lives per continue and a limited number of continues. When you reach zero lives, you can continue from the start of the current act — with one critical exception covered in the Act 6 section.

## Ninpo Sub-Weapons Guide

### Throwing Star (3 Spiritual Energy)
Your starting sub-weapon. Fires a small shuriken horizontally at mid-height. Cheap, useful for picking off enemies at range when you don't want to close in. The low cost makes it your default option when conserving energy.

### Windmill Throwing Star (5 Spiritual Energy)
A boomeranging shuriken that travels forward, drops slightly, then curves back toward Ryu. Hits enemies twice if they're in the return path. Better than the regular Throwing Star against clustered enemies and useful for hitting enemies around corners. The return arc takes practice to judge.

### Art of the Fire Wheel (5 Spiritual Energy)
Fires a ring of fire that spirals upward from Ryu's position. Hits everything above you, making it excellent against enemies on elevated platforms and against certain bosses who hover overhead. Strong damage output. Becomes one of your most-used weapons from Act 3 onward.

### Jump and Slash (5 Spiritual Energy)
Activates mid-jump and triggers an aerial sword flurry that deals massive damage to anything Ryu passes through. The single most powerful sub-weapon in the game — it can kill most regular enemies instantly and shreds boss health bars. The cost is moderate and the damage-per-energy-point ratio is the best of any option. When in doubt, Jump and Slash. The weapon auto-activates during a jump if you trigger it correctly — press Up + B just after jumping.

### Art of the Invincible Fire Wheel (0 Spiritual Energy)
Makes Ryu temporarily invincible (roughly five seconds) surrounded by a damaging fire ring. Costs no Spiritual Energy to use but does have a short recharge period before you can use any sub-weapon again. Excellent for surviving heavy enemy density sections and buying time in desperate situations. Does not consume energy, so there is no reason not to use it whenever the window of invincibility will help.

## Act-by-Act Walkthrough

### Act 1 — Destiny

**Stages:** 1-1, 1-2, 1-3

Act 1 is the tutorial in everything but name. The enemies here are basic urban types — street thugs, knife-throwers, and occasionally a panther. None of them are serious threats if you keep moving and slash on sight.

**1-1:** Straight urban rooftop run. Jump between platforms, slash anyone who gets close. You'll encounter your first knife-throwers — they throw arcing knives that you can jump over, then close in to slash before they throw again. The stage ends at a doorway. Collect every orb you see.

**1-2:** Indoor section. Narrower passages, more enemies in tight spaces. Start practicing wall-jumps here — there are vertical shafts where you must jump between walls to ascend. Stay patient and take one enemy at a time.

**1-3:** Rooftop exterior leading to the boss. Contains your first eagle encounter. Eagles dive from above in a swooping arc — the safe approach is to wall-cling and slash them as they pass, or jump into them from below with a timed slash. Never try to fight an eagle while standing on a narrow platform; one hit while standing near an edge usually means falling to your death.

**Boss: Barbarian** (Jay's Bar)

The Barbarian is a large swordsman who charges across the floor slashing horizontally. His attack pattern has a delay between swings. The safe approach: stay moving backward when he charges, let him swing, then close in and slash two or three times before backing off again. Jump over him when he gets close if you need to reposition. Jump and Slash handles him quickly if you have the Spiritual Energy, though saving it for later acts is wise since Act 1's boss is manageable without it. Four to five solid hits with your sword ends the fight.

---

### Act 2 — The Escape

**Stages:** 2-1, 2-2, 2-3

The difficulty steps up noticeably. Knife-lobbers appear — they stand on elevated positions and throw arcing knives that are annoying to dodge on narrow ground. Machine gun commandos fire in bursts at ground level; approach them from the side and slash before they react. Wall-climbing becomes essential in 2-2's vertical sections.

**2-1:** Outdoor terrain, multiple elevation changes. The Windmill Throwing Star is useful here for hitting elevated knife-lobbers without climbing to their level. Keep Spiritual Energy above 10.

**2-2:** Introduces vertical shaft sections requiring wall-jumping. Take your time. If you rush a wall-jump sequence and miss a cling, the fall likely kills you. This stage is where most first-time players start losing lives consistently.

**2-3:** Leads to the boss. Enemy density is higher. Save at least 15 Spiritual Energy.

**Boss: Bomberhead**

Bomberhead is tall and wields a chain sickle that swings horizontally at mid-height. The chain sickle is his only attack. The key insight: he always moves toward you, and the sickle hits at chest height, not at your feet. Duck under the sickle, wait for the swing to pass, then slash his legs. Alternatively, wall-cling to his side as he approaches and drop-slash him while he's adjusting. Jump and Slash over his head also works — his height makes landing hits easy on the descent. He has more health than the Barbarian, but the sickle's predictable timing makes this fight cleaner once you recognize the rhythm.

---

### Act 3 — The Chase

**Stages:** 3-1, 3-2, 3-3

Eagles appear in greater numbers. Jaguars — faster, more aggressive versions of the Act 1 panthers — charge suddenly from off-screen. Bazooka soldiers fire explosive shells in an arc. The bazooka shells are the main new threat: they arc across the screen and explode on contact with the ground, creating a small burst radius. Jump over them, then close in before the soldier reloads.

**3-1:** Mountain paths. Jaguars often appear from ledge edges where you can't see them coming. When approaching a ledge lip, slow down and be ready to slash immediately on seeing movement. Art of the Fire Wheel handles multiple jaguars at once.

**3-2:** Cave section with irregular terrain. Eagles appear in tight spaces here — cling to walls and let them come to you rather than trying to chase them across gaps.

**3-3:** Leads to Basaquer. Keep Spiritual Energy at 20+.

**Boss: Basaquer**

Basaquer jumps side-to-side across the boss room and fires three bullets per jump — a spread pattern that covers the floor at different heights. The bullets travel horizontally, so your goal is to jump over the lower ones and duck the higher ones, which takes a few deaths to learn the exact pattern. Basaquer has limited jump range, so positioning yourself near the center of the room and reacting to which side he jumps toward gives you the most options. Close-range slashing is viable between his jump attacks. Art of the Fire Wheel launched upward when he's above you deals solid damage. Jump and Slash eliminates him quickly if you can time the approach to his landing.

---

### Act 4 — A Trap

**Stages:** 4-1, 4-2, 4-3

Green ninjas appear — faster and more agile than previous humanoid enemies, they close distance rapidly and are harder to interrupt mid-attack. Rocket ninjas are new and dangerous: they fly horizontally across the screen at full speed and can clip you mid-jump if you're not watching the edge of the screen. When you enter a room where rocket ninjas spawn, attack the moment you see movement at the screen edge, before they've reached full speed. Statues fire fireballs at set intervals — time your approach between shots and slash them quickly.

**4-1 through 4-2:** Increasing enemy density. The Windmill Throwing Star is valuable in these stages for hitting enemies from safer distances. Conserve Spiritual Energy — you need it for the boss.

**Boss: Kelbeross**

Kelbeross is actually two creatures: a real boss and a shadow decoy that mirrors his movements. Both deal damage on contact. The real Kelbeross cycles through a movement pattern — high jump, low jump, charge — and the decoy mirrors this but slightly out of phase. Your goal is to identify which one is the real Kelbeross (it is usually the one that approaches you more aggressively, though this takes observation during the fight) and land hits on it.

Jump and Slash handles Kelbeross more effectively than any other approach because the aerial attack damages anything it passes through, meaning you can hit both the real boss and the decoy simultaneously if they're close together. Land Jump and Slash on the real one consistently and the fight ends before the decoy becomes a problem. Without Jump and Slash, fight defensively — one jump pattern at a time, land a single slash, reposition. The decoy copies attacks but cannot be killed; focus entirely on the real one.

---

### Act 5 — Life or Death

**Stages:** 5-1, 5-2, 5-3

The difficulty escalates sharply. Scythe-throwers appear — they throw spinning scythes that arc unpredictably and are difficult to dodge in tight corridors. Flea-men bounce rapidly across the screen in erratic patterns and hit hard on contact. Multiple rocket ninja waves arrive in sequence.

**5-2 — The Nails of Lukifell:** This section of Act 5 is the game's most notorious regular stage. A gauntlet of successive platforming challenges with enemy spawns timed to the worst possible moments. Take it one screen at a time, use Art of the Invincible Fire Wheel liberally through dense enemy clusters, and resist the urge to rush. Rushing kills you. Patience kills the stage.

**Boss: Bloody Malth**

Bloody Malth floats across the top of the room and hurls energy orbs that travel erratically downward. The orbs don't follow a strict pattern — they vary in descent angle and speed, which makes the fight genuinely difficult to approach with a memorized dodge sequence. Three viable strategies:

1. **Jump and Slash aggression:** Rush him immediately and activate Jump and Slash on every possible jump. Malth dies fast enough that you can absorb some orb damage if your health is high.
2. **Damage race:** Stay moving horizontally under him, constantly jumping and slashing upward, and accept that some orbs will hit you. If your health is full entering the fight, you can usually kill him before he kills you.
3. **Precise dodging:** Stand roughly centered, watch each orb's descent angle from where it spawns, and adjust position laterally. Jump only when an orb would hit you at ground level. This is the lowest-damage approach but sustainable if you're low on health.

Enter this fight with maximum health and at least 20 Spiritual Energy.

---

### Act 6 — Fall of the Demon

**Stages:** 6-1, 6-2, 6-3, 6-4 (boss gauntlet)

**Read this before starting Act 6.**

Act 6 ends with three consecutive boss fights. If you die at any point in the Act 6 boss gauntlet — including at the very last boss — you restart at the beginning of 6-1, not at the checkpoint before the bosses. This is the correct behavior, not a glitch. The one mercy: any boss you have already defeated stays dead. If you beat the Masked Devil and then die to Jaquio, you restart 6-1 but only face Jaquio and Jashin when you reach the gauntlet again.

This means Act 6 is a test of stamina. You need to clear 6-1 cleanly, reach 6-4 with good health, and then clear three boss fights in sequence. Practice the path through 6-1 until you can do it without taking significant damage.

**6-1:** You begin near where you fought Malth. Navigate back through the environment — this stage reuses some earlier structure and introduces a final wave of difficult enemies including more green ninjas and rocket ninjas at close quarters. Art of the Fire Wheel is excellent here for crowd control on the vertical sections.

**6-2 and 6-3:** Shorter connective stages. Enemy density is high. Move deliberately, clear your path before advancing, and use Invincible Fire Wheel when surrounded.

**Entering the Boss Gauntlet (6-4):** Before the final three bosses, your health refills. You receive another refill before the second boss. These are the only full heals in the sequence. Do not waste them by taking unnecessary hits in 6-1 through 6-3.

---

### Boss: The Masked Devil (Act 6, fight 1)

The Masked Devil advances toward Ryu steadily, accompanied by a red orb that orbits him. He can shoot projectiles from this orb. His walk speed is constant and he does not jump, making him simpler in movement than any previous boss — but his proximity damage and the orbiting projectile make him dangerous up close.

The red orb is the key: destroy it first. Slash the orb repeatedly as you back up to stay out of reach. Once the orb is gone, Masked Devil is slower to deal damage. Continue slashing him directly. Jump and Slash over his head with a landing slash combo works well here. He has moderate health — persistence wins this fight.

---

### Boss: Jaquio (Act 6, fight 2)

Jaquio flies in patterns near the top of the screen and fires homing fireballs downward. The fireballs curve toward Ryu's position and are harder to dodge than anything you've faced previously. They are not perfectly homing — they adjust once and then continue on that trajectory — so a short sidestep after their initial curve often clears them.

The challenge is reaching Jaquio himself while dodging fireballs. Art of the Fire Wheel is excellent because it shoots upward toward where he hovers. Jump and Slash reaches him on an upward arc if you time the jump to his position. Between fireball salvos, identify his position and commit to an attack approach. He has high health and the fight is long.

The slash-cancel technique matters here: when you do reach him via a jump, swing twice in the air before landing to maximize damage per contact. Every lost second costs you additional fireball salvos.

---

### Boss: Jashin — The Demon (Act 6, fight 3)

Jashin is the final boss and a multi-phase encounter. He appears first as a large sculpted head that fires projectiles; destroy the head. The demon's heart then becomes exposed below the destroyed head — this is the actual target. The heart also fires projectiles and is the demon's true life gauge.

Phase 1 (Head): Stand at mid-distance and time your slashes to the head between its projectile bursts. Jump and Slash is effective. Do not stand directly beneath the head — it fires straight down as well as at angles.

Phase 2 (Heart): The exposed heart is lower than the head was. Slash it directly, dodging the curved projectile arcs. The heart's projectiles follow a wider spread pattern than the head's. Keep moving laterally and slash during gaps. Jump and Slash depletes the heart rapidly if you can land it consistently.

Enter this fight with full health and maximum Spiritual Energy. Use Jump and Slash on every opening in Phase 1 to conserve health for Phase 2.

## Secrets and Tips

**Secret Passage in 4-2:** A section of wall can be passed through to access a hidden area with a 1-Up. Walk slowly through apparently solid wall sections in the darker corridor areas of this stage. The location is near the halfway point of the stage.

**Farming Spiritual Energy:** Before major bosses, identify the last enemy before the boss door. If it drops Spiritual Energy orbs consistently, kill it, collect the orb, and use the checkpoint mechanic to do it repeatedly if needed. Less efficient than playing well but useful when preparing for the Act 6 gauntlet.

**Eagle handling:** Eagles dive in an arc from upper-right to lower-left by default. Wall-cling on the right side of a passage and slash them as they enter your range. Never fight eagles on open ground over gaps.

**Jump and Slash priority:** If you have a choice between a Throwing Star and Jump and Slash pick-up, always take Jump and Slash. The damage output difference is so large that conserving it for fights you genuinely need it for is more efficient than spending Throwing Stars constantly.

**Health Item locations:** Health restores (potions) appear in breakable objects throughout stages — candles, lanterns, and pots that you slash or jump-kick open. Slash every breakable object you pass. Some contain nothing, some contain health, some contain Spiritual Energy orbs, and a few contain 1-Ups. The specific objects that contain items are consistent across runs, so once you learn them, you'll never miss a restore.

**Act 6 rehearsal:** Before committing to a full Act 6 run, practice just 6-1 repeatedly until you can clear it with minimal damage. The loss of an entire Act 6 run to a bad 6-1 is the most demoralizing way to lose late time, and most runs that fail on the final bosses are actually lost in 6-1.

**The continue screen:** When given the option to continue, always do so if you are past Act 3. The game will send you to the beginning of the current act, which is significantly better than restarting entirely. You have enough continues to work through the game with patience — runs that start over entirely are usually a choice, not a necessity.

## Final Notes

Ninja Gaiden is a game that teaches through repetition. Every enemy pattern, every boss phase, every platform gap is designed to be learned and eventually mastered. The first time you reach the Act 6 boss gauntlet you will almost certainly lose. The second and third times you'll get further. By the time you see Jashin's heart, you'll know the path through 6-1 by feel and the earlier bosses will feel like opening stretches. That accumulation of mastery is exactly what the game is designed to produce.

The cinematic cutscenes reward every time you complete an act. Ryu's story concludes in a way that earns the difficulty you just pushed through. Watch them.
