# Metroid — NES Walkthrough

**Platform:** NES | **Developer:** Nintendo R&D1 / Intelligent Systems | **Year:** 1986 (JP), 1987 (US)

---

## Introduction

Metroid drops you into the alien planet Zebes with no map, no direction, and no explanation. Your job is to find bounty hunter Samus Aran's power-ups scattered across a sprawling, interconnected underground world, defeat two of the Space Pirate's bosses, and destroy the Metroid breeding colony at Zebes's core. Nobody tells you how to do any of this. The game expects you to bomb every suspicious wall, explore every dead end, and figure it out.

That ethos makes Metroid one of the most atmospheric games on the NES and one of the most disorienting. The world loops and connects in ways that aren't obvious from the screen layout, and without a map you will get lost. Save states on emulation make the experience dramatically more manageable — use them freely. The game is still hard even with that safety net.

The original Metroid uses a **password system** instead of battery saves. Every time Samus dies, you get a new password that records your progress. Write it down or screenshot it. On emulation, save states are easier, but the password system still works and some passwords are worth knowing.

This walkthrough covers the recommended route, every major item, all boss strategies, and the endings. It does not include exhaustive missile tank locations — there are 21 missile tanks in the game and tracking every one would double the length of this guide. The ones mentioned are the most useful.

## Controls

| Button | Action |
|---|---|
| D-Pad | Move left/right, aim up (hold Up), crouch/Morph Ball (hold Down) |
| A | Jump |
| B | Fire |
| Start | Pause |
| Select | (No function during gameplay) |

**Holding Up while firing** shoots straight up. This is the only way to fire upward — there is no diagonal aiming. A lot of boss fights revolve around positioning so you can shoot up into a weak point.

**Morphing into the Morph Ball:** Hold Down while standing still. Samus curls into a ball and you move with the D-pad. You cannot fire in ball form except with Bombs, which you place by pressing B.

## World Overview

Zebes is divided into five areas. They are connected to each other through corridors and vertical shafts, not through a clean hub-and-spoke layout. Getting your mental map right is the most important skill in this game.

### Brinstar

The starting area and the hub of the game. Brinstar has two color zones — a blue-gray rocky section near the start, and a golden-tan section further in. It connects to Norfair below, Kraid's Lair to the right, and Tourian above-left. Most of the important early upgrades are here. Brinstar is also where you learn the fundamental lesson of Metroid: **bomb every suspicious floor and wall**. The game is built around hidden passages that only open when you lay a bomb in the right spot.

### Norfair

Below Brinstar, accessed by dropping through vertical shafts. The color palette shifts to orange and red — you are deeper underground. Norfair holds many of the best upgrades (Screw Attack, Hi-Jump Boots, Wave Beam) and is significantly harder than Brinstar. It also branches into Ridley's Lair.

### Kraid's Lair (Hideout I)

Off to the right of Brinstar, once you have Bombs. Kraid is one of the two bosses you must defeat before Tourian unlocks. His lair is a vertical dungeon filled with tough enemies and tight corridors.

### Ridley's Lair (Hideout II)

Deeper in Norfair, branching off from the lower sections. Ridley is the second required boss. His lair has some of the most punishing enemies in the game. Come here well-supplied.

### Tourian

The final area, accessed from the upper-left of Brinstar once both Kraid and Ridley are dead. Tourian is not a dungeon — it is a gauntlet. No items, just Metroids and Mother Brain at the end. You must bring Ice Beam and plenty of missiles. Do not enter Tourian without Ice Beam.

## All Items and Where to Find Them

### Morph Ball (Brinstar)

The first upgrade you will find, in the right portion of early Brinstar. Samus cannot progress without this. It unlocks bomb passages and low corridors. If you have not found it within the first ten minutes of play, you missed a right-branching passage near the start.

### Bombs (Brinstar)

Found in Brinstar, left-center section. Requires the Morph Ball. Once you have Bombs, the game opens up — most secret passages in Brinstar and Kraid's Lair require bomb-jumping or bomb-blasting floors and ceilings. When in doubt, bomb it.

### Long Beam (Brinstar)

Extends your shots to the full width of the screen. Before Long Beam, your bullets disappear after traveling a short distance. After Long Beam, they cross the room. Pick this up as soon as possible — it makes every fight easier.

### Ice Beam (Brinstar and Norfair)

There are two Ice Beam locations — one in Brinstar, one in Norfair. You only need one. The Ice Beam is **mandatory** to complete the game. It freezes enemies, which is the only safe way to cross certain rooms, and it is the only way to kill Metroids. Frozen Metroids can be destroyed with five missiles each. Without Ice Beam you will be eaten alive in Tourian.

**Important:** Picking up the Wave Beam **replaces** the Ice Beam if you have it. Do not pick up Wave Beam after Ice Beam unless you are prepared to replace it. The Ice Beam in Brinstar is safer to collect without the Wave Beam risk.

### Wave Beam (Norfair)

Fires a wavy shot that travels through walls. Useful for hitting enemies behind barriers but incompatible with Ice Beam — picking one replaces the other. Get Ice Beam and skip Wave Beam unless you know what you are doing.

### Varia Suit (Norfair or Brinstar — two locations)

Halves all damage taken. This is the single most important upgrade for survival. Find it before going into Ridley's Lair or you will spend most of that dungeon dying. The Norfair location requires navigating deeper into the lower shafts; the Brinstar location requires bombing through a hidden floor in the upper-blue section.

### Hi-Jump Boots (Norfair)

Increases jump height substantially. Required to reach some items in Norfair and opens up new traversal options throughout the game. Without Hi-Jump Boots, certain corridors above Samus's natural jump height are inaccessible.

### Screw Attack (Norfair)

The best combat upgrade in the game. Every spinning jump kills any enemy it touches except bosses. With Screw Attack, most of Zebes's normal enemy population becomes trivial. It requires Hi-Jump Boots to reach effectively.

### Energy Tanks

Energy Tanks increase your maximum health by 100 each. There are eight in the game. You start at 99 energy; every tank adds 100. Collecting all eight gives you 899 maximum health, which makes the final areas much more survivable. Locations:

- **Brinstar:** Three tanks. One is near the Long Beam, reachable early. The others require Bombs and more extensive exploration of the golden Brinstar section.
- **Norfair:** One tank, deeper in the orange sections.
- **Kraid's Lair:** Two tanks. The lair is worth clearing not just for the boss but for these.
- **Ridley's Lair:** Two tanks. Same logic — clear the lair on the way to Ridley and collect both.

### Missile Tanks

Each tank adds 5 missiles to your maximum. You start with no missiles and must find the first tank before you can use missile doors. Missile doors are the orange doors that block key areas — you need at least one missile to open them. There are 21 tanks total; collecting all of them gives you 255 missiles maximum.

You do not need all 21. You need enough to beat the bosses and Tourian. Forty or fifty is more than enough for a comfortable run. Collect tanks as you find them naturally exploring each area.

## Walkthrough

### Step 1: Brinstar — Early Exploration

Start in the blue Brinstar section. Head right from the spawn point. You will immediately encounter Zoomer enemies (small circular creatures that walk along surfaces) and find your first missile tank after a little exploration. Continue right to find the **Morph Ball** — it is not hidden, just placed at the end of a horizontal corridor.

With the Morph Ball, go back left and into the areas below the starting point. Some corridors are only accessible in ball form. Bomb suspicious walls and floors — the game rewards players who probe everything. Find the **Bombs**, then the **Long Beam** further right in the golden section of Brinstar.

Explore the upper portions of Brinstar to find energy tanks. The Varia Suit can be found in a hidden section of upper Brinstar if you bomb through a disguised floor in the blue-ceiling area.

### Step 2: Kraid's Lair

Once you have Bombs and Morph Ball, Kraid's Lair is accessible from the right side of Brinstar. You will need at least one missile to open the orange doors, so make sure you have found missile tanks first.

Kraid's Lair is a vertical dungeon. Descend carefully. The enemies here — Rippers (floating fish), Gamets (fast-flying bugs), and Waver clusters — are more dangerous than Brinstar's. Collect the two Energy Tanks and four missile tanks on your way down. By the time you reach Kraid, you should have a solid missile supply.

**Kraid:** The boss room has a static platform to Kraid's right and a larger platform in the center. Kraid fires three belly spikes in sequence and occasionally sends fireballs. The most reliable strategy: stand on the center platform and fire missiles at his face. When he throws belly spikes, jump them — the spikes travel in a low arc and a standing jump clears them. When he fires fireballs, duck or jump back to the side platform momentarily. Kraid has very high health; be patient and keep hitting his face with missiles. Your arm cannon also damages him but slowly. Bring at least 30 missiles for a comfortable fight.

An alternative method: Morph Ball in the corner near his body and bomb continuously. You take hits but deal damage very fast. Use this if you are impatient and have energy to spare.

Defeating Kraid makes one of the four statue heads in Tourian lower, opening the final area once Ridley also falls.

### Step 3: Norfair

Return to Brinstar and descend into Norfair via the vertical yellow shaft. Norfair's earliest sections have Ripper II (blue Rippers that move faster) and Magdolite (fire-spitting floating slugs). The Hi-Jump Boots are relatively near the entrance to Norfair — find them before descending further.

The Screw Attack is deeper in Norfair, past corridors only accessible with Hi-Jump. Getting both before entering Ridley's Lair is strongly recommended. Screw Attack in particular turns the entire lair from difficult to manageable.

Also collect the Wave Beam if you want it — but if you have Ice Beam already, skip it. The Ice Beam in Norfair is in the lower sections; pick up whichever one you find first (Brinstar Ice Beam is earlier and safer to grab before Wave Beam becomes a temptation).

### Step 4: Ridley's Lair

Ridley's Lair branches off from deep Norfair. It is harder than Kraid's Lair in every respect. The Geemers (crawling spiked creatures), Violas (fast projectile-throwing enemies), and Wall Crawlers in this area are relentless. Varia Suit is essential here — if you are not wearing it, halve your expected survivability.

Collect both Energy Tanks and all three missile tanks in the lair on the way down. By the time you reach Ridley you should have significant health and at least 60 missiles.

**Ridley:** He flies back and forth and fires fireballs from his mouth. The key to this fight is learning his range. When he shoots close-range fireballs, back away to the far platform and fire missiles from distance. When he shoots long-range fireballs — identifiable by them traveling across most of the screen — stand directly adjacent to him, since his long shots sail over you at close range.

Ridley has very high health. Missile him consistently and the fight is mechanical rather than difficult once you understand the range logic. Arm cannon shots also damage him, but missiles are far more efficient. Bring 50+ missiles if possible.

Ridley's defeat lowers the second statue head in Tourian.

### Step 5: Tourian

With both bosses dead, return to upper Brinstar and take the shaft that leads to Tourian. Both statue heads are now lowered, and the gate opens.

Tourian is short but brutal. The corridor leading to Mother Brain is lined with Rinkas — floating ring-shaped energy beings that spawn infinitely and track Samus. They cannot be killed. And this corridor is also where the Metroids are.

**Metroids:** Each Metroid must be hit with the Ice Beam to freeze it, then hit with exactly five missiles while frozen. It will thaw after a few seconds — if it does before dying, freeze it again. Frozen Metroids are stationary; use that time efficiently. You will face roughly five Metroids in Tourian's corridors before Mother Brain.

The trick to surviving the Metroid gauntlet: freeze each Metroid the moment you see it. Do not let them grab Samus — a grabbing Metroid drains health very fast and you must freeze-and-missile it off. Move quickly through the corridor once a Metroid is destroyed; the Rinkas respawn constantly and standing still is dangerous.

**Mother Brain:** The final boss room has a long glass tube on the left. Rinkas spawn from generators in the floor — shoot the generators with missiles first to remove the infinite Rinka spawns, then destroy as many as you can reach. You can fire through the generators' holes, so prioritize the ones on your side of the room.

Once the generators are clear, shoot a missile at the glass tube. One missile breaks the glass and exposes Mother Brain's brain. From this point, fire missiles directly at Mother Brain through the opening. She fires Rinkas back at you; dodge them by jumping and crouching. Mother Brain cannot move and has no melee attack — it is a sustained damage-dealing fight against a stationary target with a lot of health. Bring 50+ missiles and enough health to absorb Rinka hits. Fire consistently; the fight ends when her health is exhausted.

After Mother Brain dies you have a short time limit to escape Zebes. Run right, exit the way you came in, and Zebes explodes behind you.

## Endings

The ending sequence changes based on how fast you clear the game. Time is measured from the moment you start a run to the moment Mother Brain dies. Four outcomes:

| Time | Ending |
|---|---|
| Under 1 hour | Samus removes her entire Power Suit and stands in her combat bikini; also gets a congratulatory "YOU'RE GREAT" screen |
| 1–3 hours | Samus removes her helmet. Her face and hair are visible |
| 3–5 hours | Samus removes her helmet briefly |
| Over 5 hours | The standard ending — no reveal |

These are the original NES endings. The time is actual in-game playtime. With save states compressing real-time, a thorough exploration run will still hit the 3–5 hour window. The under-1-hour ending requires a practiced and efficient route; it is the foundation of Metroid speedrunning, which this game essentially invented as a concept.

## Passwords and Codes

Metroid uses a 24-character password grid. When Samus dies, the game generates a password encoding her current item collection, health, and area progress. Write it down or screenshot the screen.

**Generating a password mid-game:** On controller 2 (not controller 1), press Up + A simultaneously. This triggers an instant death that generates a password for your current state without having to find an enemy to die to. Useful for saving progress before a dangerous section.

### Notable Passwords

**NARPAS SWORD0 000000 000000**

All items, infinite health, infinite missiles. Samus starts in Brinstar with everything except Energy Tanks. Both bosses are still alive. Use this to explore Tourian immediately or to experiment with the full power-up set. The "0" characters are the number zero; the password is case-insensitive.

**JUSTIN BAILEY ------ ------**

An accidental password discovered by a player named Justin Bailey who found his name worked. Starts Samus in Brinstar without her Power Suit — her sprite is her in a combat leotard with green hair. Gives most items but not full completion. Also starts with Kraid and Ridley alive. The suit-free Samus sprite is the same as the best-ending reveal.

**000000 000000 000000 000000** (all zeros)

Starts a fresh game exactly as if you had just started, but with a specific configuration of items depending on variation. The all-zero password with some fills in missile packs; varies slightly.

To generate a password for a specific item set, use a Metroid password generator — search "Metroid NES password generator" and fill in what you want. The password encodes every item pickup as a bit in the 24-character string.

## Tips for Emulation

**Save states:** Use them after every significant item pickup and before every boss fight. The original game had no save, only passwords. Emulation removes the password friction entirely. There is no shame in saving before Ridley when you have spent thirty minutes reaching him.

**No in-game map:** Metroid gives you nothing. Draw your own map on paper if you are prone to getting lost, or pull up an external map for reference. The game rewards players who internalize the layout, but there is no rule that you must do it blind.

**Bomb everything:** Hidden passages make up a large fraction of item locations. Any floor or wall that looks slightly different from its neighbors is suspicious. When stuck, morph and bomb the nearest wall.

**Ice Beam before Tourian:** This cannot be overstated. Metroids without Ice Beam cannot be killed. You will die.

**Do not pick up Wave Beam if you have Ice Beam:** The beams are mutually exclusive. Wave Beam is the only upgrade in the game that is a trap.

**Energy Tank priority:** The Kraid and Ridley lair tanks are the most important ones to collect, because they are in areas you are already clearing. Do not enter a boss fight with fewer than 300 total health if you can avoid it.
