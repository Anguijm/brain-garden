# Crash Bandicoot 2: Cortex Strikes Back (PlayStation)

**Developer:** Naughty Dog | **Publisher:** Sony | **Year:** 1997

---

## Introduction

The middle Crash game, and the one that fixed the first. *Crash Bandicoot* was punishing, had no save system worth the name, and its levels were largely corridors. The sequel added **saving anywhere**, a hub world, far better level variety, and a much fairer difficulty curve.

Naughty Dog achieved the PlayStation's most detailed character models by rendering along fixed camera paths rather than in free 3D — the corridors are a technical decision, and the reason Crash looks better than anything around him.

---

## Controls

- **D-pad / stick:** Move. **X:** Jump. **Square:** Spin attack.
- **Circle / R1:** **Slide** — and **slide then jump** goes further than either alone.
- **Down + X:** Body slam, breaks metal-banded crates.

**The slide-jump is the game's hidden depth.** It covers more distance than a running jump and it is required for several gems.

---

## Structure

A **hub with five warp rooms**, each holding five levels plus a boss. Crystals are the main objective — one per level, usually on the critical path.

**Gems are the real content**, and there are two kinds:

**Clear gems** for breaking **every crate** in a level — which means you cannot die once, since dying resets crate counts for the section.

**Coloured gems**, hidden down secret routes, which unlock alternate paths in *other* levels.

---

## Secret routes

Several levels have hidden exits — a wall you slide through, a platform you reach by refusing to take the obvious jump, a death route entered by not dying. **Death routes** are the notable ones: enter a checkpoint-free path only if you have reached that point without losing a life, and the reward is a gem.

---

## Strategy

**Break every crate as you go** if you want the gem; going back means replaying the level.

**Slide-jump for distance**, always.

**Do not spin nitro crates.** Green nitro explodes on contact — only the green exclamation-mark switch clears them.

**Look for routes that punish greed.** Several gems are behind ignoring an obvious bonus.

**Body slam metal-banded crates**; spin does nothing to them.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's PlayStation core, and benefits well from raised internal resolution.

The fixed-path camera means the game does not suffer the wobbly-polygon look of free-roaming PS1 3D — it is one of the better-preserved visual styles of the era.
