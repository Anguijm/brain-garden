# Chrono Cross (PlayStation)

**Developer:** Square | **Publisher:** Square | **Year:** 1999 (US 2000)

---

## Introduction

The sequel to [Chrono Trigger](topics/games/snes/chrono-trigger), and one of the most divisive follow-ups ever made. It shares a world and some threads, but almost no characters, and it deliberately complicates the earlier game's ending in ways a portion of the audience has never forgiven.

Taken on its own terms it is superb: Yasunori Mitsuda's best score, a genuinely novel battle system, and **forty-five recruitable characters**.

Serge, a boy from a fishing village, walks into a parallel world where he died ten years ago.

---

## Controls

- **X:** Confirm. **Circle:** Cancel. **Triangle:** Menu. **Square:** Map.

---

## Combat — stamina, not turns

No experience points from ordinary fights, and no turn order in the usual sense.

Each character has a **stamina bar**. Attacking spends stamina; it refills while others act. You choose between **weak, medium and fierce** attacks, costing 1, 2 and 3 stamina, with correspondingly lower hit chances.

**Landing weaker attacks raises the accuracy of stronger ones this turn.** So the rhythm is: weak, weak, fierce — build accuracy, then commit.

**Elements** are the magic system: consumable-but-refilling abilities slotted into a grid that expands as you progress. Casting also costs stamina.

**The Field Effect** is the clever part: every element cast tints the battlefield toward its colour, and casting an element matching the field strengthens it while the opposite weakens. Both sides share this, so you can be set up against.

---

## Levelling without experience

**Ordinary battles give almost nothing.** Stats rise only after **boss fights**, through "star levels", and there is a cap on how much any character can gain between them.

**This means grinding is pointless**, by design. You cannot overlevel your way past a wall; you have to play better or build better.

---

## Forty-five characters

Recruited through choices, side paths and sometimes simply talking to the right person. **Many are mutually exclusive** — recruiting one locks out another. No single playthrough gets everyone.

Most are mechanically thin, but the breadth is the point.

---

## Strategy

**Weak-weak-fierce.** Learn this rhythm immediately; it is the whole battle system.

**Watch the Field Effect** before casting a summon — they require a matching field.

**Do not grind.** It does nothing.

**Accept you will miss characters.** New Game Plus exists for that reason.
