## The hidden requirement that hides half a game

**Symphony of the Night** appears to end after a few hours. Beating that boss normally gives a short,
flat ending and roughly 20% map completion. The real game — an entire **inverted second castle** —
opens only if you equip three specific items beforehand, and **nothing in the game tells you this**.
It is the most famous hidden requirement in the genre and a first-time player will miss it.

## Tank controls were a solution, not laziness

**Resident Evil 2** and **Silent Hill** both use them, and both do so for the same reason: the camera
cuts between fixed angles. "Up" meaning *the way the character faces* keeps your movement consistent
across a cut, where screen-relative controls would reverse your direction every time the shot
changed. The retro-styled horror games being made today deliberately reintroduce them.

## One case where you should NOT raise the resolution

Emulation usually rewards higher internal resolution, but **Silent Hill is the exception**. Its fog,
dithering and low-resolution textures were tuned for a soft composite signal; rendered sharply, the
fog banding becomes visible and the effect that carries the whole game weakens. Native resolution
with a CRT shader is closer to what Team Silent built.

**Final Fantasy VII** and **Chrono Cross** have a related problem: their backgrounds are fixed
pre-rendered images, so sharpening the 3D characters makes the mismatch more obvious, not less.

**Tekken 3**, **Spyro** and the Crash games are fully 3D and sharpen cleanly.

## Multi-disc

**Final Fantasy VII** (3 discs), **Metal Gear Solid** (2), **Chrono Cross** (2) and **Resident Evil 2**
(2, one per character) each appear as a single entry here, grouped by an `.m3u` playlist with the
individual discs hidden.
