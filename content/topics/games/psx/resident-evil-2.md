# Resident Evil 2: Dual Shock Ver. (PlayStation)

**Developer:** Capcom | **Publisher:** Capcom | **Year:** 1998

---

## Introduction

The game that turned Resident Evil from a cult success into a franchise. Where the first was one mansion, this is a whole city falling — Raccoon City, forty-eight hours after the outbreak, following rookie officer **Leon Kennedy** and college student **Claire Redfield**.

An entire earlier version was built to 60–80% completion and scrapped by director Hideki Kamiya as not good enough. Fans call it *Resident Evil 1.5*; it leaked years later.

---

## Controls — tank controls

- **Up:** Walk forward **in the direction the character faces**.
- **Left / Right:** **Rotate**, not strafe.
- **R1 + Up:** Run. **R1:** Aim. **X while aiming:** Fire.
- **Circle:** Action. **Triangle:** Status/inventory.

**You cannot move and shoot.** Aiming plants your feet.

Tank controls are not laziness: the camera cuts between fixed angles, and "up" meaning "the way the character faces" keeps movement consistent across a cut. Screen-relative controls would reverse your direction every time the shot changed.

---

## The Zapping System

The reason this game is structured unusually. You pick Leon or Claire, and finish their campaign — that is **Scenario A**.

Then you play the *other* character's **Scenario B**, which runs concurrently through the same city. **What you did in A changes B**: doors you unlocked stay unlocked, items you took are gone, and Mr. X — an unkillable pursuer — appears only in the B scenario.

**Four combinations exist** (Leon A/Claire B, Claire A/Leon B, and the reverse pairs), and the true ending requires playing an A and a B. Playing only one campaign sees roughly half the game.

---

## Survival horror economics

**Ammunition is scarce; most zombies should be avoided**, not killed. A zombie left alive is a zombie you walk past next time.

**Inventory slots are limited** — Leon has 8, Claire has 8, and every key, herb and clip competes.

**Saving costs an ink ribbon**, and they are finite.

**Zombies get back up.** A downed zombie is not dead unless the head is gone.

---

## Strategy

**Run past what you can.** Doorways reset rooms.

**Combine herbs** rather than using them singly — green plus red plus blue is a full heal with cure.

**Leave a weapon in the item box** before points of no return.

**Aim up at Lickers**, and avoid running near them — they hunt by sound.

**Play an A then a B scenario.** Anything else is half the story.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's PlayStation core. **Two discs — one per character — grouped into a single entry** by an `.m3u` playlist here.

**This is the Dual Shock version**, which adds analogue stick support and vibration. It also **replaced the original's soundtrack** with a rearranged one, and a vocal minority prefers the original. Analogue control is the better trade on a modern pad.

The 2019 remake is a different game built on modern controls, not a remaster.
