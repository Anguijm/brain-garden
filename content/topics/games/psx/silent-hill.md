# Silent Hill (PlayStation)

**Developer:** Konami (Team Silent) | **Publisher:** Konami | **Year:** 1999

---

## Introduction

Where *Resident Evil* is a horror action game about monsters, *Silent Hill* is a horror game about dread. Harry Mason crashes his car outside a small American town looking for his adopted daughter, and the town is empty, and then it is not.

The famous technical detail: the PlayStation could not draw the town at any distance, so Team Silent filled it with **fog**. The limitation became the identity — you cannot see what is coming, and the radio bursts into static when something is near.

It is fully 3D where its contemporaries used pre-rendered backgrounds, which is why it has aged into looking deliberately unreal.

---

## Controls

- **D-pad / stick:** Move (tank-style by default; a 2D control option exists).
- **X:** Action / confirm. **Square:** Attack / fire. **Circle:** Run.
- **Triangle:** Map. **Select:** Inventory.

---

## The radio and the flashlight

**The radio emits static when monsters are near** and gets louder as they close. It is your only warning through the fog, and turning it off makes the game far more dangerous — and far more frightening.

**The flashlight** lets you see in the dark areas, and simultaneously **makes you visible to enemies**. Turning it off is sometimes correct.

---

## Combat, deliberately bad

Harry is a writer, not a soldier. He swings a pipe clumsily, aims a pistol shakily, and gets exhausted. **This is intentional** — the fear comes from being poorly equipped.

**Running past enemies is usually correct.** Almost nothing must be killed.

---

## The Otherworld

The town periodically transforms — rust, chain-link, blood, darkness — into a nightmare version of the same geography. The shift is the game's signature and it is never explained mechanically, only thematically.

---

## Multiple endings

Determined by actions across the game: whether you save a certain character, whether you find specific items, and how you handle the final sequence. **There are five**, including a joke UFO ending, and the "good" ones require specific optional steps.

---

## Strategy

**Keep the radio on.** Turning it off is a difficulty increase disguised as an atmosphere choice.

**Map everything.** Harry annotates the map automatically — read it, it marks locked doors.

**Run rather than fight.** Ammunition is for bosses.

**Save often.** Progress between save points can be long.

**Look up the ending requirements** if you want a specific one; they are not guessable.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs well on Batocera's PlayStation core.

**Do not raise internal resolution far.** This is unusual advice, but Silent Hill's fog, dithering and low-resolution textures were tuned for a soft composite-video signal. At high resolution the fog banding becomes visible and the effect weakens. Native resolution with a CRT shader is genuinely closer to the intended experience.
