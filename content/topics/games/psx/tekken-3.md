# Tekken 3 (PlayStation)

**Developer:** Namco | **Publisher:** Namco | **Year:** 1998

---

## Introduction

One of the best-selling and best-regarded fighting games ever made, and a conversion that had no business being as good as it was — the arcade version ran on hardware far beyond the PlayStation, and Namco rebuilt it to fit while keeping it feeling identical.

It added **Tekken Force**, a side-scrolling beat 'em up mode, and **Tekken Ball**, a volleyball game, both of which are more substantial than console-exclusive extras usually were.

---

## Controls

Four buttons, mapped to limbs — which is the series' defining idea:

- **Square:** Left punch. **Triangle:** Right punch.
- **X:** Left kick. **Circle:** Right kick.
- **Back:** Block. **Down-back:** Crouch block.

**Each button is a limb, not a strength.** This is why Tekken's move lists are enormous and why inputs read like `f, f+2` rather than quarter-circles.

---

## What changed in Tekken 3

**Movement became three-dimensional.** Earlier Tekken games were 2D planes; here you can **sidestep** into the background or foreground by tapping up or down. That single addition changed the series permanently.

**Air juggles** became central. Launch an opponent and continue hitting them before they land — most serious damage comes from juggles.

---

## Fundamentals

**Block low by default.** Most attacks are mid or low; standing block loses to sweeps.

**Sidestep, then punish.** Many strong moves are linear and whiff entirely if you step.

**Learn your character's 10-hit string** — and then learn that it is mostly a trap, because they are interruptible. Knowing where they break matters more than executing them.

**Throws are escaped** by pressing the matching limb button as they land.

---

## The roster

Twenty-three characters. **Jin Kazama**, **Ling Xiaoyu**, **Hwoarang**, **Eddy Gordo** and **Bryan Fury** debut here. **Eddy** is famously effective for beginners — his capoeira strings are hard to read and mostly safe — which is either a virtue or a nuisance depending on which side of the controller you are.

---

## Strategy

**Pick one character and learn ten moves**, not a hundred.

**Sidestep constantly.**

**Crouch block, stand to punish.**

**Practise juggle starters** — the damage difference is enormous.

**Play Tekken Force.** It is a genuine bonus mode, not a menu item.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs very well on Batocera's PlayStation core, and this is a game where **raising internal resolution pays off dramatically** — it is fully 3D with no pre-rendered art, so everything sharpens together.

The PlayStation version's loading and character models were cut down from the arcade original, but the gameplay is the arcade's.
