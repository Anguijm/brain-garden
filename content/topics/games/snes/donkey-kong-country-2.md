# Donkey Kong Country 2: Diddy's Kong Quest (SNES)

**Developer:** Rare | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 1995

---

## Introduction

The middle game of Rare's trilogy and, by most accounts, the best of the three. Donkey Kong has been captured by K. Rool, now styling himself a pirate, and **Diddy and Dixie** go after him alone.

The first *Donkey Kong Country* sold on its pre-rendered graphics, which looked astonishing in 1994 and dated quickly. This one sold on level design, which has not dated at all.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move; down to crouch.
- **B:** Jump. **Y:** Run, and pick up / throw a barrel or your partner.
- **A:** Swap between Diddy and Dixie.
- **R:** Team-up — carry your partner, then throw them.

---

## Diddy and Dixie

They are not skins:

- **Diddy** is faster and jumps further. His cartwheel covers ground.
- **Dixie** has **helicopter hair** — hold jump after leaving a ledge and she floats down slowly. This is a genuine second jump and it is the difference between reaching a bonus room and not.

**Throwing your partner** is a required mechanic. Dixie throwing Diddy reaches high ledges; Diddy throwing Dixie lets her float across gaps he cannot cross.

---

## Animal buddies

Rambi the rhino charges, Squawks the parrot flies and lights caves, Squitter the spider **shoots web platforms you can stand on**, Rattly the snake jumps enormously, and Enguarde the swordfish handles water.

**Squitter's web platforms** are the most mechanically interesting and appear in some of the game's best stages.

---

## What you are actually collecting

- **KONG letters**, one set per level, for extra lives
- **DK Coins**, one hidden per level, tracked by a character called Cranky
- **Bonus rooms**, two or three per level, each a short challenge
- **Kremkoins**, earned from bonus rooms, spent to open the **Lost World**

**102% completion** requires every DK Coin and every bonus room, and it opens the true final area. The percentage above 100 is deliberate and is a Rare joke.

---

## Strategy

**Use Dixie for anything with a gap.** Her float trivialises platforming Diddy has to nail.

**Look above and below the visible path.** Bonus barrels are hidden in walls, ceilings and off-screen ledges.

**Team-up throws reach most secrets.** If a ledge looks impossible, throw your partner at it.

**Save your Kremkoins.** They are the Lost World currency and there are exactly enough.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's SNES core with **rewind enabled**, which is welcome — this game is considerably harder than the first, especially in the Lost World.

David Wise's soundtrack is regularly named among the best on the system; the underwater theme "Aquatic Ambiance" belongs to the first game, but "Stickerbush Symphony" here is the one people remember.
