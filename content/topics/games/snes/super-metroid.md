# Super Metroid (SNES)

**Developer:** Nintendo R&D1 / Intelligent Systems | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 1994

---

## Introduction

The game that named a genre. "Metroidvania" is half this and half *Symphony of the Night*, and this is the half that came first.

Samus returns to Zebes after the events of [Metroid II](topics/games/gb/metroid-ii), chasing Ridley, who has stolen the last Metroid. There is almost no text. The story is told through the environment, and it works.

Thirty years on it is still the reference point for how to build an interconnected world that teaches you without telling you anything.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move; down to morph ball.
- **A:** Jump. **B:** Run/Speed Booster. **X:** Shoot. **Y:** Run.
- **L / R:** Aim diagonally up and down. **Select:** Cycle special weapons. **Start:** Map.

**The controls are reconfigurable and the defaults are unusual.** Most players remap jump and shoot. It is worth doing in the options menu before starting rather than fighting it for three hours.

---

## The design nobody has improved on

Zebes is a single connected space. There are no levels. You go where your equipment lets you, and every ability doubles as a key:

| Ability | Opens |
|---|---|
| **Morph Ball / Bombs** | Small passages, bomb-jumping |
| **High Jump / Space Jump** | Vertical shafts |
| **Speed Booster** | Speed blocks, shinespark |
| **Grapple Beam** | Gaps and swinging routes |
| **Gravity Suit** | Free movement underwater and in lava |
| **Screw Attack** | Destroys enemies and specific blocks on contact |

**The map is the game's memory.** It marks doors you could not open and rooms with items you could not reach. Reading it is the intended way to decide where to go.

---

## Sequence breaking

Super Metroid is famously **breakable**, and Nintendo knew. Techniques like the mockball, wall-jumping past intended gates and bomb-jumping let a skilled player collect items in almost any order. Speedruns finish it in under half an hour.

**None of this is required.** But the game silently rewards experimentation in a way modern design rarely does, and the fact that a wall-jump you discovered yourself opens a route the designers anticipated is a large part of why it endures.

---

## Strategy

**Shoot and bomb everything.** Breakable blocks are visually identical to solid ones. This is the single most important habit.

**Learn the wall jump.** The game teaches it in an optional room with animals, and it is easy to miss entirely.

**Save often, in different slots.** It is possible, though rare, to strand yourself.

**Watch your item percentage.** The ending changes based on completion and time.

**Charge beam plus a special weapon combine.** Charged shots with Ice, Wave, Spazer or Plasma selected do something different from either alone.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's SNES core with **rewind enabled**.

The soundtrack is one of the best on the system and the mix is dense — worth hearing on something better than a TV speaker.

Widely regarded as one of the greatest video games ever made, and it is one of the rare cases where that reputation is not nostalgia. [Metroid Fusion](topics/games/gba/metroid-fusion) on this box is the direct sequel in tone, though far more directed.
