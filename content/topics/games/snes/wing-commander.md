# Wing Commander (SNES)

**Developer:** Origin Systems / Mindscape | **Publisher:** Mindscape | **Year:** 1992

---

## Introduction

A port of the 1990 PC game that more or less invented the cinematic space combat simulator. You are a rookie pilot on the carrier *Tiger's Claw*, fighting the cat-like Kilrathi, and between missions you walk around the ship talking to your squadmates in a bar.

**The between-mission material is why it mattered.** Before this, a space shooter was a space shooter. Chris Roberts built a war story around it with characters who could die permanently and a campaign that branched based on whether you won.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Pitch and yaw.
- **A:** Fire guns. **B:** Fire missiles. **Y:** Cycle weapons. **X:** Cycle targets.
- **L / R:** Roll, and afterburner on some configurations.
- **Start:** Navigation and communications menus.

The SNES pad is a compromise for a game designed around a joystick and a full keyboard. It works, but expect the controls to feel indirect for the first few missions.

---

## The branching campaign

**Losing does not end the game.** Fail a mission and the campaign branches down a losing path — you retreat to a different system and fight a harder war. Win consistently and you advance along the winning track toward the good ending.

This means a run where you struggle tells a different story rather than stopping. It also means you can end the game having lost the war.

---

## Wingmen

You fly with a wingman who has a personality, a skill level, and a life. Order them to attack, break formation or return to base with the communications menu.

**They can die permanently.** A wingman killed is gone for the rest of the campaign, replaced by someone else, and their absence is noted in the bar.

---

## Combat

Energy management is the core: **guns drain a shared pool** that recharges, shields regenerate, armour does not. Missiles are limited and should be saved for targets that are hard to hold in a gunsight.

**Do not fly straight.** The Kilrathi lead their shots, and the single most common cause of death is holding a steady line while lining up a kill.

---

## Strategy

**Use the afterburner to close, then cut it.** Overshooting a target hands them your tail.

**Watch the shield indicator by facing.** Turning a fresh shield face toward the enemy buys time.

**Order your wingman to break and attack** rather than leaving them on your wing; they survive better with something to do.

**Save between missions.** The campaign branch is determined at mission end.

## Emulation Notes

Runs on Batocera's SNES core with **rewind enabled**.

**The SNES port runs noticeably slower than the PC original** — the hardware is doing filled-polygon 3D it was never designed for, and the frame rate shows it in busy dogfights. It is playable and it is the version on this box; be aware that the PC release is the definitive one.
