# Street Fighter II Turbo: Hyper Fighting (SNES)

**Developer:** Capcom | **Publisher:** Capcom | **Year:** 1993

---

## Introduction

The home version of *Hyper Fighting*, the third arcade revision of *Street Fighter II* and the one competitive players consider the peak of the original design.

What "Turbo" means literally: the game runs faster. Capcom made it that way because arcade operators were hacking their machines to speed the game up, and Capcom decided to ship it officially.

---

## Controls

Six buttons, which the SNES pad has exactly:

- **Y / X / L:** Light, medium, heavy **punch**
- **B / A / R:** Light, medium, heavy **kick**
- **D-Pad:** Move, jump (up), crouch (down), block (back)

**This is why the Super Nintendo won the Street Fighter war.** The Mega Drive's standard pad had three buttons, and the six-button pad arrived later — a fact that shaped which console serious players bought in 1993. The Mega Drive version of *Mortal Kombat II* on this box has the same lineage problem, and the six-button pad configuration is set on this machine for that reason.

---

## Motions

- **Fireball (Hadouken):** down, down-forward, forward + punch
- **Dragon punch (Shoryuken):** forward, down, down-forward + punch
- **Hurricane kick:** down, down-back, back + kick
- **Charge moves** (Guile, Blanka, Honda): hold back or down for about two seconds, then the opposite direction + button

**Charge characters are the easier entry point.** Holding a direction and pressing forward is far more forgiving than the dragon-punch motion.

---

## Twelve characters

The eight originals plus the four bosses — Balrog, Vega, Sagat, M. Bison — playable here, which the first home version did not allow.

**Turbo is where the roster balance settled.** Every character has an answer to every other, which was not true in the earlier revisions.

---

## Fundamentals

The things that actually win matches, in order:

1. **Block.** Holding back stops most of what kills new players.
2. **Anti-air.** Punish jump-ins. If you never punish a jump, you will be jumped on forever.
3. **Spacing.** Most rounds are decided by who controls the distance, not who lands the flashy move.
4. **Throws.** Close range, and they beat blocking.

Special moves come fifth. That ordering is counterintuitive and it is correct.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's SNES core.

**Rewind is enabled but is close to useless here** — a fighting game against the computer does not have the kind of mistake rewind fixes, and against a second player it is meaningless.

The SNES pad layout maps cleanly onto a modern controller's face buttons and shoulders, which is how it is configured on this box.
