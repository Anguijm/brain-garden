# Final Fantasy III (SNES)

**Developer:** Square | **Publisher:** Square | **Year:** 1994 (US)

---

## Introduction

**This is Final Fantasy VI.** Square only released three of the first six games in North America and renumbered them as they went, so the sixth Japanese entry shipped here as the third. Every release since has used the correct number.

You also have the [Game Boy Advance version](topics/games/gba/final-fantasy-vi-advance) on this box. That one has the better translation and the bug fixes; this one has the better sound.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move. **A:** Confirm. **B:** Cancel / dash.
- **X:** Menu. **Y:** Map on the world screen. **L / R:** Cycle pages.

---

## No protagonist

Fourteen playable characters, and the game refuses to pick one. Terra opens it, Celes carries the middle, and the second half explicitly scatters the party so you rebuild it from whoever you choose to find.

**Several are permanently missable.** Umaro, Gogo, Mog and Shadow all require specific actions, and the game will let you reach the ending without them and never mention it.

---

## The midpoint

Roughly halfway, **the villain wins.** Kefka succeeds, the world is torn apart, and what follows is not a setback but a permanent change: the World of Ruin is a different map with a different tone and an open structure where the first half was linear.

It remains one of the most effective structural turns in the genre, and it is why people who bounce off the first ten hours are told to keep going.

---

## Espers and the sameness problem

Espers are summons that teach magic and grant stat bonuses on level-up. **Any character can learn any spell**, so a party left to drift becomes four identical mages.

The game does not stop this, and it is the standard criticism of its balance. If you want the cast to stay distinct, assign espers by role deliberately — Sabin physical, Edgar on tools, Cyan on Bushido — rather than teaching everyone Ultima.

---

## Character commands worth learning

- **Sabin's Blitz** — fighting-game inputs, and his best damage all game
- **Gau's Rage** — learned from monsters on the Veldt, enormous and badly explained
- **Setzer's Slot** — random, and one outcome is a guaranteed kill
- **Edgar's Tools** — reliable, ignores most defences
- **Cyan's Bushido** — charge-based, slow, very strong at full charge

---

## Strategy

**Wait for Shadow on the Floating Continent.** Leaving early loses him permanently, and the game gives you every reason to run.

**Equip espers for stat gains before levelling.** The bonuses compound across the whole game.

**Save before the opera house.** It is timed and easy to fail.

**Recruit everyone in the World of Ruin** before the final dungeon. Each has a scene worth seeing.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's SNES core with **rewind enabled**.

**Two bugs from the original are intact here.** The Evade stat does nothing — the code checks Magic Evade twice — and the Vanish/Doom combination instantly kills almost anything, including bosses that should be immune. The Game Boy Advance version fixes both. Whether that is an argument for or against this version depends on whether you consider them part of the artefact.
