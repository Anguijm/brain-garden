# Super Castlevania IV (SNES)

**Developer:** Konami | **Publisher:** Konami | **Year:** 1991

---

## Introduction

A remake of the original *Castlevania* rather than a sequel, and the game where Konami showed what the Super Nintendo could do. Simon Belmont walks to Dracula's castle again, and this time the whip works properly.

It is the most **accessible** classic-era Castlevania by a wide margin, and among purists that is also the complaint.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move; down to crouch.
- **B:** Jump. **Y:** Whip — **and here it aims in eight directions**.
- **Hold Y:** The whip goes limp and can be **waggled** with the D-pad, dealing small damage and deflecting projectiles.
- **X:** Sub-weapon. **L / R:** Nothing standard.

### The eight-direction whip

Every earlier Castlevania let you whip **forward only**. Here you can whip up, down, and on every diagonal.

**This single change is why the game is considered easy.** Enemies above and below you stop being threats. It is also, mechanically, the most satisfying whip in the series, and the games after this went back to forward-only because of how much it defused.

---

## Grappling

Whip a **ceiling ring** and Simon swings from it. These sections are set pieces and they are the closest the classic series gets to traversal design.

---

## Sub-weapons

Axe, holy water, cross, dagger, stopwatch — collected from candles, powered by hearts (which are ammunition here, not health).

**Holy water is the strongest**, as in every Castlevania: it stuns most enemies and does high damage against bosses. The cross is the best crowd-clear.

---

## What the hardware is doing

Stage 4's rotating room and the swinging chandeliers were the reason this game appeared in shops as a demo. Mode 7 scaling and rotation are used throughout, and unusually they are used for level design rather than decoration — the room that rotates around you is genuinely disorienting to platform through.

---

## Strategy

**Use the diagonal whip constantly.** Players coming from the NES games instinctively line up horizontally and make the game harder than it is.

**Waggle the whip against projectiles.** Holding Y and moving deflects bones and fireballs that would otherwise be unavoidable.

**Hold onto holy water for bosses.**

**Hearts are ammunition.** Running dry before a boss is the usual cause of a lost run.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's SNES core with **rewind enabled**.

The soundtrack is a high point for the system's audio hardware, and "Simon's Theme" here is the arrangement most people know. [Castlevania: Bloodlines](topics/games/megadrive/castlevania-bloodlines) on the Mega Drive is the same era's other console Castlevania and a much harder game; [Aria of Sorrow](topics/games/gba/castlevania-aria-of-sorrow) on this box is where the series went afterwards.
