## The numbering mess

The cartridge labelled **Final Fantasy III** is *Final Fantasy VI*. Square released only three of
the first six games in North America and renumbered them on the way, so the sixth became the third.
Every release since has used the real number, which is why the [Game Boy Advance version](topics/games/gba/final-fantasy-vi-advance)
on this box is called *Final Fantasy VI Advance*. Same game, two names, both here.

## Six buttons, and why the console war turned on it

**Street Fighter II Turbo** needs six attack buttons, and the SNES pad has exactly six. The Mega
Drive shipped with three, and its six-button pad arrived late. That hardware decision is a real
part of why serious fighting-game players bought Nintendo in 1993, and it is still felt on this
box: the Mega Drive is configured for the six-button pad specifically so
[Mortal Kombat II](topics/games/megadrive/mortal-kombat-ii) has a low punch and a high punch.

## Two bugs left intact

The SNES **Final Fantasy III** has a broken Evade stat that does nothing, and the Vanish/Doom
combination kills almost any target instantly, bosses included. The Game Boy Advance version fixes
both. Whether that makes the SNES version worse or simply the original artefact is a genuine
argument.

## The one that was a commercial failure

**EarthBound** was marketed in America with scratch-and-sniff cards and the slogan "this game
stinks", sold badly, and became a cult object afterwards. Its rolling HP meter — damage applies
gradually, so a fatal blow is survivable if you win or heal in time — has never been meaningfully
copied by anything.
