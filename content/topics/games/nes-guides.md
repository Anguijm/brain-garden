# NES game guides

Walkthroughs, secrets, and tips for the twelve NES games on the [Batocera top-games list](batocera-top-games). Each entry covers the fastest route through the game, the secrets worth knowing, and the things that will kill you if nobody tells you first. These are playing guides, not completion guides — the goal is to get through the game and have fun, not to document every pixel.

ROMs are installed on the UM790Pro at `/userdata/roms/nes/`. Save states are enabled; use them.

## Super Mario Bros. 3

**The basics:** Eight worlds, each with a map screen. You choose which levels to play on the map; completing a level marks it done. Airship levels at the end of each world hold a Koopaling boss — beat them to get a Wand and unlock the next world.

**Power-ups that matter:**
- **Super Leaf (Raccoon Mario):** The best power-up in the game. Flying requires a full sprint and a held A press. Once airborne you can fly indefinitely by tapping A. Gets you to secret areas and over obstacles that would otherwise require lives.
- **Tanooki Suit:** Raccoon Mario but you can also turn into a statue to dodge enemies. Rare.
- **Frog Suit:** Needed for Water World; move faster in water, hop awkwardly on land.
- **Hammer Bros. Suit:** Throws hammers, crouch to block most projectiles. Extremely powerful.
- **P-Wing:** One-time instant-fly for an entire level. Save for the levels you hate.

**Inventory system:** Items you collect on the map carry over. Hoard Mushrooms and P-Wings. Before a hard level, open the inventory (Select on the map) and equip something useful.

**World 1 secrets:**
- World 1-3: There's a White Mushroom House — get to the flagpole in 1-3 with a coin count ending in 2 (22, 32, etc.) and a path appears on the map. Gives a P-Wing.
- World 1 Fortress: You can skip it by flying over the top of the screen.

**Warp whistles (skip worlds):**
- **Whistle 1:** World 1-3. Near the end, duck on the white platform behind the tall black pipe structure for five seconds. You fall through to a hidden area. Grab the chest for a Toad House, then open it for the whistle.
- **Whistle 2:** World 1 Fortress. In the room with the moving platforms, go to the chest near the end and press Up while standing on it — you access a secret area, then ground-pound through a hidden brick block to find it.
- **Whistle 3:** World 2 map. Beat World 2 quickly; a Hammer Bros. sometimes blocks a Toad House — if the Toad House contains a whistle, it will be there regardless of which Bros. you encounter. (Note: the third whistle isn't necessary — two will warp you to World 8 directly.)

Using a whistle opens the Warp Zone. Use two whistles to go directly to World 8.

**World 8 (Dark Land):** Long. Tank levels, airship gauntlet, Bowser's castle. The castle is linear but Bowser is easy — jump on his head three times in the airship room, the first hit requires timing past his fire breath, subsequent hits are straightforward.

**Boss patterns (Koopalings):**
- Larry (W1): Simple jump-on-head three times.
- Morton (W2): Stomps create shockwaves — jump each shockwave, hit him when he's stunned. Three hits.
- Wendy (W3): Bouncing rings. Hit her when the rings clear, three times.
- Iggy (W4): On a ball above lava. Hit three times, harder version moves faster.
- Roy (W5): Creates a shockwave when hitting the ground. Jump it, hit him.
- Lemmy (W6): Hides among bouncing balls, hit the real one three times.
- Larry again (W7): Same as W1.
- Bowser: Three hits to the head. Each hit he moves faster.

**Lives farming:** World 1-1 has a shell near the end. Position Mario at the right step, kick the shell into the steps and hold Right while standing on the step — infinite 1-Ups until you let go.

## Mega Man 2

**Boss order (easiest to hardest, gaining weapons as you go):**

1. **Metal Man** — Start here. His stage is short and his weakness is his own weapon (Metal Blade). Dodge his saw blades by jumping at the right height. Three full arm-cannon bars to kill him.
2. **Air Man** — Difficult without Metal Man's weapon. The Metal Blade cuts through his tornadoes. Aim slightly down-left to hit him through the screen.
3. **Bubble Man** — Use Metal Blade. Underwater stage; stay near the floor, avoid the spike ceiling.
4. **Quick Man** — The hardest stage in the game. The disappearing block rooms with laser beams: use Flash Stopper (from Flash Man) to freeze the beams if you have it, or memorize the pattern. Three screens of this. Quick Man himself is hit by Flash Stopper for massive damage.
5. **Crash Man** — Use Air Shooter. Stage has Doc Robots later in the game that are harder than the boss.
6. **Flash Man** — Use Crash Bomber. He freezes time; hit him during the freeze window.
7. **Heat Man** — Use Bubble Lead. His stage has disappearing blocks over lava. Take your time.
8. **Wood Man** — Use Metal Blade or Atomic Fire. Leaf Shield is annoying; dash in when it deploys to get close.

**Dr. Wily's fortress (4 stages):**
- Stage 1: Guts-Dozer boss. Use Crash Bomber on the eye.
- Stage 2: Picopico-kun swarm. Crash Bomber.
- Stage 3: Dragon boss in water. Use Crash Bomber on his head when he opens.
- Stage 4: Boobeam Trap. You need exactly 5 Crash Bombs. Do not waste any before this fight.
- **Wily himself:** Two forms. First form: Alien illusion — use Bubble Lead (it's immune to everything else). Second form: Direct hit with arm cannon.

**Essential secrets:**
- **Item 1, 2, 3:** Beat Air Man (Item 2), Flash Man (Item 3), Heat Man (Item 1). These are platforms/hooks that Mega Man summons. Item 1 is a rising platform, Item 2 is a jet platform, Item 3 is a wall-climbing hook. Required to reach certain areas.
- **Energy tanks:** Found in stages. Never use them until you're stuck on a boss. Wily has six fights worth of bosses — bring four tanks minimum.
- **Metal Blade:** Fires in eight directions and costs almost no weapon energy. Use it constantly. It replaces the arm cannon for most situations.
- **Yashichi (1-Up):** In the Crash Man stage, there's a room with a large yellow health item. Before the zip-line section, destroy the blocks to reveal a Yashichi — fully refills all weapons and health.

## Mega Man 3

**Boss order:**

Mega Man 3 introduces the slide mechanic and Rush (dog companion). Rush Coil lets you reach higher platforms; Rush Marine handles water; Rush Jet is a rideable platform.

1. **Shadow Man** — Start here, his stage is manageable and the Top Spin you get is one of the most powerful weapons in the game (if energy-inefficient). His weakness is the Spark Shock (from Spark Man), but you can kill him with the arm cannon.
2. **Snake Man** — Hit with Needle Cannon (Needle Man's weapon). The Search Snake weapon is useful for Doc Robot fights later.
3. **Spark Man** — Use Shadow Blade (Shadow Man). Stage has two Rush Jet sections.
4. **Gemini Man** — Use Spark Shock. His stage has ice floors and he splits into two — hit the real one (the one that moves toward you first).
5. **Hard Man** — Use Gemini Laser. He pounds the floor; jump each shockwave and fire in the air.
6. **Top Man** — Use Hard Knuckle. Three hits ends it.
7. **Magnet Man** — Use Top Spin. One hit kills if you time it right, but Top Spin drains fast. Arm cannon works if you're patient.
8. **Needle Man** — Use Magnet Missiles. His stage is the hardest in the game — spike columns everywhere.

**Doc Robots (re-fights):** Four fortress stages each contain two Doc Robot fights using Mega Man 2 boss patterns. Stock up on energy tanks before attempting them. Weakness weapons from MM2 still apply.

**Break Man encounters:** Proto Man ambushes you between stages. He slides, shoots, and jumps. He is not defeatable — you fight until he leaves or kills you. Survive three encounters.

**Wily stages:** Six stages. The final Wily fight is a two-phase battle similar to MM2 — Gamma robot (arm cannon), then Wily himself (arm cannon to the capsule).

**Secrets:**
- **Rush Jet exploit:** On Rush Jet, fly to the left edge of a room and pass through walls into bonus areas with extra lives and energy tanks. Works in several stages.
- **Slide under enemies:** The slide lets you pass under bullets and through certain enemy attacks. Gemini's laser can be slid under.
- **Energy tank farming:** Stage select lets you replay stages. Doc Robot stages have energy tank drops; replay Needle Man's stage for tanks.

## Metroid

**Save system:** Metroid has no in-game save on the NES. Use save states at every item pickup. The password system works but produces long strings.

**The map (from memory — there's no in-game map):**

Brinstar → Norfair → Kraid's lair → Ridley's lair → Tourian. Tourian is the final area; you need both Kraid and Ridley defeated to open it.

**Essential items and where to find them:**
- **Maru Mari (Morph Ball):** Brinstar, very early. Required for everything. Lets Samus roll into small passages.
- **Bombs:** Morph Ball bombs. In Brinstar. Required for most secret passages.
- **Long Beam:** Brinstar. Makes your beam hit across the full screen. Get this early.
- **Ice Beam:** Norfair. Freezes enemies — most importantly, freezes Metroids. Required to defeat them safely.
- **Wave Beam:** Norfair. Fires through walls. Useful but not essential.
- **High Jump Boots:** Norfair. Dramatically increases jump height. Makes most traversal much easier.
- **Screw Attack:** Norfair. Makes the spinning jump kill everything it touches. The best offensive upgrade.
- **Varia Suit:** Reduces damage taken by half. Somewhere in the top of Norfair. Find this before going deep.
- **Energy Tanks:** Scattered throughout. Each adds 100 energy. Collect all of them.
- **Missiles:** Required for boss doors (five missiles per boss door). Stock up. Boss doors are the red gates.

**Boss fights:**
- **Kraid:** Two segments. Fire missiles into his open mouth. He throws spikes from his body; the pattern is predictable. Stand on the platforms to his left and time your shots between his attacks.
- **Ridley:** Flies back and forth. Stand on the platform below him and fire missiles upward into him. He fires fireballs; the timing window to avoid them is narrow. Ice Beam helps if you need to clear the smaller enemies.
- **Mother Brain:** First, destroy the Rinkas (floating rings) with missiles, then fire missiles through the glass tube into Mother Brain's brain. She cannot move — she just fires back. Bring full health and at least 75 missiles. The tube takes about 30 missiles; Mother Brain takes another 30. Energy tanks are mandatory.
- **Metroids (in Tourian):** You will face many. Ice Beam freezes them; then fire five missiles into each frozen Metroid. They thaw quickly — refreeze if needed. Without Ice Beam this is extremely difficult.

**Ending times:** Samus reveals more of her suit (and herself) based on your completion time. Under an hour: best ending. These are visible even in emulation with save states — though save states obviously compress actual playtime.

**Password shortcuts:**
- `NARPAS SWORD0 000000 000000` — Infinite missiles, all items, all bosses dead. Lets you skip to Tourian.

## Contra

**The code:** Up Up Down Down Left Right Left Right B A Start. 30 lives. Use it. Nobody is ashamed.

**Weapons (always take these, never drop them):**
- **Spread Gun (S):** The best weapon in the game. Fires in five directions simultaneously. Never drop it voluntarily. If you die holding it, pick up another before advancing.
- **Laser (L):** Long range, penetrates enemies. Second-best for bosses.
- **Machine Gun (M):** Rapid fire. Acceptable substitute if Spread is unavailable.
- **Fireball (F):** Arcs; situationally useful on stages with elevated enemies.

**Stage by stage:**
1. **Jungle:** Run right, shoot everything. The Spread Gun appears on the third or fourth capsule. Grab it immediately. Boss is a turret — hit the central core.
2. **Base 1 (top-down):** Shoot the sensor eyes in the upper corners of each door to advance. Central core boss — Spread Gun makes it trivial.
3. **Waterfall:** Climb, shoot upward, grab Spread Gun again. The boss is a head with an open mouth — shoot into the mouth.
4. **Base 2 (top-down):** Same structure as Base 1. Spread Gun clears it rapidly.
5. **Snow Field:** Similar to Jungle but with more bullets. Boss is a snowman with cannons on the arms — shoot the core in the chest.
6. **Energy Zone (top-down):** Boss is a large core with rotating guns. Dodge the rotation pattern and fire into the center.
7. **Hangar:** The hardest regular stage. Turrets on both sides, flame jets in the floor. Stay moving, shoot constantly. Boss is a mouth with a rotating barrier — wait for the gap and fire.
8. **Alien Lair:** Final stage. Red floating brains (hit them), then the final boss — a ribcage structure with a heart at the center. Destroy all the egg sacs on the left and right walls, then fire into the pulsating mouth/heart. It opens briefly — time your shots.

**Two-player note:** Player 2 (Lance) is functionally identical to Player 1 (Bill). The game is significantly easier with two players — more firepower and you can revive if one player still has lives.

## Castlevania

**Subweapon management:** Your secondary weapon (holy water, dagger, boomerang, etc.) is carried between stages. Holy water and the cross are generally the best; knives are excellent for fast damage.

**Whip upgrades:** Your leather whip upgrades to a chain whip (longer reach) then Vampire Killer (most powerful). Upgrades come from candles. Protect your upgrade — getting hit on stairs sends you flying and the fall often kills.

**Stage 1-3 (Dracula's castle entrance):**
- The Wicked Witch boss has three heads on Stage 1. Hit the center head repeatedly with the whip while avoiding the homing projectiles. Holy water on the floor deals damage to all heads simultaneously.

**Stage 4-6 (the clock tower area):**
- Stage 4 has the infamous staircase section where Medusa Heads float in a wave pattern. They knock you off ledges. Learn the timing. Holy water placed at the top of the stairs creates a damage zone they fly through.
- Stage 5 boss: Mummies. Two of them. Holy water on the floor stuns them; whip while they're stunned.
- Stage 6 boss: Frankenstein's Monster and Igor. Kill Igor (the small one) first; he's the most dangerous. Frankenstein is slow but his floor-smash creates fire patches. The cross boomerang hits both from a distance.

**Stage 7-9 (the keep):**
- Stage 7 is the hardest platforming in the game — stairs over pits, more Medusa Heads. Save state generously.
- Stage 8 boss: Death (Grim Reaper). Scythes orbit him in a pattern. The cross boomerang can clear several scythes simultaneously. Stay mobile, whip his body when the scythes open a path.
- Stage 9 boss: Count Dracula. Two phases.
  - Phase 1: He teleports around the room and throws fireballs. He always teleports to one of three spots (near the left wall, near the right wall, or the center). Track his position after each teleport and whip when he appears. Holy water on a corner forces him to teleport away from it.
  - Phase 2: His head detaches and flies at you, firing three-way fireballs. The head moves in a predictable swooping arc. The cross boomerang is the most effective weapon here — it orbits and hits him regardless of your aim. Stay in the center of the room and time your jumps over the low-flying patterns.

**Hearts (secondary weapon ammunition):** Candles refill hearts. Before boss fights, clear every candle in the preceding room.

**Best secondary weapons:**
- **Holy water:** Creates a damage area on the floor. Excellent against bosses that stay grounded.
- **Cross (boomerang):** Wide arc, returns to you, hits twice. Best against Death and Dracula.
- **Watch (stopwatch):** Freezes enemies. Situationally useful against Medusa Heads.

## Tecmo Super Bowl

**The game:** Eight-man rosters with real NFL players, four offensive plays and four defensive plays. Pre-snap play selection from a fixed set — you win by picking the play the defense doesn't pick.

**Team tiers:**
- **Tier 1 (dominant):** San Francisco 49ers (Jerry Rice is the best receiver in the game by a significant margin), Chicago Bears (Jim McMahon + Walter Payton), Los Angeles Raiders (Bo Jackson, though he's inconsistent), Cincinnati Bengals (Boomer Esiason, very accurate).
- **Tier 2 (competitive):** Minnesota Vikings, New York Giants, Houston Oilers.
- **Avoid:** Any team without a clear superstar. The stat differences are large.

**Bo Jackson:** His speed stat is maxed. On running plays, if he breaks the line he will outrun every defender on the screen. He occasionally refuses to get tackled at all — a feature, not a bug.

**Jerry Rice:** Runs the fastest routes in the game. On any pass play targeting him (he's WR1 on SF), if you put the cursor on him before the snap and throw immediately at release, he'll be open more often than not.

**Defensive strategy:** The computer predictably runs certain plays from certain formations. Once you identify the pattern (watch two plays and you'll see it), call the corresponding defense. A matched defensive call forces a fumble or interception nearly every time.

**Kicking:** Hold A for a full power kick and press A again at the peak of the power meter. Onside kicks are recoverable — the CPU rarely fields them cleanly.

**Two-minute drill:** Spike the ball (select Run play, snap it, press it immediately) to stop the clock without using a timeout.

## DuckTales

**Structure:** Five worlds in any order — the Amazon, Transylvania, the Himalayas, the African Mines, and the Moon. Each has a treasure at the end. Collect all five to reach the final castle.

**Pogo mechanic:** Hold Down and press B while in the air. Scrooge plants his cane and bounces. The pogo lets you hit enemies safely from above (most enemies are deadly to touch from any other angle), break blocks, and reach high platforms. Master this first — everything in the game depends on it.

**Stage order (easiest to hardest):**
1. **Amazon:** Tutorial difficulty. Linear. Treasure is straightforward.
2. **African Mines:** Short, slightly trickier platforming.
3. **Transylvania:** Longer. Has a key puzzle to access the boss area — find all three keys in the stage.
4. **Himalayas:** Ice physics and longer fall pits. The Yeti boss is the hardest non-final boss.
5. **Moon:** The most iconic stage in the game (the music alone justifies playing it). Gravity sections, alien enemies, the final area before the endgame castle.

**Hidden treasure chests:** Each stage has several treasure chests worth large amounts of money. The game tracks your total score and gives different endings based on how much you collected. For the best ending, collect everything.

**Secret treasure locations:**
- Amazon: Near the beginning, pogo through a breakable floor.
- Himalayas: Behind a wall on the left side of the penguin section.
- Moon: Above the main path in the first half, reachable by bouncing on a specific platform.

**Bosses:**
- **Pete (Amazon):** Throws barrels downward. Stand to the side and pogo his head.
- **Flintheart Glomgold (African Mines):** Hops toward you. Pogo him as he lands.
- **Magica De Spell (Transylvania):** Shoots a projectile, then becomes vulnerable. Pogo.
- **Yeti (Himalayas):** Tosses ice blocks in an arc. Stand in the gap between arc peaks and pogo when he passes.
- **Alien (Moon):** Flies in a pattern. Time the pogo hits between his movement cycles.
- **Dracula Duck (Castle):** Multiple phases. Keep bouncing; he has more health than the other bosses.

**Health:** You start with three hearts and can find more (up to four). The cane swing (press B without Down held) damages enemies in front of you and can break blocks — the only way to hit certain enemies safely without the pogo.

## Battletoads

**The code:** The game has no code. Save states are mandatory. This is one of the hardest games on the NES.

**Lives:** You start with three and earn extras rarely. Each continues resets progress to the world start.

**Stage 1 (Ragnarok's Canyon):** Tutorial. Punches and kicks everything. Smash attacks build up (press and hold attack for a charged hit). The first stage is the game teaching you the moveset.

**Stage 2 (Wookie Hole):** Introduction to the rope sections. Swinging and grabbing ropes. Manageable.

**Stage 3 (Turbo Tunnel):** This is where most players stop. High-speed hoverbike racing with walls appearing from both sides. The pattern must be memorized — there is no way to react fast enough. The sequence:
- This stage is pure memorization. Save state at every checkpoint (the wide clear sections). Learn each segment individually. Full pattern documentation is widely available — do not try to learn it blind.

**Stage 4 (Arctic Cavern):** Ice physics. Punch the icicles before walking under them (they fall). The pole sections require precise timing.

**Stage 5 (Surf City):** Surfboard riding. Hold Up on the d-pad to accelerate, Down to slow. Hit the snakes without hitting the walls.

**Stage 6 (Karnath's Lair):** Snake-riding in 3D perspective. Slow down to manage curves.

**Stage 7 (Volkmire's Inferno):** Rocket bike, harder than Turbo Tunnel. Save state at every opening.

**Stage 8 (Intruder Excluder):** Climb the tower fighting enemies. The elevator sections require staying on the platform.

**Stage 9 (Terra Tubes):** Water stage with current. Some paths are dead ends. The boss requires emptying his health bar before the screen scrolls off.

**Stage 10 (Rat Race):** Race the clock to reach the end before the rats eat the hostages.

**Stage 11 (Clinger Winger):** Chase a ship on a single-direction track while a spinning obstacle closes in from behind. Fastest-reaction puzzle in the game.

**Stage 12 (Skin of Your Teeth):** Boss gauntlet. Save states between each fight.

**Combat basics:** Jump-kick (jump then attack) hits airborne enemies. The smash attack (hold and release) launches enemies. The headbutt (run into an enemy) stuns. Two-player is easier — enemies split their attention and you can revive each other by running to a downed partner.

## Ninja Gaiden

**Story:** Told in cinematic cutscenes between stages — the first NES game to use them. Ryu Hayabusa is searching for his father's killer. Watch them.

**Wall-climbing:** Ryu can cling to any wall by jumping into it. Jump between walls to climb without handholds. This is the most important mechanic — almost every difficult section in the game requires wall-jumping.

**Ninja powers (sub-weapons):**
- **Fire Wheel:** Orbits Ryu, burns enemies on contact. Excellent defensive option.
- **Jump & Slash:** Powerful downward attack in the air. High damage.
- **Windmill Throwing Star:** Wide arc shot. Good range.
- **Invincible Fire Wheel:** Larger version, costs more, hits harder.
- **Vacuum Wave:** Long-range horizontal slash.

Sub-weapons cost Spiritual Strength (the number at the bottom). Run out and you're left with the sword.

**Stage 1:**
- Short and forgiving. The eagle enemies that swoop at you are the main threat. Jump into walls and slash them before they reach you.
- Boss: Barbarian. Jump over his sword swings, slash his body. Three to four hits.

**Stage 2:**
- Introduces vertical wall-climbing sections. Climb up, avoid the Jaquio soldiers.
- Boss: Two-headed demon bird. It charges horizontally; jump over each charge and slash.

**Stage 3:**
- Moving platforms over gaps. Time the wall jumps carefully.
- Boss: Bloody Malth. He has a shield; hit him on the right side where the shield doesn't cover.

**Stage 4:**
- Enemy density increases sharply. Eagles and soldiers simultaneously. The Fire Wheel orbiting Ryu kills many of them automatically.
- Boss: Basaquer. Jumps around the room, creates fireballs. Stay mobile.

**Stage 5:**
- The hardest regular stage. Moving platforms, enemies that spawn infinitely. Do not stop moving.
- Boss: Kelbeross — two dogs. The main dog bounces; the shadow clone copies. Hit both simultaneously with sub-weapons if possible, or take out one quickly.

**Stage 6:**
- Fortress interior. Multiple bosses in sequence at the end.
- Act 6-1: Final enemy section.
- Act 6-2: Jaquio boss. He teleports. Hit him mid-teleport if you can time it.
- Act 6-3: Demon boss. Larger health bar, harder pattern.
- Act 6-4 (true final): The game forces you back to Act 6-1 if you lose here without a continue. Final boss has two phases — a rock creature, then the actual Jaquio in demon form. Fire Wheel is your friend.

**The respawn trap:** Enemies respawn when you scroll back even slightly. On difficult sections with narrow platforms, never scroll backward — you will refill the room with enemies.

## Rygar

**The world:** Rygar is open with an overworld (Dorago's Palace) split into named regions. You'll revisit areas as you gain new tools. Think of it as a proto-Metroidvania — the grappling hook and raft open new sections of the map.

**The Diskarmor:** Rygar's weapon is a yo-yo-like disk on a chain. Short-range (Down + Attack) does more damage; long-range is safer. Most enemies are easier to hit at long range.

**Essential tools:**
- **Crossbow:** Found early. Shoots forward. Lets you hit enemies at maximum range.
- **Grappling Hook (Locket):** Required to cross large gaps and reach new areas of the map.
- **Raft:** Required to cross water sections to reach the northern areas of the overworld.
- **Indora's Bow:** Obtained near the end. Significantly more powerful than the Diskarmor.

**Tones (the upgrade system):** Killing enemies drops Tones, which accumulate in your stats. You permanently upgrade Attack, Defense, and Endurance. You never lose progress even when you die — the overworld saves between deaths.

**Gaining power before going further:**
- If an area is killing you, grind the nearest enemy room until your stats are high enough. There's no time pressure.

**Sage Tablets:** Found in specific rooms on the overworld. Each Tablet teaches you the location of the next key item or secret. Collect them to understand where to go next.

**Critical checkpoints:**
1. Find the Crossbow in Ligar's Sector.
2. Find the Grappling Hook in Dorago's Palace (upper section) — requires the Crossbow to reach.
3. Find the Raft in Sagila's Sector.
4. Reach the Sky Kingdom via the grappling hook sections.
5. Get Indora's Bow before the final boss.

**Final boss (Ligar):** Multi-phase. He has enormous health. The Indora's Bow is required to deal damage at the rate needed to survive the encounter. The final form requires hitting his exposed core — aim for it between his attacks.

## Bionic Commando

**Core mechanic:** You cannot jump. The bionic arm is your only way to gain elevation — fire it at a ceiling or ledge to attach, then swing or retract to climb. Every screen in the game must be read in terms of what you can grapple. If you see a ledge or ceiling, it is probably a grapple point.

**The overworld:** Split into numbered areas on a map screen. Areas are connected by roads with enemy troops that intercept you between zones. You can fight them or avoid them.

**Communication tower mechanic:** Many areas have communication towers that you can capture. Some transmissions reveal enemy plans, item locations, and boss weaknesses. Others are gibberish or propaganda. Read everything — one transmission tells you how to defeat the final boss and you need that information.

**Essential items:**
- **Machine Gun:** Better sustained fire than the starting rifle.
- **Rocket Launcher:** Required for certain armored enemies and for damaging the final boss.
- **Neutral Areas (orange on the map):** Non-enemy areas where you can resupply ammunition and health. Visit them between dangerous missions.

**The grapple in combat:** The bionic arm can grab enemy soldiers and use them as shields or drag them toward you. Against groups of enemies, this is often safer than shooting.

**Stage progression order:**
- Start with Areas 1 and 2 (tutorial difficulty).
- Capture the communication tower in each area before moving to the boss.
- Areas 3–9 build in difficulty. Area 6 introduces the helicopter enemies.
- Area 9 has the first encounter with a Generalissimo — armored boss requiring the Rocket Launcher.
- Area 12 and above are the endgame military headquarters.

**The final area (Albatros facility):**
- MasterD (the final boss) and his commanders are in the inner complex.
- You need the Super Joe rescue: he's a hostage in the facility.
- **Final boss secret:** The communication towers told you this — the final boss's head is his weak point. When his helmet comes off at the end of the confrontation, fire your rifle at his exposed head. The explosion that follows is the reason the game is rated for ages 13+.

**Grapple timing:** The arm has a reach limit. If you fire too late on a swing you'll fall. Learn to fire the grapple at the peak of a swing rather than at the bottom — it extends your reach and gives more time to correct your arc.
