# GoldenEye 007 (N64)

**Developer:** Rare | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 1997

---

## Introduction

GoldenEye is the console shooter that proved the genre could work without a mouse, and it did it by not being a Doom clone. The missions have objectives rather than exits. You are told to disable an alarm, photograph a piece of equipment, plant a covert modem, or keep a hostage alive, and the level does not end until you have done them. Difficulty does not just make enemies tougher — it adds objectives. Agent is the short version of a mission, Secret Agent adds tasks, 00 Agent adds more, so the same level is genuinely three different missions.

The other half of its reputation is the four-player split-screen deathmatch, which for several years was the default way console owners played a shooter with friends in the same room.

Two things have aged. The framerate drops hard in busy rooms, and the default control scheme puts aiming on the analogue stick with the C-buttons for strafing, which feels wrong to anyone raised on twin sticks.

---

## Controls

The default is **1.1 Honey**: stick aims and moves, C-buttons strafe and step, Z fires, R crouches, B changes weapon, A activates.

The scheme most people should actually use is **1.2 Solitaire**, which inverts look, or **2.1 Kissy**, which is the two-controller setup. If you have two controllers, 2.1 Kissy gives you one stick for movement and one for aim — genuine dual-analogue in 1997, and it is by a distance the most comfortable way to play this game today. On an emulator you can map both halves to one modern pad's two sticks and get modern controls out of a 1997 game.

- **Z:** Fire.
- **R:** Crouch. Hold to duck; crouching tightens your aim considerably.
- **A:** Activate — doors, switches, objective items.
- **B:** Cycle weapon.
- **Hold R + aim:** Precision aiming mode. Slows you down, lets you place shots exactly. This is how you get headshots.
- **Start:** Watch — mission objectives, map, and the save/quit menu.

### Aiming Properly

Body shots take several rounds. Headshots kill in one on most enemies. The precision-aim mode (hold R) is not optional if you want to finish 00 Agent runs, because ammunition is limited and every wasted magazine matters.

Shooting an enemy in the leg makes them limp; in the arm makes them drop the weapon; in the hand while they are throwing a grenade drops it at their feet. This per-limb hit detection was unusual for the time and it is still the most satisfying thing in the game.

## Difficulty Tiers

| Tier | What changes |
|------|--------------|
| Agent | Base objectives. Enemies are inaccurate, you take little damage, ammo is plentiful. |
| Secret Agent | Extra objectives. Enemies more accurate, less body armour available. |
| 00 Agent | More objectives again. Enemies shoot hard and often; you die fast. |
| 007 Mode | Unlocked after 00 Agent. You set enemy health, damage, accuracy and reaction time yourself. |

Play a level on Agent first to learn the layout, then repeat it on the higher tiers. The extra objectives on Secret Agent and 00 Agent are usually in rooms you already walked through, so the second run is about route rather than discovery.

## Mission Notes

**Dam.** The opening. Objectives are simple; the value is learning that the game expects you to check the Watch for what you are supposed to be doing. The bungee jump at the end is a cutscene, not a jump you must make.

**Facility.** The vent opening, and the first mission where a hostage matters — Doak must be found and must survive, and on higher tiers so must the scientists. Enemies who see the alarm reach it fast, so kill guards near alarm panels first.

**Runway.** Open ground, a tank, and very little cover. Move along the edges.

**Surface.** Snow, guard huts, and the first genuinely stealth-shaped level. Alarms summon reinforcements indefinitely.

**Bunker.** Tight corridors and cameras. Shoot the cameras.

**Silo.** A timer. Plant the charges on the correct panels — the objective screen names them — and get out. Do not sightsee.

**Frigate.** Hostages who die to your own stray fire. Use the sniper rifle and take single, aimed shots.

**Surface 2.** The return trip, harder.

**Bunker 2.** Starts with you disarmed in a cell. Improvise.

**Statue.** Fog, statues as cover, and a scripted meeting.

**Archives.** Close-quarters, and Natalya must survive while she follows you badly.

**Streets.** The tank. Drive it; it absorbs everything.

**Depot, Train, Jungle, Control, Caverns, Cradle, Aztec, Egyptian.** The back half escalates steadily. Control is the notorious one because Natalya stands in an exposed room while waves of guards arrive from three directions and she must not die. Clear the flanking approaches before you let the sequence start.

Aztec and Egyptian are bonus levels, unlocked by completing the campaign on Secret Agent and 00 Agent respectively.

## Multiplayer

Four-player split screen, chosen character, chosen weapon set, chosen scenario. The two things worth knowing:

**Weapon sets matter more than the map.** Slappers Only turns the game into a comedy; Power Weapons turns it into rocket chaos; Licence to Kill makes every hit fatal for everyone, which is the most tense and the fairest.

**Oddjob is the argument.** He is shorter than every other character, which means shots aimed at head height miss him entirely. Most groups eventually ban him. This is the correct decision.

Maps worth playing: Complex (the competitive default, symmetric and vertical), Facility (tight, chaotic), Temple (the most balanced large map), Stack (small and constant), and Caves (confusing, which some people like).

---

## Cheats

Cheats unlock by completing a level under a target time on a given difficulty. They are strict — the Facility invincibility cheat requires 2:05 on 00 Agent, which is a genuine speedrun.

Unlocked cheats are toggled from the mission select screen, and using one disables the completion record for that run. The paintball mode, DK mode (enormous heads) and tiny Bond are cosmetic; invincibility, all weapons and infinite ammo are the ones people chase.

## Emulation Notes

GoldenEye is more demanding to emulate accurately than most N64 games because of how it uses the RDP, and it is one of the titles where video plugin choice visibly matters. On Batocera, if you get missing textures or a black screen, switch plugin before assuming a bad ROM.

The genuine improvement emulation offers is controls. Map the 2.1 Kissy two-controller scheme onto one modern gamepad's two analogue sticks, then invert the look axis if you prefer, and GoldenEye plays like a modern shooter. Doing this once is worth more than any other setting on this page.

Framerate: the original runs in the teens in heavy rooms and emulation does not fix that, because it is the game's own frame pacing rather than the hardware. It is authentic and it is still unpleasant.
