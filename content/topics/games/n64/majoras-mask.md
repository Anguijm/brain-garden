# The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask (N64)

**Developer:** Nintendo EAD | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 2000

---

## Introduction

Majora's Mask was built in about a year on Ocarina of Time's engine, and the shortcut it took turned into its defining idea. Rather than build a bigger world, Nintendo built a small one and made you live the same three days in it over and over. A moon is falling on Termina. You have seventy-two hours. When the clock runs out you play the Song of Time and go back to the first morning, keeping your masks, songs, and key items but losing your rupees, your consumables, and every piece of progress that was not a permanent one.

That structure changes what the game is about. Ocarina is about a world you save. Majora is about a town full of people whose last three days you keep watching, and the side quests are not distractions from the plot — they are the plot. The Anju and Kafei quest, in which a bride waits for a fiancé who has been transformed and trapped, spans the whole three days and is better written than the main story.

There are only four dungeons. Do not let that fool you into thinking it is short.

---

## The Three-Day Clock

Seventy-two in-game hours run about fifty-four minutes of real time at normal speed.

**Inverted Song of Time** (play the Song of Time backwards: C-Down, A, C-Right, C-Down, A, C-Right) halves the clock speed. Learn it immediately and use it always. It is the difference between a stressful game and a manageable one.

**Song of Double Time** (C-Right, C-Right, A, A, C-Down, C-Down) jumps forward to the next dawn or dusk. Use it to skip dead time when you are waiting for a scheduled event.

### What Survives a Reset

| Kept | Lost |
|------|------|
| Masks | Rupees not in the bank |
| Songs | Bombs, arrows, Deku sticks, nuts |
| Bow, Hookshot, Bombs, Lens, bottles | Dungeon keys and dungeon progress |
| Sword and shield upgrades | Non-permanent side-quest progress |
| Heart Containers, Heart Pieces | Consumed items given to NPCs |
| Bank deposits and the bank stamp | |

Bank your rupees the moment you have a meaningful amount. The bank remembers you across cycles and it is the only way to accumulate money.

## The Transformation Masks

Three masks turn Link into somebody else, and each one is a full moveset rather than a costume.

**Deku Link.** Spins to attack, launches from Deku Flowers into a hovering glide, skips across water for a few bounces, and shoots bubbles. Small enough to fit through low passages. The glide is a genuine traversal tool.

**Goron Link.** Punches, ground-pounds, and curls into a rolling ball that is the fastest movement in the game. Rolling drains magic but crosses the whole of Termina Field in seconds, and with enough speed the roll grows spikes that damage anything it touches. Goron Link sinks in water and walks along the bottom of lava.

**Zora Link.** Swims properly — fast, with a magic boost — walks on the sea floor, throws his fins as boomerangs, and generates an electric barrier. The swimming is the best in any Zelda game.

You start the game as Deku Link and only get Link's own form back partway through the first cycle.

## Dungeons

Four, plus the moon.

**Woodfall Temple.** Deku-focused. Reached by reviving the swamp and riding a Deku Flower up. Item: Hero's Bow. Boss is Odolwa, a masked warrior who leaps and swings: shoot him with the bow to stagger, then close with the sword. The Deku Princess must be rescued to fully resolve the region.

**Snowhead Temple.** Goron-focused, built as a tower around a central pillar you gradually open up. Item: Fire Arrows. Boss is Goht, a mechanical bull that runs a circular track: put on the Goron Mask, roll after it, and ram it at speed. This is the most unusual boss fight in any Zelda.

**Great Bay Temple.** Zora-focused and water-driven, with a set of currents you redirect by turning valves. This is the dungeon most people find confusing, and the reason is that the water flow changes what is passable in ways that are not visible from where you turn the valve. Item: Ice Arrows. Boss is Gyorg, a giant fish: hit it from the platform when it jumps, then finish it underwater as Zora Link.

**Stone Tower Temple.** The best dungeon in the game and one of the best in the series. Partway through you flip the entire temple upside down with a Light Arrow on a coloured block, and every room you have already solved becomes a new room. Item: Light Arrows. Boss is Twinmold, two enormous sand worms: use the Giant's Mask, which makes Link huge, and punch them.

**The Moon.** Four trial rooms guarded by children in boss masks, each a short themed challenge, then Majora itself — three forms, escalating. The Fierce Deity's Mask, if you have earned it by collecting all twenty other masks, makes the final fight trivial. That is intentional; it is the reward for having done everything.

## Masks Worth Chasing

There are twenty-four in total, three of them the transformations. The ones that change how you play:

- **Bunny Hood.** Doubles running speed. Wear it constantly outside of combat.
- **Great Fairy's Mask.** Attracts stray fairies from across a room. Needed for the Great Fairy quests.
- **Stone Mask.** Makes you invisible to most enemies and guards. Trivialises several areas.
- **Bremen Mask.** Marches chickens into a line behind you. Needed for a Heart Piece, and quietly one of the funniest things in the game.
- **Couple's Mask.** The reward for completing the Anju and Kafei quest. Do this one.
- **Fierce Deity's Mask.** Given by the moon children if you bring all twenty other masks. Usable only in boss rooms. A long sword, enormous damage, no magic cost.

## Anju and Kafei

The three-day side quest that the game is really built around. Anju is waiting at the Stock Pot Inn to marry Kafei, who has vanished. He has actually been turned into a child by the Skull Kid and is hiding in Clock Town.

The chain runs across all three days on a strict schedule, involves the Kafei's Mask, a letter to Kafei posted in a mailbox, the Postman's routine, a hideout in the laundry pool, and a confrontation with Sakon the thief on the final night. Missing a step by an hour breaks the chain and you restart the cycle.

Get the Inverted Song of Time running before you attempt it. Write down the times. The reward is the Couple's Mask and, more to the point, the best-written sequence in the game.

---

## Bottles

Six of them, and several are behind substantial quests — the Romani Ranch alien defence, the Beaver Brothers' race, Koume's swamp tour, the Goron race, the Gorman milk delivery. Bottled fairies revive you on death. Chateau Romani, bought at the ranch, gives unlimited magic for the rest of the cycle, which makes Goron rolling and Zora swimming free.

## Emulation Notes

Majora's Mask uses the N64 Expansion Pak, the 4 MB memory upgrade, and will not run without it. Every emulator can supply it, but if the game refuses to boot past the title, check that the Expansion Pak or 8 MB RDRAM option is enabled in the core settings. This is the single most common Majora's Mask emulation problem and it looks like a bad ROM when it is not.

Beyond that it behaves like Ocarina. Mupen64Plus handles it; try alternate video plugins if Stone Tower's flipped geometry renders wrong.
