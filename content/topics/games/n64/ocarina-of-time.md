# The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (N64)

**Developer:** Nintendo EAD | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 1998

---

## Introduction

Ocarina of Time is the game that worked out how a 3D action-adventure should feel, and almost every one made since has borrowed from it. The two inventions that matter are Z-targeting and the context-sensitive action button. Z-targeting locks the camera onto an enemy or a person so that your movement becomes circular strafing around them, which solves the problem that killed most early 3D combat: you no longer have to aim and move and steer a camera at the same time. The context button changes what it does based on where you are standing, so one button covers climb, open, throw, talk, roll and grab without the controller needing twelve buttons.

The story splits Link's life in two. You play the first third as a child in Kokiri Forest, pull the Master Sword, and wake seven years later as an adult in a Hyrule that Ganondorf has already conquered. From that point you move between the two eras at will, and several puzzles depend on doing something as a child that has grown or decayed by the time you return as an adult.

Budget forty to fifty hours for a first playthrough that does the side content. A focused run is closer to twenty-five.

## Controls

- **Control Stick:** Move. Analogue — a light push walks quietly, a full push runs.
- **A:** Context action. The green label above the button always tells you what it will do right now. Roll (while running), climb, open, talk, grab, throw, enter.
- **B:** Sword. Tap for a horizontal slash; hold to charge the spin attack and release.
- **Z:** Target lock. Hold to lock onto the nearest enemy or interactive character. With nothing to lock, Z centres the camera behind Link, which is the single most useful thing you can learn to do reflexively.
- **R:** Shield. Held while Z-targeting, Link raises the shield and can back-walk while covered. Tap R with no target to crouch behind the shield.
- **C-Up:** First-person look. Also the button for the Ocarina prompt in some contexts.
- **C-Left / C-Down / C-Right:** Three assignable item slots. You will be reassigning these constantly.
- **Start:** Menu — equipment, quest status, map, and the item assignment screen.

### Z-Targeting Properly

New players hold Z only during combat. Use it for navigation as well. With no enemy in range, tapping Z snaps the camera behind Link, which fixes almost every camera complaint people have about this game. In combat, Z plus back on the stick is a hop backwards, Z plus left or right is a sidestep hop, and both are faster and safer than turning and running.

Z-targeting also aims. Lock a target, then pull the slingshot, bow or hookshot in first person and the reticle starts on the locked enemy.

## The Ocarina

Songs are played by pressing the five note buttons: A, C-Down, C-Right, C-Left, C-Up. You learn twelve.

**Warp songs** teleport you to a fixed point. They are the difference between a pleasant playthrough and a tedious one, so learn them as they become available rather than saving them up.

The Sun's Song is the one people forget. ReDeads — the shambling corpses that freeze you with a scream — are the most unpleasant enemy in the game, and the Sun's Song paralyses them outright so you can walk past or kill them for free.

## Dungeon Walkthrough

### Child Era

**Inside the Deku Tree.** The tutorial dungeon. Teaches the Slingshot, ladders, and the idea that a dungeon has a map and a compass in it. Boss is Gohma, a one-eyed spider on the ceiling: shoot the eye with the slingshot when it glows red, she drops stunned, then hit her with the sword. Repeat.

**Dodongo's Cavern.** Introduces Bombs and the idea that a dungeon room can be reshaped. Bomb Flowers on walls can be picked and thrown. Boss is King Dodongo, who rolls at you and inhales: throw a bomb into his open mouth while he is inhaling, he detonates and curls up, then hit him. When he rolls, sidestep with Z rather than running.

**Inside Jabu-Jabu's Belly.** Introduces the Boomerang and the escort mechanic, since you carry Princess Ruto for a large part of it. The Boomerang stuns and retrieves. Boss is Barinade, a jellyfish on a stalk: boomerang the tentacles connecting it to the ceiling, then boomerang the outer ring of jellyfish, then hit the core.

After Jabu-Jabu you collect the three Spiritual Stones, open the Door of Time, and pull the Master Sword.

### Adult Era

**Forest Temple.** Item: Fairy Bow. The temple is built around four Poe sisters whose torches control a rotating room. This is the first dungeon where the geometry itself is the puzzle, and it is where a lot of first-time players stall. Boss is Phantom Ganon, who rides out of a set of paintings: pick the real one (the horse in the true painting is drawn sharply and gets larger; the fakes stay small), shoot him with the bow, and in phase two reflect his energy ball back with the sword like tennis.

**Fire Temple.** Item: Megaton Hammer. You need the Goron Tunic to survive Death Mountain Crater; buy it in Goron City or get it from Link the Goron after freeing him. The temple is a rescue mission with a timer feel to it. Boss is Volvagia, a lava dragon: hammer its head when it surfaces from a hole, then hit it while it is down.

**Water Temple.** Item: Longshot. This is the famously difficult one, and the reason is water levels. Three Triforce-marked plaques set the level to low, middle or high, and you must play Zelda's Lullaby at each. The frustration comes from having to open the menu to equip and unequip the Iron Boots for every dive, which the 3DS remake later fixed by moving them to an item slot. On original hardware you simply live with it. Keep a mental map of which doors are reachable at which level. Boss is Morpha, a water amoeba: Longshot the nucleus out of the water and slash it.

**Shadow Temple.** Item: Hover Boots. Requires the Lens of Truth, bought or won in the Bottom of the Well as a child. Half of this temple is invisible: false floors, hidden platforms, illusory walls. Keep the Lens up and watch your magic. Boss is Bongo Bongo, a giant hand-and-drum: use the Lens to see him, shoot the hands with arrows, then hit the eye.

**Spirit Temple.** Items: Silver Gauntlets (child), Mirror Shield (adult). The only dungeon you enter in both eras, and the two halves interlock — the child opens paths the adult later uses. Boss is Twinrova, two witches who cast fire and ice: absorb three of the same element with the Mirror Shield and fire it back at them.

**Ganon's Castle.** Six barrier rooms, one per medallion, then the tower. Boss is Ganondorf: reflect his energy balls with the sword, hit him with Light Arrows, close in. After the castle collapses, Ganon himself: he knocks the Master Sword away, so fight with the Biggoron Sword or the Megaton Hammer, target the tail, and take up the Master Sword again for the finish.

## Things Worth Doing

**The Biggoron Sword trade sequence.** A long chain of timed deliveries starting with the Pocket Egg in Kakariko. It ends with a two-handed sword that does double the Master Sword's damage and cannot be used with a shield. Worth it. The timed legs are the sore point: keep Epona and use warp songs.

**Bottles.** You can carry four. One from the Kakariko cucco game as a child, one from Lake Hylia's fishing pond, one from Princess Ruto inside Jabu-Jabu, one from the Kakariko graveyard Poe seller as an adult. Fill them with fairies — a fairy in a bottle revives you automatically on death, which is the closest thing the game has to an extra life.

**Heart Pieces.** Four make a heart container. There are thirty-six.

**Gold Skulltulas.** One hundred of them, hidden across both eras. The rewards come at ten, twenty, thirty, forty, fifty and one hundred. The fifty reward is the Giant's Wallet; the hundred reward is an infinite-rupee token that most players never see.

## Emulation Notes

This guide assumes the North American V1.2 ROM, which is the final revision and the safest one for emulation. Mupen64Plus on Batocera handles the game well. If you see graphical glitches in the Fire Temple or the Water Temple, try switching the video plugin — GlideN64 and ParaLLEl-RDP handle this game differently and one usually looks right where the other does not.

The one control note specific to emulation: the N64 controller's analogue stick has a much wider range than a modern thumbstick, and games calibrated for it feel twitchy on a Xbox-style pad. If Link runs when you meant him to walk, lower the analogue sensitivity in the core options.
