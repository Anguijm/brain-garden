# Super Mario 64 (N64)

**Developer:** Nintendo EAD | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 1996

---

## Introduction

Super Mario 64 was a launch title, and it invented the vocabulary that 3D platformers have used ever since. The important design choice is that the levels are not obstacle courses with an exit at the end. They are small open spaces, and each one holds seven Stars that you collect by doing seven different things in the same space. You pick a Star from a list before you enter, the level reconfigures slightly around that objective, and you are dropped in to solve it.

The second important choice is that the movement is the reward. Mario has a triple jump, a long jump, a backflip, a side flip, a wall kick, a ground pound, a dive and a slide kick, and the game is built so that a player who has learned all of them travels through a level in a completely different way from one who has not. People are still finding new movement in this game thirty years later.

There are 120 Stars. You need 70 to open the final door. The remaining 50 are for you.

## Controls

- **Control Stick:** Move. Fully analogue — tilt determines whether Mario tiptoes, walks or runs.
- **A:** Jump.
- **B:** Punch. While holding an object, B throws it. In water, B is a swim stroke.
- **Z:** Crouch. In the air, Z is a ground pound.
- **R:** Crawl (held on the ground). Also the camera-behind button in some modes.
- **C-buttons:** Camera. C-Up enters Mario's first-person view; C-Left and C-Right rotate; C-Down zooms out.

### The Move List

This is the whole game. Learn all of it.

| Move | Input | Use |
|------|-------|-----|
| Double jump | A, then A as you land | Slightly higher |
| Triple jump | A, A, A in rhythm while running | Highest standing jump; big forward arc |
| Long jump | Run, Z, then A | Fastest horizontal travel in the game |
| Backflip | Stand still, Z, then A | High vertical, backwards |
| Side flip | Run, tap the stick the opposite way, A | High vertical, changes facing |
| Wall kick | A immediately on touching a wall mid-jump | Climb vertical shafts; the key to half the level design |
| Ground pound | Z in the air | Break blocks, hit switches, stop dead |
| Dive | Run, then B | Long low leap; cancel into a slide |
| Slide kick | Run, Z + B | Fast, ends prone |
| Kick | B in the air | Damage; also extends a jump slightly |
| Crawl | Hold Z while moving | Fit through low gaps |

The long jump is the one that changes how the game feels. Run, tap Z, press A, and Mario covers ground faster than running. Chained long jumps across open terrain are how experienced players cross Bob-omb Battlefield in seconds.

The wall kick is the one that changes what is reachable. Many Stars that look like they need a specific power-up are reachable with a wall kick off nearby geometry.

## The Camera

Lakitu films Mario from a hovering camera, and you steer it. This is the part of the game that has aged least well, and the fix is to treat the camera as an input you use constantly rather than something that follows you.

C-Left and C-Right rotate in fixed increments. R switches between Lakitu's trailing camera and a fixed Mario-relative camera, and the fixed one is better for precise platforming because it stops swinging around while you jump. C-Up enters first person, which is how you scout a long jump before committing to it.

When a jump looks impossible, rotate the camera first. It is usually a depth-perception problem, not a distance problem.

## Caps

**Wing Cap (red block).** Flight. Triple jump while wearing it to take off, then pull back on the stick to climb and push forward to dive. Speed comes from diving; altitude comes from trading that speed back. Unlocked by the Tower of the Wind above the castle courtyard.

**Metal Cap (green block).** Invulnerable, immune to fire and gas, sinks in water and walks along the bottom, cannot be knocked back. Unlocked in the castle basement.

**Vanish Cap (blue block).** Walk through certain wire-mesh walls, temporary invulnerability. Unlocked in the castle moat after draining it.

The Metal Cap and Vanish Cap combine in one late Star that requires both at once.

## The Fifteen Courses

**1. Bob-omb Battlefield.** The teaching level. Open hill, cannons, a boss at the summit. King Bob-omb is grabbed from behind with B and thrown three times; walk backwards toward the arena edge so each throw counts.

**2. Whomp's Fortress.** Vertical. Introduces the idea that the level changes shape between Star objectives. The Whomp King is beaten by ground-pounding his back after he slams down.

**3. Jolly Roger Bay.** Water. Swimming is on B for strokes, and the important thing is that Mario drowns on a real timer — surface or find a bubble. The sunken ship's eels are the memorable hazard.

**4. Cool, Cool Mountain.** A long slide, a penguin race, and a baby penguin you carry down the mountain and return to its mother. The slide has a shortcut through a hole in the wall near the start that most players find by accident.

**5. Big Boo's Haunt.** The haunted house. Boos vanish when you look at them and appear when you turn away, so you attack them by turning the camera, not by chasing them.

**6. Hazy Maze Cave.** Toxic gas, an underground lake, and a rideable plesiosaur called Dorrie whose head you ground-pound to lower.

**7. Lethal Lava Land.** Everything hurts. The Metal Cap trivialises most of it. The bully enemies are pushed into the lava rather than hit.

**8. Shifting Sand Land.** Quicksand that kills instantly in the dark patches and slowly in the light ones. The pyramid interior is a separate small dungeon.

**9. Dire, Dire Docks.** Water plus the submarine from Bowser's stage. The whirlpool will drag you in and drown you if you swim near the centre.

**10. Snowman's Land.** Ice physics and a wind-blowing snowman whose gust you shelter from behind a penguin.

**11. Wet-Dry World.** The gimmick level: the water height is set by which height you touch the crystal switch at as you enter. Jump high before touching it for high water, dive low for low water.

**12. Tall, Tall Mountain.** Vertical and narrow, with a monkey who steals your hat and a mushroom-top slide.

**13. Tiny-Huge Island.** Two versions of the same island, one scaled tiny and one scaled huge, and you swap between them through pipes. The enemies change size with the world; you do not.

**14. Tick Tock Clock.** The clock's mechanisms move at a speed set by where the minute hand points when you jump into the painting. Enter at 12 o'clock and everything stops moving, which makes several Stars far easier.

**15. Rainbow Ride.** Flying carpets over an abyss, and the hardest platforming in the game. The Wing Cap is available here; use it rather than fighting the carpets.

## Bowser

Three fights, all the same mechanic. Grab his tail with B, rotate the control stick to spin him up to speed, and release toward one of the bombs at the arena edge. The trick people miss is that the throw direction is set by where the stick is pointing at the moment of release, not by how you spun.

- **Bowser in the Dark World** (8 Stars). One bomb hit.
- **Bowser in the Fire Sea** (30 Stars). Two hits, tilting platforms.
- **Bowser in the Sky** (70 Stars). Three hits, the arena breaks apart, and he throws fire that leaves lingering rings you must jump.

---

## The 120-Star Run

Beyond the 70 needed for the ending: 15 courses at 7 Stars each is 105, and the remaining 15 come from the castle's secret Stars — the three cap courses, the two slides, the Princess's Secret Slide, the Toads who hand you Stars in the corridors, and the Wing/Metal/Vanish cap challenges in the basement and moat.

Collecting all 120 and then visiting the castle roof gets you a short scene with Yoshi, an extra-life bonus and a slightly modified triple jump. It is a reward for completionists rather than a gameplay change.

## Emulation Notes

Super Mario 64 emulates almost perfectly and is a good first test of an N64 setup. On Batocera, Mupen64Plus with either GlideN64 or ParaLLEl-RDP is fine.

The control caveat matters more here than in most N64 games. Mario's speed is genuinely analogue, and a modern thumbstick with a smaller physical throw makes it hard to walk rather than run — which breaks the tightrope sections and the penguin-carrying. If Mario feels twitchy, reduce the stick sensitivity or increase the deadzone in the core options until a half-tilt gives a walk.
