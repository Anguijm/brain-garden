# Perfect Dark (N64)

**Developer:** Rare | **Publisher:** Rare / Nintendo | **Year:** 2000

---

## Introduction

Perfect Dark is GoldenEye's engine given three more years and no licence to work around. Rare kept the objective-driven mission structure and the per-limb hit detection, then rebuilt everything else. The campaign is longer and denser, the weapons are inventive rather than realistic, and the multiplayer is the most complete package on the console — bots, a full customisation system, a co-operative mode, and a mode where a second player controls the enemies.

You play Joanna Dark, a Carrington Institute agent, through a conspiracy involving a rival corporation and two alien factions. The plot is convoluted and largely beside the point. The game is about the missions and the multiplayer.

The cost of all that ambition is performance. Perfect Dark pushed the N64 harder than almost anything else on it and the framerate shows it.

## Controls

Same scheme family as GoldenEye. The default is stick-to-aim with C-buttons for strafe, and the same advice applies: if you have two controllers, or an emulator that lets you map two analogue sticks, use the dual-analogue configuration. It turns a 2000 game into something that controls like a modern shooter, and it is the single best change you can make.

- **Z:** Fire.
- **R:** Crouch, and hold-to-aim precision mode.
- **B:** Cycle weapon.
- **A:** Use / activate.
- **Left C / Right C:** Secondary function of the current weapon, and weapon-specific modes.

### Secondary Fire

Almost every weapon in Perfect Dark has a **second function** on a separate button, and they are not minor variants. The Falcon 2 pistol can be used as a club. The Laptop Gun deploys as an autonomous sentry turret. The CMP150 tags a target and then homes on it. The Farsight sees and shoots through walls. The Dragon can be dropped as a proximity mine.

The game does not explain these well. Go into the Carrington Institute's firing range and work through every weapon's second function before you play the campaign, because half the intended solutions to missions depend on knowing them.

## Weapons Worth Knowing

**Laptop Gun.** A submachine gun that folds into a deployable sentry turret. Place it covering a corridor and let it do the work. The best weapon in the game for solo campaign play.

**Farsight XR-20.** Sees enemies through walls and shoots through them. Extremely powerful, notoriously divisive in multiplayer, and banned in most serious groups.

**Reaper.** A minigun that grinds enemies at close range on its secondary.

**Slayer.** A rocket launcher whose secondary lets you fly the rocket in first person.

**Falcon 2 (silenced / scoped).** The all-round pistol, and dual-wielding two of them is viable throughout.

**Timed / Proximity / Remote Mines.** The remote mine plus a good ambush spot is the classic Perfect Dark multiplayer setup.

**CamSpy.** Not a weapon — a remote-controlled flying camera used for reconnaissance in several missions.

## Difficulty Tiers

As in GoldenEye, difficulty adds objectives rather than just raising numbers.

| Tier | What changes |
|------|--------------|
| Agent | Fewest objectives, forgiving enemies |
| Special Agent | More objectives, tougher enemies, less ammunition |
| Perfect Agent | Most objectives, enemies are accurate and fast |
| Perfect Dark (unlockable) | Custom settings you configure yourself |

Cheats are unlocked by completing missions under target times on specified difficulties, exactly as in GoldenEye, and the targets are tight.

## Campaign Notes

**dataDyne Central: Defection.** The opening. Teaches the CamSpy, the door decoder and the disguise mechanic. On higher tiers it adds a hostage.

**dataDyne Research: Investigation** and **dataDyne Central: Extraction.** The dataDyne trilogy. Extraction ends in a helicopter escape and is the first genuine difficulty step.

**Carrington Villa: Hostage One.** An outdoor level with a sniper and hostages who are killed by carelessness.

**Chicago: Stealth.** Small, dark and genuinely stealth-shaped. Use the silenced weapons.

**G5 Building: Reconnaissance.** Interior infiltration; the Laptop Gun turret pays off here.

**Area 51: Infiltration / Rescue / Escape.** Three linked missions in the middle of the game and the campaign's strongest stretch. Infiltration is a long approach; Rescue involves protecting an escort who is fragile; Escape is a fighting withdrawal.

**Air Base, Air Force One, Crash Site.** An aircraft sequence including a mid-flight section and a survival crash aftermath.

**Pelagic II, Deep Sea.** Underwater base levels with a zero-gravity section that reorients the controls.

**Carrington Institute: Defense.** Defending your own home base, which the game has been teaching you the layout of the whole time through the firing range and training rooms. A very effective piece of design.

**Attack Ship, Skedar Ruins.** The finale, on the alien homeworld, against the Skedar leader.

## Multiplayer: The Combat Simulator

This is the reason to own Perfect Dark.

**Bots (Simulants).** You can fill a match with computer-controlled opponents at eight difficulty levels from MeatSim up to PerfectSim, plus specialist behaviours — a bot that only uses one weapon, a bot that hunts the player in the lead, a bot that avoids combat. This meant Perfect Dark had a genuinely playable multiplayer for one person, which almost nothing else on the console did.

**Scenarios.** Combat (deathmatch), Hold the Briefcase, Hacker Central, Pop a Cap (assassination), King of the Hill, Capture the Case.

**Customisation.** Weapon sets, arena, handicap, time or score limits, teams. Everything is configurable and settings are saved.

**Challenges.** Thirty preset scenarios of escalating difficulty that unlock characters, arenas and options as you clear them.

---

## Co-op and Counter-Op

**Co-operative mode** puts a second player in the campaign alongside you, with objectives shared. This works well and the missions hold up with two.

**Counter-Operative mode** is the unusual one. The second player controls the enemies — spawning into a random guard, and when that guard dies, spawning into another one. Player one is trying to complete the mission; player two is trying to stop them. Nothing else on the N64 did this and very little since has either. It is worth trying at least once.

## Emulation Notes

Perfect Dark **requires the N64 Expansion Pak** for the campaign. Without the 4 MB upgrade, only a reduced multiplayer mode is available. If the game boots but the campaign is greyed out, enable the Expansion Pak or 8 MB RDRAM option in the core settings.

Perfect Dark is one of the most demanding N64 games to emulate and one of the ones where plugin choice most visibly matters. Expect to try more than one video plugin. ParaLLEl-RDP is the accuracy-first choice; GlideN64 is faster but has historically had Rare-specific glitches.

The genuine gain from emulation here is performance. On original hardware Perfect Dark drops into the low teens in busy firefights, and that is not a cartridge fault — the game is simply asking too much of the console. Emulation on adequate hardware removes it, and the game plays substantially better as a result. Combined with dual-analogue controls, an emulated Perfect Dark is a noticeably better game than the one that shipped in 2000.
