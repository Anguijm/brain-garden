# Star Fox 64 (N64)

**Developer:** Nintendo EAD | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 1997

---

## Introduction

Star Fox 64 is short on purpose. A single run through the game takes about an hour, and that is the design: it is an arcade game with a branching campaign, meant to be played again and again along different routes. There are fifteen levels but only seven in any one playthrough, and which seven depends on how well you performed in the previous ones.

The campaign has a fully voiced cast who talk to you continuously while you fly, and the wingmen are not decoration. Falco, Peppy and Slippy get chased by enemies and call for help, and whether you go and shoot the enemy off their tail changes what happens to them for the rest of the run. Let Slippy get hit enough and he leaves for repairs and is not there for the next level.

It was also the first game bundled with the Rumble Pak.

## Controls

- **Control Stick:** Steer the Arwing.
- **A:** Laser. Hold to charge a lock-on shot, then release.
- **B:** Bomb (Nova Bomb). Limited supply. Detonates on impact or when you press B again mid-flight.
- **Z / R:** Bank left / right. Tapped twice quickly, they perform a barrel roll.
- **Z + R together:** Brake. **C-Down:** Boost.
- **C-Up / C-Down:** Boost and brake in All-Range Mode.

### The Barrel Roll

Double-tap Z or R. The Arwing spins on its axis and **deflects incoming laser fire**. This is not cosmetic. It is the single most important defensive move in the game and it also briefly increases your speed.

Peppy's "Do a barrel roll!" became a meme, which is unfortunate, because it is genuinely correct advice and new players die constantly for ignoring it. When lasers are coming at you, roll.

### The U-Turn and the Somersault

In All-Range Mode (open arena levels rather than rails), **pull back on the stick and press C-Down** for a somersault that flips you 180 degrees, and **pull back and brake** for a tight U-turn. Dogfights are won by whoever can turn to face the other first, and these two moves are how you do that.

## Charged Shots and Lock-On

Hold A. A ring closes on a target. Release, and the shot homes. A charged shot does far more damage than tapped fire and it will follow a target around corners.

Against bosses, the pattern that works is: charge while you dodge, release when the weak point is exposed, repeat. Mashing A is the beginner habit and it wastes the whole damage output of the ship.

---

## Health, Wingmen and Upgrades

**Silver rings** restore one unit of shield. **Gold rings** — three of them — increase your maximum shield permanently for the run. Flying through a formation of gold rings is worth a detour.

**Twin Blasters** upgrade your laser to a dual shot; a second upgrade makes it a wide beam.

**Your wingmen are a resource.** Falco, Peppy and Slippy each get chased. Shoot the enemy off their tail and they thank you and stay in the fight; ignore them and they take damage and eventually retreat to base, which means they are absent from later levels and you lose their assistance and their score contribution. Saving all three every level is part of playing well.

## The Branching Map

You always start at **Corneria** and always finish at **Venom**. Everything between depends on your route.

Routes split by what you do in a level — usually by taking a hidden path, beating a boss a particular way, or hitting a target — and the game shows the branch on the map screen between missions.

The three broad paths:

- **Easy path:** Corneria → Sector Y → Katina → Solar → Macbeth → Area 6 → Venom (first)
- **Medium path:** varies through Aquas, Zoness, Bolse
- **Hard path:** Corneria → Meteo → Katina → Sector X → Titania → Bolse → Venom (second)

The **true ending** requires the harder route to Venom, where you fly through Andross's base to the second Venom variant and fight the real Andross rather than the shortened version. Getting there requires taking the difficult branch at each split, so a first playthrough on the easy path gives you a real but lesser ending.

Level highlights worth knowing:

**Corneria.** The tutorial. Taking the low route under the waterfall opens the harder branch.

**Meteo.** An asteroid field. The warp gate here is hidden and leads to Katina by a different route.

**Katina.** All-Range Mode, defending a base against a mothership. Shoot the core when the hatch opens.

**Sector X.** Contains a warp gate; whether you take it changes the branch.

**Titania.** You fly the Landmaster tank rather than the Arwing. Slower, hovers with a boost, and the fight against Goras is a ground battle.

**Zoness.** A polluted ocean with searchlights. Being spotted summons reinforcements, so shoot the lights.

**Aquas.** The submarine level, in the Blue-Marine. Disliked by most players — the controls are sluggish and visibility is poor.

**Macbeth.** A train chase. Hitting the eight track switches and then the final switch derails the train, which is the good outcome and the branch condition.

**Area 6.** The hardest level in the game and the gateway to the true Venom. Continuous heavy fire; barrel roll constantly.

**Venom.** Andross. First form is a face with hands — shoot the hands, then the eyes. The true version continues into a second phase where he becomes a brain with two eyes, and then an escape sequence through a collapsing tunnel where James McCloud appears.

## Medals

Each level awards a medal for reaching a hit-count target **with all wingmen alive**. Both conditions matter and the wingman condition is what catches people.

Collecting all medals on a full run unlocks **Expert Mode**, which increases enemy aggression and removes some rings. Medals in Expert Mode unlock the sound test and the ability to fly the Arwing in the multiplayer mode's on-foot maps.

---

## Multiplayer

Four-player versus dogfighting in Arwings, Landmasters or on foot, across a handful of arenas. It is a small mode compared to GoldenEye's, but the Arwing dogfights are genuinely fun and the on-foot mode is an oddity worth seeing once.

## Emulation Notes

Star Fox 64 emulates well and is not demanding. Mupen64Plus handles it without fuss.

The one thing lost in emulation is rumble. The game shipped with the Rumble Pak and its feedback is tied closely to taking hits and firing charged shots. Most emulator setups can pass rumble through to a modern pad — enable it in the core options if your controller supports it, because the game feels noticeably flatter without it.

Note the ROM naming: the Japanese release is **Lylat Wars** in Europe and **Star Fox 64** in North America, so a European set will list it under Lylat Wars.
