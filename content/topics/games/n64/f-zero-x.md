# F-Zero X (N64)

**Developer:** Nintendo EAD | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 1998

---

## Introduction

F-Zero X made a trade that no racing game has been willing to make since: it threw away visual detail almost entirely in order to run thirty cars at a locked sixty frames per second. The result looks plain even for 1998 and feels faster than racing games made twenty-five years later, because the sense of speed comes from frame rate and track geometry rather than from motion blur.

The tracks are the other half. They are ribbons of road suspended in space that loop, twist through cylinders, run along the inside of tubes, and split. There is no scenery to speak of and no need for any.

The rule that defines the game: **your energy bar is both your health and your boost fuel.** Hitting a wall costs energy. Boosting costs energy. Running out means your machine explodes. Every lap is a decision about how much of your survival to spend on speed, and there is a recharge strip on each track that you have to plan your route around.

---

## Controls

- **Control Stick:** Steer.
- **A:** Accelerate.
- **B:** Brake.
- **Z:** Boost. Unavailable on lap 1; costs energy.
- **L / R:** Air brakes. Held individually, they tighten a turn on that side without scrubbing as much speed as braking. Held together, they slow you.
- **Z + left or right on the stick:** Side attack. Shoves a rival sideways.
- **A + B together, or the spin input:** Spin attack. Damages every machine around you.

### The Air Brakes

This is the technique that separates people who finish from people who win. Tapping the air brake on the inside of a corner rotates the machine more sharply than steering alone, at a much smaller speed cost than the brake. Cornering in F-Zero X is steering plus inside air brake, applied together, released together.

### Attacks

Ramming rivals is not incidental, it is scoring. A side attack shoves a machine into a wall or off the track. A spin attack damages everything nearby. Destroyed rivals are removed from the race. Both attacks cost you a little energy and both are how you clear a crowded pack on lap one.

---

## Energy Management

The single skill the game asks for.

- Lap 1: no boost available. Use it to position yourself and to attack.
- Laps 2 and 3: boost is available and costs energy every time.
- The **pit strip** — a coloured section of track, usually on the start-finish straight — recharges energy while you drive over it. Drive over the full length of it, not a corner of it.

The winning pattern on most tracks is to boost hard on the long straights, take the pit strip fully every lap, and avoid walls entirely. Wall contact is the biggest avoidable energy loss in the game, and on the harder cups a single bad scrape ends a run.

---

## Machines

Thirty pilots, thirty machines, and each is defined by a **Body / Boost / Grip** rating plus a weight.

- **Body** is how much damage the machine takes from collisions.
- **Boost** is how much speed the boost gives.
- **Grip** is cornering.

There is also a **balance slider** you set before each race, trading acceleration against maximum speed. Set it toward acceleration on twisting technical tracks and toward top speed on tracks with long straights. Most players leave it in the middle and lose time for it.

Recommended starting machines: the **Blue Falcon** (Captain Falcon) is the balanced default and a fine machine to learn on. The **Golden Fox** has enormous boost and no body, which makes it fast and fragile. The **Fire Stingray** has high top speed and poor acceleration, which suits Silence and other open tracks.

---

## Cups and Modes

**Grand Prix.** Four cups — Jack, Queen, King, Joker — each six tracks, at Novice, Standard, Expert and Master difficulty. Master is genuinely hard and the AI will attack you.

**Death Race.** One long track, thirty rivals, and the goal is to destroy all of them. A time-attack of violence.

**Time Attack.** Solo laps against the clock, with ghost data.

**VS Battle.** Split screen for two to four players. The frame rate drops from sixty as players are added, which is the trade the engine makes.

**X Cup.** The reason people still talk about this game. The X Cup generates six tracks **randomly** every time you enter it, using the same geometry rules the designers used. It is not a set of hidden tracks being shuffled — the courses are constructed on the fly, and some of them are absurd. This gave a 1998 cartridge effectively unlimited content.

Unlocks come from winning cups: beating a cup at a difficulty unlocks the next difficulty, and clearing cups unlocks the later ones plus additional machines and the X Cup.

## Driving Better

**Do not touch the walls.** Everything else is secondary. Energy spent on wall contact is energy you cannot spend on boost.

**Boost out of corners, not into them.** Boosting on entry sends you into a wall. Boost when the machine is already pointed down a straight.

**Take the whole pit strip.** Clipping the edge gives you a fraction of the recharge. Line up for it on the previous corner.

**Use the spin attack in the pack on lap one.** Thirty machines is a lot of traffic and thinning it early makes laps two and three cleaner.

**Learn to read the track ahead in the tube sections.** On the cylindrical courses, the horizon does not tell you where the road goes. Memorisation is genuinely required, which is why the game rewards repeat play.

## Emulation Notes

F-Zero X is one of the cleanest N64 games to emulate. It is deliberately simple graphically, and the flat-shaded polygons that made it fast on real hardware also make it undemanding for a plugin. Mupen64Plus on Batocera runs it without special configuration.

The important thing is frame rate. This game's entire identity is sixty frames per second and it should never drop. If it does, that is a host performance or plugin problem worth fixing, because a stuttering F-Zero X is a different and much worse game.

The Japanese release had a 64DD expansion, the F-Zero X Expansion Kit, which added a track editor. That expansion requires 64DD emulation and is not part of the standard cartridge ROM.
