# Paper Mario (N64)

**Developer:** Intelligent Systems | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 2001

---

## Introduction

Paper Mario arrived at the very end of the N64's life and is one of the last great games on the system. It is a turn-based role-playing game that solves the genre's main problem — that turn-based combat is passive — by making every attack and every defence require a button press at the right moment. These are the **action commands**, and they mean you are never watching a fight, you are playing one.

The presentation is the other half. Flat paper characters in three-dimensional card-stock worlds, which was a stylistic choice made partly to fit the N64's limits and which turned out to be timeless. The writing is sharper than any Mario game before it: the NPCs have opinions, the villains have personalities, and Bowser is genuinely funny.

Eight chapters, roughly thirty hours, and it is friendly to newcomers without being shallow.

## Combat

Combat is turn-based, one enemy party against Mario and one partner.

**Action commands** are the whole system. When you attack, a prompt appears — press A at the right instant, mash a button, hold and release a stick, time a sequence. A successful command roughly doubles the damage. When an enemy attacks you, pressing A just before the hit lands is a **guard** that reduces damage by one, and a tighter window gives a **Superguard** that negates it entirely and can reflect damage back.

Learning the guard timing is the difference between a comfortable playthrough and a frustrating one. Practise it on early enemies where a mistake costs nothing.

**First strike.** Hit an enemy with your hammer or a jump in the overworld before combat starts and you begin the fight with free damage. Let them touch you first and they get it instead. Always open the fight yourself.

### Stats

You level up by earning Star Points, and each level you choose one of three upgrades:

- **HP +5**
- **FP +5** (Flower Points, the magic pool for special moves)
- **BP +3** (Badge Points, the budget for equipping badges)

**BP is the one that matters most.** Badges are the customisation system and BP is what lets you wear them. A common recommendation is to favour BP early, take HP when you feel fragile, and take FP only if you lean heavily on special attacks.

## Badges

Badges are found, bought and won, and they attach abilities to Mario at a BP cost. There are around eighty. The system is the game's real depth: a badge loadout defines how you fight far more than your level does.

Notable ones:

- **Power Bounce** — jump repeatedly in one turn until you miss the timing. The strongest damage badge in the game in the hands of someone with good rhythm.
- **Multibounce** — one jump hits every enemy.
- **Power Smash / Mega Smash** — hammer damage upgrades.
- **Quick Change** — swap partners without using your turn. Expensive and excellent.
- **Double Dip** — use two items in one turn.
- **Zap Tap** — damages enemies that touch you; also prevents some contact hazards.
- **Peekaboo** — shows enemy HP.
- **Danger Mario setups** — badges like Power Rush that boost damage when your HP is very low. A high-risk build that experienced players enjoy.

Badges can be freely swapped at any time outside combat, so carry the whole collection and reconfigure for boss fights.

## Partners

Eight partners, one per chapter, and you switch between them freely. Each has an overworld ability and a combat role.

| Partner | Overworld | Combat |
|---------|-----------|--------|
| Goombario | Tattle — describes any enemy or object | Headbonk; reliable early damage |
| Kooper | Throws his shell along the ground to hit distant switches | Shell Toss |
| Bombette | Blows up cracked walls | Bomb; area damage |
| Parakarry | Carries Mario across small gaps | Sky Dive; hits airborne enemies |
| Lady Bow | Turns Mario invisible briefly | Outta Sight; strong slap; makes Mario untouchable for a turn |
| Watt | Lights dark rooms and reveals hidden things | Electric attacks; ignores defence |
| Sushie | Swims, carrying Mario over water | Belly Flop, Water Block |
| Lakilester | Carries Mario over spikes and lava | Spiny Flip |

**Tattle** is more useful than it looks. Goombario will describe almost anything in the game and often gives the hint you need. Use him on bosses to learn their weaknesses.

Partners level up separately, using Super Blocks found in each chapter. Upgrade the partners you actually use.

## The Eight Chapters

**Prologue.** Bowser takes the Star Rod, steals Peach's castle into the sky, and beats Mario in an unwinnable fight. You wake in Goomba Village.

**Chapter 1 — Storming the Castle.** Koopa Bros. Fortress. Kooper joins. The boss is the four Koopa Bros., fought as a stack.

**Chapter 2 — The Mystery of the Crystal Palace.** Dry Dry Desert and Dry Dry Ruins. Bombette joins in Chapter 1's tail; here it is the desert and Tutankoopa.

**Chapter 3 — The "Invincible" Tubba Blubba.** Forever Forest and Boo's Mansion. Lady Bow joins. Tubba Blubba genuinely cannot be hurt until you find and destroy his heart, which is hiding separately — a good structural twist.

**Chapter 4 — Trials in the Toy Box.** Shy Guy's Toy Box, entered through a chest in Mario's own house. Watt joins. General Guy and his tank.

**Chapter 5 — Hot Hot Times on Lavalava Island.** Yoshi's island and a volcano. Sushie joins. The Lava Piranha.

**Chapter 6 — Dark Days in Flower Fields.** A garden world reached by a beanstalk. Lakilester joins. Huff N. Puff.

**Chapter 7 — A Star Spirit on Ice.** Shiver Region and the Crystal Palace, the game's best dungeon, built around mirrors and duplicates. The Crystal King.

**Chapter 8 — A Star-Powered Showdown.** Bowser's Castle and Peach's Castle. Bowser has the Star Rod and is invincible; the Star Spirits you have rescued are what strip it from him, and Peach's wish is the mechanic that turns the fight.

Between chapters you play short **Peach interludes** where she sneaks around her occupied castle, eavesdrops on Bowser, and sends Mario information. These are not filler; they set up the finale.

## Star Spirits and Star Power

Rescuing each Star Spirit grants a Star Power move, used in combat from a separate meter:

Refresh (heal), Lullaby (sleep), Star Storm (damage all), Chill Out (lower attack), Time Out (stop), Up & Away (remove weak enemies), Star Beam (cancels Bowser's Star Rod invincibility), Peach Beam (the upgraded version).

**Star Beam is mandatory for the final fight.** Bowser cannot be damaged while the Star Rod protects him, and Star Beam is what removes it.

## Things Worth Doing

**Tattle everything.** Goombario's log fills up and it is both useful and well written.

**Star Pieces.** Hidden throughout the world, often under bushes or behind scenery. Traded to Merlow for badges. There are 160.

**Chuck Quizmo.** A travelling quiz-show worm who appears in towns and asks a question about the game. Correct answers give Star Pieces. He moves around, so check each town.

**Dojo challenges.** In Toad Town, a sequence of fights against increasingly strong opponents, each giving a badge.

## Emulation Notes

Paper Mario is a late-generation N64 title that uses the hardware heavily, and historically it was one of the harder games to emulate correctly — the flat-sprite-in-3D-space rendering exposed plugin bugs that other games did not. Modern emulation handles it, but it is a good stress test.

On Batocera with Mupen64Plus, if you see the paper characters rendering with wrong depth, black boxes around sprites, or broken transition effects, switch the video plugin. ParaLLEl-RDP is generally the safest choice for this game because it emulates the hardware rasteriser more literally than the faster plugins do.
