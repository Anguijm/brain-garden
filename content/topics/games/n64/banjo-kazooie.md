# Banjo-Kazooie (N64)

**Developer:** Rare | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 1998

---

## Introduction

Banjo-Kazooie is Rare's answer to Super Mario 64, and where Mario is about movement, Banjo is about worlds. Nine of them, each a self-contained themed space dense with things to find, and each one is designed as a puzzle box rather than an obstacle course. The best of them — Freezeezy Peak, Click Clock Wood — are still among the best-designed levels in the genre.

The hook is the two-currency economy. **Jiggies**, the golden jigsaw pieces, are the Stars: ten per world, spent on opening new worlds. **Notes**, the musical notes, are scattered through each world and their total unlocks the note doors that gate your progress up Gruntilda's Lair. You need both, and the design assumes you will leave a world with some of each still uncollected and come back later with a new move.

The bear carries the bird in his backpack, and all the interesting abilities come from the bird. That division of labour is the joke and it is also the moveset.

## Controls and the Moveset

You learn moves from Bottles the mole, who pops out of molehills throughout the game. Every move has a distinct input, and by the end you have around twenty.

| Move | Input | Use |
|------|-------|-----|
| Claw swipe | B | Basic melee |
| Rat-a-tat rap | B in the air | Air attack, also breaks things below |
| Forward roll | Z + B | Charge attack, breaks certain objects |
| Beak barge | Z + B (standing) | Headbutt; opens some doors |
| Feathery flap | A, A | Double jump |
| Flap flip | Z then A | High backflip |
| Beak buster | Z in the air | Ground pound |
| Talon trot | Z (held) | Run on Kazooie's legs; climbs steep slopes |
| Beak bomb | B while flying | Aerial dive attack |
| Shock spring jump | Jump on a green pad | Very high jump |
| Flight | Jump on a red feather pad, then A | Flying, costs red feathers |
| Wonderwing | Z + C-Up | Invincible charge, costs gold feathers |
| Wading boots | Walk into green boots | Cross hazardous ground briefly |
| Stilt stride | Walk into stilt pads | Cross deep sludge |
| Beak bayonet | Z + B underwater | Underwater attack |
| Turbo talon trainers | Walk into shoe pads | Very fast running, timed |

**Talon trot** is the one to internalise. Held Z, Kazooie's legs carry Banjo, and this both runs faster and climbs slopes that otherwise slide you back down. A large number of "how do I get up there" problems are just talon trot.

**Wonderwing** is the escape hatch. It costs gold feathers, which are scarce, and it makes you invincible while charging. Save it for the enemies that genuinely cannot be beaten another way.

## The Collectibles

- **Jiggies (10 per world, 100 total).** Spent at Bottles' picture puzzles in the Lair to open new worlds.
- **Musical Notes (100 per world).** Open note doors in the Lair. On the original N64 release, notes reset to zero if you leave a world and re-enter — your recorded high score per world is what counts, so a partial run is not wasted, but a full sweep in one visit is the efficient way. This was changed in the Xbox Live Arcade version; on the N64 ROM, it is the original behaviour.
- **Jinjos (5 per world).** Five orange creatures hidden in each world; finding all five gives a Jiggy.
- **Empty Honeycombs (24 total).** Six make a new health segment.
- **Mumbo Tokens.** Paid to Mumbo Jumbo the shaman to transform Banjo.

## Mumbo Transformations

Mumbo turns Banjo into something else for a fee, one form per world, and each is required for Jiggies in that world.

| World | Form | Why |
|-------|------|-----|
| Mumbo's Mountain | Termite | Climb steep walls |
| Bubblegloop Swamp | Crocodile | Swim in acid, bite |
| Freezeezy Peak | Walrus | Survive the ice, push through snow |
| Mad Monster Mansion | Pumpkin | Fit through small gaps |
| Click Clock Wood | Bee | Fly freely, enter the hive |

You cannot use most of the normal moveset while transformed, and you must return to Mumbo's skull to change back.

## The Nine Worlds

**1. Mumbo's Mountain.** The teaching world. Small, open, and it introduces Mumbo and the termite.

**2. Treasure Trove Cove.** Beach and a pirate ship. Contains Nipper the hermit crab and the lighthouse. The sand-castle floor puzzle spells a cheat code.

**3. Clanker's Cavern.** A polluted underwater world around a mechanical shark. The most disliked world in the game, because it is heavily underwater and the swimming controls are the weakest part of Banjo. Take it slow and mind your air.

**4. Bubblegloop Swamp.** Acid water, Mr Vile's eating contest, and Tiptup's turtle choir. The crocodile transformation is mandatory here.

**5. Freezeezy Peak.** A Christmas world built around an enormous snowman. Wide, vertical, and full of flight sections. Widely considered one of the best levels Rare ever made.

**6. Gobi's Valley.** Desert and pyramids. Contains the Ancient Ones and a lot of flight pads. The sand-slide sections are unforgiving.

**7. Mad Monster Mansion.** A haunted house with a genuinely large interior. The church organ puzzle and Napper the ghost are the memorable pieces. The pumpkin transformation opens most of it.

**8. Rusty Bucket Bay.** The difficulty spike. An oil-slicked cargo ship where the water damages you continuously, the engine room has a rotating propeller that kills instantly, and the whole level is unfriendly. Most players' least favourite. Take the engine room slowly and use the whistle switches.

**9. Click Clock Wood.** The best world in the game. One tree, four seasons, and you enter it four times — spring, summer, autumn, winter — with the world changed each time. Something you plant in spring is grown by summer; a nest you cannot reach in spring is reachable in autumn because the leaves have fallen. It is a genuinely clever piece of design and it is the last world for good reason.

## Gruntilda's Lair and the Ending

The Lair is the hub, and it is itself a level with Jiggies, notes and honeycombs in it. Note doors gate your ascent; the note requirements climb steeply, so a world you swept fully early pays off later.

The final confrontation is **Grunty's Furnace Fun**, a board game quiz where Gruntilda asks you questions about the game you have just played — the music of each world, the appearance of characters, the details of rooms. It is famously the thing that stops people, because it tests attention rather than skill, and a wrong answer costs health. If you have played attentively you will pass. If you rushed, you will not.

Then the fight with Gruntilda on the roof, which is a flying battle using the beak bomb and the Jinjonator.

## Emulation Notes

Banjo-Kazooie is a Rare title and Rare pushed the N64 hardware in ways that make its games among the trickier ones to emulate cleanly. Mupen64Plus on Batocera runs it, but if you see missing water surfaces, black textures in Clanker's Cavern, or the level-transition effects rendering wrong, change the video plugin. GlideN64 and ParaLLEl-RDP behave differently on Rare games and one of them is usually right.

Note that the N64 note-reset behaviour described above is authentic to this ROM. If you are used to the Xbox Live Arcade version, the difference will be noticeable and it is not an emulation fault.
