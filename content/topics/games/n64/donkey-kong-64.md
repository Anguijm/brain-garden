# Donkey Kong 64 (N64)

**Developer:** Rare | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 1999

---

## Introduction

Donkey Kong 64 is Banjo-Kazooie scaled up until the scaling became the problem. It is a large, technically impressive, genuinely well-built collectathon that made one decision too many: five playable Kongs, each with their own colour-coded version of nearly every collectible, in eight large worlds.

The consequence is that walking through a room shows you bananas you cannot pick up, switches you cannot press and barrels you cannot enter, because you are the wrong Kong. Getting everything means walking the same ground five times. The game is remembered as the moment the collectathon genre ate itself, and that reputation is deserved — but the individual worlds, the movement, and the multiplayer are all good, and if you play it without trying to reach 101% it holds up better than its reputation suggests.

Play it for the worlds. Do not play it for the completion percentage unless that is genuinely the thing you enjoy.

---

## The Five Kongs

Each Kong has a colour, a weapon, an instrument and two or three special moves. Everything in the game is keyed to one of them.

| Kong | Colour | Weapon | Instrument | Signature moves |
|------|--------|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| Donkey Kong | Yellow | Coconut Shooter | Bongos | Strong Kong (invincible), Gorilla Grab, Gorilla Gone |
| Diddy Kong | Red | Peanut Popguns | Guitar | Rocketbarrel Boost (flight), Simian Spring, Charge |
| Lanky Kong | Blue | Grape Shooter | Trombone | OrangStand (stilt walk up slopes), OrangSprint, Baboon Balloon |
| Tiny Kong | Purple | Feather Bow | Saxophone | Mini-Monkey (shrink), Pony Tail Twirl (glide), Monkeyport |
| Chunky Kong | Green | Pineapple Launcher | Triangle | Hunky Chunky (grow), Primate Punch, Gorilla Gone equivalent |

Kongs are swapped at Tag Barrels, which are scattered through every world. You will use them constantly. A room that looks impassable is usually a room you are in as the wrong Kong.

## The Collectibles

This is the list that made the game notorious.

- **Golden Bananas (201).** The Jiggy equivalent. Each is behind a specific Kong's specific ability.
- **Coloured Bananas (2,500).** 500 per Kong, colour-coded, spent at Cranky's for moves. You can only collect your own colour.
- **Banana Coins.** Also colour-coded per Kong. Currency for the shops.
- **Banana Medals (40).** Awarded for collecting 75 coloured bananas of your colour in a world. Needed to enter Jetpac.
- **Blueprints (40).** One per Kong per world, dropped by Kasplat enemies, traded to Snide to reduce the boss timer.
- **Battle Crowns (10).** Won in enemy-arena fights.
- **Boss Keys (8).** One per world, opens the Hideout Helm gates.
- **Rainbow Coins (16).** Dug up with the Shockwave move.
- **Nintendo and Rareware Coins.** Two special coins won by beating the arcade Donkey Kong and Jetpac respectively, playable inside the game.

101% completion requires all of it. Reaching 100% and finishing the game requires far less.

## The Shopkeepers

**Cranky Kong** sells moves for coloured bananas. His moves are the ones that open the world up, and you should visit him every time you have bananas to spend.

**Funky Kong** sells weapons and weapon upgrades for coins.

**Candy Kong** sells instruments and instrument upgrades. Instruments trigger scripted events — playing the right instrument on the right pad opens things — and the ammunition for them is limited.

**Snide** takes blueprints and, in exchange, shortens the Hideout Helm timer at the end of the game. This is the one that is easy to ignore and then regret, because Hideout Helm is a timed sequence and every blueprint you handed in buys you seconds.

## The Eight Worlds

**1. Jungle Japes.** The teaching world. Introduces tag barrels and the Kong-specific gating. Boss: Army Dillo.

**2. Angry Aztec.** Desert and temples. Introduces the vulture race and Llama's temple. Boss: Dogadon.

**3. Frantic Factory.** A toy factory, and home to the fully playable original arcade Donkey Kong, which you must beat for the Nintendo Coin. Boss: Mad Jack, a jack-in-the-box that hops between pillars and is the first genuinely demanding fight.

**4. Gloomy Galleon.** Underwater and the world most people dislike, for the same reason as Clanker's Cavern in Banjo — swimming, air management, and murky visibility. Boss: Puftoss.

**5. Fungi Forest.** A day/night cycle world where switches change the time and different things are accessible in each. Structurally the most interesting world in the game. Boss: Dogadon again, harder.

**6. Crystal Caves.** Ice, caves, and a lot of vertical movement. Boss: Army Dillo again, harder.

**7. Creepy Castle.** Haunted castle, a crypt, a library, and a rather good mine-cart section. Boss: King Kut Out, a cardboard cutout that moves between positions.

**8. Hideout Helm.** Not a world so much as a timed finale. A countdown runs while you complete five Kong-specific music-pad rooms to shut down the Blast-o-Matic. The blueprints you gave Snide add time. Fail and you restart the sequence.

Then **K. Rool** himself, a five-round boss fight where each round is fought by a different Kong.

## Playing It Sanely

**Do not sweep a world on your first pass.** Enter, do what your current Kong can do, move on. Come back when you have more moves. Trying to 100% a world before leaving is how people burn out in Angry Aztec and never finish.

**Buy Cranky's moves in order and immediately.** Almost every gate in the game is a missing move rather than a missing skill.

**Hand every blueprint to Snide as you get them.** They do nothing in your pocket, and Hideout Helm's timer is the one genuinely stressful thing in the game.

**The Kong barrels tell you who to be.** Each world's barrels are marked with a Kong's face. If you cannot progress, the answer is nearly always a tag barrel you walked past.

---

## Multiplayer

DK64 includes a competent four-player split-screen deathmatch and a battle arena mode, using the Kongs' weapons. It is not the reason anyone bought the game, but it is genuinely playable and holds up better than expected.

It also contains two complete arcade games — the original **Donkey Kong** and Rare's own **Jetpac** — as playable minigames, both required for full completion.

## Emulation Notes

Donkey Kong 64 requires the N64 Expansion Pak and will not run without it. Most emulators enable it by default, Batocera included, so this rarely bites. When the game does hang at the title screen, check that setting before suspecting the ROM: it is a configuration problem that presents as a bad dump.

Beyond that, it is a Rare title and shares Banjo's plugin sensitivity. If the world geometry or the water renders wrong, change the video plugin rather than the ROM.
