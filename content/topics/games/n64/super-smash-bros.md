# Super Smash Bros. (N64)

**Developer:** HAL Laboratory | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 1999

---

## Introduction

Super Smash Bros. is a fighting game that threw out the fighting-game rulebook. There are no health bars. Every character has a damage percentage that starts at zero and climbs, and the higher it climbs the further they fly when hit. You do not knock somebody out by depleting them; you knock them off the screen. That single change makes the game readable to people who have never played a fighter, because you can see who is losing at a glance.

It also has no motion inputs. Every special move is a direction plus B. The depth is in spacing, in reading an opponent's recovery, and in knowing which of your moves send an opponent sideways versus upward.

Twelve characters, nine stages, and four-player free-for-alls that are still the reason people plug an N64 in.

---

## Controls

- **Control Stick:** Move, and the direction component of every attack.
- **A:** Normal attacks. Tilt the stick as you press for up, down or side variants. Flick the stick hard and press A together for a smash attack, which is the big one.
- **B:** Special moves. Neutral B, Up B, Down B, and side B — four per character.
- **R / Z:** Shield. A bubble that shrinks as it takes damage and breaks if you hold it too long.
- **Shield + direction:** Dodge roll.
- **Z (or shield + A):** Grab. Grabs beat shields; that is the rock-paper-scissors of the game.
- **Up B:** Almost always the recovery move that gets you back to the stage after being knocked off.

### The Three Attack Types

**Tilts** (light stick + A) are fast and safe, and they are what you use most. **Smashes** (hard stick flick + A) are slow, strong, and how you actually take a stock. **Aerials** (A while in the air) are the heart of the game — most of the real combat happens off the ground.

Short hop, which is tapping the jump input rather than holding it, keeps you low so your aerials land on a grounded opponent. Learning to short hop reliably is the largest single jump in skill available in this game.

## The Twelve Characters

**Starting eight:** Mario, Donkey Kong, Link, Samus, Yoshi, Kirby, Fox, Pikachu.

**Unlockable four:**

| Character | How to unlock |
|-----------|---------------|
| Jigglypuff | Beat 1P Game on any difficulty, then win the challenge match |
| Captain Falcon | Beat 1P Game in under 20 minutes on Normal or higher |
| Ness | Beat 1P Game on Normal or higher with three lives and no continues |
| Luigi | Beat Break the Targets with all eight starting characters |

Each unlock triggers a one-on-one match against that character; you have to win it.

### Who Is Actually Good

The competitive answer, which has been stable for a very long time: **Pikachu** is the best character in the game and it is not close. Fast, heavy for his size, an up-special recovery that covers enormous distance in two directions, and a down-special thunder that hits hard. **Kirby** was dominant in the game's first year and remains strong. **Fox** and **Captain Falcon** are the next tier down and reward technical play.

At the bottom, **Link** and **Samus** are slow with poor recoveries, and **Ness** has the most difficult recovery in the game — his up-special sends a projectile that must come back and hit *him* to launch him, which is unforgiving off-stage.

This ordering matters if you are playing seriously. In a four-player party match with items on, it matters far less, because item chaos flattens the difference.

## Stages

Nine, plus the unlockable one.

Peach's Castle, Sector Z, Congo Jungle, Planet Zebes, Hyrule Castle, Yoshi's Island, Dream Land, Saffron City, Mushroom Kingdom (unlocked by playing 50 matches).

Competitive play settles on **Dream Land**, which is a flat platform layout with mild wind and no stage hazards, and it is the closest thing the game has to a neutral field. Hyrule Castle is the party favourite and the competitive nightmare, because the tornado on the right side throws people around at random.

---

## Modes

**1P Game.** A ladder of fights ending with Master Hand. Between rounds there are bonus stages — Break the Targets and Board the Platforms — and the target stage is where most players learn their character's movement properly, because it forces you to use the recovery moves precisely.

**VS Mode.** The reason the game exists. Stock or time, items on or off, one to four players with computer opponents filling empty slots.

**Training.** Set a character, a stage and a CPU behaviour. Useful for learning combos and, more usefully, for learning how far off-stage your recovery actually reaches.

## Playing Better

**Damage percentage is a resource, not a health bar.** At low percent, you are safe and should be attacking aggressively. At high percent, one smash attack ends you, so your job changes to not getting hit while you land damage in small safe pieces.

**Directional influence.** While you are being launched, holding the stick perpendicular to your flight path bends the trajectory. This does not stop the knockback, but it moves where you land, and at high percentages it is the difference between dying and grabbing the ledge. Almost nobody discovers this on their own.

**Edge-guarding wins games.** When an opponent is knocked off, do not wait on the stage for them to come back. Go out and hit them, or drop an attack on the ledge they must grab. Most stocks in a good match are taken off-stage, not on it.

**Shield-grab.** If someone is throwing attacks at your shield, grab them. Grabs go through shields, and this is what punishes an opponent who is mashing.

**Items.** With items on, the Hammer and the Beam Sword change matches on their own. Turning items off is not snobbery; it makes the game a test of the fighting rather than of who was standing near the crate.

## Emulation Notes

Smash 64 runs well on Mupen64Plus. It is not visually demanding and rarely needs plugin fiddling.

The thing to get right is inputs. Smash relies on the difference between a light stick tilt and a hard stick flick, and on a modern controller with a shorter stick throw the game will read tilts as smashes constantly. If your character keeps launching a smash attack when you wanted a tilt, raise the deadzone and reduce the analogue sensitivity until the two are distinguishable. This one setting is worth more than everything else on this page.

Four-player local play needs four controllers mapped in Batocera before you launch. Map them all first, then start the game; hot-plugging a pad mid-session usually will not be picked up.
