# Power Stone 2 (Dreamcast)

**Developer:** Capcom | **Publisher:** Capcom | **Year:** 2000

---

## Introduction

A four-player arena fighting game in which the arena is as dangerous as the opponents. *Power Stone 2* expanded the original from two players to four and turned the stages into hazards — a ship that breaks apart while you fight on it, a castle that floods, a plane you fall out of.

It is closer to *Super Smash Bros.* than to *Street Fighter*, and it is one of the best local multiplayer games ever made. It was also very expensive on the second-hand market for years before the *Power Stone Collection* on PSP.

---

## Controls

- **Stick:** Move freely in a 3D arena.
- **A:** Jump. **B:** Attack. **X:** Throw / pick up object.
- **Y:** Guard. **Triggers:** Special once powered.

---

## Power Stones

Three gems drop into the arena during a fight. **Collect all three and you transform** — a super mode with a new moveset, a huge damage boost and two devastating specials, lasting a limited time.

**The whole match is a fight over the stones.** Hitting a transformed player makes them drop stones. A player about to collect their third is the immediate priority for everyone else, and this creates the natural rubber-banding that makes four-player matches chaotic and fair at the same time.

---

## Everything is a weapon

Tables, chairs, barrels, brooms, swords, rocket launchers, flamethrowers. **You pick up and throw almost anything in the environment**, and weapons spawn constantly.

**Crafting.** New to this game: collected items can be **combined into new weapons and gear** between matches, which unlocks progressively stranger equipment.

---

## The stages

Stages are multi-phase set pieces, not backdrops. A fight starts on a ship's deck, the ship is attacked, the deck breaks, you fall to a lower level, the level floods. **Positioning matters because the stage is about to change.**

---

## Strategy

**Contest every stone.** Letting someone transform unopposed loses the round.

**Attack the transformed player as a group** in four-player. They will drop stones.

**Use the environment.** Thrown objects do real damage and cost nothing.

**Learn the stage phases.** Knowing that the floor is about to give way is worth more than a combo.

**Guard-cancel out of corners.** Being cornered in a 3D arena is death by pile-on.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Flycast core at high internal resolution.

**Four controllers work.** This is the game on the box most worth having spare pads for — it is designed entirely around four people in a room and it is much less interesting alone.
