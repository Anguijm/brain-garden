# Marvel vs. Capcom 2: New Age of Heroes (Dreamcast)

**Developer:** Capcom | **Publisher:** Capcom | **Year:** 2000

---

## Introduction

**Fifty-six characters**, three-on-three team battles, and a game so unbalanced that its imbalance became the reason people love it. Marvel vs. Capcom 2 was a competitive fixture for well over a decade despite a roster where perhaps eight characters are genuinely viable.

The Dreamcast port is arcade-accurate and was, for years, the only good home version — the game vanished from digital storefronts when the Marvel licence lapsed and physical copies became expensive.

---

## Controls

Six buttons in the arcade, and the Dreamcast pad has enough:

- **A / B:** Light and medium punch. **X / Y:** Light and medium kick.
- **L:** Assist 1 (partner attacks and leaves). **R:** Assist 2.
- **Two attack buttons together:** Tag in that partner.

---

## Teams and assists

You pick **three characters** and one **assist type** for each. Only one fights at a time; the others can be called in for a single attack, or tagged in fully.

**Assists are the core of the game.** A character on their own is manageable; a character covered by a well-chosen assist is a wall. The assist type you pick — anti-air, projectile, launcher — matters as much as the character.

---

## The hyper combo meter

Builds as you fight, up to five stocks. Spend one for a super. **Spend three for a Delayed Hyper Combo chain**, where all three characters' supers fire in sequence — the signature spectacle of the game.

---

## The tier problem, honestly stated

The competitive consensus is that four characters — **Magneto, Storm, Sentinel and Cable** — dominate, with a small second tier behind them. Most of the fifty-six are not viable at a high level.

**This does not matter for casual play at all.** The roster's breadth is the appeal: it is a game where Ryu fights Spider-Man alongside Mega Man and a giant robot, and the pure spectacle of a full team super is the point.

---

## Fundamentals

**Air combos.** Launch with a heavy attack, jump after them, continue the combo airborne. This is the basic damage tool and nearly every character has one.

**Push blocking.** Block and press two attack buttons to shove the attacker away, escaping pressure. Essential and widely unknown.

**Snap back.** Forces an opponent to swap characters, which is how you get at a weak team member.

---

## Strategy

**Pick assists that cover your point character's weakness**, not three characters you like separately.

**Learn one air combo.** It is most of your damage.

**Push block out of pressure.** Blocking forever loses.

**Do not chase the tier list** unless you are playing someone serious.

## Emulation Notes

Runs well on Batocera's Flycast core. It is a 2D sprite game on 3D backgrounds — **raising internal resolution sharpens the backgrounds but the sprites stay native**, which some people find mismatched. Native resolution with a CRT-style shader is a defensible alternative here.

The Dreamcast version is arcade-accurate and was the reference home version for years.
