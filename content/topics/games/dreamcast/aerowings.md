# AeroWings (Dreamcast)

**Developer:** CRI | **Publisher:** Crave Entertainment | **Year:** 1999

---

## Introduction

A flight simulator built around the **Blue Impulse**, the Japan Air Self-Defense Force's aerobatic display team. Known in Japan as *Aero Dancing*, renamed AeroWings for the Western release.

**It is a training simulator, not a combat game.** There are no weapons. The entire game is about flying precisely: holding formation, executing display manoeuvres to tolerance, and passing checkrides.

That makes it a niche title, and the reason it is worth having is that it is one of very few serious flight simulators on any console of its era, and the Dreamcast rendered it at a frame rate the PlayStation could not have managed.

---

## Controls

- **Stick:** Pitch and roll. **L / R triggers:** Rudder (yaw).
- **A:** Throttle up. **B:** Throttle down.
- **X / Y:** Airbrake, landing gear, camera.
- **Start:** Pause and instrument options.

**The analogue stick is doing real work here.** Small inputs matter, and heavy-handed flying fails the tolerance checks immediately.

---

## Structure

**Training mode** is the core: a progression of exercises from basic takeoff and level flight through turns, rolls, loops and formation positions. Each is scored against a tolerance and you must pass to progress.

**Mission mode** strings manoeuvres into full display routines.

**Free flight** lets you fly the map without scoring.

**Unlocking aircraft** is done by completing training, and the roster covers JASDF types plus some recognisable Western aircraft.

---

## What it demands

**Precision over reflex.** A manoeuvre is judged on altitude held, heading maintained, bank angle, and smoothness. Passing is a matter of small corrections, not fast ones.

**Formation flying** is the hardest part: holding a position relative to a lead aircraft that is itself manoeuvring, using tiny throttle and stick adjustments.

---

## Strategy

**Trim your inputs.** Fly with fingertips, not fists.

**Watch the artificial horizon**, not the scenery.

**Throttle is a control surface.** Most formation errors are speed errors, not stick errors.

**Repeat the early exercises** until they are boring. The later ones assume that fluency.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs well on Batocera's Flycast core with a modern analogue stick, which is arguably better than the original Dreamcast pad for this.

**Both AeroWings games on this box are the North American releases.** The Japanese *Aero Dancing* series is more extensive and largely untranslated; the two Western releases are what run here. [AeroWings 2: Airstrike](topics/games/dreamcast/aerowings-2) adds mission variety and is the better of the pair.
