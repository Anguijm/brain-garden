# Jet Grind Radio (Dreamcast)

**Developer:** Smilebit | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 2000

---

## Introduction

Known as **Jet Set Radio** everywhere except North America, where Sega renamed it for reasons no one has satisfactorily explained. It is the game that introduced **cel-shading** to a mass audience — the hard-outlined, flat-coloured cartoon look that is now a standard rendering style and was startling in 2000.

You are a member of the GGs, a gang of rollerblading graffiti artists in Tokyo-to, spraying over rival tags while the police escalate from truncheons to helicopters to tanks.

The soundtrack — Hideki Naganuma's — is as celebrated as the visuals.

---

## Controls

- **Stick:** Skate. **A:** Jump. **B:** Spray / attack.
- **X / Y:** Camera. **L / R:** Camera rotate.
- **Grind:** Skate onto a rail and you grind automatically; jump between rails to chain.

---

## The spray mechanic

Small tags are sprayed with a button press. **Large tags require a directional sequence** — the game shows a series of stick motions and you perform them while standing at the wall, vulnerable, while police shoot at you.

On the original release this used analogue stick gestures that were genuinely fiddly. **It is the one part of the game that has aged least well**, and it is worth knowing before the first big tag frustrates you.

---

## Structure

Each stage is an open area with a set of tag locations and a time limit. Complete the tags, escape, move on. Between missions there are rival challenges — races and tag battles — that unlock new characters.

**Graffiti Souls** are hidden collectibles that unlock new tag designs, and they are scattered in places that require real skating skill to reach.

---

## The police

They escalate. Early stages have officers who chase you; later ones have riot squads, helicopters and eventually a tank. **You cannot fight them meaningfully** — the answer is always to keep moving, because standing still to tag is the only time you are vulnerable.

---

## Strategy

**Learn the rails first, tags second.** A stage you can traverse fluidly is a stage where tagging is safe.

**Tag the ground-level marks last.** They are the easiest and you will want them when the police have arrived.

**Refill spray cans constantly.** Running dry mid-tag is the most common failure.

**Use the camera.** The fixed-ish camera fights you and the manual rotate is essential on the vertical stages.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs very well on Batocera's Flycast core.

**Increase the internal resolution.** Cel-shading is line art, and line art at high resolution looks dramatically cleaner — this is one of the games where emulation is unambiguously better than the original hardware.

A sequel, *Jet Set Radio Future*, was Xbox-exclusive and has never been ported.
