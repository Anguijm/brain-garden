# Sega Dreamcast Game Guides

Sega's last console, launched in 1998 and discontinued in 2001. It shipped with a modem as
standard when no console went online, it ran arcade conversions better than the arcades, and it
died before the PlayStation 2 had proved anything. Everything on this list came out inside a
two-and-a-half year window.

## The port that beat the arcade

**Soulcalibur** on Dreamcast is visually better than the arcade machine it came from, with an
extra single-player mode on top. That had essentially never happened before, and it is why this
conversion is still cited as the best ever made.

## One thing you cannot get back

**Phantasy Star Online** was the first console online RPG, and its servers are gone. What runs here
is the offline campaign — real, and worth playing — but the game that mattered was four strangers
from different countries coordinating through a symbol-chat system because they had no shared
language. That version of it no longer exists anywhere.

## One deliberate exception to the US-only rule

**Ikaruga** is the Japanese release, because there was never a Western Dreamcast version — it
reached the West on GameCube. It plays language-independently with English menus. Every other disc
here is the North American release, including **both AeroWings games**, which meant the Japanese
*Aero Dancing* import that had been considered for this shelf was unnecessary.

## Multi-disc games

**Shenmue** (3 discs), **Skies of Arcadia** (2) and **Code Veronica** (2) each appear as a single
entry on the box. An `.m3u` playlist groups them and the individual discs are hidden, so a
three-disc game does not occupy three tiles. Disc changes happen from the emulator's disc-control
menu without quitting.

## Emulation

The Dreamcast is one of the easier consoles to emulate well and **Flycast** is mature. Higher
internal resolution helps almost everything here, and helps **Jet Grind Radio** most — cel-shading
is line art, and line art rewards resolution. The exception is **Marvel vs. Capcom 2**, whose 2D
sprites stay native no matter what the backgrounds do.

**Ikaruga renders as a tall strip with black bars.** That is correct: it is a vertically-oriented
arcade game and the screen is meant to be rotated.
