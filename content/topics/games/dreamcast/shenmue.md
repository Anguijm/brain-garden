# Shenmue (Dreamcast)

**Developer:** Sega AM2 | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 2000 (US)

---

## Introduction

The most expensive video game ever made at the time — figures around \$47 to \$70 million are cited, against a Dreamcast install base that could never have repaid it. Yu Suzuki, who had made *Out Run*, *Virtua Fighter* and *Space Harrier*, spent years building a simulation of a Yokosuka suburb in 1986 in which a teenager looks for his father's killer.

**It invented things that are now everywhere** and were unheard of then: a full day/night cycle with weather drawn from real 1986 records, shops that open and close on schedules, non-player characters with individual daily routines, and the button-prompt cutscene that the industry later named the quick-time event.

It is also slow, stilted, and frequently boring in ways its defenders will freely admit. **You should know what you are getting into.**

---

## Controls

- **Stick:** Move. **A:** Interact / confirm. **B:** Cancel.
- **X / Y:** Context actions. **L / R:** Look around, first-person view.
- **Start:** Menu, notebook, save.

---

## What you actually do

**Ask people questions.** The core loop is finding someone who knows something, asking them, and being pointed at someone else. You take notes — the game keeps a notebook — and you follow leads around a town where everyone has somewhere to be.

**Wait for time to pass.** Shops open at set hours. A contact might only be somewhere at 7pm. The game expects you to fill the intervening hours, and provides an arcade, a convenience store, capsule toy machines, a cat to feed and forklift racing.

**Fight, occasionally.** The combat is *Virtua Fighter*'s engine, and it is good — but there is not much of it.

**Press buttons when told.** Quick-time events during set pieces.

---

## The forklift

Partway through, Ryo takes a job at the harbour driving a forklift, and **you drive a forklift to work every morning for an in-game week**, with a race against the other drivers each day.

This is either the moment the game's commitment to mundanity becomes transcendent, or the moment you stop playing. It is genuinely divisive and it is not a small section.

---

## Three discs, and where they go

Disc 1 is Yokosuka and the search for leads. Disc 2 moves to the harbour. Disc 3 is largely the finale. **The game ends on a cliffhanger** and is continued in *Shenmue II*, which itself does not conclude the story — *Shenmue III* arrived in 2019 and did not finish it either.

---

## Strategy

**Take the notebook seriously.** It is the only record of what you were told.

**Ask everyone about everything.** Progress is gated on conversations, not on skill.

**Do not fight the clock.** If you are told to come back at eight, go do something else; there is no way to skip time efficiently, and that is deliberate.

**Save often.** Saving is at the notebook and it is the only way out.

## Emulation Notes

Runs well on Batocera's Flycast core, which is one of the more mature emulators available — the Dreamcast is comparatively easy to emulate and Flycast is very good at it.

**This is a three-disc game.** On this box the three discs are grouped into a single entry by an `.m3u` playlist, and disc changes are made from the emulator's disc-control menu without leaving the game.

**Widescreen and higher internal resolution both work**, and this game benefits from them considerably.
