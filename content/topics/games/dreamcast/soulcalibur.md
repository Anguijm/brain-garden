# Soulcalibur (Dreamcast)

**Developer:** Namco | **Publisher:** Namco | **Year:** 1999

---

## Introduction

**The best arcade-to-console conversion ever made**, and that is close to a consensus position rather than an opinion. Namco did not port the arcade game — they rebuilt it, with higher-resolution models, more detailed stages, better lighting and an entire additional single-player mode.

The Dreamcast version is **visually superior to the arcade original**, which had essentially never happened before. It reviewed at near-perfect scores and it is still the game people point to when arguing the Dreamcast was ahead of its time.

---

## Controls

- **Stick / D-Pad:** Move; **hold back to guard**, and the guard is directional.
- **A:** Horizontal attack. **B:** Vertical attack. **X:** Kick. **Y / R:** Guard.
- **Eight-way run:** Hold a diagonal and the character runs around the arena in 3D.

---

## The eight-way run, which is the system

Most fighting games of the era were 2D planes with 3D graphics. Soulcalibur lets you **move freely around your opponent** by holding a direction, and this changes everything:

**Vertical attacks (B)** are strong but travel in a line, and can be **sidestepped**.
**Horizontal attacks (A)** are weaker but sweep wide, and **catch someone stepping**.

So the core mental game is: they are going to step, so I swing wide; they expect wide, so I commit vertically. It is a genuine rock-paper-scissors layered under the weapon combat.

---

## Ring outs

**Falling out of the arena loses the round instantly**, regardless of health. Arenas have edges, and pushing someone toward one changes the entire calculus of a fight — a player with a health lead near an edge is not safe.

---

## Guard impact

Press guard at the exact moment an attack lands and you **deflect it**, leaving the attacker open. It is the game's parry, it is generous by modern standards, and it is what separates competent players from good ones.

---

## Mission Battle mode

Exclusive to this version. A map of missions, each with a modifier — fight on a narrow bridge, fight while poisoned, fight three opponents — unlocking weapons, characters and art. **It is a substantial single-player game** and the reason the Dreamcast version is the definitive one.

---

## Strategy

**Guard is directional.** Holding back blocks high and mid; you must crouch for lows.

**Step, do not block, against verticals.** Sidestepping beats a whole class of attacks outright.

**Watch the ring edge.** Positional awareness wins more rounds than combos.

**Learn one character's three or four best moves** rather than a full move list.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Flycast core, and **at higher internal resolution it looks genuinely modern** — the models were built with more geometry than the hardware could comfortably display.

Fighting games are the one genre where emulation input lag matters most; on this box, with a wired display path, it is fine for single-player and casual versus.
