# Resident Evil: Code Veronica (Dreamcast)

**Developer:** Capcom | **Publisher:** Capcom | **Year:** 2000

---

## Introduction

The first Resident Evil built in **full real-time 3D**. The three PlayStation games used pre-rendered background paintings with 3D characters walking over them; Code Veronica renders the environments as actual geometry with a moving camera.

It is also, in story terms, the real *Resident Evil 3* — it continues Claire Redfield's story directly from *Resident Evil 2*, resolves the Umbrella arc, and reintroduces Chris. The game numbered 3 was a side story about Jill.

Dreamcast-exclusive at launch, and a genuine system seller.

---

## Controls — tank controls, explained

- **Up:** Walk forward **in the direction the character faces**.
- **Left / Right:** **Rotate** the character, not strafe.
- **Down:** Back up. **Down + B:** Quick 180-degree turn.
- **A:** Action. **B:** Run (hold). **R:** Ready weapon. **A while aiming:** Fire.

**You cannot move and shoot.** Aiming roots you in place, deliberately.

Tank controls are widely disliked and they are not an accident: with a camera that cuts between angles, "up" meaning "the way the character faces" keeps movement consistent across a cut, where "up means screen-up" would reverse your direction every time the camera changed. **The retro-style remakes reverted to this scheme for the same reason.**

## Survival horror economics

**Ammunition is scarce and most enemies should be avoided.** Running past a zombie costs nothing; killing one costs bullets you will want later.

**Inventory slots are limited** and everything competes — keys, health, ammunition, and the ink ribbons needed to save.

**Saving costs an ink ribbon.** They are finite. Saving carelessly early can leave you unable to save later, and the game will not warn you.

---

## The mid-game trap

Partway through, **you switch characters** and anything the first character left in a storage box is what the second character has. The game gives no explicit warning that you are about to lose access.

**Before any point of no return, leave a strong weapon and healing in the item box.** This is the single most important piece of advice about Code Veronica, and it has stranded a lot of playthroughs.

---

## Strategy

**Run past what you can.**

**Learn the 180-degree turn.** It is the difference between escaping and being cornered.

**Ration ink ribbons.** Save at meaningful progress, not every room.

**Combine herbs** rather than using them singly.

**Aim up and down.** Some enemies require it and the game never says so clearly.
