# AeroWings 2: Airstrike (Dreamcast)

**Developer:** CRI | **Publisher:** Crave Entertainment | **Year:** 2000

---

## Introduction

The sequel to [AeroWings](topics/games/dreamcast/aerowings), and the better game — because it adds the thing the first one deliberately lacked: **actual missions with weapons**.

The aerobatic training from the first game is still here, and still the foundation. What is new is a campaign of combat and tactical sorties — interception, escort, ground attack, reconnaissance — flown to the same precision standards.

---

## Controls

- **Stick:** Pitch and roll. **L / R:** Rudder.
- **A / B:** Throttle up and down.
- **X:** Fire selected weapon. **Y:** Cycle weapons and targets.
- **Start:** Pause, map, objectives.

---

## What is new

**Weapons and combat.** Air-to-air missiles, guns, air-to-ground ordnance, with a lock-on system and countermeasures.

**A mission structure** with objectives, wingmen and failure conditions, rather than pure exercises.

**A much larger aircraft roster**, unlocked by campaign progress.

**Retained:** the full aerobatic training suite, including formation flying, so the first game's content is largely subsumed.

---

## How it flies

**It is still a simulator, not an arcade shooter.** Aircraft stall, energy management matters, and a turn fought badly bleeds speed you cannot recover. If you come to it expecting *Ace Combat* — which is an arcade flight game with a simulator's aesthetic — you will find this heavier and less forgiving.

**Missiles need parameters.** Firing outside range or off-boresight wastes them, and you carry few.

---

## Strategy

**Do the training first**, even for the combat campaign. Formation and precision exercises teach the flight model you will need under fire.

**Manage energy.** Altitude is speed you have not spent yet. A fight that starts high is a fight you are winning.

**Do not turn with everything.** Extend, climb, come back. Turning contests bleed you dry.

**Check missile range before firing.** Ordnance is limited.

**Use the rudder.** The triggers are not decorative; coordinated turns are faster and more stable.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs well on Batocera's Flycast core, and benefits from a modern controller's analogue triggers for rudder control — the original Dreamcast triggers were analogue too, so this maps naturally.

This is the North American release. Higher internal resolution helps considerably, since the game's draw distance and horizon detail were among its selling points.
