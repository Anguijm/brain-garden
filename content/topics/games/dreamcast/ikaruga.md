# Ikaruga (Dreamcast)

**Developer:** Treasure | **Publisher:** ESP | **Year:** 2002 (Japan)

---

## Introduction

Treasure's shooter, and one of the most tightly designed games ever made. It has **one idea**, executed with total commitment, and the whole game is built out of it.

**Your ship can switch between black and white polarity.** Bullets of your own colour are absorbed and charge your weapon. Bullets of the opposite colour kill you.

That is the entire mechanic, and from it comes a game that is simultaneously a bullet-hell shooter and a puzzle where the correct answer is to fly *into* the gunfire.

**Region note:** this is the Japanese release. It is a documented exception to this library's US-only rule, because there was never a Western Dreamcast release — Ikaruga came to the West on GameCube and later on digital platforms. **The game is language-independent in play**, with menus in English.

---

## Controls

- **Stick / D-Pad:** Move.
- **A:** Fire (hold for auto).
- **B:** **Switch polarity** — the whole game.
- **X / Y:** Release the charged homing shot.

---

## How polarity actually works

**Same colour: absorbed.** Flying into black bullets while black is safe and fills your energy gauge.

**Opposite colour: death.** One hit.

**Damage doubles against the opposite colour.** Black shots do double damage to white enemies.

So each moment poses the same question: do I switch to absorb this stream, or stay to kill this enemy faster? The two goals conflict constantly and that tension is the game.

---

## The chain scoring system

Destroying **three enemies of the same colour in a row** scores a chain. Chain repeatedly and the multiplier climbs enormously.

**Breaking the chain — killing one enemy of the wrong colour — resets it.** So high-level play means ignoring the enemy about to shoot you because it is the wrong colour, and killing things in a specific memorised order.

**This is where Ikaruga stops being a shooter and becomes something closer to a rhythm game.** The stages are fixed, the enemy order never varies, and mastery is route memorisation.

---

## Structure

**Five stages. Around twenty-five minutes to finish.**

That is the whole game, and it is deliberate. Ikaruga is not long; it is deep. It expects hundreds of runs at the same five stages, chasing a better chain.

---

## Strategy

**Absorb, do not dodge.** New players dodge everything out of instinct. The correct response to a wall of black bullets is to turn black and fly through.

**Watch the energy gauge.** Absorbed bullets charge the homing release, which is your bomb.

**Learn one stage at a time.** Do not attempt the full run early.

**Ignore score at first.** Survive first, chain later, or you will do neither.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Flycast core.

**The screen is vertically oriented.** Ikaruga is a "tate" game, designed for a monitor rotated ninety degrees. On a normal horizontal display it renders in a tall strip with black bars either side — this is correct, not a fault. The emulator can rotate the image if you ever mount a screen sideways.

Treasure also made [Gunstar Heroes](topics/games/megadrive/gunstar-heroes) on the Mega Drive, on this box, and the boss-design lineage is direct.
