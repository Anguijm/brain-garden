# Game Boy Game Guides

Standalone walkthroughs for the original Game Boy titles on the UM790Pro. The hardware was
underpowered even in 1989 — monochrome, no backlight, four shades of green — and it won anyway
on battery life, price and library. These are the library.

## Three games that are not what their names suggest

**Donkey Kong (1994)** is not the arcade game. It opens as a faithful conversion of the four
1981 stages, then Donkey Kong grabs Pauline and climbs away and **97 more stages** unfold as
puzzle-platforming. People bounce off it in the first ten minutes. The arcade original is also
on this box under MAME; they are entirely different games.

**Final Fantasy Adventure** is not a Final Fantasy game. It is the **first Mana game**,
*Seiken Densetsu*, and the direct ancestor of Secret of Mana. Square attached the FF brand for
the West. The same game shipped as *Mystic Quest* in Europe and was remade as *Sword of Mana*
and *Adventures of Mana* — four names, one game.

**Kirby's Dream Land** has **no copy abilities**. Swallowing an enemy to steal its power
arrives a year later in *Kirby's Adventure* on NES. Here inhaling is purely a weapon.

## One that cannot be finished alone

**Pokémon Red** has four Pokémon that evolve only by trading — Kadabra, Machoke, Graveler,
Haunter — plus version exclusives requiring a second cartridge. On a single emulated copy the
Pokédex cannot be completed without link emulation. Worth knowing before you invest thirty
hours in it.

## The palette

These games were drawn for a screen with four shades of olive green and no backlight. On a
bright 4K panel they render as stark black-on-white by default, which is not how anyone saw
them. Batocera's `gb` shaderset applies a DMG colourisation that gets closer, and the same
handheld-nebula shaders now applied to Game Gear exist for Game Boy if you want the shell and
LCD grid too.
