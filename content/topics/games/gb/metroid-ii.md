# Metroid II: Return of Samus (Game Boy)

**Developer:** Nintendo R&D1 | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 1991

---

## Introduction

Metroid II sends Samus to SR388 with a single instruction: exterminate the Metroids. All of them. The game tracks how many remain in a counter on screen, and that counter is the entire structure — kill enough and the planet's acid level drops, opening the way deeper.

It is the most focused game in the series and the strangest-feeling. There is no map. The world is a vertical descent through caverns that look alike on a small monochrome screen, and getting lost is a normal part of playing it.

It also introduced most of the series' permanent vocabulary: the Spider Ball, the Space Jump, the Plasma Beam, and the Varia Suit as a visible upgrade.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move. Down to morph into a ball.
- **A:** Jump. **B:** Shoot. **Select:** cycle missiles/beams. **Start:** status.
- **Up:** aim upward while shooting.

---

## Upgrades

| Upgrade | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Morph Ball / Bombs** | Roll into gaps; bombs break blocks and boost you upward |
| **Spider Ball** | Cling to and roll along any surface. The most useful traversal item in the game |
| **Spring Ball** | Jump while in ball form |
| **High Jump / Space Jump** | Space Jump allows indefinite mid-air jumps once the rhythm is learned |
| **Screw Attack** | Damages enemies on contact while jumping |
| **Varia Suit** | Halves damage taken |
| **Beams** (Ice, Wave, Spazer, Plasma) | Only one beam at a time — swapping means giving one up |

**The Spider Ball changes the game.** Vertical shafts that look impassable become trivial once you can roll up walls. If a route seems blocked, try clinging.

**Space Jump takes practice.** The timing is a rhythmic tap at the apex of each jump; missed timing drops you. It is worth spending a few minutes on flat ground learning it.

---

## The Metroid counter

The number on screen is how many Metroids remain on the planet. Killing them lowers the acid blocking your descent, so progress is strictly: hunt the local Metroids, watch the acid drop, go deeper.

**Metroid forms escalate**: Alpha, Gamma, Zeta, Omega, and finally the Queen. Each is larger, tougher and more aggressive than the last, and missiles are the answer to all of them.

**If the acid has not dropped, you have missed one.** That is the commonest way to get stuck — a Metroid tucked in a side passage you walked past.

---

## Strategy

**Missiles are your real weapon.** The beam is for small enemies; every Metroid fight is a missile fight. Farm missile refills before descending.

**Map on paper, or accept getting lost.** There is genuinely no in-game map. Some players sketch one; others treat the disorientation as part of the atmosphere. Either is valid — but do not expect the game to help.

**Bomb suspicious floors and walls.** Breakable blocks are frequent and rarely signposted.

**When stuck, count Metroids.** The counter tells you whether the problem is a missed kill or a missed passage.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy core. **Rewind is enabled**, which is genuinely valuable here — Metroid II's hazards are frequently off-screen on the small display, and the Space Jump's timing is unforgiving until it clicks.

The monochrome art is very dark by design and can be hard to read on a bright panel; if you struggle to distinguish background from foreground, a Game Boy palette shader helps considerably.

The ROM is `Metroid II - Return of Samus (World)` — one cartridge image worldwide, so it is the correct North American file. A 2017 3DS remake (*Samus Returns*) and a fan remake (*AM2R*) both exist and both add a map; this is the 1991 original.
