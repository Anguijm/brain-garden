# Gargoyle's Quest (Game Boy)

**Developer:** Capcom | **Publisher:** Capcom | **Year:** 1990

---

## Introduction

Gargoyle's Quest is a spin-off of *Ghosts 'n Goblins* in which you play **Red Arremer** — the flying red demon that tormented Arthur and is widely considered the hardest ordinary enemy in that series. Capcom gave the villain his own game, named him Firebrand, and built something genuinely strange around him.

It is half action-platformer and half RPG: overhead world map with random encounters and towns, dropping into side-scrolling action stages. The mix was unusual in 1990 and still reads as unusual now.

Its reputation is difficulty, and that reputation is earned — but most of it comes from one mechanic people misuse.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move; up/down to climb walls while clinging.
- **A:** Jump. **Hold A after jumping to hover.**
- **B:** Fire projectile.
- **Against a wall in mid-air:** Firebrand clings, and can climb up or down.

### Hovering, which is the whole game

Firebrand cannot fly freely. He jumps, and while airborne, **holding A makes him hover in place for a limited time** — a couple of seconds at first, longer as you upgrade.

The hover meter refills only when you land. So a jump-hover-jump chain across a long gap drains it, and running out mid-air drops you.

**Players who treat it as flight die constantly. Players who treat it as an extended jump do fine.** Plan each crossing as a jump with a controlled float at the end, not as free movement.

---

## Wall clinging

Touch a vertical surface in mid-air and Firebrand grips it, then climbs. Combined with hovering, this is how you scale the game's vertical stages — jump, hover to the wall, cling, climb, jump again.

---

## Progression

**Vials** are currency, dropped by enemies and found in stages.

**Upgrades come from story progress**, not shops, and each expands what you can do:

| Upgrade | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Wing upgrades** | Longer hover duration |
| **Claw upgrades** | Higher jump |
| **Buster upgrades** | Stronger projectile; some break specific blocks |
| **Talismans** | Access to new regions |

**The projectile types matter as keys.** Certain blocks yield only to a specific buster, so an area that seems impassable usually needs an upgrade you have not collected rather than skill you have not developed.

---

## Structure

The overworld is traversed top-down with random encounters that drop into short combat stages. Towns hold NPCs who give directions and story. Major locations are full side-scrolling levels ending in a boss.

**Deaths return you to the start of the current stage**, not the whole game, which keeps it fair despite the difficulty.

---

## Strategy

**Never treat hover as flight.** Land often. The meter only refills on the ground.

**Cling to everything.** Vertical routes are usually safer than horizontal ones.

**Talk to townspeople.** Directions are conversational, and the game does not otherwise signpost.

**When stuck, look for a missed upgrade.** Progress here is gated by ability, and the game rarely tells you which one it wants.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy core with **rewind enabled**, which suits a game whose deaths come from mistimed hovers over instant-death pits.

The ROM is `Gargoyle's Quest (USA, Europe)`. A sequel, *Gargoyle's Quest II*, followed on NES and Game Boy, and *Demon's Crest* on SNES completes the trilogy — all starring the same character, and all worth knowing about if this one lands.
