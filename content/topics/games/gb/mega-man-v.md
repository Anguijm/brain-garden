# Mega Man V (Game Boy)

**Developer:** Minakuchi Engineering | **Publisher:** Capcom | **Year:** 1994

---

## Introduction

Mega Man V is the best of the five Game Boy Mega Man games and the only one with a wholly original cast. The first four adapted NES bosses onto the handheld; this one invents the **Stardroids** — eight robots named after planets — and builds fresh stages around them.

It also gives Mega Man the **Mega Arm**, a rocket fist that replaces the standard buster charge, and Tango, a robotic cat support unit. It arrived in 1994, late in the Game Boy's life, and looks and plays noticeably better than its predecessors.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move, duck.
- **A:** Jump.
- **B:** Fire. **Hold to charge** the Mega Arm.
- **Select:** Weapon menu.

### The Mega Arm

Charging and releasing fires Mega Man's fist, which travels forward, **grabs items on contact**, and returns. It is both a stronger attack and a retrieval tool — useful for collecting energy pickups across gaps you cannot cross.

Unlike the NES charge shot, the Mega Arm is a projectile that must return before you can fire another, so spamming it is not possible.

---

## The Stardroids and weapon order

Eight bosses, each weak to another's weapon. Playing in the right order turns hard fights into short ones.

| Boss | Weak to |
|---|---|
| **Mercury** | Salt Water |
| **Venus** | Bubble Bomb |
| **Mars** | Photon Missile |
| **Jupiter** | Electric Shock |
| **Saturn** | Black Hole |
| **Uranus** | Deep Digger |
| **Neptune** | Grab Buster |
| **Pluto** | Break Dash |

A workable order: **Neptune → Mars → Jupiter → Saturn → Pluto → Uranus → Mercury → Venus**, though several routes work.

**Every boss is beatable with the Mega Arm alone** if you learn the pattern — the weakness weapon is a shortcut, not a requirement.

---

## Tango

A robotic cat found in a stage, usable as a support item: he curls into a ball and bounces around the screen damaging enemies. Costs weapon energy, and he is genuinely useful in crowded rooms.

---

## After the eight

Beating the Stardroids opens the second half — a space station, a confrontation with Terra, and Dr Wily, who is behind it as always. The late stages are harder than anything before them and assume a full weapon set.

---

## Strategy

**Learn the boss order.** It is the single largest difficulty lever in any Mega Man game.

**Use the Mega Arm to grab pickups.** Health across a gap is reachable.

**Charge constantly.** Hold B when nothing needs shooting so the shot is ready when something does.

**Slide is absent.** Unlike the NES entries from Mega Man 3 onward, there is no slide here — do not expect it.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy core with **rewind enabled**, which suits a game built on precise jumps over instant-death spikes and pits.

The ROM is `Mega Man V (USA) (SGB Enhanced)`. The Super Game Boy enhancement adds a colour palette and border when run through SGB emulation. Worth noting the numbering confusion: this is the fifth **Game Boy** Mega Man, unrelated to *Mega Man 5* on NES.
