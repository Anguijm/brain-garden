# Pokémon Red (Game Boy)

**Developer:** Game Freak | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 1996 (JP) / 1998 (US)

---

## Introduction

Pokémon Red and Blue arrived in North America in 1998, two years after Japan, on hardware that was nearly a decade old — and became one of the largest phenomena in the medium's history. The design reason is simple: it is an RPG whose party is assembled by the player from 151 possibilities, and the collecting is the point rather than a side activity.

Red and Blue differ only in a handful of version-exclusive Pokémon and were built to be traded between. Playing one alone means a few species are unobtainable, which was entirely deliberate.

Eight gyms, the Elite Four, and roughly thirty hours.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move / navigate.
- **A:** Confirm, talk, examine.
- **B:** Cancel, and **hold to run from the menu**.
- **Start:** Menu — party, bag, save, Pokédex.

---

## Combat basics

Turn-based, one Pokémon out per side, four moves each.

**Type matchups decide fights.** Water beats Fire, Fire beats Grass, Grass beats Water, Electric beats Water and Flying, Ground beats Electric, and so on. A super-effective hit does double damage; a resisted one, half. Getting the matchup right matters more than levels within reason.

**Status effects are unusually strong in this generation.** Sleep and Freeze in particular can remove an opponent from the fight entirely, and paralysis both halves speed and causes missed turns.

---

## Generation 1's peculiarities

This game is famously idiosyncratic, and knowing the quirks changes how you play it.

**Psychic is broken.** In Gen 1 Psychic has essentially no counter — Bug moves were meant to check it and are pitifully weak, and Ghost was accidentally coded not to hit it. A high-level Psychic type walks through most of the game.

**Special is one stat.** Later generations split Special Attack and Special Defence. Here it is a single value, meaning a Pokémon with high Special is excellent at both attacking and resisting on that side.

**The physical/special split does not exist by move**, only by type. Every Fire move is special, every Normal move is physical, regardless of what the move looks like.

**Critical hits scale with Speed**, so fast Pokémon crit far more often.

## Route through the game

**Pallet Town** — choose a starter. Bulbasaur is the easiest opening (advantage over the first two gyms), Squirtle the safest overall, Charmander the hardest early and strong later.

**Gyms in order:** Pewter (Rock), Cerulean (Water), Vermilion (Electric), Celadon (Grass), Fuchsia (Poison), Saffron (Psychic), Cinnabar (Fire), Viridian (Ground).

**Team Rocket** interrupts throughout — Mt. Moon, the Game Corner, Silph Co., and finally at Viridian Gym.

**Victory Road**, then the **Elite Four**: Lorelei (Ice/Water), Bruno (Fighting/Rock), Agatha (Ghost/Poison), Lance (Dragon), then your rival.

**You cannot heal between Elite Four fights**, so arrive with a full bag of restoratives. This catches out nearly every first-time player.

---

## Strategy

**Build a balanced six**, but do not over-spread. Four well-levelled Pokémon beat six under-levelled ones, and the game's experience is finite per playthrough.

**Catch a Psychic type.** Abra (evolving to Kadabra and Alakazam) is available early at the Game Corner and trivialises much of the mid-game.

**Teach a water Pokémon Surf.** It is both a strong attack and the field move that opens the map.

**Stock up before the Elite Four.** Full Restores, Revives, and enough of them. There is no shop between the fights.

**Save before anything irreversible** — legendary encounters especially. Articuno, Zapdos, Moltres and Mewtwo are one-off, and fleeing or fainting them loses them for the run.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy core. **Rewind is enabled** on this system, which in Pokémon amounts to save-scumming — undoing a failed legendary catch or a critical hit. That is your call; the game was built around permanence.

The genuine limitation is **trading**. Several Pokémon evolve only by trade (Kadabra to Alakazam, Machoke to Machamp, Graveler to Golem, Haunter to Gengar), and version exclusives require a second cartridge. On a single emulated copy those are unobtainable without link emulation, so a solo Pokédex cannot be completed.

The ROM is `Pokemon - Red Version (USA, Europe) (SGB Enhanced)`. The SGB enhancement adds a colour palette when run through Super Game Boy emulation.
