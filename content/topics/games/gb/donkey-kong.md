# Donkey Kong (Game Boy)

**Developer:** Nintendo R&D1 | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 1994

---

## Introduction

This is not a port of the 1981 arcade game, and that misunderstanding costs people one of the best games on the Game Boy.

It **opens** as a faithful conversion — the four original arcade stages, girders and barrels and rivets. Finish them and instead of looping, Donkey Kong grabs Pauline and climbs away, and the game reveals itself: **97 more stages** of puzzle-platforming with a Mario who has an entirely new moveset.

It is often called the true precursor to *Mario vs. Donkey Kong*, and it is the most inventive thing R&D1 made on the handheld.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move, duck.
- **A:** Jump.
- **B:** Pick up / throw the held object.
- **Down then A:** Backflip.
- **A at the peak of a backflip:** Helicopter spin — extra height and distance.
- **Down + A while running:** Handstand — walk on your hands, which lets you carry certain objects on your feet.
- **Up:** Grab an overhead bar; **A** to swing and release.

### The moveset is the game

Mario here can backflip, handstand, triple-jump, swing from bars, and hang from ledges. Stages are built assuming all of it. A stage that seems impossible is almost always asking for a move you have not used — most often the **backflip-into-helicopter**, which reaches heights nothing else can.

---

## Structure

Each stage is a two-part puzzle:

1. **Find the key** and carry it to the locked door.
2. Do it before the timer runs out.

Carrying the key restricts you — you cannot backflip while holding it, and setting it down starts a countdown before it returns to its origin. That constraint is where the puzzle lives: get yourself somewhere, then work out how to get the key there too.

Every fourth stage is a **Donkey Kong fight**, escalating from the arcade original to genuinely elaborate confrontations.

---

## Strategy

**Learn the backflip-helicopter early.** Down, A, then A again at the apex. It is the highest jump in the game and dozens of stages need it.

**Plan the key route, not your route.** You can usually reach the door easily; the puzzle is the key's path.

**Use handstands to carry things on your feet** — some objects can only be moved that way.

**Watch the timer, but do not rush.** Most stages give enough time to think once, not to experiment repeatedly.

**Falls hurt.** Mario takes damage from long drops unless he lands on his feet from a controlled descent.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy core with **rewind enabled**, which suits a puzzle-platformer where a single mistimed jump loses a stage's progress.

The ROM is `Donkey Kong (Japan, USA) (SGB Enhanced)` — one cartridge image served both regions, so it is the correct North American file despite the label, and the Super Game Boy enhancement adds colour and a border under SGB emulation.

**Do not confuse it with the arcade original**, which is also emulated on this box under MAME. This is the 1994 Game Boy game, and it is four times the size of what it appears to be for the first ten minutes.
