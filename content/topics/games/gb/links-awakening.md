# The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening (Game Boy)

**Developer:** Nintendo EAD | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 1993

---

## Introduction

Link's Awakening began as an after-hours experiment — staff porting *A Link to the Past* to the Game Boy for fun — and became the strangest and best-loved game in the series. There is no Zelda, no Ganon and no Triforce. Link is shipwrecked on Koholint Island, and to leave he must wake the Wind Fish, which means the island and everyone on it may not survive the waking.

That premise gives it a melancholy nothing else in the series has, and the island is dense with jokes, cameos from other Nintendo games, and characters who seem to half-know what they are.

Eight dungeons, one island, roughly fifteen hours.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move.
- **A / B:** The two item slots. **Everything is an assignable item, including the sword and shield.**
- **Start:** Inventory. **Select:** Map.

### The two-button problem

This is the defining friction of the game. With only A and B, and both needed for the sword and shield in combat, every use of another item means opening the menu and reassigning.

Rooms that require the Feather to jump a gap while fighting, or the Bracelet to lift a pot mid-combat, mean constant menu visits. It is the one thing the 2019 Switch remake fixed. Get used to it — you will open that menu hundreds of times.

---

## Key items

| Item | Use |
|---|---|
| **Sword** | Attack. Also cuts bushes. |
| **Shield** | Blocks, and pushes some enemies back |
| **Roc's Feather** | Jump. Transforms what is reachable |
| **Power Bracelet** | Lift rocks and pots |
| **Pegasus Boots** | Charge and dash; breaks cracked walls |
| **Hookshot** | Cross gaps, stun enemies, pull yourself in |
| **Flippers** | Swim in deep water |
| **Ocarina** | Three songs, one of which warps |
| **Magic Rod / Bombs / Bow** | Combat and puzzles |

**The Feather and the Boots together** open most of the island's secrets. Dashing and jumping in combination reaches ledges that look decorative.

---

## The eight dungeons

Tail Cave, Bottle Grotto, Key Cavern, Angler's Tunnel, Catfish's Maw, Face Shrine, Eagle's Tower, Turtle Rock. Each holds an Instrument of the Sirens, and all eight are needed to wake the Wind Fish.

**Eagle's Tower is the one people remember**, and not fondly: you must destroy four support pillars by carrying an iron ball around the floor, collapsing the tower to reach the top. It is the most mechanically unusual dungeon in the series and the easiest to get stuck in.

**Bosses are item puzzles.** Each dungeon's boss is beaten with the item found inside it — which is the series' oldest convention, and reliable here.

---

## The trading sequence

A long chain of item swaps starting with a Yoshi Doll from the Trendy Game and ending with the Magnifying Lens, which is required to read the book that reveals the last secret. It is not optional, and it runs across the whole island.

If you find yourself unable to finish, an unfinished trade chain is a common cause.

---

## Strategy

**Talk to everyone, repeatedly.** The island's hint system is conversational, and dialogue changes as the story advances.

**Use the Pegasus Boots on every cracked wall.** Secrets are frequently behind them and the cracks are subtle on a small monochrome screen.

**Keep the Feather equipped when exploring.** More of the island opens with a jump than with anything else.

**Save often.** The game has a proper save system; use it before dungeons.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy core, and **rewind is enabled**, which softens the two-button problem's worst consequence — dying because you were in a menu.

The ROM is `Legend of Zelda, The - Link's Awakening (USA, Europe) (Rev 2)`. Rev 2 is the final revision of the original monochrome release and the one to have. Note this is **not** *Link's Awakening DX*, the 1998 Game Boy Color version, which adds colour, a colour dungeon and a photographer. DX is generally considered the better version — worth knowing it exists if you want it.
