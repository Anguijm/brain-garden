# Shinobi (Game Gear)

**Developer:** Sega | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1991

---

## Introduction

Known in Japan as *The GG Shinobi*, this is not a port of the arcade Shinobi. It is an original design built around one idea: **you play five different ninja, and you swap between them at will.**

You start as Joe Musashi and rescue four others, each with a distinct ability. Once rescued, any of them can be selected instantly during play, and the level design assumes you will keep swapping. It is closer to a Mega Man structure than to the arcade game, and it is one of the strongest action games on the handheld.

Five stages to rescue the ninja, then the final assault.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move. Up to climb, down to crouch.
- **Button 1:** Jump. Against a wall, jump again to wall-climb.
- **Button 2:** Attack — shuriken at range, sword up close.
- **Start:** Pause / **swap ninja**.

### Wall climbing

Jump at a wall and Joe clings, then jump again to ascend. Every ninja can do this, and most vertical sections assume it. It is the movement skill the game is built on.

---

## The five ninja

| Ninja | Colour | Ability |
|---|---|---|
| **Joe Musashi** | Red | Balanced. Standard shuriken and sword. The default. |
| **Sasuke** | Pink | **Double jump.** Reaches platforms nobody else can. |
| **Hayate** | Blue | **Ranged fire attack**, and can cross water safely. |
| **Fuma** | Green | **Grappling / climbing** — scales surfaces others cannot. |
| **Musashi (Yellow)** | Yellow | **Invulnerable to fire**, strong close attack. |

**Swapping is instant and free**, and health is tracked per ninja. That is the mechanic that gives the game its shape: a ninja who is nearly dead can be benched, and a fresh one brought in.

---

## Structure

The first five stages can be tackled in **any order**, and each ends with rescuing one ninja. Because abilities carry across, the stage you attempt second is easier than the stage you attempt first — the game gets progressively gentler as your roster fills, then hard again at the end.

**A sensible order:** start with the stage that suits Joe's plain moveset, then use each new ninja to make the next one easier. There is no wrong order, only slower ones.

The final stage requires all five and switches between them constantly — it is the exam for the whole mechanic.

---

## Strategy

**Swap constantly.** New players pick a favourite and struggle. The design expects you to change ninja several times per screen: Hayate to cross water, Sasuke to reach a ledge, Yellow to walk through fire.

**Health is per ninja, so spread the damage.** Taking a hit on a ninja you are about to bench costs nothing.

**Use the right tool rather than fighting through.** Almost every hard section has a ninja who makes it trivial. If something feels unfair, you are probably using the wrong one.

**Wall-climb everywhere.** Vertical routes often bypass the dangerous ground path entirely.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Gear core, and **rewind is enabled** on this system — useful here mainly for the platforming over instant-death hazards rather than for combat, which is forgiving.

A sequel, *Shinobi II: The Silent Fury*, followed and refines the same idea; it is on the same source if you want it.

The ROM is `Shinobi (World)`, a single cartridge image for all regions, so it is the correct North American file despite carrying no `(USA)` tag.
