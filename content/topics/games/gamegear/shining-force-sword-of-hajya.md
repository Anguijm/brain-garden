# Shining Force: The Sword of Hajya (Game Gear)

**Developer:** Sonic! Software Planning | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1993

---

## Introduction

The Sword of Hajya is a full tactical RPG on a handheld, and it does not compromise much to get there. Grid-based battles, a party you recruit and promote, permanent-feeling losses, and a story that sits between the two Genesis Shining Force games.

It is the second Game Gear entry — the first, *Shining Force Gaiden*, was Japan-only, and this one carries its story forward. You need no knowledge of it to follow along.

Where it differs from the Genesis games: battles are smaller, there is no town exploration to speak of, and the whole thing is structured as a chain of battles with story between them. That suits a handheld and it makes the game brisk.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move the cursor.
- **Button 1:** Select / confirm.
- **Button 2:** Cancel / back.
- **Start:** Battle menu — status, options, and the all-important speed settings.

---

## How a battle works

Turn order is by unit **Agility**, and both sides act in one interleaved order rather than side-by-side turns. So "my turn" is really "this unit's turn," and a fast enemy can act twice before a slow ally acts once.

Each unit on its turn can **move and then act**: attack, cast, use an item, or stay. Moving without acting is often correct.

**Victory is usually killing the enemy leader**, not clearing the map. Many battles can be shortened dramatically by pushing to the leader, though doing so costs you the experience from everything you skipped.

---

## Death, and why it is survivable

An ally reduced to zero HP is **out for the rest of the battle**, and must be revived afterwards at a church for money. They do not die permanently.

That is much gentler than it first appears, and it means aggressive play is affordable. What you cannot afford is losing the **main character** — if he falls, the battle ends immediately and you retry.

---

## Promotion

At level 10 a character can promote to an advanced class, resetting to level 1 with better growth and usually a new ability.

**Do not promote at exactly 10.** Levels gained before promotion carry statistical value forward, so most players wait until the high teens or around 20 before promoting. Promoting immediately produces a weaker end-game character.

---

## Party notes

You recruit far more characters than you can field, and fielding one means investing experience in it.

**Pick a core and stick with it.** Spreading experience across everyone produces a party of under-levelled units that all struggle. Eight to ten committed characters beats twenty part-timers.

**You need a healer.** Possibly two. Healing spells also gain experience for the caster, so a healer levels by doing their job.

**Mages are glass.** Enormous damage, minimal defence. Keep them behind the melee line and out of enemy movement range — which means counting squares, not eyeballing it.

---

## Strategy

**Count enemy movement range before you end a move.** Almost every avoidable loss in this game is a unit parked one square inside an enemy's reach.

**Fight in a line, not a scatter.** Advancing as a block means enemies engage your front rank while your ranged units answer. Scattered units get surrounded individually.

**Terrain matters.** Forest and hills give defensive bonuses; standing in open ground to save a turn is usually a bad trade.

**Grind lightly if stuck.** Battles can be re-entered, and a couple of levels solves most walls. The game does not punish this.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Gear core, and being menu-driven with static screens it reads well at 4K with integer scaling.

**Rewind is enabled** on this system, and as with Military Madness on TurboGrafx it is arguably too strong here — undoing a move after seeing the enemy's counter removes the tactical commitment the design depends on. Save states between battles are the more honest convenience.

The North American release is `Shining Force - The Sword of Hajya (USA)`. Its predecessor, *Shining Force Gaiden*, never left Japan; a fan translation exists but is outside this library's US-only rule.
