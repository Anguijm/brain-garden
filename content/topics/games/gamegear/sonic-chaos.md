# Sonic Chaos (Game Gear)

**Developer:** Aspect | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1993

---

## Introduction

Sonic Chaos is the third 8-bit Sonic and the most generous of them. Where the first Game Gear Sonic was cramped and punishing, this one is fast, forgiving and built by a studio that had learned what the small screen could take.

The important change: **you do not die from touching an enemy with zero rings** in most situations the way the first game punished you, and rings scatter and can be recovered, which the 1991 game did not allow. The result is a Sonic game that lets you actually go fast.

It also adds Tails as a playable character with genuine flight, and the Spin Dash, which the first two 8-bit games lacked.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move. Down to crouch.
- **Button 1 / 2:** Jump.
- **Down + Jump:** **Spin Dash** — charge in place, release to launch. This exists here and it changes everything about how the game moves.
- **As Tails, repeatedly tap Jump in mid-air:** fly.

---

## Sonic or Tails

**Sonic** has the Spin Dash and the Strike Dash (a horizontal rocket move found as a monitor), and plays as a speed character.

**Tails** flies. He is slower on the ground, but flight trivialises much of the platforming and reaches areas Sonic must work for. He also cannot get the good ending.

**Only Sonic can collect the Chaos Emeralds**, so a completionist run has to be Sonic. Tails is the relaxed option.

---

## Rings and Emeralds

Rings behave properly here — scattered on damage, recoverable, and functioning as the health buffer the series intends.

**Chaos Emeralds are in Special Stages**, entered by finishing an act with 100 rings. There are five. Collecting all five with Sonic gives the good ending; missing any gives a truncated one.

The Special Stages are straightforward collect-a-thons rather than the disorienting mazes of other entries, which is another mercy.

---

## Zone notes

Six zones, each with two acts plus a boss act.

**Turquoise Hill** — the opening, fast and open. Learn the Spin Dash here.

**Gigalopolis** — mechanical, with moving platforms and crushers.

**Sleeping Egg** — the gimmick zone: bouncy surfaces and springs everywhere.

**Mecha Green Hill** — a mechanised take on the familiar theme, with more vertical routes.

**Aqua Planet** — water, but far less oppressive than Labyrinth Zone in the first game. Air bubbles are reasonably placed.

**Electric Egg** — the finale: electrified hazards, teleporters, and Robotnik.

---

## Strategy

**Spin Dash out of everything.** Landing from a jump, coming out of water, starting a slope — the Spin Dash gets speed back immediately. It is the single biggest difference from the 1991 game.

**Take the high routes.** As in the Genesis games, upper paths are faster and safer and hold more rings. Being at speed usually puts you on them automatically.

**For Emeralds, farm rings before finishing an act.** Reaching 100 is the only way into a Special Stage, so if you are at 80 near the goal, go back for the ring boxes rather than finishing.

**Play as Sonic first.** Tails is fun but locks you out of the real ending.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Gear core, and **rewind is enabled** — though this is one of the games on the system that needs it least, being deliberately forgiving.

Same presentation note as the other Game Gear titles: the palette was chosen for a dim backlit LCD, so on a bright 4K panel it reads more saturated than intended. An LCD-simulating shader gets closer to the original look if you want it.

Sonic Chaos also appeared on the Master System with a slightly larger visible playfield.
