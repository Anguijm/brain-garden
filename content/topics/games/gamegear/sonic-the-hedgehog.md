# Sonic the Hedgehog (Game Gear)

**Developer:** Ancient / Sega | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1991

---

## Introduction

This is not a port of the Genesis game. It shares a name, a hedgehog and some zone titles, and almost nothing else: different level layouts, different physics, different bosses, and a different design philosophy. Ancient (Yuzo Koshiro's studio) built it from scratch for 8-bit hardware, and the result is a slower, more careful platformer than its big brother.

That is the thing to adjust to. The Genesis game is about carrying momentum. This one is about precise jumps on a screen too small to see them coming. Running at full speed into the unknown is how you die.

Six zones of three acts each. It is short, and its reputation for difficulty comes almost entirely from the screen size.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move. Down to crouch.
- **Button 1 / 2:** Jump.
- **Down + Jump:** No spin dash — this game predates it. Rolling is done by crouching while moving.

**There is no spin dash.** Sonic 2 on Game Gear adds it; this one does not. If you have muscle memory from later Sonic games, that is the habit to unlearn.

---

## Rings, and how they differ here

**You keep only one ring when hit, not a scattered cloud.** On the Genesis, taking damage with 50 rings sprays them everywhere and you can recover a few. Here, being hit costs you effectively everything.

That single change makes the game far more cautious. Rings are still your health, but they are not a buffer you can rebuild mid-fight — so avoiding damage matters much more than collecting.

**Rings are also the Chaos Emerald mechanism.** Emeralds are hidden in the levels themselves rather than in special stages, which is unusual for the series.

---

## Zone notes

**Green Hill** — the familiar opening, redesigned. Gentler than it looks.

**Bridge** — collapsing bridges over water. Movement here should be deliberate; the screen does not show you enough to react at speed.

**Jungle** — vertical climbing, and the first zone where the small screen genuinely fights you. Vines and moving platforms above your sight line.

**Labyrinth** — underwater, with the air countdown. The most disliked zone, for the same reason as the Genesis version, plus a screen that hides the air bubbles until you are close.

**Scrap Brain** — industrial hazards, conveyor belts, crushing machinery. Precision platforming.

**Sky Base** — the finale. Electrified floors, wind, and a final Robotnik fight.

---

## Bosses

Robotnik appears at the end of each zone's third act, and the fights are simpler than the Genesis equivalents — usually a handful of hits on a predictable pattern.

The exception is the **final fight in Sky Base**, which has no rings in the arena. You get one hit's worth of margin, which is none. Learn the pattern before committing.

---

## Strategy

**Slow down.** The Game Gear screen shows less than the Genesis, and this game's levels contain hazards placed where a fast-moving player cannot react. Walking through unfamiliar sections and running through familiar ones is the correct approach.

**Hold a ring at all costs.** With one ring you survive a hit; with zero you die. Grabbing a single ring before a boss is worth a detour.

**Look before you drop.** Falls into spikes and pits are the commonest deaths, and they are almost always into something that was off-screen.

**Chaos Emeralds are in the levels.** One per zone, hidden off the main path. Collecting all six changes the ending.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Gear core, and **rewind is enabled** on this system — which is more useful here than almost anywhere else on the box, because the deaths that define this game are blind falls into off-screen hazards. Unwinding two seconds converts a memorisation exercise into an ordinary platformer.

One presentation note worth knowing: the Game Gear's screen was 160x144 and backlit, with colours chosen to look right on that specific washed-out LCD. On a bright modern 4K panel the palette can look garish compared to how it appeared in 1991. Batocera has LCD-simulating shaders if you want the original look; without one, expect it to be more saturated than intended.

This game was also released on the Master System, with a slightly larger visible area that makes the platforming meaningfully easier.
