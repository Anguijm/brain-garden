# Crystal Warriors (Game Gear)

**Developer:** Sega | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1991

---

## Introduction

Crystal Warriors is a tactical RPG built around elements. Every unit belongs to one of four — **Fire, Water, Wind, Earth** — and the elemental relationships decide most fights before positioning does.

It is simpler than Shining Force and quite different in feel: fewer units, a monster-capturing mechanic, and battles that turn on matchups rather than on careful line-holding. It arrived early in the Game Gear's life and it is one of the more interesting things on the system.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move the cursor.
- **Button 1:** Select / confirm.
- **Button 2:** Cancel.
- **Start:** Menu — status, magic, items.

---

## The element wheel

This is the game.

**Fire beats Wind. Wind beats Earth. Earth beats Water. Water beats Fire.**

Attacking with an advantage does substantially more damage and takes less in return. Attacking into a disadvantage is close to pointless. Nearly every battle is decided by which of your units meets which of theirs, and a wrongly-matched attack wastes a turn you cannot spare.

Before moving anything, look at what it will end up adjacent to.

---

## Monsters, and capturing them

Defeated enemy monsters can be **captured** and added to your force, which is the mechanic that gives the game its identity. A captured monster fights for you and fills gaps in your elemental coverage.

**Capture deliberately.** If your party is short of a Water unit, find a Water monster and take it rather than killing it. A balanced force that covers all four elements can answer anything; a force of three Fire units will hit a wall the first time it meets a Water-heavy map.

---

## Magic

Each caster has spells tied to their element, covering damage, healing and buffs. MP is limited and does not refill mid-battle, so spells are a resource for the whole map rather than per fight.

**Healing is the most valuable magic.** As in most tactical games, the ability to keep a front-line unit alive is worth more than extra damage.

---

## Strategy

**Match elements before you commit.** The single most common mistake is moving a unit into range of something it is weak against. Count the squares.

**Keep a balanced force.** Four elements, covered. When the game hands you a map full of one element, you want the counter available rather than a party that all struggles equally.

**Capture rather than kill when you are short.** Experience lost on a capture is repaid many times by having the right tool later.

**Protect the leader.** As in the genre generally, losing the main character ends the battle.

**Terrain still matters**, though less than elements. Given a choice between good ground and a good matchup, take the matchup.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Gear core, and as a grid-and-menu game it benefits from **integer scaling** at 4K for legibility.

**Rewind is enabled** on this system. The same caveat as the other strategy games applies: undoing a move after seeing its outcome removes the commitment the design is built on. Save states between battles are the more honest convenience.

A sequel, *Royal Stone*, followed in Japan only and was never translated officially, so it falls outside this library's US-only rule.
