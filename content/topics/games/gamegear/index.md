# Game Gear Game Guides

Standalone walkthroughs for the Game Gear titles on the UM790Pro. Sega's handheld lost to the
Game Boy on battery life and price, not on hardware — it was a colour, backlit, Master
System-class machine competing with a monochrome one, and its library reflects that ambition.

## The colour problem, which affects every game here

The Game Gear's screen was 160x144, backlit, and dim, and artists chose palettes that looked
right *on that screen*. Displayed on a bright modern 4K panel with no correction, Game Gear
games look considerably more saturated and flatter than they were meant to. Ristar's Sonata
planet and the Sonic games suffer most.

Batocera ships LCD-simulating shaders that approximate the original response. Whether to use
one is taste — but if these look garish, that is why, and it is fixable.

**Integer scaling** helps the other way: 160x144 divides cleanly into 4K, so the pixel art
stays crisp rather than smeared. It is on for this box.

## Rewind, and where it does harm

Rewind is enabled for this system. It earns its place on **Sonic the Hedgehog**, whose deaths
are overwhelmingly blind falls into hazards the small screen never showed you.

It is worth *not* using on **Shining Force: The Sword of Hajya** and **Crystal Warriors**.
Undoing a move after seeing the enemy's answer removes the tactical commitment those games are
built on, in the same way it does for Military Madness on TurboGrafx-16.

## Two still missing

The top-10 list names ten; eight are on the box. **Columns** and **Dragon Crystal** were not in
the source collection. Both had US Game Gear releases, so they are a sourcing gap rather than a
regional exclusion.
