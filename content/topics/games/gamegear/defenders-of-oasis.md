# Defenders of Oasis (Game Gear)

**Developer:** Sega / Sanritsu | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1992

---

## Introduction

Defenders of Oasis is a traditional turn-based RPG with an Arabian Nights setting — a prince, a genie, a usurped throne. It is short by the standards of the genre, perhaps ten hours, and that brevity is a feature: it tells a complete story without the padding that made most 8-bit RPGs a slog.

It is also unusually generous. Encounter rates are moderate, the difficulty curve is gentle, and the game rarely demands grinding. For a handheld RPG of 1992 that is remarkable, and it is why this one is remembered when most Game Gear RPGs are not.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move / navigate menus.
- **Button 1:** Confirm / talk / examine.
- **Button 2:** Cancel / open menu.
- **Start:** Menu — status, items, equipment, magic.

---

## Combat

Standard turn-based, first-person view of the enemy party, commands chosen each round.

- **Attack** — physical, uses equipped weapon.
- **Magic** — costs MP, includes damage, healing and status effects.
- **Item** — consumables; also the escape route when a fight turns.
- **Defend** — halves incoming damage for the round. Genuinely useful against boss wind-ups.
- **Run** — usually succeeds against ordinary encounters.

**The genie is your main asset.** Summoned magic through him is stronger than anything else you have for much of the game, and MP management is largely about keeping enough in reserve for him.

---

## Party and progression

You gather a small party as the story moves. There is no recruitment pool or class system — the party is the party, which keeps the game focused.

**Equipment matters more than levels.** New weapons and armour appear in each town, and buying the current tier makes a larger difference than two or three levels. Money is better spent on gear than hoarded.

**Healing items are cheap.** Carry more than you think you need; the game expects you to use them rather than retreat to an inn.

---

## Structure

The world opens gradually: town, surrounding area, dungeon, next region. Progress is gated by story events rather than by keys or items, so if you cannot proceed the answer is usually an NPC you have not spoken to.

**Dungeons are compact.** They are mazes but not cruel ones, and most have a visible logic. Mapping is not required.

**Bosses telegraph.** Each has a pattern of strong attacks separated by weaker rounds; defending on the wind-up turn and healing early rather than late handles nearly all of them.

---

## Strategy

**Talk to everyone in every town, twice.** Dialogue changes after story events, and the game hides both direction and optional items in conversation.

**Buy the best gear you can afford on arriving in a new town**, before exploring the area around it. The difficulty of the next region assumes you did.

**Keep MP for the genie.** Spending it on routine encounters leaves you flat for the fight that matters.

**Do not fear running.** Ordinary encounters give modest experience and the game does not require grinding. Escaping to preserve resources is a legitimate strategy here in a way it is not in most RPGs of the era.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Gear core. As a text-heavy menu-driven game it is one of the better showcases for integer scaling at 4K — the small font stays legible instead of blurring.

**Rewind is enabled** on this system. In an RPG its main use is undoing a fatal encounter rather than reloading an old save, which is a reasonable convenience given the game has battery saves and does not otherwise punish you.

The North American release is `Defenders of Oasis (USA, Europe)`. In Japan it is *Shadam Crusader: Harukanaru Oukoku*.
