# Ristar (Game Gear)

**Developer:** Sega | **Publisher:** Sega | **Year:** 1995

---

## Introduction

Ristar arrived at the very end of the 8-bit era and is one of the best-looking things on the hardware. You play a small star-shaped creature whose entire moveset is his arms: they stretch out in eight directions, grab things, and pull.

That grab is the game. You do not jump on enemies — you seize them and headbutt them. You do not climb ledges — you grab the lip and haul yourself up. You do not swing on a hook so much as grab it and orbit. Every problem is "what do I grab, and in which direction."

The Game Gear version is a different game from the Genesis one, not a downscale: shorter, restructured, and built for the smaller screen.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move.
- **Button 2:** Jump.
- **Button 1:** Stretch arms in the direction held. With no direction, straight ahead.
- **Button 1 + direction, in the air:** Grab in that diagonal.

### The grab, in detail

Hold a direction and press Button 1 and Ristar's arms extend that way. If they connect with an **enemy**, he pulls himself into it head-first, which is the attack. If they connect with **terrain or a handle**, he pulls himself to it, which is the movement.

The same input does both, and which one happens depends entirely on what you aimed at. Learning to aim the grab diagonally is what separates a struggling player from a fluid one.

---

## Level structure

Each planet has two acts and a boss. The themes are elemental and each introduces a mechanic:

- **Flora** — the tutorial planet. Grab enemies, grab ledges, learn the rhythm.
- **Undertow** — water. Swimming changes the grab's reach and Ristar sinks slowly.
- **Scorch** — fire and heat hazards.
- **Sonata** — the music planet, and the best-looking one. Grabbing objects plays notes.
- **Freon** — ice, sliding, and the least forgiving platforming.

**Bosses are grab puzzles.** Every one is beaten by working out what to grab and when, rather than by attacking repeatedly. If a boss seems invulnerable, you have not found the grabbable part.

---

## Strategy

**Grab everything.** Blocks, enemies, decorative handles, ledges. The game hides bonus rooms and items behind grabs that are not signposted, and there is no penalty for trying.

**Use grabs to cross gaps.** Ristar's jump is unremarkable; his reach is not. Aiming a diagonal grab at a ledge across a gap covers ground his legs cannot.

**Climb by grabbing, not jumping.** At a wall, grab the top edge and he pulls up. This is faster and safer than a jump, and in several places it is the only way.

**Bosses: look for the odd part.** Something on each boss is grabbable and it is usually visually distinct.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Gear core. **Rewind is enabled** on this system.

Ristar is one of the strongest arguments on this box for a **Game Gear LCD shader**. The art was drawn for a small backlit screen with a particular colour response, and Sonata's palette especially was tuned for that. At 4K without a shader it looks bright and slightly flat; with one it looks like the artists intended.

The Genesis version is a longer and more celebrated game. This is not a lesser port of it — it is a separate design, and worth playing on its own terms rather than as a consolation.
