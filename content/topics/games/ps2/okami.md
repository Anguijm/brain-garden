# Ōkami (PlayStation 2)

**Developer:** Clover Studio | **Publisher:** Capcom | **Year:** 2006

---

## Introduction

You play a wolf who is the sun goddess Amaterasu, restoring colour to a world drained of it, in a game drawn to look like **sumi-e ink-wash painting on textured paper**.

It is structurally a Zelda game — dungeons, tools, a hub world, a companion who talks too much — and it is one of the most visually distinctive games ever made. Clover Studio was shut down shortly after it released, having sold poorly.

The file on this box is the **North American release, titled Okami**.

---

## Controls

- **Left stick:** Move. **Right stick:** Camera.
- **X:** Jump. **Square:** Attack. **Triangle:** Special / dig.
- **R1:** **Enter the Celestial Brush** — freezes the world and turns the screen into paper.
- **Circle:** Confirm / interact.

---

## The Celestial Brush

Hold R1 and the game pauses, the screen becomes a canvas, and **you draw with the stick**. The shape you draw is the ability you use.

Thirteen brush techniques are learned over the game:

| Draw | Effect |
|---|---|
| **A circle in the sky** | Sunrise — turns night to day |
| **A line through an enemy** | Power Slash — cuts |
| **A crescent** | Turns day to night |
| **Loops over water** | Waterspout |
| **A line from a flame to a target** | Inferno |
| **Zigzag from lightning** | Thunderstorm |
| **A circle on a withered tree** | Bloom — restores life |
| **A spiral** | Whirlwind |

**Brush techniques are weapons, keys, and puzzle tools at once.** Power Slash cuts enemies, cuts ropes, cuts boulders, and hits switches. When stuck, the question is always which brush stroke the environment is asking for.

**On original hardware this was drawn with the analogue stick**, which is imprecise — the Wii version used the pointer and was better for it. On this box it is the stick, so expect the occasional stroke to be misread as a different technique.

## Structure

A large open world in three regions, dungeons, and a great deal of optional content — feeding animals, restoring cursed land, digging up treasure, fishing.

**Restoring cursed zones and feeding wildlife grants Praise**, which is the levelling currency for health, ink and purse. **This is the main way to get stronger**, and players who ignore it find the later game hard.

---

## Strategy

**Feed every animal you pass.** Praise is levelling.

**Bloom every withered tree.** Same reason, larger reward.

**Try Power Slash on everything.** It is the most broadly useful brush technique by a distance.

**Refill ink** by not spamming brush strokes in combat; ink pots regenerate slowly.

**The opening is long.** Roughly an hour of setup before it opens up, which is the most common reason people stop.

## Emulation Notes

Runs well on Batocera's PS2 core, and **this is one of the best arguments for PS2 emulation on the box**: the paper-texture cel-shaded art was designed at a resolution the console could not deliver, and at 3x or 4x it looks like the concept art.

An HD remaster exists on modern platforms and is the technically better version. The PS2 original is what runs here and it holds up.
