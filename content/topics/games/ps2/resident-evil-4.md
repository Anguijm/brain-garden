# Resident Evil 4 (PlayStation 2)

**Developer:** Capcom Production Studio 4 | **Publisher:** Capcom | **Year:** 2005

---

## Introduction

The game that **reinvented the third-person shooter**. The over-the-shoulder camera, the contextual aim, the pacing of resource scarcity against action — Gears of War, Dead Space, The Last of Us and dozens of others descend directly from what Shinji Mikami built here.

Leon Kennedy, from *Resident Evil 2*, is sent to rural Spain to recover the President's daughter. The zombies are gone; the enemies here are people controlled by a parasite, and they run, coordinate, and use weapons.

It went through four discarded versions before this one, one of which became *Devil May Cry*.

---

## Controls

- **Left stick:** Move. **R1:** **Aim** — this roots you in place and gives a laser sight.
- **Square:** Fire while aiming. **X:** Action / context.
- **Triangle:** Melee, when an enemy is staggered.
- **L1:** Ready knife.

**You still cannot move and shoot.** That is deliberate and it is the last thread connecting this to the older games — every shot is a commitment, and the tension comes from choosing when to stop moving.

---

## The stagger-and-melee loop

Shooting an enemy in the **leg** makes them stumble; in the **head** makes them clutch their face. Either state opens a **melee prompt** — a roundhouse kick or a suplex that knocks down everything nearby and **costs no ammunition**.

**This is the core economy of the game.** Ammunition is scarce; melee is free. A player who shoots centre-mass runs dry. A player who shoots legs and kicks never does.

---

## The attaché case

Your inventory is a **briefcase grid**, and every item has a physical shape. Guns take large rectangles, herbs small squares. **Managing the case is a genuine puzzle** and larger cases are bought as upgrades.

---

## The merchant

A hooded trader appears throughout, buying treasure and selling weapons and **upgrades** — firepower, capacity, reload speed. Money comes from treasure hidden in the environment and from selling gems combined into more valuable pieces.

**Upgrade one weapon deeply rather than several shallowly.** Spreading money is the most common mistake.

---

## Ashley

For much of the game you escort the President's daughter. She can be told to **wait or follow**, hidden in dumpsters, and she can be carried off by enemies, which ends the game.

She is much better designed than her reputation suggests, but she does change how you fight — you cannot simply run.

---

## Strategy

**Shoot legs, then melee.** This is the single most important habit.

**Sell everything you are not using.** Money is upgrades and upgrades are survival.

**Combine gems into treasure** before selling; a completed piece is worth far more than parts.

**Use the knife on crates and downed enemies.** It is free.

**Tell Ashley to wait** before a fight, then collect her.

## Emulation Notes

Runs well on Batocera's PS2 core.

**The PS2 version is the weakest of the three original ports** — it was a conversion from the GameCube original, with reduced lighting, lower-resolution textures and a shorter draw distance, because the PS2 had less graphics memory. It does include **Separate Ways**, an extra campaign the GameCube version lacked.

Emulation at higher internal resolution recovers a lot of the sharpness, though not the lighting.
