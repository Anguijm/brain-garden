# Dragon Quest VIII: Journey of the Cursed King (PlayStation 2)

**Developer:** Level-5 | **Publisher:** Square Enix | **Year:** 2005 (US)

---

## Introduction

**The game that finally made Dragon Quest work in the West.** The series is the biggest RPG franchise in Japan by a wide margin and had never translated that abroad; this entry, with Akira Toriyama's designs rendered in full cel-shaded 3D and a genuinely excellent English localisation, changed that.

It is also the first Dragon Quest in full 3D with a free camera and a continuous overworld you can walk across.

You are a mute guard escorting a king and his daughter, who have been transformed into a troll and a horse.

---

## Controls

- **Left stick:** Move. **Right stick:** Camera.
- **X:** Confirm / talk. **Circle:** Cancel. **Triangle:** Menu.
- **L1 / R1:** Rotate camera. **Select:** Map.

---

## Traditional to the bone

Dragon Quest does not innovate and does not apologise for it:

**Random encounters** — though monsters are visible on the field here, a first for the series.

**Turn-based combat**, front-facing, with no positioning.

**Four party members**, each with fixed roles.

**Grinding is expected.** The game is tuned assuming you fight what you meet and occasionally more. This is a real difference from contemporary Final Fantasy and it catches people out — **if a boss feels impossible, you are probably underlevelled, and that is the intended signal.**

---

## Tension

Spend a turn "psyching up" and your next attack does far more. Stack it multiple turns for enormous damage.

**Tension is the answer to most boss fights.** A character psyching up while others heal is the standard shape of a hard battle.

---

## The skill point system

Each character allocates points across weapon types and a personal skill tree. **Choices are effectively permanent**, and the trees are deep enough that a character built badly stays bad.

Broad rule: put points in **one weapon type per character** plus their personal tree, rather than spreading.

---

## Alchemy Pot

Combine two items to make a third, in a pot on your wagon. **The best equipment in the game is alchemised, not bought**, and the recipes are largely undocumented in game.

---

## Strategy

**Psych up for bosses.**

**Commit skill points to one weapon per character.**

**Use the Alchemy Pot constantly** — check it whenever you get new materials.

**Fight what you meet.** Avoiding encounters leaves you underlevelled for the next boss.

**Talk to everyone after every plot event.** Dragon Quest hides a great deal in incidental dialogue.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs well on Batocera's PS2 core. The cel-shaded art scales cleanly, so **higher internal resolution is very worthwhile**.

A 3DS version added party members, voice acting and removed random encounters entirely. **This PS2 original has the better visuals**, and the 3DS version's additions come with a lower resolution and frame rate. This is the version most people consider definitive.

Fast-forward, now on R3, helps with the overworld traversal.
