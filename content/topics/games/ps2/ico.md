# Ico (PlayStation 2)

**Developer:** Team Ico | **Publisher:** Sony | **Year:** 2001

---

## Introduction

A boy with horns is sealed in a castle as a sacrifice, escapes, and finds a girl he cannot talk to. The whole game is leading her out.

Fumito Ueda designed it by **"subtractive design"** — starting from a normal game and removing everything that broke the atmosphere. No heads-up display, no health bar, no music for most of its length, no inventory, almost no dialogue, and what dialogue exists is in an invented language.

It sold poorly and influenced an enormous number of designers. *Shadow of the Colossus* and *The Last Guardian* are its successors.

---

## Controls

- **Left stick:** Move. **Right stick:** Camera.
- **X:** Jump. **Square:** Swing your weapon.
- **R1:** **Hold Yorda's hand** — and this is the button the game is about.
- **Triangle:** Climb / context. **Circle:** Call to Yorda.

---

## Holding hands

Yorda cannot climb well, cannot fight, cannot cross gaps, and does not understand where you are going.

**R1 takes her hand and leads her.** She resists slightly, she stumbles, she is slower than you. The controller rumbles with the connection.

**This is not a mechanic bolted onto a puzzle game; it is the point.** Every puzzle is about getting a person who cannot do what you can do to somewhere she needs to be — pulling her up, calling her across a plank, telling her to wait while you work a mechanism, then coming back for her.

**She is not an escort-mission burden.** The game is careful never to make her stupid, only limited.

---

## The shadows

Black shapes emerge from the ground and try to drag Yorda into portals. **You have a stick, and later a sword.**

Combat is deliberately clumsy — wide, slow swings that miss. **If Yorda is pulled into a portal, the game ends.** So the fights are not about killing shadows efficiently; they are about panic and protecting someone.

---

## Puzzles

Environmental and physical: move a crate, pull a chain, swing on a rope, redirect a bomb, work out that the windmill is a lift. **There are no puzzle items and no puzzle logic beyond what you can see**, which is why it holds up.

**When stuck, look for what Yorda can do that you cannot** — chiefly opening the doors covered in glowing sigils, which only she can open.

---

## Strategy

**Call Yorda constantly.** She waits where you leave her and she will not follow unprompted.

**Look at the whole room.** The camera pulls back deliberately to show you the solution.

**Fight near Yorda, not toward the shadows.** Chasing them away from her is how she gets taken.

**Save at the stone benches.** They are the only save points and they are also the only rest.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs well on Batocera's PS2 core — it is much lighter than [Shadow of the Colossus](topics/games/ps2/shadow-of-the-colossus).

**Raise the internal resolution.** Ico's visual signature is heavy bloom and blown-out light through windows, and at higher resolution the effect is cleaner rather than diminished.

The European release added content, including a two-player mode and extra scenes, that the North American version lacks. **This is the North American release.**
