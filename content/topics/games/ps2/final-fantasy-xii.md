# Final Fantasy XII (PlayStation 2)

**Developer:** Square Enix | **Publisher:** Square Enix | **Year:** 2006

---

## Introduction

The most divisive mainline Final Fantasy, and the one whose reputation has improved most over time. Yasumi Matsuno — of [Final Fantasy Tactics](topics/games/psp/final-fantasy-tactics-war-of-the-lions) and *Tactics Ogre* — directed it, in the same world of Ivalice, until illness forced him off the project partway through.

It is a political drama about a small kingdom caught between two empires, and it is notably light on the personal melodrama the series usually runs on.

---

## Controls

- **Left stick:** Move. **Right stick:** Camera.
- **X:** Confirm / attack. **Circle:** Cancel. **Triangle:** Target.
- **R2:** Switch leader. **L1:** Map.

---

## The Gambit system

The reason people argued about this game.

**There are no random encounters** — enemies are visible in the field and combat happens where you stand. Your party fights in real time on a timer, and **you program your allies' behaviour** with a list of if-then rules called Gambits:

```
1. Ally: HP < 70%        → Cure
2. Ally: status = Poison → Antidote
3. Foe: flying           → Attack with bow
4. Foe: nearest visible  → Attack
```

Rules run top to bottom, and the first one that matches fires.

**The criticism:** "the game plays itself." Set good gambits and you can walk through ordinary fights without touching a button.

**The defence:** writing the gambits *is* the play. It is a game about building a system that handles the routine so you can intervene on the exceptional, and a boss will punish a lazy gambit list immediately.

**Either way, know it going in.** People who expect to press attack repeatedly find it hollow.

## The License Board

Everything — every spell, every piece of equipment, every stat boost — must be unlocked on a shared grid using License Points before a character can use it, **even if you own the item**.

**In this original release the board is shared**, so all six characters can become identical. The later *Zodiac Age* version split it into twelve job boards and is generally considered the better design.

---

## Hunts

A bounty board system: accept a mark, find the monster, kill it. **The hunts are the best content in the game** and several are far harder than the main story. They are also how you get the strongest equipment.

---

## Strategy

**Write gambits early and revise them constantly.** They are your character build.

**Put status cures above attack rules.** Ordering is everything.

**Do the hunts.** The main story alone leaves you underequipped.

**Do not spend License Points randomly.** The shared board tempts you into making everyone the same.

**Turn off gambits for bosses** and control the party manually.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs well on Batocera's PS2 core, and is one of the games where **the fast-forward you now have on R3 is genuinely useful** — the field areas are large and the walking is long.

*The Zodiac Age* is the modern remaster with the twelve-job board, a faster game speed and a reworked score. It is a better version. This PS2 original is the one Matsuno's team shipped.
