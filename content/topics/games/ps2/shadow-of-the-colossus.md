# Shadow of the Colossus (PlayStation 2)

**Developer:** Team Ico | **Publisher:** Sony | **Year:** 2005

---

## Introduction

A game consisting **entirely of boss fights**, with nothing in between but riding a horse across an empty land. There are sixteen colossi and no other enemies at all. No towns, no items to collect, no dialogue beyond a handful of lines.

Fumito Ueda's second game after [Ico](topics/games/ps2/ico), and the one most often cited in arguments that games can be art. It was central to a legal case in the United States about whether video games deserve First Amendment protection.

A young man brings a dead girl to a forbidden temple and is told he can restore her by killing sixteen giants.

---

## Controls

- **Left stick:** Move. **Right stick:** Camera.
- **X:** Jump. **Square:** Attack / stab.
- **R1:** **Grip** — hold to cling to fur, ledges, anything.
- **Triangle:** Mount horse / special. **Circle:** Whistle for Agro.
- **L1:** Lock on and centre camera on the colossus.

---

## Stamina, which is the whole tension

Holding R1 to grip drains a **stamina ring**. When it empties, you let go.

Every colossus fight is therefore: climb during a moment of calm, hold on while it thrashes, find a flat spot to release and recover, then continue. **The colossus shaking you off is not random — it is you running out of grip.**

**Stamina and health both grow permanently** by finding hidden fruit and white-tailed lizards scattered across the world, which is the only collecting the game has.

---

## How a colossus works

Each is a puzzle in the shape of an animal. The sequence is always:

1. **Find it.** Raise your sword in sunlight and the reflected light converges toward the next colossus. This is the only navigation aid.
2. **Get onto it.** This is usually the actual puzzle — using terrain, provoking a charge, shooting a weak point with an arrow, or finding the one reachable ledge.
3. **Climb to the glowing sigils.** Stab them.

**No colossus is a fight in the ordinary sense.** You cannot damage them by attacking normally, and most of them will not even fight back until you are on them.

---

## Agro

Your horse is not a vehicle. **Agro has her own mind** — she will not turn on a sixpence, she avoids obstacles on her own, and she must be trusted rather than steered precisely. Several colossi are fought from horseback.

People who fight the horse controls hate them. The design intent is a horse that behaves like an animal, and it does.

---

## Strategy

**Use the sword-light navigation.** There is no map worth reading.

**Watch stamina, not health.** Falling off is the main failure.

**Look for the fur.** Anything grippable is visually distinct, and colossi are designed to be readable.

**Collect fruit and lizards while riding.** The later colossi assume a larger stamina ring.

**Do not rush the approach.** Half of each fight is working out how to get on.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs on Batocera's PS2 core. **This is a demanding game to emulate** — it pushed the PlayStation 2 past its limits and famously dropped to around 20 frames per second on real hardware in the worst fights.

Emulation can hold a **steadier frame rate than the original console ever managed**, which is the strongest argument for playing it this way. Higher internal resolution helps a great deal with the fur and the distant vistas.

A full remake exists on PS4. The PS2 original is a different-looking game with, some argue, better atmosphere.
