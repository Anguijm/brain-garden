# Wario Land 3 (Game Boy Color)

**Developer:** Nintendo R&D1 | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 2000

---

## Introduction

A platformer in which **you cannot die and cannot lose**, built by taking that away and finding out what is left. What is left turns out to be one of the best games on the system.

Wario has no health bar and no lives. Enemies do not kill him — they **transform** him, and each transformation is a temporary state with its own movement rules. The entire game is a puzzle box where the enemies are the tools.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move; down to crouch/crawl.
- **A:** Jump.
- **B:** Shoulder charge — the attack, and also how you break blocks and cross gaps.
- **Down + A:** Ground pound.

---

## Transformations, which are the mechanics

Getting hit changes Wario. Each state is a movement ability you cannot otherwise access:

| Hit by | Wario becomes | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Fire enemy | **On fire** | Runs uncontrollably, burns through obstacles |
| Zombie | **Zombie** | Sinks through thin floors, cannot jump |
| Spear/flat | **Flat** | Slips under narrow gaps, floats down |
| Bee sting | **Puffy** | Floats upward like a balloon |
| Snowball | **Snowman** | Rolls, crushes, slides |
| Bouncy enemy | **Bouncy ball** | Bounces to unreachable heights |
| Drunk | **Drunk** | Staggers, uncontrolled, breaks certain blocks |
| Vampire bat | **Vampire** | Flies toward enemies |

**The core loop is deliberately getting hit by the right enemy in the right place.** Players who instinctively avoid damage — which is every platformer instinct there is — get stuck immediately. Damage is the key ring.

---

## Structure

Twenty-five levels, each holding **four treasures**. You cannot get all four on a first visit; most need abilities you have not found yet.

**Every treasure you collect changes the world.** It might open a new area, drain water somewhere, switch a level between day and night, or grant Wario a permanent new move. So the game is a loop: enter a level, take what you can reach, come back later when the world has changed.

Levels also have **day and night versions** with different layouts, enemies and reachable treasures.

---

## Strategy

**When stuck, ask which transformation you need, not which jump you missed.**

**Re-read levels after every treasure.** The map is not static, and the game does a poor job of telling you what just changed.

**Golf.** The boss fights are a golf minigame. This is not a joke and it catches everyone off guard.

**Keep a note of levels with treasures you could see but not reach.** The game tracks this, but its own tracking is easy to misread.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy Color core. **Rewind is enabled** but is much less relevant here than in most platformers, because there is no death to rewind out of.
