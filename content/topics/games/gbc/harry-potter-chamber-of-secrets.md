# Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets (Game Boy Color)

**Developer:** Griptonite Games | **Publisher:** Electronic Arts | **Year:** 2002

---

## Introduction

Licensed handheld games from this era are usually disposable. This one is not, and the reason is that Griptonite built a **proper turn-based role-playing game** rather than a platformer with a film licence stapled on.

It is one of the last commercial Game Boy Color releases, shipping in 2002 when the Game Boy Advance was already the current system. It has a small but genuine reputation among people who played it as children and were surprised, years later, to find it holds up.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move around Hogwarts.
- **A:** Interact, cast the selected spell, confirm.
- **B:** Cancel.
- **Start:** Menu — spells, inventory, cards.

---

## Spells as an RPG system

Spells are learned in class, and classes are **minigames**: trace a wand pattern, match a rhythm, follow an input sequence. Do well and you learn the spell properly.

In battle, spells are the move list. Each has a magic cost and a role:

- **Flipendo** — knockback, and the general-purpose puzzle tool for pushing blocks and hitting switches
- **Rictusempra** — the reliable damage spell
- **Alohomora** — locked doors
- **Lumos** — dark rooms
- **Diffindo** — cuts vines and obstacles

**Flipendo is the one you use for everything outside combat.** Most environmental puzzles reduce to pointing it at the correct object.

---

## Structure

Hogwarts is an explorable overworld with classes, side quests and secrets, following the plot of the second book. Combat is **turn-based with random encounters** — Cornish pixies, spiders, and the rest.

There is a **collectible card system** (famous wizards) that rewards thorough exploration, and a Quidditch element.

---

## Strategy

**Do the class minigames properly.** Sloppy spellcasting means weak spells later, and the game does not tell you this clearly.

**Talk to every portrait and student.** Side quests and card locations come from conversation only.

**Save before boss encounters.** Healing items are limited and there is no grinding out of a bad fight.

**Collect the cards as you go.** Backtracking Hogwarts at the end is tedious.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy Color core with **rewind enabled**, which helps in the timing-based spell classes.

Note that this is a completely different game from the Game Boy Advance version of the same name, which is an isometric action game. The Color version is the RPG.
