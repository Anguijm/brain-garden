# Resident Evil Gaiden (Game Boy Color)

**Developer:** M4 Limited | **Publisher:** Capcom | **Year:** 2002

---

## Introduction

A curiosity, and worth playing as one. Capcom outsourced a Resident Evil to a British studio for the Game Boy Color, and what came back was a top-down adventure with a combat system unlike anything else in the series.

**It is not canon.** Capcom has been explicit about that, which is convenient, because the plot involves Leon Kennedy being infected on a cruise ship in ways the main series would rather not account for.

You play as **Barry Burton** and, later, Leon, aboard the *Starlight*.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move (top-down, free movement — no tank controls).
- **A:** Interact / confirm. **B:** Cancel.
- **Start:** Inventory.

---

## The combat bar, which is the whole point

Encounter an enemy and the view switches to a **first-person perspective with a cursor sliding back and forth along a bar**. Press the button when the cursor is over the marked zone to land a hit; the closer to centre, the more damage.

Miss, and the enemy closes and bites you.

**It is a timing minigame, not a shooter.** People expecting Resident Evil combat find it bizarre; taken on its own terms it is tense, because the bar speeds up as enemies get closer and panic genuinely costs you.

---

## Survival horror inventory

Limited slots, ink ribbons for saving, herbs for healing, keys and documents taking up the same space as ammunition. The series' inventory pressure survives intact and is arguably harsher here because the slots are fewer.

---

## Structure

The ship is explored deck by deck, unlocking as you find keys, with the usual Resident Evil texture of documents explaining what went wrong. There is a partner-swap element between Barry and Leon.

---

## Strategy

**Learn the bar rhythm per enemy type.** Each has a different cursor speed, and the difference between comfortable and dead is knowing which you are facing before the bar starts.

**Run past what you can.** Ammunition is scarce and most encounters are optional.

**Ration ink ribbons.** This is a short game, but a save wasted early is felt late.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs on Batocera's Game Boy Color core with **rewind enabled**, which takes most of the sting out of a mistimed combat bar.

Reviews at the time were mixed and it remains the odd one out in the series. It is short — a few hours — and it has never been re-released, so this is the only practical way to see it.
