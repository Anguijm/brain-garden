# The Legend of Zelda: Oracle of Ages (Game Boy Color)

**Developer:** Flagship (Capcom) | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 2001

---

## Introduction

Nintendo handed Zelda to Capcom, and Capcom's Flagship studio made two games at once. **Oracle of Ages** is the puzzle half; [Oracle of Seasons](topics/games/gbc/oracle-of-seasons) is the combat half. They released the same day and are designed to be played as a pair.

Ages is set in Labrynna. The oracle Nayru is possessed by Veran, Sorceress of Shadows, and the land's history starts being rewritten. Your tool is the **Harp of Ages**, which moves you between the present and four hundred years earlier.

This is the harder of the two games, and it is the one to play second if you only have appetite for one at a time.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move.
- **A / B:** Two equipped items, freely assigned — sword, shield, harp, seed satchel, whatever you are carrying.
- **Start:** Inventory. **Select:** Map.

Only **two** items can be equipped at once, and the game constantly asks you to swap. This is the defining friction of both Oracle games.

---

## Time travel, which is the whole game

The Harp of Ages jumps you between two eras at fixed portals, and later, once you have a better harp tune, anywhere.

**What you do in the past changes the present.** A sapling planted four hundred years ago is a full tree now. A boulder moved then is gone now. A character helped in the past remembers you in the present.

**The trap most people fall into:** treating the eras as two separate maps. They are one map at two moments. When a present-day route is blocked, the answer is almost always to travel back, change the terrain, and return — not to look harder in the present.

## Animal companions

Three companions exist across both games, and which one you get depends on secrets you find:

| Companion | Ability |
|---|---|
| **Ricky** (kangaroo) | Punches through obstacles, jumps cliffs |
| **Dimitri** (Dodongo) | Swims, survives waterfalls and rapids |
| **Moosh** (winged bear) | Hovers over gaps, ground-pounds |

---

## Linked games, and the real ending

Finish either Oracle game and you receive a **password**. Enter it when starting the other, and that game becomes a **linked game**: your rings carry over, characters reference what you already did, extra content appears, and the ending continues past the local villain to **Ganon himself**.

**Played unlinked, both games have truncated endings.** This is not a bonus, it is the intended shape of the story. Play one, take the password, play the other linked.

A third game was planned — the trilogy was to be linked three ways — and was cut because the password system could not carry it.

---

## Rings

Rings are passive upgrades found, bought and traded throughout. Some double damage taken and dealt, some change Link's form, some are pure curiosities. They store in a ring box that starts tiny. Linked games carry the whole collection over.

---

## Strategy

**Map the portals before you need them.** Knowing where you can cross eras converts most "stuck" moments into a short walk.

**Buy the seed satchel upgrades.** Seeds are ammunition, keys and puzzle tools all at once.

**Ages punishes brute force.** If a room is resisting, the answer is a mechanism, not a better sword.
