# Pokémon Crystal (Game Boy Color)

**Developer:** Game Freak | **Publisher:** Nintendo | **Year:** 2001

---

## Introduction

The definitive version of the second Pokémon generation. Crystal is to [Gold](topics/games/gbc/pokemon-gold) what Yellow was to Red and Blue: the same Johto adventure with everything added.

Three things make it the one to play. It is the **first Pokémon game that lets you play as a girl**. Every Pokémon gets an **animated entry sprite** rather than a static one. And there is a dedicated **Suicune storyline** running through the game, with the roaming legendary given an actual arc instead of a random encounter table.

Generation 2 is also, structurally, the largest Pokémon game ever made — and nothing since has repeated the trick.

---

## The thing Gold, Silver and Crystal do that nothing else does

You beat the eighth Johto gym, defeat the Elite Four, and the credits roll. **Then you get on a boat and the entire Kanto region from Red and Blue opens up.** Eight more gyms, sixteen badges total, and a final fight at the top of Mt. Silver against **Red** — the silent protagonist of the previous game, waiting at level 81.

No Pokémon game since has shipped two full regions. It was achieved through aggressive compression and it is why Kanto is somewhat thinner here than in Red and Blue, but it is still an entire second game bolted onto the end of the first.

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move. **A:** Confirm/interact. **B:** Cancel/run (hold).
- **Start:** Menu. **Select:** Register an item for quick use.

---

## Systems worth knowing

**The internal clock.** Generation 2 introduced a real-time clock. Some Pokémon appear only at night, others only in the morning; certain events fire on specific days of the week. On emulation the clock reads the host system's time, so this works normally.

**Held items.** Pokémon can carry an item into battle. This is the generation that introduced it, and it is central to competent play — Leftovers, berries, type-boosting items.

**The Dark and Steel types** arrive here, added specifically to check Psychic, which was overwhelming in Generation 1.

**Breeding.** Day Care produces eggs, which is how you get strong movesets and is a genuine time sink.

---

## Route

Eight Johto gyms — Falkner (Flying), Bugsy (Bug), Whitney (Normal), Morty (Ghost), Chuck (Fighting), Jasmine (Steel), Pryce (Ice), Clair (Dragon) — then Victory Road, the Elite Four, and Lance.

**Whitney's Miltank stops more runs than any other early gym in the series.** It uses Rollout, which doubles in power every consecutive turn, and Milk Drink to heal. Bring a Fighting type, or a Pokémon that can break the Rollout chain by forcing a switch.

After the credits: the boat to Vermilion, eight Kanto gyms, and Red.

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy Color core with **rewind enabled**.

**The clock behaves.** Generation 2 cartridges famously had a battery that eventually died and took the time system with it; emulated saves have no such battery, so the day/night cycle simply works forever.

**Trade evolutions still cannot happen alone.** Kadabra, Machoke, Graveler, Haunter and now Onix, Scyther, Poliwhirl and Seadra all evolve only by trading. Without link emulation the Pokédex cannot be completed on a single copy — worth knowing before committing forty hours.
