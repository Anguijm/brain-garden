# Alone in the Dark: The New Nightmare (Game Boy Color)

**Developer:** Pocket Studios | **Publisher:** Infogrames | **Year:** 2001

---

## Introduction

A survival horror game on a handheld with four buttons and no backlight, and it works better than it has any right to. This is a handheld reworking of the PlayStation and Dreamcast *New Nightmare*, not a straight port, rebuilt as a top-down adventure.

You play **Edward Carnby** or **Aline Cedrac** on Shadow Island, investigating a death and finding the usual Lovecraftian answer.

The technical achievement is the point of interest: pre-rendered backgrounds, a working light-and-dark mechanic, and atmosphere the hardware should not support.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move.
- **A:** Action / fire. **B:** Cancel.
- **Start:** Inventory and map.

---

## Light, which is the mechanic

Creatures here are made of darkness and **cannot be hurt by bullets alone**. Your torch is the actual weapon: light weakens them, and only then does gunfire finish them.

Batteries are finite. Leaving the torch on constantly runs you dry; keeping it off leaves you unable to fight. Managing that balance is what the game is about, and it is the same idea the console versions were built on, preserved intact on hardware with no lighting engine.

---

## Two characters

Edward and Aline follow different routes through the island with different puzzles. Edward's path is combat-weighted; Aline's leans on puzzles and documents. Playing both is how you get the whole story.

---

## Structure

Room-to-room exploration with keys, documents, inventory puzzles and periodic set pieces. Save points are fixed.

---

## Strategy

**Torch off while walking known corridors, on before entering anything new.** Battery discipline is the difficulty curve.

**Read the documents.** Puzzle solutions are in them, unmarked.

**Map as you go.** The island loops back on itself frequently and the in-game map is thin.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs on Batocera's Game Boy Color core with **rewind enabled**.

Worth knowing what it is before starting: this is a competent handheld horror game that impressed on release for what it squeezed out of the hardware, not a lost classic. The console version of *The New Nightmare* is a different and larger game.
