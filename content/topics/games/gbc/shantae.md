# Shantae (Game Boy Color)

**Developer:** WayForward | **Publisher:** Capcom | **Year:** 2002

---

## Introduction

Shantae arrived in **June 2002**, three months after the Game Boy Advance had already taken over, on a system nobody was making games for any more. Capcom printed a small run, it sold poorly, and it became one of the most technically accomplished games on the hardware that almost nobody played.

**Original cartridges now sell for several hundred dollars.** The game itself went on to spawn a series that is still running.

You play Shantae, a half-genie guarding Scuttle Town, chasing the pirate Risky Boots.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move; up/down on ladders.
- **A:** Jump.
- **B:** **Hair whip** — Shantae attacks by flipping her ponytail. Charge it for a spin.
- **Select / Start:** Items and dance menu.

---

## Dancing, which is how you transform

Shantae learns **belly dances**, each of which turns her into an animal with its own traversal ability:

| Dance | Form | Ability |
|---|---|---|
| Monkey | Monkey | Climbs walls, fits through small gaps |
| Elephant | Elephant | Smashes blocks, heavy |
| Spider | Spider | Walks on ceilings |
| Harpy | Harpy | Flies freely |

Each is a metroidvania key: areas seen early become reachable only once you have the right dance.

**The dances are input as a short rhythm on the D-pad**, which is charming the first time and tedious the fiftieth. Later games in the series replaced this.

---

## Day and night

The world runs on a clock. Towns behave differently after dark, some enemies only appear at night, and certain shops and characters are time-gated. **A warp squid or an inn lets you skip ahead** rather than waiting.

---

## Structure

Four labyrinths, each ending in a boss and granting a dance. Between them, an overworld that opens up as your movement grows, plus towns for shopping and story.

**The game is not generous with direction.** Townspeople hold the actual hints and the game expects you to talk to everyone, repeatedly, after every story beat.

---

## Strategy

**Buy the hair upgrades early.** Attack power is the difference between a fun labyrinth and a slog.

**Talk to everyone twice.** Dialogue changes as the plot moves and it is frequently the only signpost you get.

**Use warps.** The overworld is large and backtracking on foot eats the clock.

**When stuck, list the dances you have and what each one bypasses.** The answer is nearly always a form you have not thought to use.

---

## Emulation Notes

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy Color core with **rewind enabled**.

Technically it is showing off throughout: large multi-jointed sprites, parallax scrolling, and animation quality the hardware was not really meant to deliver. It looks best with the Game Boy Color's colour palette intact rather than through a green DMG shader.
