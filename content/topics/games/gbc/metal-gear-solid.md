# Metal Gear Solid (Game Boy Color)

**Developer:** Konami TDK / KCEJ | **Publisher:** Konami | **Year:** 2000

---

## Introduction

**This is not a port of the PlayStation game.** It shares a name in the West and almost nothing else. In Japan it is called *Metal Gear: Ghost Babel*, which is the honest title: an original side-story, built for the hardware, by people who understood what the hardware could do.

It is routinely ranked among the best games on the Game Boy Color, and among the best Metal Gear games full stop. Hideo Kojima supervised rather than directed.

Set seven years after the original *Metal Gear*, on Galuade — the rebuilt Outer Heaven.

---

## Controls

- **D-Pad:** Move.
- **A:** Use weapon. **B:** Use item.
- **Select:** Weapon/item menus. **Start:** Pause.
- **Hold a direction against a wall:** Press flat, then peek around corners.

The top-down view returns from the 8-bit Metal Gear games, but with the PlayStation game's alert system, codec conversations and boss design.

---

## Stealth, and the alert cascade

Guards have **vision cones** shown on the radar. Enter one and you are spotted.

Being seen triggers three stages:

1. **Alert** — guards converge on you, reinforcements arrive continuously.
2. **Evasion** — you have broken line of sight; they are searching.
3. **Caution** — they have given up but stay alert for a while.

**Fighting through an Alert is almost never correct.** Break line of sight, hide, and wait the cascade out. The game is generous about hiding places and stingy about ammunition.

---

## Tools

Cardboard box, chaff grenades (disable cameras and radar), thermal goggles, remote-guided missiles, C4, and a suppressed pistol. Every one has a specific puzzle it solves; none is a general answer.

**Chaff grenades are the most under-used item.** They blind the electronic half of the security system entirely for a few seconds.

---

## Structure

Thirteen missions, each a self-contained map with a distinct objective, connected by codec conversations. It is tighter and more level-based than the PlayStation game's continuous facility.

Boss fights are set-piece puzzles rather than shootouts — work out the mechanic, then execute it.

**There are also 180 VR missions** as a separate mode, which is a substantial game in itself.

---

## Strategy

**Watch the radar, not the screen.** Guard cones tell you everything; the sprite view tells you almost nothing.

**Knock on walls.** Tapping draws a guard to investigate, which is how you move a patrol off a route you need.

**Save ammunition for bosses.** Regular guards should be avoided, not shot.

**Take the codec calls.** They carry hints, and several are required to progress.

Runs perfectly on Batocera's Game Boy Color core with **rewind enabled**, which suits a stealth game where a single mistake cascades into a five-minute alert.

Never re-released on a modern platform, and absent from the Master Collection, so emulation is realistically the only way to play it.
