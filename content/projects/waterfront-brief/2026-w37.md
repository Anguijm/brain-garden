## Operations Maintenance & Readiness

### Destroyer modernization periods are landing on time, and the reason is the shape of the job

Two destroyers came out of their modernization periods early this summer, and the programme
behind them says it has been on time eleven times out of twelve. The reason is that the Navy
made the job smaller and gave it one owner, and both halves matter to anyone who plans an
availability.

FACT: the Navy announced on June 26, 2026 that USS Chung-Hoon (DDG 93) and USS James E. Williams
(DDG 95) had completed Depot Modernization Periods under the DDG Modernization 2.0 programme,
with General Dynamics NASSCO as the lead maintenance activity and the Mid-Atlantic and Southwest
Regional Maintenance Centers as the Navy supervisory activities, per the NAVSEA release carried
by DVIDS. The lead maintenance activity does the work; the supervisory activity is the Navy
office that oversees it. FACT: Naval News reported the same day that both ships were accepted ahead of schedule. A Depot
Modernization Period is a scheduled availability whose main purpose is installing new equipment
rather than repairing old.

The eleven-of-twelve figure is older than those deliveries. FACT: Vice Adm. John Gumbleton said
at the Combined Naval Event in June 2025 that "in [the new] construct, we have been on time 11 of
12 times, and the one outlier was two weeks late," and that the Navy had "made the conscious
decision to prioritize predictability over deferring modernization," per Naval News's June 2026
report of the remark. FACT: Naval News's account of that keynote records that the approach grew
out of the Ticonderoga cruiser experience, puts a single accountable individual in charge, and
uses two 150-day periods.

FACT: USNI News reported in January 2025 that the first four
ships, Pinckney, James E. Williams, Chung-Hoon and Halsey, receive the upgrade in two availability
phases, the SLQ-32(V)7 electronic warfare suite first, after which Capt. Moore said they serve in
the fleet "for some time" before a second Depot Modernization Period installs the SPY-6 radar,
and that USS Sterett will be the first to take the whole package in a single availability "within
the next five years." FACT: Naval News's June 2026 description puts the radar in the second step for all four, with
the first step complete on three and under way on Halsey. FACT: USNI News reported in January 2023 that a Navy official put the time to install all three
systems on a ship at 18 to 24 months.

**The construct exists because the last modernization programme failed.** That programme was
the cruisers. The plan was to take the Ticonderoga-class cruisers out of service in turn, rebuild
each one over four years, and return them to the fleet. FACT: GAO reported in 2025 that the Navy
planned to modernize eleven cruisers and now expects to return only three, per GAO-25-106749.
FACT: the same report says the five cruisers that reached their modernization periods cost about
\$881 million more than the planned \$2.44 billion, a 36 percent increase, and that each ran three
to nearly five years past its four-year plan. Every cruiser that started was late. Under DDG MOD
2.0, on Gumbleton's count, the worst destroyer was two weeks late. Attack submarines are the
other reference point, because they are the Navy's other large depot programme and the one this
brief has followed. FACT: GAO reported in August 2026 that the public shipyards completed about
11 percent of attack submarine depot maintenance on time from fiscal years 2016 through 2025, per
GAO-26-109256, the report No. 7 covered.

The construct is not the only way to a date, and the government yards are naming their own
mechanics. FACT: SRF-JRMC in Yokosuka delivered USS Preble (DDG 88) on schedule on August 24
after an approximately \$33 million maintenance and modernization package, per the command's DVIDS release of September 9; NAVSEA describes that command as "a
medium-sized, full-service, non-nuclear naval shipyard," per its page on working in Japan. FACT: Pearl Harbor Naval Shipyard undocked USS Illinois on June 24 after "front-loading
key milestones to significantly speed up the final pier-side certification process," with the
project supervisor, Gilbert Gould, saying that "by maximizing productivity and testing in dry
dock, the project is on track to complete the shortest end game that the shipyard has performed
to date," per NAVSEA's July 7 release; the yard then undocked USS North Carolina on time on
August 12, per its August 12 release. The end game is the pier-side testing and certification that follows undocking.

Assessment: what DDG MOD 2.0 changed is not the yards or the trades but the size of the promise.
The first period carries the electronic warfare suite and the cooling for what comes later; the radar, the largest part of a package USNI's 2023 figure says takes up to two years to
install, has not been installed on any of the four two-step ships. Eleven of twelve is a count of the small periods. The prediction is that the rate falls when the radar periods begin, because replacing a ship's
main sensor is a job the size of a cruiser rebuild, and that it does not fall to the cruiser
record, because the two things the Navy says it changed after the cruisers, scope fixed before
the ship arrives and one person who owns the whole job, are the two things that decide whether a
period ends on its date.

**Bottom line:** plan the next availability as a fixed-length block with its scope locked
before the ship arrives, put growth that is not safety or certification into a defined later
period, and ask which pier-side tests could move into the dock phase; a DDG MOD 2.0 hull at your
pier between periods carries a pending radar period, so ask the planning yard which step it is in
before the package is written.

### Puget Sound this month: one submarine dock back, one about to empty

Two of the three submarine docks at Bremerton that the Navy closed on earthquake risk in 2023
change state this month, and that moves the queue for every attack boat waiting on the West
Coast.

FACT: USNI News reported on January 27, 2023 that the Navy had temporarily closed four docks used
to overhaul nuclear submarines in Washington, three at Puget Sound Naval Shipyard and Intermediate
Maintenance Facility and the delta pier at the Trident Refit Facility in Bangor, after finding them at risk of earthquake damage.
FACT: the Navy announced on August 28, 2026 that it marked completion of the Dry Dock 4 seismic
retrofit on August 26, that the original scope ran five months ahead of schedule, that One Big Beautiful Bill Act funds took the contract from \$377.7 million to \$517 million for
added work, and that modeling cut the anchors needed from an estimated 1,000 to 600. FACT: the same release says the added funds paid to remove 500 decommissioned keel blocks,
giving the yard laydown space.

The other dock has held one boat for three years. FACT: NAVSEA told Naval News in August 2023
that USS Connecticut (SSN 22) was in Dry Dock 5 at Puget Sound in an Extended Docking Selected
Restricted Availability, docked on July 12, 2023. FACT: a Navy spokesperson told The War Zone in
July 2025 that the availability formally began in February 2023, that the October 2021 grounding
damage was being repaired inside it, and that the boat was expected back in late 2026; the same
report notes the Navy had told Naval News in March 2023 that the availability was "notionally
sized at 31 months." FACT: the New Haven Register reported on June 1, 2026 that the boat was expected to resume
service in September, and that the Navy's shipbuilding plan published in May set a 2031
retirement date, which that plan lists as recycling in 2031, per the Navy's May 2026 long-range
shipbuilding plan. An Extended Docking Selected Restricted Availability is a submarine's deepest
scheduled maintenance short of an overhaul; the collision repair was folded into it.



Assessment: Bremerton gains submarine docking capacity twice in one quarter, a retrofitted dock
on August 26 and, if the Register's September date holds, an emptied one after 38 months with a
single hull in it against a plan of 31. The prediction is
that a docking is moved earlier at Puget Sound this autumn, that the attack boat which had been
waiting pier-side moves first, and that GAO's idle-day count, 3,454 days across fiscal 2021 to
2025, shows the change only in fiscal 2027 data, because the days already lost are booked. The
risk is that the two docks arrive together, so two dockings' worth of pre-docking work lands on
the same planning staff at once.

**Bottom line:** if your shop feeds Puget Sound, whether from the Northwest RMC, the Everett
detachment or a contractor, expect two dockings' worth of pre-docking work in the same window
this autumn and staff the planning side before the hulls move; and take the keel-block lesson
home, because 500 idle blocks were laydown space the yard did not know it had.

## Maintenance Engineering & Technologies

### The tank you never enter: a camera on a stalk is going to all seven regional maintenance centers

A tank inspection tool that keeps people out of the tank is being fielded across the surface
fleet's maintenance centers this fiscal year, and the gain is in planning before it is in safety.

FACT: the Naval Research Laboratory said on May 29, 2026 that it has fielded the next generation
of its Insertable Stalk Inspection System, ISIS360, a non-entry inspection technology that is
"collapsing inspection timelines from days or weeks to hours," and that it is working with
Commander, Navy Regional Maintenance Center to expand it to seven regional maintenance centers in
fiscal 2026, per the laboratory's DVIDS release. FACT: the same release says tank inspections were historically done on a fixed interval, with the
tank emptied, cleaned and prepared for entry regardless of condition, that entry required a
gas-free certificate, lighting and scaffolding, and that aboard USS America in March 2025, 64
tanks were inspected, 49 of them with ISIS360, in many cases without gas-free certification or
entry. A gas-free certificate is the sign-off that a confined space is safe to enter, and getting
one is a scheduled evolution with its own people.

FACT: NRL says the system's algorithm returns the percentage of surface corroded, that a
fuel-safe configuration inspects fuel tanks without emptying them, and that results export to the Surface Maintenance Engineering Planning Program, SURFMEPP.
FACT: SURFMEPP "provides centralized life cycle maintenance engineering, as well as class
maintenance and modernization planning" for surface ships, per GAO-25-106990. FACT: NRL's corrosion branch head, Ted Lemieux, said "the real savings come in improved
planning," preventing "surprises that turn into schedule delays."

FACT: Southeast RMC said on May 7, 2026 that it fielded ISIS360 on USS Iwo Jima in April 2025,
imaging 31 tanks, that it had used the original ISIS "about 10 years ago," and that Mid-Atlantic
RMC led the effort, per the command's DVIDS release. FACT: SRF-JRMC said on July 9, 2026
that NRL and CNRMC delivered the system to Yokosuka and Sasebo in May, that its corrosion control
branch head, Kouji Koizumi, called it "permanently adopted," and that "significant defects still
require a follow-up human-entry assessment," which he called "relatively rare"; the release's photo caption shows a locally built grating over
the void opening so the stalk goes in without a fall hazard.

Assessment: the tool changes the question from "which tanks are due" to "which tanks failed the
camera," and that moves the tank line of a work package from the availability into the window
before it. It is a coating and corrosion tool; nothing in the releases says it gauges plate
thickness, so structural condition still needs entry or ultrasonic testing. The prediction is that centers which run the camera pier-side before the package locks see
their tank scope shrink and their gas-free schedule compress within a year, and that the
availabilities which still grow from tank findings are at yards that run it after arrival.

**Bottom line:** run the camera during continuous maintenance or the pier-side window before the
availability, not on day one of it, so the tanks that fail are in the package and the tanks that
pass are out of it; build the grating fixture before the equipment arrives, and size the gas-free
and entry team for the fails rather than for every tank.

## Waterfront Ops & Production

### The expeditionary repair capacity No. 2 said to watch has been packed into a container

The mobile repair capability this brief flagged in July now exists as a loaded container with a
72-hour standard attached to it, and the first rehearsal on the Pacific side was in April.

FACT: SIMA Norfolk said on July 22, 2026 that its sailors loaded a container with industrial plant
equipment, piping, metal stock and other material for transport to the 6th Fleet area to support
an Expeditionary Repair Availability during the Ship Wartime Repair and Maintenance Exercise
scheduled for August, that the work supports the Navy's Expeditionary Maintenance and Repair
Container initiative to pre-stage standardized repair packages, and that "the long-term objective
is to position expeditionary maintenance capabilities that can arrive within 72 hours," per the
command's DVIDS release. A Shore Intermediate Maintenance Activity is the sailor-manned repair shop at a homeport, one
level below a depot; an Expeditionary Repair Availability is a repair period outside the
maintenance plan.

FACT: U.S. Pacific Fleet said on April 4, 2026 that USS Ashland completed a Ship Wartime Repair
and Maintenance Exercise in the Philippines, with the ship's commanding officer, Cmdr. Adam
Peeples, describing "complex repairs" conducted "shoulder-to-shoulder with our Philippine allies"
while the ship stayed ready to respond. No. 2 closed its battle-damage article with the line that
"the near-term moves worth watching are mobile and expeditionary repair capacity and firm
allied-yard agreements."

Assessment: the container is a shop leaving its building, and the 72-hour standard is a packing
list before it is a skills problem. Results from the August exercise are not published, so
nothing here says the standard was met.
The prediction is that the next container announced is a Pacific one, because the April rehearsal
was in 7th Fleet and the Atlantic exercise now has a template, and that the first argument it
produces is about which qualified people go with it.

**Bottom line:** list the machines and the qualifications in your shop that would have to leave
with a container, and write down what the shop cannot do for the weeks they are gone; that list is
the expeditionary plan, and it is also the case for a second machine.

*Sources: [SIMA Norfolk via DVIDS, Jul 22, 2026](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/570402/sima-norfolk-establishes-expeditionary-maintenance-capability-advance-fleet-readiness); [U.S. Pacific Fleet, Apr 4, 2026](https://www.cpf.navy.mil/Newsroom/News/Article/4452493/uss-ashland-completes-ship-wartime-repair-and-maintenance-exercise-in-philippin/); [The Waterfront Brief No. 2](2026-w30)*

### Six things to watch

Six developments that are not yet stories.

FACT: the August 13 presidential memorandum directs a plan, due within 90 days, to establish a
Component Repair Center "to receive new and refurbish old components and parts as required by the
submarine repair industrial base," holding at least one ship set of spares for five submarine
classes, per the White House text. That puts the plan at about November 11.

FACT: the President signed H.R. 6500 on September 2, a continuing resolution funding the
government through December 11, 2026, per the White House. FACT: Inside Defense reported that it
keeps spending largely at fiscal 2026 levels while letting the Navy obligate nearly \$2.61 billion
above that rate for cost growth on ships already under contract, and section 126 of the enrolled
text lists those lines by programme, the largest being \$566,542,000 for Columbia-class submarines,
\$561,131,000 for Virginia-class submarines and \$379,200,000 for carrier refueling overhauls. No
line covers ship maintenance.

FACT: The Korea Times reported on September 10 that DAPA vice minister Kim Il-dong said Korean
shipbuilders have won 12 US Navy maintenance contracts since 2024, five this year, and that "MRO
is not an end in itself, but a gateway" to combat-vessel maintenance and shipbuilding. FACT: Naval
News reported on September 10 that Mitsubishi Heavy Industries' existing commitments leave it
"very limited spare capacity" to support a US frigate build, and The Maritime Executive reported
on September 1 that HD Hyundai's Ulsan yard entered its fourth consecutive year of strikes.

FACT: The Maritime Executive reported on September 7 that Austal confirmed talks with a US
investment firm, Wildcat Infrastructure, and later that Austal's September 9 filing put Wildcat's
non-binding indication at \$1.25 to \$1.35 billion enterprise value, against Hanwha's earlier
offer.

FACT: Michael Cadenazzi, assistant secretary of war for industrial base policy, told a House
appropriations field hearing on September 4 that the defense industrial base has about 400,000
unfilled skilled-trade jobs, a figure that could reach 2.5 million within a decade, per DoD News
on September 8.

Assessment: decide which component work you would keep before the center names a site; under
the CR, ship maintenance runs at last year's rate through the first quarter of the year No. 8
said would need more; Korea's count is the allied-capacity number to plan against, and Japan's
answer is why the count is Korea's; the Austal story runs when a binding offer exists; and the
trades figure is national, so read it as the pool your pipeline draws from, not your gap.

**Bottom line:** put November 11 and December 11 on the calendar, settle before the first which
component refurbishment stays in-house, and expect no new maintenance money before the second.
