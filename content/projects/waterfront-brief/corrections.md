# Corrections

This page opens on 2026-09-19. It lists every correction the editor has ruled on a
released issue of The Waterfront Brief since that day, newest first, and it records the
two earlier changes made to released issues without a ruling. A released issue is frozen:
nothing in it changes on the desk's own judgment, not a fact, not a label, not a comma. A
correction happens only when the editor rules that a claim was wrong. It then shows in
three places: a dated editor's note directly under the issue's opening paragraph, an entry
here, and the print edition re-rendered so the note prints.

Two changes were made before this policy existed, by the desk and without a ruling. They
are recorded here because the policy now forbids exactly that. On 2026-09-18 the desk
applied a cosmetic spacing fix to all ten released issues, No. 1 to No. 10. On 2026-09-19
the desk changed three claims in No. 1 and No. 8 and re-rendered the print edition of all
ten issues, to get an unrelated publish through a check. The editor then ruled to keep the
three claim changes; they are the three entries dated 2026-09-19 below, each with its
dated note in the issue. The re-rendered print editions of the other eight issues had no
ruling, and this paragraph is their record.

## 2026-11-25, No. 8 (week 36): North Dakota "first ever", the boat count, and the sea-trials date

- **Issue:** [Vol. 1, No. 8, week 36](projects/waterfront-brief/2026-w36)
- **What changed:** In the Block III submarine maintenance article, three statements, plus the standfirst and headline wording that carried the first one.
- **Before:** "completing the first depot-level maintenance period ever performed on a Block III Virginia-class submarine" (headline and standfirst: "the first Block III submarine maintenance cycle")
- **After:** "completing its first depot-level maintenance period on a Block III Virginia-class submarine" (headline: "its first"; standfirst: "Portsmouth's first"; series index entry, changed the same day: "its first")
- **Why:** The Navy's own release (https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/News-Stories/display-news/Article/4586562/pnsy-returns-north-dakota-to-the-fleet/) calls it "PNSY's first" such availability on a Block III boat. Norfolk Naval Shipyard had docked USS John Warner earlier, which the Navy calls the first Block III dry docking at any public shipyard (https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/News-Stories/display-news/Article/4450770/norfolk-naval-shipyard-undocks-uss-john-warner/).
- **Before:** "The 11 Virginia-class boats built before USS North Dakota"
- **After:** "The 10 Virginia-class boats built before USS North Dakota"
- **Why:** North Dakota is the 11th Virginia-class boat; the issue's own source says "the first ten" used the older bow (https://www.armyrecognition.com/news/navy-news/2026/uss-north-dakota-ssn-784-rejoins-navy-gao-submarine-crisis).
- **Before:** "The boat completed sea trials and pier-side work by August 20"
- **After:** "The boat left Portsmouth for sea trials on August 20 and reached New London on August 27"
- **Why:** The same source says the boat departed for sea trials on Aug. 20 and arrived at Naval Submarine Base New London on Aug. 27. Found while re-checking No. 8 against its sources for No. 20's North Dakota article.
- **Ruled by:** the editor, 2026-11-25 (tap "Correct all three").

## 2026-11-20, No. 19 (week 47): Senate repair money article, five statements tightened

- **Issue:** [Vol. 1, No. 19, week 47](projects/waterfront-brief/2026-w47)
- **What changed:** After release, a sentence-by-sentence audit against the staged sources found five statements stronger than their sources. All five are reworded; no figure changed.
- **Before:** a new Senate report would show it only for money from fiscal 2024 on / Its by-ship funding table covers the current year and the three before it, so it would show ship repair's money from fiscal 2024 on / explanatory statement, released Nov. 16 / it is not out of line with the other services / a late bill on closed money is paid out of money meant for this year's work / about 60 percent more unpaid dollars
- **After:** a new Senate report would track repair spending by ship, not what is left to cancel / The report counts money spent by ship during the current year and the three before it / explanatory statement for the 2027 defense bill (archived Nov. 18) / across the whole Navy operations account, of which ship depot maintenance is one part ... as a share it ran below the Army and Air Force / can be charged to money meant for this year's work, up to the 1 percent cap / about 55 to 65 percent more unpaid dollars
- **Why:** The Senate statement limits the report's period, not the year of the money; 31 U.S.C. 1553(b) says a late bill "may be charged," capped at 1 percent; the Nov. 16 date was the desk's own file label, not in the document; the Treasury figures cover the whole account, not ship depot maintenance; the exact rises are 54 and 64 percent.
- **Ruled by:** the editor, 2026-11-20.

## 2026-10-24, No. 11 (week 39): who owns intermediate maintenance

- **Issue:** [Vol. 1, No. 11, week 39](projects/waterfront-brief/2026-w39)
- **What changed:** In the Mayport SIMA article, the Assessment no longer says seven recommendations are unstarted and nobody is designated to own intermediate maintenance periods; it now says GAO's September 2024 count had seven recommendations still open and no single owner designated.
- **Before:** Nothing that would move it is in place: seven recommendations unstarted, and nobody designated to own how these periods perform.
- **After:** GAO's September 2024 count, the one this article relied on, had seven recommendations still open and no single owner designated for these periods.
- **Why:** The present tense was out of date when the issue ran, and "unstarted" overstated GAO's "not implemented". GAO's recommendation page (https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-22-104510) lists the 2022 single-owner recommendation as closed: in December 2025 the Navy issued OPNAVINST 4700.7N, with NAVSEA as lead, though GAO notes "it still appears that multiple entities share responsibility". GAO's page for the sailor-maintenance report (https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-106525) shows one of its seven recommendations closed on Navy documentation from September 2025.
- **Ruled by:** the editor, 2026-10-24.
