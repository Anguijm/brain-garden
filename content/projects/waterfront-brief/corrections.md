# Corrections

This page opens on 2026-09-19. It lists every correction the editor has ruled on a
released issue of The Waterfront Brief since that day, newest first, and it records the
two earlier changes made to released issues without a ruling. A released issue is frozen:
nothing in it changes on the desk's own judgment, not a fact, not a label, not a comma. A
correction happens only when the editor rules that a claim was wrong. It then shows in
three places: a dated editor's note directly under the issue's opening paragraph, an entry
here, and the print edition re-rendered so the note prints.

Two changes were made before this policy existed, by the desk and without a ruling. They
are recorded here because the policy now forbids exactly that. On 2026-09-18 the desk
applied a cosmetic spacing fix to all ten released issues, No. 1 to No. 10. On 2026-09-19
the desk changed three claims in No. 1 and No. 8 and re-rendered the print edition of all
ten issues, to get an unrelated publish through a check. The editor then ruled to keep the
three claim changes; they are the three entries dated 2026-09-19 below, each with its
dated note in the issue. The re-rendered print editions of the other eight issues had no
ruling, and this paragraph is their record.

## 2026-09-19, No. 8 (week 36): the Block III boat count credited to Navy documentation

- **Issue:** [Vol. 1, No. 8, week 36](projects/waterfront-brief/2026-w36)
- **What changed:** In the Block III submarine maintenance article, the eight-boat figure is now attributed "per Army Recognition's reporting" instead of "per Navy program documentation".
- **Before:** eight submarines share the Block III configuration, starting with USS North Dakota, per Navy program documentation.
- **After:** eight submarines share the Block III configuration, starting with USS North Dakota, per Army Recognition's reporting.
- **Why:** The only source this desk read for that figure was an Army Recognition news analysis, which is trade press, not a Navy document. The claim now runs at that report's strength; a figure that comes only from trade press is written as trade press.
- **Ruled by:** the editor, 2026-09-19.

## 2026-09-19, No. 1 (week 29): the year read into the Gunsan Shipyard sale date

- **Issue:** [Vol. 1, No. 1, week 29](projects/waterfront-brief/2026-w29)
- **What changed:** In the Korea repair-bench article, the closing date of the Gunsan Shipyard sale now reads "June 26, with no year" instead of "June 26, 2026".
- **Before:** The Maritime Executive reports (undated page; the sale it describes closed June 26, 2026) that Korea's Gunsan Shipyard will build ships again after nine years as the new J Ocean Heavy Industries ...
- **After:** The Maritime Executive reports (undated page; it gives the sale's closing date only as June 26, with no year) that Korea's Gunsan Shipyard will build ships again after nine years as the new J Ocean Heavy Industries ...
- **Why:** The page says only "June 26" and never gives the year; this desk supplied 2026. The page's own context suggests the year is probably not 2026, since it describes HJ Heavy Industries' January to March 2026 work on USNS Amelia Earhart as current. An undated page is called undated, and the desk never infers a year.
- **Ruled by:** the editor, 2026-09-19.

## 2026-09-19, No. 1 (week 29): the date read into an undated Maritime Executive page (Romulus production)

- **Issue:** [Vol. 1, No. 1, week 29](projects/waterfront-brief/2026-w29)
- **What changed:** In the article on HII spreading Romulus production to small yards, the parenthetical dating the undated Maritime Executive page now says "no year appears anywhere on it" instead of "internal references put it mid-2026".
- **Before:** The Maritime Executive reports (undated page; internal references put it mid-2026) that HII has contracted a second Louisiana workboat yard, Halimar Shipyard of Morgan City, to expand production of its Romulus 151 unmanned surface vessel ...
- **After:** The Maritime Executive reports (undated page; no year appears anywhere on it) that HII has contracted a second Louisiana workboat yard, Halimar Shipyard of Morgan City, to expand production of its Romulus 151 unmanned surface vessel ...
- **Why:** The page's only time markers are "in May" and "earlier this year", with no year anywhere; mid-2026 was this desk's inference, presented as if the page carried it. A parenthetical that looks like dating diligence is itself a claim, and an undated page is called undated.
- **Ruled by:** the editor, 2026-09-19.
